What changed, and why

The eight-day plan needed days the calendar doesn't have anymore. These are the swaps; everything else on the menu stands.

Duck does the confit's job

Turkey leg: 24 h cure + 3½ h confit + overnight press
Pressed duck confit — D'Artagnan, fully cooked, from Whole Foods

Warm the legs 10 minutes to loosen, pull the meat, press it in a lined loaf pan Friday afternoon, unmold and portion Saturday, sear at 7:35. Same crisp block on the plate, zero cure time. The menu reads "turkey & duck." If the store has no confit, the main is roast breast alone and the stuffing carries the table — still a good plate.

Truffle butter instead of fresh

1 oz fresh white Alba, Sabatino, shaved tableside
Black truffle butter whipped into the pommes purée; Truffle Zest at the table if the store has it

Sabatino ships Monday–Thursday only; a Friday order lands Monday. Whole Foods and The Fresh Market carry the shelf products. Purée is the right place for it anyway — fat and heat are what carry truffle. Menu line: "pommes purée, black truffle."

Stock is bought

Roasted wings and necks, 4-hour stock on Wednesday
3 quarts of bone broth; the gravy gets its turkey flavor from the roasting-pan drippings and the wine deglaze

A four-hour simmer can't run while you're at a concert. The velouté and stuffing won't know the difference; the gravy will taste like the bird because it's finished in its pan.

24-hour dry brine

48 hours, from Thursday night
Breast into the brine the minute the order lands (~2:30 Friday) — about 24 hours by roast time

Still seasons to the bone and still dries the skin. Keep it uncovered on a rack in the fridge the whole time.

Pies move to Saturday morning

Pecan Friday, apple Saturday 8 am
Crusts rolled Friday; apple in the oven at 7:20, pecan right behind it; both done by 9:45 while the cleaners work elsewhere

Apple still cools its four hours by 12:30. Pecan rests nine hours instead of a night — it sets fine. The oven is hands-off, so the cleaners aren't in your way.
