Plating plan

Seven courses, five plates each. What goes where, in what order, and how to build five at once so the first plate isn't cold when the fifth is done. Warm plates for anything hot, cold plates for anything cold, and the rim stays clean on every one.

Odd numbers, off center

Three slices, not four. The pile of salad a little off center. Nothing sits dead in the middle except the soup.

Height beats spread

Stack and lean. A low, wide plate reads as a cafeteria; a tight composition with one tall element reads as a restaurant.

Sauce under or over, never around

Gravy goes on the breast. Dressing goes on the leaves before they land. No moats.

Wipe, then walk

A damp side towel around the rim of every plate before it leaves the pass. It takes two seconds and it's the thing people notice without knowing why.

board · kitchen counter · 6:30
I

Gougères, cranberry & chili jam

Vessel: a wooden board or a warm platter · temp: gougères warm from the 6:25 re-crisp · where: the kitchen counter, not the table
  1. Jam into a small bowl with a teaspoon, set at one end of the board. Not the center.
  2. Gougères piled loosely on the other two-thirds, three or four high. They should look abundant, not arranged.
  3. Napkins beside the board. Nobody sits for this; people eat them standing with the spritz in the other hand.
Timing: board out at 6:28; first guests at 6:30. Keep five back on a tray in case the last arrival is late — they'll still be fine at 6:50.
warm shallow bowl · poured at the table · 7:00
II

Butternut velouté

Vessel: five warm low bowls (the Coupe White) · temp: bowls warmed under hot tap water and dried, soup just below a simmer, pitcher pre-warmed · at the table: the pour
  1. 6:15, at the pass: a teaspoon of lemon walnut cream at 7 o'clock in each empty bowl. Two fried sage leaves crossed on top of it.
  2. A pinch of pepitas around the sour cream. Four drops of brown butter from a teaspoon. A short thread of walnut cream across the opposite side.
  3. 6:50: velouté into the warmed pitcher. Taste one more time; it should pour like heavy cream, not coat the spoon.
  4. 7:00: empty bowls go down. Pour at each place from the side away from the garnish — about ¾ cup — so the garnish floats up intact instead of being drowned. Pour low and slow; the pitcher's lip an inch above the bowl.
Why it works: the bowl arrives looking finished and the soup arrives as a gesture. The first ten seconds of the dinner are this pour. Don't rush it.
cold salad plate · dressed 5 min before · 7:20
III

Shaved sprouts, apple, pecorino, hazelnut

Vessel: five small plates, from the fridge · temp: cold plate, cold salad · dress: 7:35, not sooner
  1. 7:15: sprouts and apple matchsticks in a big bowl, vinaigrette over, toss with your hands until every strand is coated. No pooling in the bowl — if there's dressing left at the bottom, you used too much.
  2. Lift a handful with fingertips and set it down on the plate slightly left of center, letting it fall tall. Pull a few strands up at the top. Don't press it.
  3. Five or six pecorino shavings tucked into the pile at different angles — some standing, some lying.
  4. Crushed hazelnuts scattered from a height over the top and onto the plate around it. Three parsley leaves.
  5. Wipe the rim. Five plates in four minutes.
The look: a small, tall, green nest with pale cheese and brown nuts showing through. Small plate, small portion — this is a pause between the soup and the main, not a course to fill up on.
warm dinner plate · five in eight minutes · 8:00
IV

Turkey two ways, purée, beans, cranberry, gravy

Vessel: five dinner plates from the warming drawer (170°F oven from 7:15 if no drawer) · temp: hot plates, hot food · center of table: the stuffing in its 9×13 on a folded towel, the sauceboat, the cranberry bowl, big spoons
  1. Line up all five plates. Do each step across all five before moving to the next — that's how the fifth plate is as hot as the first.
  2. Purée: a heaped large spoon set down at 9 o'clock. Press the back of the spoon into it and pull in one motion in an arc toward 4 o'clock, lifting at the end. A thick comma. If it breaks, don't fix it — scrape and redo.
  3. Breast: three slices, overlapping like fallen dominoes, laid along the swoosh from its thick end. Cut side up so the rosy edge shows.
  4. Thigh: the seared block leaning against the end of the breast at about 5 o'clock, crisp skin facing up and toward the diner. It should look like it was set down, not placed.
  5. Beans: seven or eight, all pointing the same way, in a tight bundle at 1 o'clock, almonds on top.
  6. Cranberry: one quenelle at 11 o'clock, on the plate, not on the purée. Two spoons dipped in hot water, pass the sauce back and forth three times, slide it off.
  7. Gravy: a spoonful over the breast slices only — let it run onto the purée, never onto the thigh skin. A sauceboat goes to the table for people who want more.
  8. Wipe every rim. Plates down. Then the stuffing dish, sauceboat and cranberry bowl to the center. Sit. Pass the stuffing.
The stuffing † goes out in the dish it was baked in, uncovered, top crisp, with a big serving spoon standing in it. That is the whole presentation and it is correct. Don't portion it onto plates.
Order at the pass, 7:35–7:50: gravy finishing on the burner → thigh blocks into the cast iron → beans into the pan → carve the breast while the thighs sear → plate in the order above. Fifteen minutes if everything is at the pass before you start.
frosted highball · four minutes of life · 9:00
V

Apple cider granita

Vessel: five highballs, washed after the spritz and back in the freezer since 7:15 · temp: frozen glass, frozen ice · under it: a small plate with a folded napkin
  1. 8:55: dish out of the freezer. Rake it with a fork one last time until it's all loose flakes.
  2. Highballs out. Three tablespoons into each, piled loosely — a mound, not pressed, a few drops of bourbon over. The flakes should catch light against the frosted glass.
  3. A thin apple slice standing in the top if there's one left from the salad. Teaspoon on the plate beside it.
  4. Walk them out immediately and sit down. Three sentences of introduction, maximum — it melts.
Why it's here: after the richest plate of the night, cold and tart resets everyone's palate so the pie tastes like pie. It's a four-minute course on purpose.
warm dessert plate · one proper slice · 9:10
VI

Apple pie

Vessel: five dessert plates, warmed · temp: pie warm (8 min at 300°F on a tray from 8:55) or room temperature, scoop and quenelle cold · knife: a thin one, wiped between cuts
  1. Cut the pie into eight. A proper wedge — this is the one dessert, it can carry the plate.
  2. Slice laid from just past center out toward 10 o'clock, point toward the center, crust edge to the rim. Lift with a thin spatula so the bottom crust stays whole.
  3. A small scoop of oat vanilla at 2 o'clock, not touching the slice. Scoop with a spoon dipped in hot water for a clean ball.
  4. A quenelle of whipped coconut cream at 5 o'clock, on the plate, in the open space.
  5. Nothing on the pie. Wipe the rim.
The pie † is cut and served, not plated into something else. No crumbles, no reconstruction. The plate is just how it arrives.
at the machine · made to order · 9:30
VII

Espresso from the Bambino

Vessel: espresso cups warmed on top of the machine · where: the kitchen — this course moves people off the table · beside it: small glasses for bourbon
  1. Take orders at the table — espresso, latte, decaf if you have it — then stand up and make them. People will follow you.
  2. Each cup on its saucer with a small spoon; lattes in the taller cups with the oat milk steamed tight and glossy.
  3. Bourbon poured a finger deep in small glasses, set next to whoever says yes. The last of the prosecco for whoever wants that instead.
This is the loose course. No plating rules. The point is that the dinner ends in the kitchen, standing, with the machine hissing — the opposite of the poured soup three hours earlier.

The pass

Tools, laid out by 6:00

  • Two large spoons (purée swoosh) and two small spoons standing in a mug of hot water (quenelles, ice cream)
  • Fish spatula and tongs; thin slicing knife; carving board with a juice groove
  • Warmed pitcher for the velouté; warmed sauceboat; ladle
  • Six damp side towels, folded, one per course
  • Instant-read thermometer (the bird at 6:15 and again before it comes out)

Plates

  • Warm: five low bowls (hot tap water, dried, 6:10) · five dinner plates (warming drawer or 170°F oven from 7:15) · five dessert plates (same, from 8:45)
  • Cold: five salad plates (fridge from 3:30) · six highballs with a rosemary sprig frozen to the wall (spritz on arrival, from 1:45) · the same highballs washed at 7:15 and re-frozen for the granita
  • Five small plates and napkins under the granita glasses

The service rhythm

  1. 7:00pour the soup, sit
  2. 7:15clear bowls to the pass; cut the salad apple
  3. 7:20dress, plate, serve the salad, sit
  4. 7:35clear; gravy, thighs, beans, carve — 15 minutes at the pass with the kitchen door open and the music up
  5. 8:00five mains plated in one pass; stuffing dish, sauceboat, cranberry bowl out; serve at 8:05, sit
  6. 8:55clear; granita into the frozen highballs; serve at 9:00, sit
  7. 8:45granita, sit — four minutes
  8. 9:10pie sliced and plated, coconut cream whipped; serve at 9:15, sit
  9. 9:30coffee orders; the room moves to the kitchen

If you're plating alone

  • Carry two plates at a time, serve women first if that's the table's style, or clockwise from the guest on your right — pick one and don't think about it again
  • Plates sit on the pass under a loose sheet of foil for the two minutes it takes to deliver the first four; the warming drawer did its job
  • Clearing: never stack at the table. Two plates in, two plates out, every trip
  • Introduce each course in one sentence as the last plate goes down, then sit. The menu card does the rest
