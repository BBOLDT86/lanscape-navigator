The idea

Precision on top, grandmother underneath.

Restaurant technique is mostly about timing, temperature, and finishing — not about reinventing what's on the plate. So the whole menu is Thanksgiving: squash, sage, turkey, cranberry, brussels sprouts, cider, apples, hazelnuts. What changes is the delivery. The soup is passed through a sieve and poured at the table. The turkey comes two ways from one spatchcocked bird: the breast sliced, the thigh seared skin-side in cast iron until it shatters. Plates are warm. Courses arrive one at a time with a pause between them.

Then the counterweight: the stuffing goes to the middle of the table in the dish it was baked in, and the pies are the pies. The contrast is the point. Guests should feel they ate at a restaurant and at home in the same sitting.

Everything on this page is sized for five, with a spare. The day's clock lives on the dashboard; this page is the meal itself — what each course is, why it's there, and how the plate is built. By 6:30 pm the only live cooking left is a pour, a sear, a carve and a bake.

Held firm †

  • The old stuffing — your card, your bread, your dish. Baseline recipe included below only so the shopping list and oven schedule are complete.
  • Apple pie — double crust, baked at midday, cooled all afternoon so it slices clean. The only pie tonight.
The Michelin touches on these two are limited to how they're served: a clean slice of the apple pie on a warm plate, oat vanilla beside it, a quenelle of whipped coconut cream. The recipes themselves do not move. The stuffing is sourdough this year and takes no eggs — the card's call.

Nothing live that can be done ahead

Velouté, granita, sauces, bar, pies and stuffing are all done by early afternoon. The evening is one roast, one bake, a sear and a reheat.

Warm plates, frozen glasses

Dinner plates go in the warming drawer at 7:15. The highballs go in the freezer at 1:45 with a rosemary sprig frozen to the wall — the spritz on arrival, then washed and re-frozen for the granita. It's the cheapest restaurant move there is.

Serve, then sit

Each course has a hard "sit down" point in the run of show. The host eats every course with the table — the plan is built so that's possible.

I Gruyère gougères, cranberry & chili jamPassed in the kitchen with the spritz while people arrive. Makes about 24 — four each. bake 2 pm400°F

Gougères

  • 1 cupwater
  • 6 tbspunsalted butter
  • ½ tspkosher salt
  • 1 cupall-purpose flour
  • 4large eggs
  • 1 cup (4 oz)Gruyère, finely grated
  • pinchnutmeg, black pepper
  1. Bring water, butter and salt to a boil. Off the heat, dump in the flour and stir hard. Back on medium 1–2 min until a film forms on the pot.
  2. Into a bowl; cool 3 min. Beat in eggs one at a time — the paste should fall from the spoon in a slow V.
  3. Fold in cheese, nutmeg, pepper. Pipe 1¼-inch mounds on parchment, 1½ inches apart. Wet fingertip to flatten peaks.
  4. Bake 400°F 22–25 min until deep golden and hollow-sounding. Don't open the oven before 18 min.

Cranberry & chili jam

  • 1 cupcranberries
  • ½ cupsugar
  • 1orange, zest and juice
  • ¼ tspchili flake
  • pinchsalt
  1. Simmer everything 10–12 min until the berries burst and it looks jammy. Cool. Keeps a week.
Ahead: jam at 12:45 with the cranberry sauce. Gougères piped and baked at 2:00, cooled on the counter. Re-crisp 3 minutes on the top rack above the bird at 6:25.
Serve: a wooden board, gougères warm, a small bowl of jam with a spoon, napkins. Nobody sits for this.
II Roasted butternut velouté, brown butter, crisp sage, pepitasGarnish sits in the empty bowl; the soup is poured over it at the table from a warm pitcher. made this morningreheat 6:15pour tableside

Velouté

  • 3 lbbutternut squash
  • 2 tbspolive oil
  • 4 tbspbutter
  • 1yellow onion, sliced
  • 2shallots, sliced
  • 1apple, peeled and diced
  • 4 cupsturkey stock (or chicken)
  • ½ cupfull-fat coconut milk
  • to tastesalt, a few drops of cider vinegar
  1. Halve squash, seed, oil, roast cut-side down at 425°F for 40 min until collapsing. Scoop.
  2. Sweat onion, shallot and apple in butter over low heat 10 min — no color.
  3. Add squash and stock. Simmer 15 min. Blend until completely smooth, then pass through a fine sieve. This is the difference between soup and velouté.
  4. Stir in the coconut milk. Season. Thin with stock to a pourable consistency — it should run off a spoon, not coat it.

Garnish

  • 4 tbspbutter, for brown butter
  • 12sage leaves
  • ⅓ cuppepitas, toasted
  • 6 tsplemon walnut cream (Forager or Kite Hill)
  • 2 tbspwalnut cream, for a thread across the top
  1. Brown the butter in a small pan until it smells nutty. Drop in the sage leaves 20 seconds until crisp; lift onto paper towel. Keep the butter.
Ahead: velouté made this morning, chilled. 6:15 reheat gently (never a boil), fry the sage, stage five warm low bowls.
Serve: in each warm bowl: a teaspoon of lemon walnut cream, two sage leaves, a pinch of pepitas, four drops of brown butter, a thread of walnut cream. Bowls go down empty. Pour from a warmed pitcher at each place — about ¾ cup. The first ten seconds of the dinner are this pour; take your time with it.

The bird, flat

  • 13 lbturkey, spatchcocked — backbone out, breastbone pressed flat
  • 3 tbspDiamond kosher salt (2 tbsp Morton), 2 tsp sugar, 1 tsp pepper
  • 4 tbspsoft butter + 6 chopped sage leaves
  • 2 tbspmelted butter, for the skin
  • 1 eachonion, carrot, celery stalk, rough cut (under the rack)
  • ½ cupdry white wine (for the gravy)
  1. 8 am: salted under the skin and over; on a rack over a sheet pan, fridge, uncovered — an eleven-hour dry brine.
  2. 3:45: out to temper. Sage butter under the breast skin. Vegetables under the rack.
  3. 5:00: skin brushed with melted butter; 425°F. Rotate at 5:45. Tent the breast loosely with foil if it's darkening fast.
  4. 6:15 first check; out at 150°F in the breast, 170°F in the thigh — usually 6:30–6:45. Rest tented on the board until 7:35; carryover takes the breast to 160°F.

Two ways: breast, and the crisp thigh

  1. 7:50: legs off at the hip joint. Thigh bones pull free after the rest; each thigh cut into three skin-on blocks — six, five plated.
  2. Cast iron hot with 1 tbsp butter. Blocks skin-side down, 2 minutes, pressed lightly, until the skin shatters. This is the second way.
  3. Breast lobes off the bone whole, sliced across the grain ⅜ inch. Two or three slices a plate.
  4. Drumsticks and wings are tomorrow's lunch. The carcass goes into the stockpot with the dishes.
Why flat: a spatchcocked 13-pounder roasts in about 90 minutes at high heat with the thighs done when the breast is, all the skin facing the heat, and it gives the oven back at 6:45 for the stuffing. A whole trussed bird would take three hours and the breast would be dry by the time the thighs were done.

Sage gravy & turkey stock

  • the backbone, neck, giblets(no liver) + onion, carrot, celery, bay, peppercorns, water to cover
  • 4 tbspbutter + ¼ cup flour
  • 3 cupsturkey stock (bought broth tops it up)
  • 2 sprigssage
  1. 8 am (stock): everything in the pot, bare simmer until noon, strain — about 2 quarts. It feeds the gravy and the stuffing.
  2. 12:45 (base): butter and flour cooked 3 minutes to blond. Stock whisked in, simmered 15 with the sage. Strained, chilled.
  3. 7:50: fat drained from the roasting pan, deglazed with ½ cup prosecco over the burner, scraped, strained into the base. Reduce 5 minutes. Season. Into a warm sauceboat.

The Old Stuffing † held firm

Use your card. Sourdough this year — one loaf, cubed and dried — and no eggs. The baseline below exists only so the oven schedule and pan count are right. If the card says different, the card wins.
  • 1 lb loafsourdough, ¾-inch cubes, dried at 250°F 40 min
  • 1 cupbutter
  • 2 cupsonion, diced
  • 1½ cupscelery, diced
  • ¼ cupfresh sage, chopped; 1 tsp thyme; ⅓ cup parsley; poultry seasoning per the card
  • 2½–3 cupsturkey stock — low end first; sourdough drinks
  • 1½ tspsalt, plenty of pepper
  1. Melt butter, sweat onion and celery 10 min. Toss with the cubes and herbs. Pour the stock over, fold until just moist — never wet.
  2. Into the buttered 9×13 at 12:15, covered, fridge.
  3. 6:45: the bird comes out, oven to 350°F. Covered 30 min, uncovered 20 until the top is crisp. Holds covered on the counter until 8:00.

Pommes purée, walnut cream

  • 2½ lbYukon Gold potatoes, peeled, in cold water
  • 1 cupcold butter, diced
  • ¾–1 cupoat milk, hot
  • 3 tbspwalnut cream — 1 cup walnuts soaked in hot water 20 min, blended with ¾ cup water and a pinch of salt until silky
  • to tastesalt
  1. 5:45: cold salted water to a simmer, 25 min until a knife slides in.
  2. Drain, rice while hot, back in the pot over low. Beat in butter a handful at a time, then hot oat milk until it just slumps, then the walnut cream last. Season.
  3. Push through the sieve once more. Hold covered in a bain-marie up to an hour.

Haricots verts amandine & cranberry

  • 1 lbgreen beans, trimmed
  • 2 tbspbutter, 1 shallot sliced
  • ⅓ cupsliced almonds, toasted
  • ½lemon
  • 12 ozcranberries
  • ¾ cupsugar, ¾ cup orange juice, zest of 1 orange
  1. Beans, 12:45: blanched 3 min in salted water, shocked, dried, bagged. 7:42: butter and shallot 1 min, beans 2 min, almonds, lemon, salt.
  2. Cranberry, 12:45: simmer everything 10–12 min until most berries burst. Cool; it sets as it chills. Half goes on the plates as a quenelle, half to a bowl for the table.
Plate (warm plates, 7:50): a spoon of purée pulled into a swoosh with the back of the spoon. Two or three breast slices fanned against it; the thigh block set at an angle, crisp side up. A small bundle of beans. A cranberry quenelle. Gravy spooned over the breast only, so the thigh skin stays crisp. Then the stuffing dish, the sauceboat and the cranberry bowl go to the center with big spoons. Sit. Pass the stuffing.
V Apple cider granita, bourbonThree spoonfuls in a frosted highball. Resets the palate before the pie. made this morninghighballs in freezer 5:45

Granita

  • 2½ cupsfresh apple cider (unfiltered)
  • ¼ cupsugar
  • 1 tbsplemon juice
  • pinchsalt
  • 2 tbspbourbon (no Calvados in the house; keeps it scrapeable)
  1. Warm cider with sugar just until dissolved. Cool, add lemon, salt, bourbon.
  2. Into a 9×13 metal pan, freezer. Scrape with a fork every 45 min for 3–4 hours until it's fluffy ice crystals. Cover.
What it's for: after the turkey, the gravy and the stuffing, three spoonfuls of cold, tart, barely sweet apple ice wipe the palate clean before the pies. It's the course that makes the dinner feel like a restaurant, for five minutes of cooking. Ahead: made at 7 am, scraped every 45 minutes through the morning, fluffed and covered by noon.
Serve: frosted highballs straight from the freezer, 3 tbsp each, a thin apple slice if you have one left from the salad. Serve and sit immediately; it has four minutes.

Pie crust † held firm

  • 2½ cupsall-purpose flour
  • 1 tspsalt
  • 1 tbspsugar
  • 1 cupcold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 6–8 tbspice water
  1. Cut butter into the dry ingredients until pea-sized. Add water a tablespoon at a time until it just holds. Two disks, wrapped, fridge. This is the apple pie's double crust — the only crust tonight.
  2. Made at 11:30, chilled 30 minutes, rolled at 12:20.

Apple pie † held firm

  • 3 lbapples — half Granny Smith, half Honeycrisp, peeled, ¼-inch slices
  • ½ cupsugar, ¼ cup brown sugar
  • 1½ tspcinnamon, ¼ tsp nutmeg
  • 1 tbsplemon juice, 1 tsp vanilla
  • 2 tbspcornstarch
  • 2 tbspbutter, dotted on top
  • washoat milk with a little maple, + turbinado
  1. Toss apples with everything but the butter; let sit 15 min, then pile into the bottom crust high in the middle. Dot with butter.
  2. Top crust on, crimp hard, vent. Chill 20 min. Brush with the oat-milk wash, turbinado.
  3. On a preheated sheet pan: 425°F 20 min, then 375°F 40–45 min until the juices bubble thick through the vents. Shield the edge with foil if it darkens.
  4. Cool all afternoon. In at 12:40, out at 1:45, sliced at 9:10.

Whipped coconut cream & service

  • 2 canscoconut cream, chilled since yesterday — the thick solids only
  • 1 tbspsugar, a drop of vanilla
  • 1 pintoat vanilla frozen dessert
  • pinchflaky salt
  1. At 9:05, scoop the solids from the cold cans into a cold bowl (save the water for a smoothie), whisk with the sugar and vanilla to soft peaks. Keep cold.
Serve: cut the pie into 8. One slice (warmed 8 min at 300°F on a tray, or room temperature — both are right) on a warm dessert plate, a small scoop of oat vanilla beside it, a quenelle of the coconut cream. Leftover pie is breakfast.

One glass

  • 1½ ozvodka
  • 1½ ozwhite cranberry juice

  1. Highball straight from the freezer. 3 oz of the base poured over the frozen sprig.

  3. In the guest's hand within a minute of the door. The sprig thaws into the drink over ten minutes and the rosemary comes up through the bubbles. No syrup needed.

The base — six glasses, bottled

  • 9 ozvodka
  • 9 ozwhite cranberry juice
  1. Bottle, fridge, 1:50. Prosecco is never batched — it goes in at the pour. Three bottles cold from 1:15: one for the spritzes, two for the table.

The frozen-rosemary highballs

  1. 1:45 — six highballs, dry. A short rosemary sprig, rinsed and patted bone dry, laid against the inside wall of each, tip up. Sprig and wall misted with a spray bottle. Freezer.
  2. Misted again at every granita scrape — 2:20, 2:55, 3:30. Three or four rounds glaze the sprig in a thin sheet of ice and fix it to the glass. Left in the freezer until 6:30.
  3. After the arrival round the glasses are collected at 7:15, washed, dried, and go straight back in — frosted again for the granita at 9:00.

The beer: Saison Dupont

Dry, peppery, bottle-conditioned, 6.5%. It cuts the butter in the gougères and the purée, stands up to the crisp thigh, and doesn't fight the cranberry. Four 19-oz cans, poured into wine glasses. A Märzen or an Oktoberfest reads more "fall" but it's sweet and heavy against the velouté — this is the better food beer. If you want the Columbus tie-in, a local saison or farmhouse ale from Land-Grant, Seventh Son or Wolf's Ridge is the swap.

Table & service

Pace

Three and a half hours, seven movements, roughly twenty minutes a course and a ten-minute gap. The gaps are where the dinner happens.

  • Clear every plate before the next course comes out — never stack at the table
  • Pour wine for the course as the plates go down, not before
  • One sentence of introduction per course, then sit. The menu card does the rest
  • Kitchen lights up, dining room lights down. The pass is visible from the table and that's fine — it's part of the theme

Equipment to have at the pass

  • Instant-read thermometer
  • Two large spoons for the purée swoosh, two small spoons for quenelles (dip in hot water between)
  • Warmed pitcher for the velouté; warmed sauceboat
  • Cast iron, fish spatula, carving board with a juice groove, sharp slicer
  • Shaker, Hawthorne strainer, jigger, bar spoon, peeler for the orange twists
  • Side towels — six, folded, at the pass
  • Warming drawer or a 170°F oven for plates after the stuffing comes out
