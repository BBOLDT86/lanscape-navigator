The idea

Precision on top, grandmother underneath.

Restaurant technique is mostly about timing, temperature, and finishing — not about reinventing what's on the plate. So the whole menu is Thanksgiving: squash, sage, turkey, cranberry, brussels sprouts, cider, apples, pecans. What changes is the delivery. The soup is passed through a sieve and poured at the table. The turkey comes two ways, with the dark meat cured, confited, pressed and seared into a crisp block. Plates are warm. Courses arrive one at a time with a pause between them.

Then the counterweight: the stuffing goes to the middle of the table in the dish it was baked in, and the pies are the pies. The contrast is the point. Guests should feel they ate at a restaurant and at home in the same sitting.

Everything on this page is sized for six. The plan runs eight days because confit, dry-brining and good stock each want a day of their own — and because the day itself should feel calm. By 6:30 pm on the 14th the only live cooking left is a pour, a sear, a carve and a bake.

Held firm †

  • The old stuffing — your card, your bread, your dish. Baseline recipe included below only so the shopping list and oven schedule are complete.
  • Apple pie — double crust, baked the morning of, cooled four hours so it slices clean.
  • Pecan pie — baked the day before; it's better after a night's rest.
The Michelin touches on these three are limited to how they're served: a slim slice of each pie on one warm plate, a quenelle beside them, flaky salt on the pecan. The recipes themselves do not move.

Nothing live that can be done ahead

Six of seven courses are finished by Friday night. Saturday's cooking is one roast, one bake, a sear and a reheat.

Warm plates, chilled coupes

Dinner plates go in the warming drawer at 7:15. Coupes for the granita go in the freezer at 6:00. It's the cheapest restaurant move there is.

Serve, then sit

Each course has a hard "sit down" point in the run of show. The host eats every course with the table — the plan is built so that's possible.

I Gruyère gougères, cranberry & chili jamPassed in the kitchen with crémant while people arrive. Makes about 24 — four each. pipe & freeze Fribake Sat 2 pm400°F

Gougères

  • 1 cupwater
  • 6 tbspunsalted butter
  • ½ tspkosher salt
  • 1 cupall-purpose flour
  • 4large eggs
  • 1 cup (4 oz)Gruyère, finely grated
  • pinchnutmeg, black pepper
  1. Bring water, butter and salt to a boil. Off the heat, dump in the flour and stir hard. Back on medium 1–2 min until a film forms on the pot.
  2. Into a bowl; cool 3 min. Beat in eggs one at a time — the paste should fall from the spoon in a slow V.
  3. Fold in cheese, nutmeg, pepper. Pipe 1¼-inch mounds on parchment, 1½ inches apart. Wet fingertip to flatten peaks.
  4. Bake 400°F 22–25 min until deep golden and hollow-sounding. Don't open the oven before 18 min.

Cranberry & chili jam

  • 1 cupcranberries
  • ½ cupsugar
  • 1orange, zest and juice
  • ¼ tspchili flake
  • pinchsalt
  1. Simmer everything 10–12 min until the berries burst and it looks jammy. Cool. Keeps a week.
Ahead: jam Tuesday. Pipe gougères Friday and freeze on the tray, then bag. Saturday 2:00 pm bake from frozen, adding 4 min. Re-crisp 5 min in the 325°F oven at 6:25 (the breast won't mind).
Serve: a wooden board, gougères warm, a small bowl of jam with a spoon, napkins. Nobody sits for this.
II Roasted butternut velouté, brown butter, crisp sage, pepitasGarnish sits in the empty bowl; the soup is poured over it at the table from a warm pitcher. make Thureheat Sat 6:15pour tableside

Velouté

  • 3 lbbutternut squash
  • 2 tbspolive oil
  • 4 tbspbutter
  • 1yellow onion, sliced
  • 2shallots, sliced
  • 1apple, peeled and diced
  • 4 cupsturkey stock (or chicken)
  • ½ cupheavy cream
  • to tastesalt, a few drops of cider vinegar
  1. Halve squash, seed, oil, roast cut-side down at 425°F for 40 min until collapsing. Scoop.
  2. Sweat onion, shallot and apple in butter over low heat 10 min — no color.
  3. Add squash and stock. Simmer 15 min. Blend until completely smooth, then pass through a fine sieve. This is the difference between soup and velouté.
  4. Stir in cream. Season. Thin with stock to a pourable consistency — it should run off a spoon, not coat it.

Garnish

  • 4 tbspbutter, for brown butter
  • 12sage leaves
  • ⅓ cuppepitas, toasted
  • 6 tspcrème fraîche
  1. Brown the butter in a small pan until it smells nutty. Drop in the sage leaves 20 seconds until crisp; lift onto paper towel. Keep the butter.
Ahead: velouté Thursday, chilled. Toast pepitas Friday. Saturday 6:15 reheat gently (do not boil — cream), fry sage, stage six warm bowls.
Serve: in each warm bowl: a teaspoon of crème fraîche, two sage leaves, a pinch of pepitas, four drops of brown butter. Bowls go down empty. Pour from a warmed pitcher at each place — about ¾ cup. The first ten seconds of the dinner are this pour; take your time with it.

IV Turkey two ways, pommes purée, haricots verts amandine, cranberry, sage gravy — and The Old StuffingThe plate is composed. The stuffing, extra gravy and cranberry go to the center of the table. † stuffing held firmcure Wed · confit Thu · brine Thu325°F → 350°F

Dry-brined roast breast

  • 6–7 lbbone-in, skin-on turkey breast
  • 1½ tbspkosher salt (Diamond; use 1 tbsp if Morton)
  • 1 tspsugar
  • 1 tbspthyme leaves, chopped
  • 1lemon, zest only
  • 4 tbspsoft butter + 6 chopped sage leaves
  • 1 eachonion, carrot, celery stalk, rough cut (for the pan)
  • ½ cupdry white wine (for the gravy)
  1. Thu by 8 pm: mix salt, sugar, thyme, zest. Loosen the skin and rub some underneath, the rest all over. On a rack over a tray, uncovered, in the fridge 48 hours.
  2. Sat 3:00: out of the fridge to temper. Pat dry. Work sage butter under the skin.
  3. Sat 5:00: on a rack over the vegetables and the neck in a roasting pan. 325°F, about 2 hours, until 155°F in the thickest part (start checking at 1:45).
  4. Out at 7:15, tented loosely, rest 30–40 min. Carryover takes it to 160–165°F. Carve the lobes off the bone, then slice across the grain.

Pressed leg confit

  • 2whole turkey legs, thigh + drumstick (about 2½ lb)
  • 2 tbspkosher salt
  • 1 tbspbrown sugar
  • 6juniper berries, crushed
  • 4garlic cloves, smashed
  • 6 sprigsthyme, 2 bay leaves, 1 tsp cracked pepper
  • 3–4 cupsduck fat (or half duck fat, half neutral oil)
  1. Wed: rub legs with the cure, bag, fridge 18–24 hours.
  2. Thu: rinse, pat very dry. Snug in a Dutch oven, submerge in melted fat. 275°F oven 3–3½ hours until the meat gives at the bone.
  3. Cool in the fat 30 min. Pull the meat; discard skin, save bones for stock. Shred, season, fold in 3 tbsp of the fat and 2 tbsp stock. Press into a plastic-lined loaf pan an inch deep, weight it, fridge overnight.
  4. Fri: unmold, trim, cut six rectangles. Keep on a tray, covered.
  5. Sat 7:35: cast iron, 1 tbsp duck fat, medium-high. 3 min a side until the edges shatter.

Sage gravy & turkey stock

  • 3 lbturkey wings, necks, backs
  • 1 eachonion, carrot, celery, plus bay and peppercorns
  • 4 qtwater
  • 4 tbspbutter + ¼ cup flour
  • 3 cupsturkey stock
  • 2 sprigssage
  1. Wed (stock): roast the wings and necks at 425°F 40 min. Into a pot with vegetables and water; simmer 4 hours, skimming. Strain — about 2½ quarts. This feeds the velouté, confit, stuffing and gravy.
  2. Fri (base): cook butter and flour 3 min to blond. Whisk in stock, simmer 15 min with sage. Strain, chill.
  3. Sat 7:35: drain fat from the breast pan, deglaze with wine over the burner, scrape, strain into the base. Reduce 5 min. Season. Strain again into a warm sauceboat.

The Old Stuffing † held firm

Use your card. This baseline exists so the shopping list, oven schedule and pan count are right. If the card says different bread, more butter, sausage, or a different pan — the card wins. Don't let anyone talk you into "elevating" it.
  • 1 lb loafday-old white bread, cubed and dried overnight
  • 1 cupbutter
  • 2 cupsonion, diced
  • 1½ cupscelery, diced
  • ¼ cupfresh sage, chopped (or 2 tsp dried)
  • 1 tspdried thyme, ⅓ cup parsley
  • 2½–3 cupsturkey stock
  • 2eggs
  • 1½ tspsalt, plenty of pepper
  1. Melt butter, sweat onion and celery 10 min. Toss with bread and herbs. Whisk eggs into stock, pour over, fold until just moist.
  2. Into a buttered 9×13. Assemble Saturday morning, covered, in the fridge.
  3. Sat 7:15: once the breast is out, oven to 350°F. Covered 30 min, uncovered 20 min until the top is crisp.

Pommes purée

  • 2½ lbYukon Gold potatoes, peeled, in cold water
  • 1 cupcold butter, diced
  • ¾–1 cupwhole milk, hot
  • to tastesalt
  1. 5:45: cold salted water, bring to a simmer, 25 min until a knife slides in.
  2. Drain, rice or food-mill while hot, back in the pot over low. Beat in butter a handful at a time, then hot milk until it just slumps. Season.
  3. For restaurant texture, push through a sieve once more. Hold covered in a bain-marie up to an hour.

Haricots verts amandine & cranberry

  • 1 lbharicots verts, trimmed
  • 2 tbspbutter, 1 shallot sliced
  • ⅓ cupsliced almonds, toasted
  • ½lemon
  • 12 ozcranberries
  • ¾ cupsugar, ¾ cup orange juice, zest of 1 orange
  1. Beans, Fri: blanch 3 min in salted water, shock in ice water, dry, bag. Sat 7:40: butter and shallot 1 min, beans 2 min, almonds, lemon, salt.
  2. Cranberry, Wed: simmer everything 10–12 min until most berries burst. Cool; it sets as it chills. Half goes on the plates as a quenelle, half to a bowl for the table.
Plate (warm plates, 7:50): a spoon of purée pulled into a swoosh with the back of the spoon. Two or three breast slices fanned against it; the confit block set at an angle, crisp side up. A small bundle of beans. A cranberry quenelle. Gravy spooned over the breast only, so the confit stays crisp. Then the stuffing dish, the sauceboat and the cranberry bowl go to the center with big spoons. Plates down — then the truffle (below). Sit. Pass the stuffing.

White Alba truffle, at the table

  • 1 ozfresh white truffle, Tuber magnatum pico — Sabatino, $232 on sale ($290 list) + overnight
  • 1truffle slicer (the flat shaver with the adjustable blade)
  • 1 jarwith a tight lid, and paper towels
  1. Order Wed Nov 11 before 2 pm EST. Sabatino ships overnight Mon–Thu on a 2 pm cutoff, so a Wednesday order lands Thursday, Friday at the latest. Don't order it today: a weekend order ships Monday, and a white truffle loses aroma every day it sits.
  2. On arrival: unwrap, brush off any soil with a dry pastry brush — never wash. Wrap loosely in a paper towel, into the jar, lid on, fridge. Change the towel every day. Put six eggs in the jar with it; Sunday breakfast is settled.
  3. Saturday 7:55: take the truffle out when the plates go down. Bring the slicer to the table. Shave thin over the purée on each plate — about 4 g a plate, eight to ten shavings. Purée, butter and heat are what release it; shave onto the hot purée, not the turkey. Whatever's left goes over the velouté bowls at Sunday's leftovers.
Why this and not the alternatives: White Alba is in season (Oct–Dec) and this is the week; black winter (Périgord) isn't in yet. The budget route is 2 oz Burgundy truffle at $106 — nutty, mild, fine shaved and warmed in the butter for the purée, but it isn't the showpiece. Urbani's 1 oz white is $220–300 with shipping at checkout; same product, no reason to switch. Landed, about $260–285: Sabatino's product page says $24.99 overnight and its policy page says $49.99 — confirm at checkout. If you'd rather have it on the velouté too, make it 2 oz ($450 on sale).

Granita

  • 2½ cupsfresh apple cider (unfiltered)
  • ¼ cupsugar
  • 1 tbsplemon juice
  • pinchsalt
  • 2 tbspCalvados (optional; keeps it scrapeable)
  1. Warm cider with sugar just until dissolved. Cool, add lemon, salt, Calvados.
  2. Into a 9×13 metal pan, freezer. Scrape with a fork every 45 min for 3–4 hours until it's fluffy ice crystals. Cover.
Ahead: Thursday afternoon while the confit is in the oven — the scraping schedule fits around it. Fluff once more Saturday morning.

Pie crust † held firm

  • 2½ cupsall-purpose flour
  • 1 tspsalt
  • 1 tbspsugar
  • 1 cupcold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 6–8 tbspice water
  1. Cut butter into the dry ingredients until pea-sized. Add water a tablespoon at a time until it just holds. Two disks, wrapped, fridge. This is the apple pie's double crust; make a half batch again for the pecan pie's single crust.
  2. Tuesday. Dough is better after two days in the fridge, and it freezes fine.

Apple pie † held firm

  • 3 lbapples — half Granny Smith, half Honeycrisp, peeled, ¼-inch slices
  • ½ cupsugar, ¼ cup brown sugar
  • 1½ tspcinnamon, ¼ tsp nutmeg
  • 1 tbsplemon juice, 1 tsp vanilla
  • 2 tbspcornstarch
  • 2 tbspbutter, dotted on top
  • 1egg, beaten, + coarse sugar
  1. Toss apples with everything but the butter and egg; let sit 15 min, then pile into the bottom crust high in the middle. Dot with butter.
  2. Top crust on, crimp hard, vent. Chill 20 min. Egg wash, coarse sugar.
  3. On a preheated sheet pan: 425°F 20 min, then 375°F 40–45 min until the juices bubble thick through the vents. Shield the edge with foil if it darkens.
  4. Cool 4 hours. Bake at 8:00 am and it's ready to slice by 1:00.

Pecan pie † held firm

  • 1single crust, blind-baked
  • 1 cuplight corn syrup
  • 1 cuppacked brown sugar
  • 3eggs
  • ⅓ cupmelted butter
  • 1 tspvanilla, ½ tsp salt
  • 2 cups (8 oz)pecan halves
  1. Blind-bake the crust at 375°F: 15 min with weights, 5 more without, until pale gold.
  2. Whisk syrup, sugar, eggs, butter, vanilla, salt. Pecans into the shell, filling over.
  3. 350°F 50–60 min: edges set, center with a slight jiggle. Cool completely. Friday — it's better the next day.

Whipped crème fraîche & service

  • ½ cupcrème fraîche
  • ½ cupheavy cream
  • 1 tbspsugar
  • 1 pintvanilla bean ice cream
  • pinchflaky salt
  1. Whip crème fraîche, cream and sugar to soft peaks at 8:40. Keep cold.
Serve: cut each pie into 10. One slim slice of apple (warmed 10 min at 300°F on a tray) and one of pecan (room temperature — it's better that way) side by side on a warm dessert plate, a small scoop of ice cream between them, a quenelle of the crème fraîche, flaky salt on the pecan only. Leftover pie is breakfast.

⁂ Rosemary & Calvados freezer martiniThe signature. Batched and kept in the freezer so it pours ice-cold and slightly viscous, into a glass with a rosemary sprig frozen to the inside. syrup Tuebatch Thuglasses frozen Fri

The batch — about ten 3-oz pours

  • 16 ozgin — London dry (Plymouth if you want it softer; vodka works for anyone who won't drink gin)
  • 5 ozdry vermouth, a fresh bottle
  • 2½ ozrosemary syrup (below)
  • 1½ ozCalvados — the same bottle as the granita and the digestif
  • 6 ozfiltered water
  • 2 pinchessalt, dissolved in the water
  1. Combine everything in a clean 1-liter bottle. The water is the dilution a stirred martini would get from ice — don't skip it or the first sip is harsh.
  2. Freezer, at least 4 hours; it won't freeze solid at this strength and it keeps a month. Thursday, after the granita is done and there's room.
  3. Pour 3 oz straight from the bottle into the frozen glass. No shaking, no stirring, no ice. Garnish is already in the glass.

Rosemary syrup

  • 1 cupsugar, 1 cup water
  • 5 sprigsrosemary
  1. Bring to a simmer, off the heat, steep 30 min, strain, fridge. Two weeks.

The frozen-rosemary glasses

  1. Six Nick & Nora or martini glasses (not the coupes — those are spoken for by the granita). Dry, in the freezer 15 min.
  2. Lay a short rosemary sprig against the inside wall of each glass. Mist the sprig and the wall with water from a spray bottle. Freezer, 20 min.
  3. Mist again, freeze again. Three or four rounds until the sprig is glazed in a thin sheet of ice and fixed to the glass. Leave them in the freezer until 6:25.
  4. Pour the martini over it; the ice melts slowly and the rosemary comes up through the drink. Thin apple slice on the rim if you want more fall in the glass.
Freezer real estate, Friday night: the martini bottle, six frosted martini glasses, six coupes (from 6:00 Saturday), the granita pan, the gougère tray, the ice cream, the big cubes. Clear a shelf Thursday.

The beer: Saison Dupont

Dry, peppery, bottle-conditioned, 6.5%. It cuts the butter in the gougères and the purée, stands up to the confit, and doesn't fight the cranberry. Two 750 ml bottles, poured into wine glasses. A Märzen or an Oktoberfest reads more "fall" but it's sweet and heavy against the velouté — this is the better food beer. If you want the Columbus tie-in, a local saison or farmhouse ale from Land-Grant, Seventh Son or Wolf's Ridge is the swap.

If something slips

  • breast slowthe salad buys you 15 min. Serve it, then check again. Mains at 8:15 is fine.
  • oven conflictthe stuffing can hold covered on the counter 30 min and go back in at 300°F to re-crisp. The breast cannot wait.
  • confit won't crisppan isn't hot enough. Wipe it, heat it 2 more minutes, try one block first.
  • purée tightensa splash of hot milk and a whisk. Never a blender.
  • truffle late or weakWhole Foods and The Fresh Market both stock Sabatino's shelf products — a jar of black truffle butter whipped into the purée at 6:10 is the fallback. Not the same, but nobody goes without.
  • martini froze slushythe water was over-measured; let the bottle sit on the counter 5 min and pour. Next batch, cut the water to 5 oz.

Table & service

The dining room already has the bones: the ash live-edge table, off-white walls, the white rug. The table stays bare down the center so the wood shows.

Setting

  • No runner, no chargers — white plates directly on the ash
  • A low centerline of greenery, under 8 inches, with brass tapers; votives at each end
  • Three glasses a seat: crémant flute, white, red. Flute cleared with the gougères board
  • Printed menu tucked into the napkin; place card above the plate
  • Water carafes at both ends; bread is not served — the gougères are the bread
  • Deep green, brass and cream, which is the house palette anyway

Pace

Three and a half hours, seven movements, roughly twenty minutes a course and a ten-minute gap. The gaps are where the dinner happens.

  • Clear every plate before the next course comes out — never stack at the table
  • Pour wine for the course as the plates go down, not before
  • One sentence of introduction per course, then sit. The menu card does the rest
  • Kitchen lights up, dining room lights down. The pass is visible from the table and that's fine — it's part of the theme

  • Side towels — six, folded, at the pass
