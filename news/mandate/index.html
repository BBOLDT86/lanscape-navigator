Two years of Project 2025: what got done, what it cost, and who paid

The Heritage Foundation’s 900-page plan was disowned by the candidate and executed by the president. By every tracker’s count more than half of it is in place. The Supreme Court has struck down its boldest pieces, its authors run the agencies they wrote about, a quarter of the senior staff has turned over, and the people it reached run from 65,000 immigrants in detention to a school in Minab, Iran.

The verdict, in short

Project 2025 worked, in the sense its authors meant. The independent trackers that score the Mandate for Leadership line by line put it between 53 and 55 percent implemented by this autumn 12, and the Heritage Foundation itself told donors in June that “over half” of it was in place 3. The administration carried out the plan’s core: a civil service that can be fired at will, agencies hollowed by layoffs and shuttered by executive order, a deportation machine funded at a scale no Congress had contemplated, and an executive that treats appropriations, independent commissions and the press corps as things to be managed rather than obeyed 89.

It did not work in the sense the voters were promised. Prices were not down on day one; the war in Ukraine did not end in 24 hours; the “worst first” deportations swept up a population three-quarters of whom had no criminal conviction 3839. And where the plan met the Constitution it mostly lost: the Supreme Court struck down the emergency tariffs 6–3 15, held the birthright-citizenship order unconstitutional 5–4 18, and refused to let the president fire a Federal Reserve governor 17, even as it handed him the power to fire everyone else 16.

The cost is where this page spends most of its time, because it is where the numbers are least often put together. Deaths in immigration detention in 2025 matched the worst year ICE has ever recorded, and the fiscal year that ended in September surpassed it 353637. A study in The Lancet projected 14 million additional deaths by 2030 from the dismantling of USAID 47. The Congressional Budget Office put the health law’s coverage loss at nearly 11 million people 49. And on the first day of the war with Iran that the campaign had promised would never come, an American missile destroyed an elementary school in Minab; Airwars identified 123 children among the dead 4344. The people responsible are named on these pages, with what they did and what became of them.

What Project 2025 is

Mandate for Leadership: The Conservative Promise is a 30-chapter policy book published by the Heritage Foundation in April 2023, edited by Paul Dans and Steven Groves, with a foreword by Heritage’s president, Kevin Roberts 1314. Each chapter takes an agency or a cluster of them, is written by a former official or an ally who expected to return, and ends in a list of instructions for the first days of a new administration. Its five sections run from “Taking the reins of government” (the White House, the budget office and the personnel agencies) through defense, the “general welfare” departments and the economy to the independent regulators 13.

The copy a reader is most likely to have seen in Congress’s files is the one entered into the record of a House Oversight subcommittee hearing on Sept. 25, 2024 12, at which Paul Winfree, the author of the Mandate’s Federal Reserve chapter, was a Republican witness; Rep. Melanie Stansbury put the book into the record and called it “the playbook that was put forward by the conservative community for the next administration” 1011. The full text is mirrored by Democracy Docket and the Internet Archive 13.

The candidate ran away from it. “I know nothing about Project 2025,” Donald Trump wrote on July 5, 2024. “I have no idea who is behind it” 6. In his debate with Kamala Harris that September he said he had not read it and would not 7. Within a week of the inauguration, Time counted nearly two-thirds of the new executive actions mirroring the Mandate, and CNN counted 36 of 53 45. On Oct. 2, 2025, during the shutdown, the president announced a meeting with “Russ Vought, he of PROJECT 2025 Fame” 7. Dans, by then out of Heritage, told NPR in January 2026: “Every day that President Trump rolls out another Project 2025 item, it’s really an endorsement of our work” 8.

How much of it is done

Three groups keep line-by-line counts. The volunteer tracker at project2025.observer scores 321 objectives across 34 agencies; on Nov. 8, 2026 it listed 141 completed, 70 in progress and 110 not started, a weighted 55 percent, with the most recent completion dated Aug. 12 1. The Center for Progressive Reform and Governing for Impact, scoring 532 recommended executive actions at 20 domestic agencies, found 283 initiated or completed at the one-year mark, 53 percent 2. Heritage’s own fundraising email in June claimed 53 percent 3. The pace is the other finding: the observer’s log shows completions falling from about 13 a month in the first half of 2025 to about one a month through 2026, as the easy executive orders ran out and the courts and the calendar caught up 1.

The Watch’s own scorecard, below and on its own page, is narrower and stricter. It tracks the proposals from each chapter that changed the most lives or the most law, and it scores them against what stands today, not what was announced: a thing the courts have enjoined is “blocked,” not done; a thing done in a weaker form is “partial.” Chapter 3 (the civil service) and Chapter 9 (USAID) are the plan’s clearest successes; Chapter 24 (the Federal Reserve) and the birthright-citizenship order are its clearest defeats; the tariffs, which the Mandate presented as a debate between two authors rather than a plan, were imposed, struck down and partly re-imposed under other statutes 15.

Where the courts stepped in

More than 550 lawsuits have been filed against the administration’s actions 50, and the pattern of the big ones is consistent: the Supreme Court gave the president his personnel powers and took away his policy shortcuts. On June 29, 2026, in Trump v. Slaughter, the court overruled Humphrey’s Executor and let the president remove the heads of independent agencies at will 16; the same day, 5–4, it held in Trump v. Cook that he could not remove Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook 17. A day later, in Trump v. Barbara, Chief Justice Roberts wrote for a 5–4 court that the order ending birthright citizenship was unconstitutional 18. In February the court had already held, 6–3, that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act does not authorize tariffs, and the Court of International Trade ordered refunds with interest 15.

Lower courts did the rest of the work. Judges found the dismantling of Voice of America unlawful 25, enjoined the Guard deployments to Portland and Chicago, where the Supreme Court declined to intervene 21, and the Ninth Circuit upheld the order ending the federalization of California’s Guard 22. The prosecutions of James Comey and Letitia James collapsed when the prosecutor who brought them was found to have been unlawfully appointed; the appeals were argued in September 19. Kilmar Abrego Garcia’s charges were dismissed as a vindictive prosecution 20. A judge found the Justice Department had likely violated the Epstein Files Transparency Act 26. On Nov. 2, 2026, the day before the midterms, the Supreme Court refused the department’s bid to set aside some 20,000 California ballots 27.

The administration won where the Mandate had planned for it to win. The court let the reductions in force proceed 23, let the Education Department’s wind-down continue, and allowed the pocket rescission of $4 billion in foreign aid that the Impoundment Control Act was written to prevent 24. The contempt inquiry into the March 2025 deportation flights, which the government has told the D.C. Circuit were ordered by Kristi Noem, was argued en banc on Sept. 29 and is pending 20.

What was promised, and what happened

PolitiFact’s MAGA-Meter, which tracks 75 second-term promises, rated 15 kept, 21 stalled, 31 in the works and 5 broken as of Oct. 26, 2026 52; the White House’s own count that week was 127 promises kept. The Watch’s list is shorter and sorted by consequence: the spoken commitments that moved votes, in the words they were spoken, against the record.

The pattern is a government that kept the promises it could keep by itself and broke the ones that depended on the world. The Jan. 6 pardons, the border emergency, Schedule F and the return to the office were done in days. What it did to people

The Mandate’s immigration chapter asked for detention at a scale that would require new camps, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act paid for them: $45 billion for detention alone, enough for 116,000 to 125,000 beds, a 308 percent increase over the 2024 budget by the American Immigration Council’s reckoning 40. The detained population passed 70,000 in January 2026, the most since records begin; 74 percent of them had no criminal conviction 38. Deaths in custody in calendar 2025 numbered 31 to 33 depending on who counts, against 11 in 2024, matching the record set in 2004 3536; fiscal 2026 ended with 36 or 37, the most in ICE’s history 37. ProPublica documented 506 U.S. citizens detained by immigration agents; the Marshall Project counted more than 6,200 children 4142. Two citizens, Renee Good and Alex Pretti, were shot dead by federal agents in Minneapolis in January 2026; no one has been charged 31.

Abroad, the dismantling of USAID in the plan’s first months was followed by a projection in The Lancet of more than 14 million additional deaths by 2030, 4.5 million of them children under five 47; a Boston University counter of deaths already attributable to the cuts passed 750,000 in January 2026 and was retired a month later at 781,343 48. Secretary Rubio told Congress in May 2025 that “no children are dying” 11. The war with Iran that began Feb. 28, 2026 has killed, by Iran’s health ministry, 3,468 people including 376 children, and 23 American troops 4546. The school in Minab is its own dossier below, with the claim this page was asked to check: 123 is the number of children Airwars identified among at least 157 dead 4344.

At home, the CBO scored the health law at 10.9 million more uninsured by 2034 and more than $1.2 trillion in Medicaid and food-stamp cuts 49; the 43-day shutdown that followed furloughed 900,000 workers and cut off November food stamps for 41 million people until the courts intervened 50; the enhanced insurance subsidies expired on Jan. 1, 2026 after a 51–48 Senate vote 49. The effective tariff rate reached about 27 percent in April 2025, the highest in a century, before the courts cut it to 12 percent 51. About 200,000 federal workers were gone by August 2025 and 9 percent of the workforce by March 2026 51.

Who made money

The stock market did what it usually does, with a detour. The S&P 500 fell 7.9 percent in the first hundred days, the worst start since Nixon, lost $6.6 trillion in the two days after the April 2 tariffs, and gained 9.5 percent on April 9 after the president posted “THIS IS A GREAT TIME TO BUY” four hours before pausing them 5758. It ended 2025 up 16 percent, the weakest first presidential year in two decades 58, set a record of 7,818.93 on Oct. 6, 2026 as the 10-year Treasury yield passed 5 percent, and lost 4.4 percent on Nov. 5, the day after the midterms, its largest one-day dollar loss ever 5556. The Federal Reserve, under its new chair Kevin Warsh, raised rates in September for the first time in three years, with inflation at 3.4 percent 66.

How this was made

The Watch built this section on Nov. 8, 2026 from seven research passes, each confined to what it could read and link: the Mandate’s text and the trackers that score it; the administration’s actions against each chapter; the court dockets and the trackers kept by Just Security, Lawfare and SCOTUSblog; the cabinet roster and every departure Brookings, the Partnership for Public Service and the press recorded; the campaign’s spoken commitments against PolitiFact, AP and Bloomberg; the human cost from ICE’s own death notices, TRAC, KFF, HRANA, Airwars, the Lancet and the CBO; and the markets from AP’s daily index reports and the companies’ filings. Where a figure rests on a secondary page (several Wikipedia hubs, a mirror of an AP column) the receipts say so, and the weekly sprint replaces it with the primary source when it can be read.

Statuses are judged against what stands today. “Done” means carried out and in force; “blocked by courts” means an injunction or a ruling is in force against it; “partial” means done in a weaker form or in part; “reversed” means done and undone. For the promises: “kept,” “partly kept,” “broken,” “stalled,” “reversed” and “too early.” For the courts: who prevailed at the latest ruling. The research budget for the first sprint ran out before several items could be sourced — Musk’s election spending, the documentaries, the private-prison donations, the 2026 measles and jobs figures among them — and those are listed as gaps rather than filled from memory. The record is kept as JSON in the repository’s news/mandate/data folder, checked by build.py, and refreshed in weekly sprints through December.
