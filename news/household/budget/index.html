The guide

Getting a transactions file from your card or bank

Almost every U.S. card and bank lets you download your activity as a spreadsheet file. The wording changes from bank to bank, but the path is nearly always the same:

  1. Sign in on the website (the phone apps often can't export). Open the card or account.
  2. Find Activity, Transactions or Statements. Look for a Download, Export or ⤓ icon near the list.
  3. Choose the format CSV (sometimes "Comma-delimited" or "Spreadsheet / Excel"). Avoid PDF, QFX, OFX and QBO: those are for other software.
  4. Pick a date range. Start with the last one to three months; you can import older months later.

Common spots: American Express: Statements & Activity → Download → CSV. Chase: account activity → the download arrow → CSV. Capital One: View Transactions → Download Transactions → CSV. Citi: Account activity → Download → CSV. Discover: All activity → Download → CSV. Bank of America and Wells Fargo: Download / Download account activity → Spreadsheet (CSV). Apple Card: Wallet → Card Balance → a monthly statement → Export Transactions (on a Mac or the web, CSV). Menus move around; if you can't find it, search your bank's help pages for "download transactions CSV".

No file option? Select the rows on the activity page, copy them, and paste them into the import box. As long as each line has a date, a description and an amount, it will read them.

How importing works (and what it does with your file)

The file is read by this page, inside your browser. It is not uploaded. The importer:

  • Finds the date, description and amount columns by their headers. Files with separate Debit and Credit columns work too.
  • Works out the sign. Some banks show purchases as positive numbers, some as negative. Leave Charges are on Detect, or set it if a file comes in backwards (your spending would show as refunds).
  • Spots payments to the card ("Payment, thank you", "Autopay"…) and keeps them out of spending. For a shared card they record who paid the bill.
  • Skips duplicates: the same date, amount and description you already have. Overlapping date ranges are fine. Two identical charges on the same day in one file are both kept.
  • Guesses a category and, for two people, a person. First from your own history (a merchant you've sorted before), otherwise from common merchant names. Guesses are marked auto until you confirm them on Categorize.

Made a mess? Undo last import on the Transactions tab removes exactly the rows that import added.

Categorizing fast: merchants, not charges

The Categorize tab groups charges by merchant and sorts the biggest dollars first, so the first ten rows usually cover most of your spending.

  • Assign categories mode: pick a category for a merchant and press Apply. Every charge from that merchant changes, and future imports from it will follow.
  • Assign people mode (two people): choose whose spending it is (, or Shared) and press Confirm. A merchant that's sometimes one and sometimes the other? Click its name to expand it and set charges one by one.
  • Need a category that isn't there? Type it into New category and add it.
  • Charges in Work (Reimbursed) are tracked on the Reimbursable tab and kept out of household spending.
  • Charges in Housing (rent, mortgage, HOA) can be shown or hidden from spending with the switch in the Data health bar.
How the settle-up is worked out (with an example)

Every charge is either one person's, or Shared. Each person's fair share is their own charges plus their percentage of the shared ones. What they actually paid is compared with that fair share, and the difference is what changes hands.

Example with a 50/50 split. In a month: spent $200 on their own things, $100 on theirs, and $1,000 went on shared things. paid $900 toward the card, paid $400.
Own charges$200$100
+ 50% of shared $1,000$500$500
Fair share$700$600
Paid$900$400
Ahead (+) / behind (−)+$200−$200

So owes $200. (If the card isn't fully paid yet, the unpaid part is shared in the same proportion, so it doesn't make anyone "behind".)

Who paid comes from your accounts. For a shared/joint card, the payment rows in its file say who paid the bill: assign each one on Settle-up. For a card one person always pays, every charge on it counts as paid by that person; that's how two separate cards still settle correctly. Cash and other out-of-pocket costs go on Other costs, with who paid and how it splits.

The Home tab can add a down-payment equalization on top, and reimbursable work spending can be netted out. Every setting is on the Settle-up tab, and the ledger there shows each line of the math.

Accounts: a shared card, separate cards, a checking account

Each import goes into an account you name (e.g. "Joint Visa", "Sam's Mastercard", "Checking"). Each account has a who pays it setting:

  • Shared (we both pay it): the payments in the file decide who paid. Typical for a joint credit card.
  • pays it / pays it: every charge counts as paid by that person. Their card payments are ignored, since they'd only double-count.

Pay a shared card from a joint checking account? Set those card payments to Joint funds on Settle-up. They then count for both of you in your split, and the money each of you put into the joint account (paychecks, transfers in) is what shows who paid, if you import that account too.

Importing a checking account? Credit-card bill payments there will show up as payments and are left out of spending, which avoids counting the same groceries twice (once on the card, once as the card bill). Paychecks and transfers in are positive deposits: the importer reads them as credits. Enter income on the Income tab instead, and delete deposit rows you don't want in spending.

Other costs, recurring bills, income and cashflow
  • Other costs holds money that never appears in an imported file: cash, Venmo, a one-off paid by check. Set who paid and how it splits, and it flows into the settle-up.
  • Recurring (on the same tab) is for flat monthly commitments that aren't on a card: rent paid by transfer, daycare, a gym paid in cash. They count in every month's cashflow.
  • Income is a month-by-month grid. Type take-home pay for each person (and anything else in "Other"). The grid subtracts card spending, housing, recurring and dated one-offs to show what you kept each month and the running total.
Home, net worth and vehicles
  • Home: home value, what's owed, and a ledger of who put money in (down payment, closing costs, extra principal). If you own it in agreed shares, it shows what one of you owes the other to even it up, and can add that to the settle-up.
  • Net worth: list savings, retirement and investment accounts with an expected yearly return and a monthly contribution, plus other assets (enter a loan as a negative number). It projects 5, 10 and 25 years ahead. These projections are illustrations, not advice.
  • Vehicles: yearly running cost of each car from miles per week, MPG, fuel price, insurance and maintenance; an odometer log; an editable maintenance schedule; and a "what if we replace it" scenario.
Saving your data: please read this one

Everything you enter is saved automatically, but only in this browser on this device. There's no server copy. That's what makes it private, and it means:

  • Clearing your browser's history/site data, using a private window, or switching browser or device starts you from empty.
  • Click ⤓ Back up at the end of each session. It downloads one small file (household-budget-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json). Keep it in a folder you back up, or your own cloud drive.
  • To pick up on another device or browser, open the tool there, go to Save & sync → Restore and choose the file. Replace swaps in the backup exactly; Merge combines it with what's already there without losing transactions.
  • Want a spreadsheet? Save & sync → Export transactions (CSV) opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets.
Using it offline: download the app

The whole tool is one file. On Save & sync, click Download this app and save household-budget.html anywhere on your computer. Double-click it to open it in your browser. It runs with no internet connection.

The downloaded copy keeps its own data, separate from this web page (browsers treat them as different places). To move your data across, back up here and restore it there, or the other way round.

Two people, two devices: syncing

Each of you can keep your own copy and import your own cards. To combine them, use Save & sync → Sync directly:

  1. One of you clicks Create invite code and sends the code (a text message is fine).
  2. The other pastes it, clicks Generate response, and sends that code back.
  3. The first pastes the response and clicks Connect & merge. Both copies now hold everything.

The two browsers connect directly to each other, encrypted. Your data doesn't pass through any server; a public "STUN" helper only helps the two browsers find each other. Add a shared passphrase for extra encryption of the codes. If a live connection won't work (some office networks block it), send a backup file instead and use Restore → Merge. Transactions always merge; nothing is lost.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

"Nothing parsed." The file needs a date, a description and an amount on each row. If you pasted, make sure the columns came along (paste from a spreadsheet keeps them). PDFs won't work; download CSV instead.

My spending shows up as refunds / negative. That file used the opposite sign. Click Undo last import, set Charges are to the other option, and import again.

The card bill shows as spending. The payment wasn't recognized. On Transactions, find the row and set its category to Payment.

I don't see last month. Check the Months range at the top, or press All. Rows dated in the future are hidden.

Is it really private? Yes. The page has no analytics and makes no network requests with your data; it doesn't need a connection once it's loaded. The only network use is the optional sync helper described above.

Does it give financial advice? No. It's a calculator for your own numbers. Projections assume steady returns and ignore taxes and fees.

How the settle-up works

Fair share = your own charges + your percentage of shared charges (+ your share of Other costs). Net = what you paid − your fair share. The person who's behind pays the other half the gap, and you're even.

  1. Set the split with the slider. 50/50, or by income.

  3. Read the bottom line. The two ledgers show every number behind it.

How vehicle costs are worked out

Yearly cost = fuel (miles a week × 52 ÷ MPG × fuel price) + insurance + maintenance. For an EV, put the cost per "gallon-equivalent" or set MPG to miles per kWh and the price to $/kWh. The odometer log works out real miles a week. The maintenance schedule uses common intervals; edit them to match your owner's manual. Values here also count toward Net worth.
