Craftmas: the craft night is the new holiday party

Instead of a cocktail party, people are asking friends over to paint ornaments, string beads and make gifts together. Etsy saw it in its search data first and named it in its Fall/Winter 2026 trend report. Below: why it works, then a planner that turns a head count and a few projects into the shopping list, the cost, the run of the night and the invitation.

Something to do with your hands

A craft gives a room of half-strangers a reason to sit together and talk without the pressure of a party where all there is to do is drink and mingle.

Everyone leaves with a gift

An ornament you made goes on a tree every year. Plenty of guests finish one for themselves and start a second for someone else.

Cheaper than catering

Most of the projects below run $5 to $20 a person in supplies, and snacks you can eat with one hand are all the food it needs.

Plan your craft night

Set how many people are coming and pick one to three projects. One project keeps it simple; two lets people move between a quick make and a slow one. The plan saves in this browser, and the link carries it if you want to send it to a co-host.

Hosting it

The craft is the easy part. These make the night.

Set up stations, not seats

One table per project, with its supplies laid out by step. People drift between them, and nobody waits on the glitter.

Cover everything first

A roll of kraft paper taped over the table doubles as a sketch pad. Keep paper towels and baby wipes on every table.

Food away from the paint

A separate snack table keeps brushes out of the dip. Things you can eat with one hand and no fork: crostini, cookies, a cheese board, a big pot of cider or cocoa.

Do a five-minute demo

Show one finished example and make the first steps in front of everyone. Then leave the sample on the table to copy.

Names and drying space

Painted and glued pieces need an hour or more to dry. Set out a labeled tray or a sheet pan per guest, and a stack of take-home boxes or bags.

End with a swap

Ask everyone to make two of something and trade one before they leave. It gives the last half hour a shape and a group photo.
