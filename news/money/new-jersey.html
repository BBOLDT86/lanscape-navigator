The money · race by race · New Jersey

In New Jersey, Republicans outspent Democrats for Tom Kean Jr. and lost

Most of the outside money in New Jersey went to one House district, the 7th. A House Republican super PAC paid for TV ads through another committee, then pulled out. In South Jersey, a late Democratic ad buy came before an upset.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 12, 2026. 21 sources. All the states › · How New Jersey voted ›

New Jersey did not elect a governor this year. Mikie Sherrill, a Democrat, was inaugurated in January as the state's 57th governor1 after winning the 2025 election. The Watch has not yet read the Federal Election Commission's figures for the state. The dollar amounts below come from filings as reported by New Jersey outlets and are attributed to them. The totals changed from week to week, so outlets that reported on different dates give different numbers.

The Senate race was not close. Sen. Cory Booker, a Democrat, beat Republican Justin Murphy 1,819,769 votes to 1,023,549, a margin of 27.3 points. Kamala Harris won the state by 5.9 points in 2024. Most of the outside money went to the 7th Congressional District, which runs across Hunterdon and Warren Counties and parts of Morris, Somerset, Sussex and Union2.

Where the money came from

The Senate money was almost all Booker's. The New Jersey Globe reported that from Jan. 1, 2021, to Oct. 14, 2026, Booker brought in $35.9 million to Murphy's raised $68,188.3 In the third quarter, according to the New Jersey Monitor, Booker spent a little less than $2.3 million, with $1 million in the form of a transfer to the Democratic State Committee.4

In the 7th District, the Democratic challenger raised more than the Republican incumbent. Rebecca Bennett, a former Navy helicopter pilot, outraised Kean through Oct. 14, $8.1 million to $6.1 million5. Outside groups spent more than either campaign. Insider NJ counted $13.5 million spent so far and all but about $200,000 in the general election6 by mid-October.

The biggest single spender was Land I Love PAC, a super PAC. Insider NJ, citing OpenSecrets data, said nearly all of its independent spending so far has been against Bennett, a total of $4.1 million.6 NJ Spotlight News reported that the group was created in May 2025 and appears to have no clear connection to New Jersey or the district.7 The FEC's summary of the committee shows $5,146,983.00 in total receipts, $679,250.00 from individuals, and $4,467,733.00 from other committees8. The Monitor reported that most of the money came from one source: nearly $4.2 million from the Congressional Leadership Fund, House Republicans' super PAC9. Kean also got help from $460,408 in digital ads and mailers from Elon Musk's America PAC, $38,976 in digital ads from the Trump-aligned super PAC Maga Inc., and $160,576 in text messaging from No Going Back PAC9.

On the Democratic side, House Majority PAC, the House Democrats' super PAC, spent the most. VoteVets.org also spent more than $1 million on ads promoting Bennett.6 Insider NJ reported, citing unnamed sources, that Republicans had spent or reserved $14 million to help reelect Kean, compared to $7.8 million meant to help Bennett6.

The 2nd District, in South Jersey, drew less money. Rep. Jeff Van Drew, the Republican incumbent, had raised nearly $2.5 million10 by Oct. 14, while his Democratic challenger, Cape May Mayor Zack Mullock, had raised $960,94810. Project 218, a liberal super PAC, reported spending nearly $1.5 million for Mullock or against Van Drew10. A group associated with the national dental lobby11 planned about $64,000 in mailers and texts for Van Drew, who is a dentist.

The ads

Land I Love PAC's ads ran on broadcast television in New York. According to Federal Communications Commission records cited by NJ Spotlight News, it bought air time for political ads to run in the expensive New York City media market, including during news, NFL games and comic shows12.

Kean's campaign and the state Republican Party aimed at Bennett's career in health care. Kean's TV ads said she was a millionaire. The New Jersey Republican State Committee ran a digital ad, "Two-Faced," saying Bennett had profited off denying health care to sick patients13. Bennett has repeatedly declined to state her net worth; her personal financial disclosure lists her financial assets at between $449,000 and $1.8 million.14

House Majority PAC reserved almost $2.5 million for cable TV advertising in the New York City area15. Its ads focused on Kean's long absence from Congress when he was treated for depression.15 In one, patients in a doctor's waiting room complain that the doctor never showed up. The PAC and Bennett's campaign paid jointly for another TV ad, which said Kean had doubled his net worth in Congress. The Globe called that claim misleading: the ad compared the $10.7 million low-end estimate from 2022 with the $21.5 million high-end estimate16 from his latest financial disclosure.

In the 2nd District, Project 218 paid for an ad tying Van Drew to Trump17. Reports differ on where it ran: NJ PBS reported $1 million in radio ads, and the New Jersey Globe reported a broadcast TV buy. Van Drew's own ads tie Mullock to electric company executives who keep raising rates.17

Whether it worked

In the 7th District, the side that spent more lost. In mid-October, the Congressional Leadership Fund and President Donald Trump's Affordability and Safety PAC are withdrawing their remaining advertising investments in Kean's district18, the New Jersey Globe reported. The New York Times had reported the decision first. Kean's campaign answered that "Republicans have massively outspent her so far, and we'll be at parity on the airwaves down the stretch."19 Bennett won, and the Associated Press called the race at 9:48 p.m. Tuesday.20 The figures were different by the end. After the election, the Globe reported that the House Democratic super PAC spent $3.1 million to help Bennett while its Republican counterpart spent $2.1 million in support of Kean, according to the research group OpenSecrets.5

In the 2nd District, the candidate with less money won. Unofficial county results reported by SNJ Today show Zack Mullock with 128,162 votes (51.1%) and Jeff Van Drew with 120,190 (47.9%)21. Van Drew conceded before the race was called21.

Booker spent more and won by a wide margin, but he had little competition. Because the race was never close, all of the money Booker has left over from this year's campaign can be used in 2028.3
