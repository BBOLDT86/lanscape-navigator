Wisconsin's record governor's race: GOP outspent Democrats nearly 2-to-1 and lost

Three billionaires sent tens of millions through the Republican Party of Wisconsin to Tom Tiffany, and a PAC funded by Dick Uihlein added nearly $20 million more. David Crowley was outspent nearly two to one and won by eight points.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 11, 2026. 18 sources. All the states › · How Wisconsin voted ›

Wisconsin's race for governor cost more than any before it, and the side that spent the most lost. WisPolitics tracked a record $195 million in spending overall in the gubernatorial race.1 That includes $123.6 million in spending by Tiffany and Republicans, who outspent Crowley and his backers nearly 2-to-1.1 Milwaukee County Executive David Crowley, a Democrat, beat U.S. Rep. Tom Tiffany, a Republican, 1,440,989 votes to 1,227,675. That is an eight-point margin in a state Donald Trump carried by 0.9 points in 2024. Because this is a state race, the Federal Election Commission has no figures for it. The numbers below come from filings with the Wisconsin Ethics Commission and from press tallies.

Where the money came from

Most of Tiffany's money came from three donors and passed through the state Republican Party. The donors were Richard and Elizabeth Uihlein, co-founders of ULINE packing supplies, and Diane Hendricks, co-founder of ABC Supply Co., a building supplies company2. Between July 28 and Sept. 23, the Uihleins each gave the state GOP $11 million, and Hendricks gave the party $5.6 million.3 In the same period the party sent Tiffany's campaign $24.2 million, according to WisPolitics. Hendricks and the Uihleins have collectively given the state GOP $39.2 million since Jan. 1, and the party transferred nearly $34.4 million to Tiffany.3

Outside groups added more. Restoration of America PAC is registered with the Wisconsin Ethics Commission as an independent expenditure committee4, which can raise and spend without limits as long as it does not coordinate with a campaign. A check of state records found a $10 million donation in August5 to the PAC from Dick Uihlein. By late October the PAC has reported almost $20 million in independent expenditures for the race6. The RGA has spent $17.9 million on independent expenditures in the governor's race since mid-July, according to its filing with the state.7 Crowley's campaign also pointed to $9.3 million in spending by First Amendment LLC, a Virginia-based group that doesn't have to disclose its donors8. Money like that, whose donors are never reported, is what "dark money" refers to.

The Democratic side used the same route on a smaller scale. Crowley had received $14.4 million in transfers from WisDems this year with another $911,440 in in-kind contributions.6 Late in the race the biggest single source was LinkedIn co-founder Reid Hoffman. Hoffman contributed $8 million to the Democratic Party of Wisconsin between September 24 and October 20.9 On the Dem side, only the Democratic Governors Association has topped $2 million in spending with more than $20 million in paid media backing Crowley and opposing Tiffany.6 Among donors who gave directly to the campaigns, Crowley actually led: Crowley has actually outraised Tiffany slightly from individual donors, at about $9 million to about $8 million2.

The ads

Tiffany has focused his spending on paid media with his reports detailing $40 million spent on TV and digital ads6. His best-known ad, "Data Center David," started airing on 08/17/2610 according to AdImpact. It used a clip of Crowley calling Wisconsin a possible global "AI and data hub" and said Crowley's campaign was funded by the people building data centers. The Milwaukee Courier reported that Crowley's money came from workers' trade unions, like laborers, electricians, pipe tradesmen, carpenters and plumbers, not major data center developers.11 Late in the race Tiffany's campaign also ran a 15-second spot about Hoffman's past ties to Jeffrey Epstein.

The Uihlein-funded PAC ran ads on crime and taxes. One TV spot using footage from a brawl at the Wisconsin State Fair to knock Crowley on crime12 said Milwaukee's murder rate had soared. WisPolitics noted that Milwaukee police figures showed murders climbed to 141 in 2025 from 134 in 2024. Year-to-date, murders are down 23%.12 A YouTube ad called Crowley the "Milwaukee Taxman." The PAC also paid for mail: in October it reported over $848,300 for mailers and phone calls/texts in opposition to Crowley.4 In the final week, the Republican Governors Association's Right Direction Wisconsin accuses Crowley of "funneling tens of millions of tax dollars to his wife's employer."7

On the Democratic side, the DGA's Protect Our Wisconsin started early. In July, before the primary, it ran 15-second spots on abortion and Jan. 6. AdImpact had tracked nearly $477,000 in buys on broadcast TV in the Milwaukee, Green Bay and La Crosse-Eau Claire markets.13 Later DGA ads went after Tiffany on data-center tax breaks and his votes on releasing the Epstein files. Crowley's own ads made the donors the subject. One Crowley ad proclaimed: "Wisconsin's not for sale, but Tom Tiffany is, and it'll cost ya."14

Did the money work?

Republicans outspent Democrats for the whole race, though the gap shrank late. In early October a WisPolitics review found that Tiffany and his backers have spent $54.8 million compared to the $21.6 million by Crowley and those boosting his campaign15 since the August primary. In the last month, from Oct. 6 through Monday, Tiffany and his allies had $6.8 million in reservations, compared to $4.8 million by Crowley and those supporting.16

Tiffany still lost by eight points. That was an 8.9-point move toward Democrats from the 2024 presidential vote. Wisconsin News Connection reported that conservative-aligned groups accounted for nearly 80% of reported outside spending in Wisconsin, yet Democrats won the historic governor's race and flipped both chambers of the state Legislature.17

The money's critics and its defenders each had a target. Crowley ran against the billionaires backing Tiffany, and Tiffany's campaign answered with Hoffman. Analysts were careful about cause and effect. Molly Carmichael of the Wisconsin Democracy Campaign said "There's no definitive answer on how much that money moves people"18. Northwestern law professor Michael Kang pointed to deep political sentiment, which outweighed even enormous campaign war chests.17 In this race, the side that spent more did not win.
