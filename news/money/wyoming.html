In Wyoming, a $1.45 Million Primary Attack Failed to Stop Eric Barlow

Wyoming's biggest outside spender was a state PAC funded through a committee tied to Harriet Hageman, and it tried to stop Eric Barlow in the governor's primary. He won anyway. Hageman spent about 70 times as much as her Senate opponent.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 9, 2026. 18 sources. All the states › · How Wyoming voted ›

The money fight that mattered most in Wyoming this year happened in August, and it wasn't about a federal seat. The Watch's read of Federal Election Commission data found no outside spending reported in the state's federal races. The heaviest spending went through committees registered with the state, in the Republican primary for governor. Much of that money came through a federal committee connected to Rep. Harriet Hageman, who was running for the U.S. Senate at the same time.

Where the money came from

State Sen. Eric Barlow, a Gillette rancher and former speaker of the state House, raised $2.05 million across the primary and general election cycles, with $1.55 million of that (75.4%) from individual sources. He spent $1.7 million.1 WyoFile reported that this set a new fundraising record for a statewide race in Wyoming.2 His largest single donor was the Civil Leadership PAC, a Gillette committee chaired by Paul Hladky, with Adrian Gerrits as treasurer.3 The Gillette News Record reported that the PAC was created by owners and employees of Cyclone Drilling4 and gave more than $160,000 to Barlow. Cowboy State Daily found that the minerals industry largely funded Barlow's political action committee contributions1.

The money against Barlow came mostly through one committee. According to state filings reported by Cowboy State Daily, Wyoming's Turn PAC spent $1,448,385 in the state's primary election5, making it the state's top-spending PAC. Of that intake, 95.2% came from the federal-level Women For Responsible Government PAC, which federal campaign filings say is affiliated or connected with the Hageman Senate Fund5. The Wyoming Tribune Eagle reported that the Massachusetts-based PAC sent $1.47 million to Wyoming's Turn between July 15 and Aug. 5, in four large contributions of $475,000, $375,000, $320,000 and $300,000.3 As of the final primary debate, that federal PAC hadn't disclosed its own donors6. A separate group with a similar name, Women for Responsible Government Action, also ran TV ads. Hageman adviser Tim Murtaugh described it as a 501(c)4 run by Hageman's allies, not Hageman7. Groups organized under section 501(c)(4) of the tax code generally don't have to name their donors. That is what the legal term "dark money" refers to.

Barlow's main rival, Superintendent of Public Instruction Megan Degenfelder, had backing from Hageman and President Donald Trump. Her campaign raised $1.28 million, including $522,000 in loans3, and $497,000 from her father8 was part of that loan total. On the other side, Keep Wyoming Working, a PAC that attacked Degenfelder, spent $228,534. Its lone contributor is a Washington DC-based Group, Government That Works PAC5.

In the Senate race, FEC figures compiled by WyoFile show Hageman raised $2,810,7549 and spent $3,254,295. Her opponent, former state Rep. James Byrd, a Democrat, raised $53,560 and spent $46,56110.

What the ads said

The best-documented ad was false. Cowboy State Daily reported that Wyoming's Turn PAC launched a mailer this week accusing Barlow, a Republican gubernatorial candidate, of sponsoring legislation to hike gas and diesel taxes.11 The bill it cited, House Bill 63 from 2020, was sponsored by the Revenue Committee, and Barlow was not then a member of it. Barlow voted against the bill, and it died on introduction.11

Before the mail went out, the attacks ran on television. A Barlow campaign statement said Women for Responsible Government Action has spent $202,265 and Wyoming's Turn PAC has spent $149,815 on TV, cable and satellite messaging7 that backed Degenfelder or opposed Barlow. Rebekah Fitzgerald, Barlow's spokeswoman, said the first TV ad against Barlow mischaracterized his votes on boys' and girls' sports.12 Cowboy State Daily found that the ad pointed to his vote against a 2023 bill, but Barlow voted in favor of that bill's 2024 successor, Senate File 9, which became law.7 Over the whole race, according to the ad tracker AdImpact as reported by the Tribune Eagle, $422,549 was spent on anti-Barlow and pro-Degenfelder ads.12

Keep Wyoming Working ran an ad against Degenfelder that opens on a female voice and an artificial intelligence-rendered avatar of Degenfelder in a sleek black suit13. The ad accused her of lobbying for tax loopholes for a company that later went bankrupt. Hageman blamed Barlow for the ad. Barlow, conversely, says he did not orchestrate the ad, and he too condemns it.13

Barlow's own campaign spent $224,664.69 on digital ads, $74,239 on radio ads, $43,675 on newspaper ads, and $385,415.55 on TV ads3 by mid-August, according to the Tribune Eagle.

Did it work?

Not against Barlow. He won the Republican primary for governor with 45% of the vote14, and Degenfelder finished second with 29%14. The biggest outside spender lost. The candidate who raised the most from his own donors won.

In the general election, Barlow faced Rebecca Bextel, the Constitution Party nominee. (The Watch's results feed lists her as an independent.) Bextel had told Tucker Carlson that Wyoming is "a very cheap state to buy politically,"15 repeating her claim that outside money bought Barlow the nomination. Her first report showed about $89,000 raised. She amended her campaign finance filings on Oct. 29 to report more than $362,000 in donations from the Wyoming Constitution Party.16 The party itself does not have to file until Nov. 13. The Democratic nominee, Ken Casner, raised zero dollars, and spent zero dollars.1 Barlow won 128,480 to 31,438 in AP's count, a 51.8-point margin that is 5.6 points wider than Trump's 2024 margin in the state. Bextel's share was about 16 percent, which WyoFile reports should make the Constitution Party the state's third major party.17

In the Senate race, the side that spent more also won. Hageman beat Byrd 122,157 to 65,097. But her 30.5-point margin was 15.7 points narrower than Trump's 46.2-point margin in 2024, even though she outspent Byrd about 70 to 1. The money doesn't explain that gap, and The Watch can't say what does.
