The money · race by race · Connecticut

Lamont's own millions met Fazio's public grant, and outside money stayed home

Gov. Ned Lamont spent $18.7 million through Oct. 27, nearly all of it his own money. Republican Ryan Fazio spent $17 million from Connecticut's public-financing grant. National groups that spent millions here in 2022 stayed out. Lamont won by 19 points.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 10, 2026. 15 sources. All the states › · How Connecticut voted ›

Connecticut's costliest contest this year was not a federal one. The Federal Election Commission data The Watch reviewed showed no outside spending in any of the state's congressional races. The money went to the governor's race. There, Gov. Ned Lamont, a Democrat, sought a third term against state Sen. Ryan Fazio, a Republican from Greenwich. Most of the story is in the two campaigns' filings with the State Elections Enforcement Commission.

Where the money came from

The two campaigns were paid for in opposite ways. Lamont opted out of public financing and has largely self-funded his campaign, as he did in his two successful runs for governor in 2018 and 20221. Fazio had to raise $325,000 to qualify for an $18 million grant for the general election2. It was the first since the General Assembly dramatically increased the public financing grant to $18 million.3

The totals ended up close. According to the CT Mirror's reading of the final pre-election reports, Lamont had spent $18.7 million of the $20 million he contributed to his own campaign account, including a $2.2 million loan; Fazio, $17 million, with $2 million left to spend.3 As of the October filing, Lamont raised $412,0002 from other donors. The money Lamont gave his campaign goes back to him if unspent.3 He spent less than he did four years ago, though accounts of the 2022 total differ: one CT Mirror story puts it at $26.2 million3, and another says he ultimately spent $25.7 million on the 2022 campaign.4

Fazio's money came late. The commission approved his public financing on Feb. 18, but he did not get his Citizens' Election Program grant of $18 million until July 151. His spokeswoman said the delay came from an investigation into a complaint filed before the GOP nominating convention in May that an independent-expenditure group, Hands Off Our Schools, and the Fazio campaign appeared to share a consultant.1 No wrongdoing was found, and the grant was paid, said the spokeswoman, Hannah Lemek.4 The CT Post quoted her differently: she said the complaint was withdrawn.5

That same group was the only visible outside spender in the general election. Hands Off Our Schools is a state independent expenditure committee, Connecticut's version of a super PAC. It is winding down with a $164,000 expenditure to produce and place digital ads promoting Fazio in the campaign's final days.6 The CT Mirror reported that the group is mostly funded by Fazio supporters in Fairfield County6 and earlier sent mail and text messages attacking Erin Stewart, the former New Britain mayor. Columnist Kevin Rennie, reading the group's filings, reported that George Fox of Stamford is the principal funder7, having given $130,000 through the end of 2025. PACs like Hands Off Our Schools can accept six-figure contributions as long as they do not coordinate with a campaign or party.6

The national money did not come. Neither the Republican Governors Association nor the Democratic Governors Association are playing in Connecticut this year — a departure from 2022, when the DGA spent $2.7 million and the RGA spent $3.7 million through PACs established in the state. Another GOP super PAC spent $1.65 million.8

What the ads said

Television was the biggest expense. Through September, Fazio spent $7.2 million to advertise on broadcast, cable or streaming television and more than $400,000 on text messages since July 1.1 His filings show $6.8 million to Strategic Media Services for television advertising and $1.2 million to Creative Direct for mailing services5, plus $344,525 for texting services to Grand Old Victory, a company owned by his campaign strategist, Tyler Flanigan1. Like Fazio, Lamont has spent about $7 million on television ads1. In the last reporting week, Oct. 21 to 27, Fazio spent more than $2 million, nearly all of it on television advertising ($1.2 million) or direct mail ($848,306). Lamont spent $1.16 million, most of it on television.3

Fazio's ads leaned on humor and electric bills. Fazio is cast as a superhero in one ad, strong enough to save Connecticut from expensive electricity and high taxes tolerated by Lamont.9 Another used a clip of the governor joking that if rates were really that high, people would be turning out the lights. In TV spots and a mailer, Fazio said "electricity rates are up 38% under Lamont, thanks to his hidden fees, and Eversource is counting on another 18%."10 A fact check of an earlier Fazio mailer noted that governors do not set electric rates, so blaming Lamont for raising electric bills that are based on rates and usage is problematic.11

Lamont ran paired 30-second spots. One was a positive ad set in a school. The other shows worried educators fretting about votes on education by Fazio in the state Senate.9 That second ad is based on Fazio's vote against a budget that increased spending for education and school meals.9 A health care ad featured President Donald Trump and publicizes the $500 million fund Lamont has used to offset actions by Trump or Congress undermining the Affordable Care Act.9 There are 15- and 30-second versions of the spots on television and digital media.9

Did it work

Lamont won 773,556 votes to 525,015, a margin of 19.1 points, according to the Associated Press count published by The New York Times. That is 4.6 points better than the Democratic margin in Connecticut's 2024 presidential vote. According to Fox61, it was Lamont who won by the largest margin he has in any of his gubernatorial races.12 Fazio conceded the race in a call to Lamont.13

The side that spent more did win, but by a small gap in money and a large one in votes. Lamont outspent Fazio by about $1.7 million through Oct. 27. The two spent about the same on television. The public grant meant Fazio could match Lamont on the air. The press reported that this did not make him well known. Polling showed Fazio struggled to make an impression on voters.14 A pre-election summary by MultiState, a government-affairs firm, said that despite Fazio's ad spending, three in ten likely voters have not heard enough about him to form an opinion15.

The national climate also counted. The CT Mirror reported that polling in Connecticut and elsewhere consistently shows voters blaming the president, his tariffs and his attack on Iran for the rising costs of heating oil and gasoline.8 Down the ballot, Republicans appear to have lost 8 seats13 in the legislature. The outside groups that might have tilted the race decided it wasn't worth the money. With public polling consistently showing Lamont with a double-digit lead over Fazio, the race has not attracted significant outside money.6
