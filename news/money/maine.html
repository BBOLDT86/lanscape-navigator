The money · race by race · Maine

In Maine, the bigger spender won the governor's race and lost the Senate race

Republican groups put well over $100 million into ads to save Susan Collins, and she lost. In the governor's race, a group tied to the Democratic Governors Association had the airwaves almost to itself.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 12, 2026. 22 sources. All the states › · How Maine voted ›

Democrats won both of Maine's top races on Nov. 3, but the money behind those wins ran in opposite directions. In the U.S. Senate race, Republican outside groups spent far more than Democrats and still lost a seat Susan Collins had held for nearly 30 years. In the governor's race, Democrats outspent Republicans by a wide margin, and Hannah Pingree won by 17 points.

A note on sources: The Watch's Federal Election Commission figures for Maine were not ready when this piece was published. The Senate dollar figures below come from the ad tracker AdImpact and from filings, as reported by Maine news outlets. The governor's race is a state race, and its money is reported to the Maine Ethics Commission.

Where the money came from

The Senate race was the most expensive in state history, with the tens of millions of dollars raised by the candidates being far surpassed by the hundreds of millions in outside spending.1 By late October, The Maine Monitor's review of AdImpact data counted more than $260 million spent or reserved on advertising2.

Most of it came from the Republican side. Maine Public reported that Republican groups put an emphasis on defending Collins' seat, spending more than $130 million through an armada of super PACs and dark money groups.3 The largest was the Senate Leadership Fund, the super PAC tied to Senate Republican leaders. Along with an offshoot, SLF is the biggest outside spender in the race, having booked nearly $50 million in Maine ads.4

The second was Pine Tree Results PAC, a super PAC run by Republican operative Scott Reed. It has booked more than $28 million in ads5, and her campaign and the Reed-led group raise money together through a joint committee.5 Its donors include Blackstone chief executive Stephen Schwarzman, who has given the new group $3 million5, and Citadel founder Ken Griffin, who donated $2.5 million6. Hedge fund manager Paul Singer and Liberty Media's John Malone gave $500,000 apiece7 in late 2025. Another $3 million came from Stronger America, Inc. That group is a 501(c)(4), and as a nonprofit "social welfare" group it is not required to release information about its donors8. That is what "dark money" means in its legal sense.

On the Democratic side, the biggest outside spender was WinSenate, a super political action committee linked to Senate Majority PAC9. It has booked $38.6 million in Maine.4 At one debate, Troy Jackson said there were "no billionaires funding me."4 Later, Jackson conceded he has billionaire support but said he owes them nothing.4

The governor's race was smaller. The race drew $20.6 million in ads including the primaries, according to AdImpact10, and Pingree's campaign spent $3.5 million on ads across the primary and general election to about $550,000 for Charles'.10 By July, Pine Tree State Prosperity, a national group affiliated with the Democratic Governors Association, has booked $11 million11 in ads. In September it cut $550,000 of that because it considered Pingree safe, and its future plans in the Maine race still topped $8 million12. Bobby Charles said in July that he had met with the Republican Governors Association and that it planned to help him financially, but AdImpact, as of Thursday, listed no future reservations in Maine from national Republican groups13. His main outside help was No Nonsense PAC, which reported nearly $1 million in spending10. Its donors included Thomas Klingenstein, a New York investor and chair of the conservative Claremont Institute, who gave $100,00014, and Roger Penske, the billionaire automotive and racing executive, who gave at least $200,000; David DeLorme, a Freeport businessman who contributed at least $200,00014.

What the ads said

Pine Tree Results PAC's ads attacked Jackson over taxes. One, titled "Taxin' Jackson," says Jackson taxed Mainers' food, your property, your car, your Social Security15. FactCheck.org found the ad's wording could give viewers the false impression that Jackson wants to increase the Social Security payroll tax, or that he wants to raise taxes on Social Security benefits.16 The ad also mentions a tax on a dog. FactCheck.org traced that to a 2003 bill that increased state licensing fees for dogs.16

In October, the Senate Leadership Fund started a set of ads, including one calling him "a crook and a creep."4 Another was a country song on Maine radio that mocks her Democratic opponent, Troy Jackson, for having children with his second cousin.17 Collins told reporters in Lewiston on Wednesday that the ad should come down.17

WinSenate ran its own ads, which will air on television stations throughout the state as well as on digital streaming platforms.18 One compared Jackson's record on health care with Collins's and said she cast the deciding vote for Medicaid cuts. The Bangor Daily News pointed out that Collins opposed the final bill, citing its projected $5.9 billion hit to Maine's Medicaid program.19 PolitiFact concluded that the ad distorts Collins' record on Medicaid.9 Jackson's own campaign spent at least $1.6 million2 on a single ad about how often Collins voted with President Donald Trump.

In the governor's race, the Democratic Governors Association's group ran two ads, "Dad" and "Could Never." They used remarks Charles made to the Christian Civic League about inviting pregnant women who are considering abortion to his office. Charles said he would uphold existing state and federal laws as governor.20 No Nonsense PAC mostly ran ads urging progressives to vote for Bennett, touting his record supporting LGBTQ+ rights21, an effort that independent candidate Rick Bennett called "cynical."10 The Charles campaign said it doesn't control it, doesn't coordinate with it22.

Did it work?

In the governor's race, the side that spent more won. Pingree beat Charles 387,226 to 265,982, a margin of 17.1 points. That is about 10 points better than Kamala Harris's 6.9-point win in Maine in 2024.

In the Senate race, the side whose outside groups spent more lost. Jackson beat Collins 375,516 to 330,994, a margin of 6.3 points, a little below Harris's 2024 margin. Money was not the only factor. Maine Public reported that polls throughout the winding and chaotic contest consistently showed that the majority of the Maine electorate was dissatisfied with Trump's second term3. It also found that the split-ticket voters who once helped Collins did not show up in enough numbers for her to overcome Jackson.3 It was her first election loss since losing the 1994 governor's race.3
