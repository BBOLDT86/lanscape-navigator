Who spent it

The committees — Super PACs, party committees and hybrids — behind the outside money, by what they spent across the races, and their record: a race counts as won when the side they spent for won it.

The buyers

The biggest contributors to those committees, by what they gave them this cycle, with the seats their money reached and how those races came out. Giving to a Super PAC is not buying a seat — the committee decides where it goes — but it is the closest the public record comes to a list of who paid for the ads. Donor lists fill in once the FEC key is set.

Where the ads went

Two measures. First, what committees reported paying Meta and Google directly, by month: a floor, because most digital buys go through media vendors whose names, not the platforms’, appear in the filings. Second, Google’s own transparency count of political advertising on its platforms (Search, YouTube and the display network), which covers every advertiser Google verified, whatever route the money took. Meta publishes its figures only in its Ad Library, which has no public file.

How we made it

  • Outside money is independent expenditures (Schedule E) aggregated by the FEC by candidate and committee for the 2025–26 cycle, including 24- and 48-hour reports; a committee’s spending “for a side” is its support of that side’s candidate plus its opposition to the other. Candidate totals are from Form 3 summaries. Winners and margins are AP’s count via The New York Times, matched by state, office, district and surname.
  • Donors are itemized receipts (Schedule A) of $10,000 and more to the committees that spent the most, summed by contributor name; a donor’s “seats” are every race the committees it funded spent on, won when that committee’s side won. Pass-through entities (ActBlue, WinRed) appear as contributors where the committee reported them that way.
  • Platform payments are itemized disbursements (Schedule B) naming Meta Platforms, Facebook or Google as the payee, $2,500 and up. They are a floor: digital buys through vendors are reported under the vendor’s name.
  • Google’s figures come from its political-ads transparency bundle: weekly spend by verified advertiser since Jan. 2025, geographic spend by state (cumulative), and per-ad spend and impression ranges with targeting. Meta has no equivalent file.
  • The written pieces are by Claude Opus 5.5 through Anthropic’s API: the data above goes in the prompt, every dollar figure must come from it or be attributed to a named filing or outlet, and every web claim carries the search’s own citation. Spending on the API is logged in spend.json.
