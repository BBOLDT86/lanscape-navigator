In Illinois, Pritzker's $90.5 Million Ran Into Almost No Opposing Ads

Gov. JB Pritzker put $90.5 million of his own money into a rematch with Darren Bailey, whose 2022 megadonor dropped out. Pritzker won by 23.5 points. Juliana Stratton won the Senate race by slightly more, with no outside money.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 11, 2026. 21 sources. All the states › · How Illinois voted ›

In Illinois, most of the money in the 2026 governor's race came from one person, and the other side barely answered it. Gov. JB Pritzker is a Democrat, a billionaire and an heir to the Hyatt hotel fortune. He poured $90.5 million into his campaign war chest this cycle1. Capitol News Illinois noted that four years earlier, Lake Forest billionaire Richard Uihlein gave Bailey $12 million and an outside anti-Pritzker PAC $42 million1. This time, Pritzker's 2026 spending has gone uncontested.1 Pritzker beat Republican Darren Bailey 2,456,413 votes to 1,495,307, a margin of 23.5 points, according to the Associated Press count reported by The New York Times.

Where the money came from

The largest single piece was a $65 million personal contribution Pritzker made to his campaign committee, JB for Governor, in August. That check left the campaign with roughly $44.4 million in cash on hand2 going into October. Even so, he spent less than he did last time: he contributed about $145 million of his own money to his 2022 reelection campaign against Darren Bailey.2 State filings show the spending speeding up through the year. The campaign spent close to $5 million in the first quarter of 2026 on paid media3. From April through June it spent more than $11 million, about two-thirds4 of it on media buys and production. From July through September it spent nearly $23.8 million2, and more than $12.4 million went toward television advertising.2

Some of the money went to other Democrats. In the third quarter the campaign transferred $3.55 million to the Democratic Party of Illinois2 and $1 million to the Cook County Democratic Party. Later, the Democratic Party of DuPage County received $200,0005.

Bailey had very little. Uihlein, his main 2022 backer, has given him just $6,900 this cycle.2 In the third quarter Bailey raised about $1.2 million and entered October with about $482,000 in the bank.6 His largest individual contributor was downstate construction company owner Sheldon Schulte, who gave the campaign $24,000.7 Much of what he raised went to raising more: in the spring quarter his campaign spent more than $900,000 on direct mail fundraising services4.

The Senate race is federal, and The Watch's data block shows no outside spending in it. A compilation of Federal Election Commission filings by the tracker VoteLog likewise counts $0 of independent expenditures disseminated after the March 17, 2026 primaries8. VoteLog also reports that Stratton's campaign has raised more: $7,230,577 to Tracy's $4,914,3638. Republican Don Tracy is a Springfield attorney whose family owns Dot Foods9. He lent his campaign $2 million in August 2025 and another $1.5 million in September 2026. The outside money in this race came earlier, in the Democratic primary. Pritzker gave $5 million to Illinois Future PAC, a super PAC run by former Pritzker aides10. The group spent $14.9 million on ads in the race boosting Stratton and attacking Krishnamoorthi.11

The ads

Pritzker's ads were mostly about Bailey. One ad claiming Bailey's school teaches that dinosaurs and humans coexisted12 showed actors in T. rex costumes. The school is Full Armor Christian Academy, which Bailey founded with his wife. Northern Public Radio reported that a textbook used there does mention this, although not in exact words.12

The closing ad was a TV ad in the Chicago area that claims Bailey has said "women should be treated inferior to men," and that he opposes abortion in cases of rape or incest.13 Related ads cite the school's curriculum, from Bob Jones University Press, on women in the workforce. The publisher told them the part about women in the workforce is "out of context"12. Bailey's wife, Cindy Bailey, held a news conference in Springfield on Oct. 27 to dispute the ads. Pritzker's campaign declined to walk back the ads.14 WGN reported the ad was unanswered by Bailey, who doesn't have resources for a counter spot.15

Bailey's filings show how little he could do. Through September, his only direct voter contact expenses were $6,000 for digital billboards and $2,500 for text messages.7 His campaign said its remaining cash would go to text messaging, digital advertising and other get-out-the-vote efforts2.

In the Senate race, Tracy put out a TV ad called "Extreme Team." It linked Stratton to Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed and streamer Hasan Piker, and called her a "Democrat socialist,"16 and it drew more than 4.3 million views on YouTube. Stratton's spokesperson said she had never met Piker. Beyond that ad, Stratton launched her general election TV ads only this month, while Tracy has relied on digital ads and highway billboards.17

Did it work?

The side that spent more won both statewide races, and the governor's race The Associated Press called the race at 7 p.m.18, the moment polls closed. Pritzker's 23.5-point margin was well above his 2022 win over Bailey, approximately 13 points19, even though he spent less than in 2022. Capitol News Illinois concluded that Bailey couldn't compete with Pritzker's vast financial resources, allowing the Democrat to control the narrative20.

Two things make it hard to give the money all the credit. First, Stratton won by 24.3 points, 2,499,776 to 1,510,621. That is slightly more than Pritzker's margin, and she had no outside money in the general election and went on TV only in October. Second, both Democrats ran 12 to 13 points ahead of the state's 2024 presidential margin of 11 points, in a year when Democrats rode a historic wave of momentum in races throughout the country.21 The results are considered unofficial until all votes have been certified by election authorities.18
