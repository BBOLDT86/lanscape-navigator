South Carolina: Outside Money Backed Graham, Andrews Won; Wilson Outspent Johnson

Super PACs tied to Senate Republicans, to Lindsey Graham's political network and to the data-center industry spent heavily for Sen. Darline Graham, who lost. In the governor's race, Alan Wilson outspent Jermaine Johnson about two to one and won.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 11, 2026. 22 sources. All the states › · How South Carolina voted ›

South Carolina's two statewide races went in opposite directions on Nov. 3, and so did the money. Annie Andrews, a Charleston pediatrician and a Democrat, beat Sen. Darline Graham 1,043,719 votes to 951,904, according to AP results published by The New York Times. That 4.5-point Democratic win came in a state President Trump carried by 17.9 points in 2024. Graham had been appointed to serve out the remainder of her brother Lindsey Graham's term after his sudden death in July.1 In the governor's race, Attorney General Alan Wilson, a Republican, beat state Rep. Jermaine Johnson, a Democrat, 1,051,018 to 960,276.

The Watch's Federal Election Commission data for South Carolina has not been read yet. The federal figures below come from filings as reported by the outlets named.

Where the money came from

Andrews had far more money in her own campaign. She raised $9.4 million in the third quarter to Graham's $2.4 million, according to FEC filings2 reported by the SC Daily Gazette. By her campaign's count she raised more than $25 million in all, while Graham raised nearly $2 million3, Semafor reported. About 95 percent of Andrews' money came from individuals4, according to The Post and Courier. The 314 Action Fund, a committee that elects Democrats with science backgrounds, bundled $3.6 million in contributions to her campaign.5

Most of the outside money went to Graham's side. As of late October, independent-expenditure filings showed PACs had spent $6.9 million opposing Andrews and $3.4 million opposing Graham, $8.4 million supporting Graham and $1.8 million supporting Andrews.2 The Senate Leadership Fund, the super PAC aligned with Senate Republican leaders, first planned to put $5 million into the race as an "insurance policy"5, according to Axios. After the election, NBC News reported the figure as $13 million to boost Graham, while Senate Majority PAC, aligned with Democratic leaders, announced a last-minute $750,000 digital buy.6

Security Is Strength PAC, a super PAC that backed Lindsey Graham for years, raised just over $4.5 million7 in the third quarter. The Post and Courier reported that much of its money is secretly sourced from a nonprofit called American Jobs and Security, long associated with the late Sen. Lindsey Graham.7 Nonprofits of that kind do not have to name their donors.

The AI and data-center industry also spent for Graham, but the totals reported for it don't agree. Fortune, citing Tech Influence Watch, reported that American Mission and Building the Future spent $4,801,981 supporting Graham8. CNN reported that Building the Future spent $2.5 million9 in the general election. Both groups are tied to the Leading the Future network, which is funded largely by Ben Horowitz and Marc Andreessen, who each gave $25 million, and Greg and Anna Brockman, who gave $25 million10. As of late October, Building the Future had not yet disclosed any donors.11 Against Graham, Fresh Start SC and New Deal For All Inc. funded close to $2.9 million in attack ads.7

The governor's race was a state race, so its money is reported to the South Carolina State Ethics Commission. In October filings, Johnson reported $867,000 raised against about $914,000 spent, leaving a little more than $135,000 in cash12. Wilson reported about $2.6 million raised, with about $1.6 million left entering election day.12 The Watch found no reporting of significant outside spending in the general election for governor.

The ads

Andrews got on the air first. Her campaign spent $6.1 million on ads from the beginning of September, according to AdImpact, while Graham's campaign spent no significant funds on ads after the August runoff.13 Andrews played to crossover voters and put her Republican father in ads.14 She also ran ads calling the incumbent "Data Center Darline," which framed Graham as more concerned with data centers than with living costs15.

Security Is Strength's best-known ad showed a fictional scene of a man in pink wearing a pink wig approaching a child in a grocery store16, and Trump reposted it. A CNN fact check found that the ad cites no source for its claim17 that Andrews supports gender surgeries for minors. Andrews has said for years that she opposes those surgeries17, and her reply ad says Graham is "lying"17.

The data-center money did not run data-center ads. Building the Future's two ads, which began Oct. 17, did not mention data centers and focused instead on Graham's work leading the state commission for blind residents.11 The Senate Leadership Fund said its money would go mainly on TV ads to "remind voters that Andrews is a Democrat."5

For governor, Wilson's closing ad features his two teenage children lamenting how much state government takes out of a paycheck18. His campaign described it as a six-figure statewide TV and digital buy. Johnson's campaign failed to book a statewide television advertising campaign needed to reach older voters19, The Post and Courier reported.

Did it work?

In the Senate race, the side with more outside money lost, and the candidate with more campaign money won. The two kinds of spending pull in different directions. In mid-October, a Post and Courier analysis counting outside spending and Graham's own found her side had outspent Andrews by some $3 million over the previous several weeks.7 Fortune called the data-center spending the AI industry's biggest bet on a competitive general election race8. CNN reported that Andrews flipped Colleton County, which voted for Trump by 19 points.20 The money was not the only factor. Graham started late, and a fall New York Times/Siena poll and criticism of Graham's inexperience21 had already made the race look competitive.

In the governor's race, the bigger spender won. Wilson outspent Johnson by a rough two-to-one margin19, according to campaign finance reports. But the 4.5-point margin was narrower than expected: the RealClearPolling average of three October polls had Wilson ahead by 11.4 points22. Readers can judge what the spending bought.
