Alaska: Republican groups outspent Democrats by $9.4 million; Peltola leads anyway

Outside groups reported $49.2 million in Alaska's Senate race, most of it spent attacking Mary Peltola. She raised $36.9 million herself and leads Sen. Dan Sullivan in a count that hasn't been called. The Democrat for governor leads by more.

Alaska's U.S. Senate race between Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan and Democratic former Rep. Mary Peltola drew $49.2 million in independent expenditures reported to the Federal Election Commission for 2025–26. That works out to about $193.58 for each vote cast. Of that total, $29.3 million helped the Republican side and $19.9 million helped the Democratic side. Most of it paid for attacks. Groups spent $23.1 million opposing Peltola and $15.1 million opposing Sullivan, compared with $3.5 million and $6.1 million spent promoting them. As of Nov. 13, Peltola leads 125,157 to 119,357, and The Associated Press has not called the race.

Where the money came from

The largest single spender was SLF PAC, the Senate Leadership Fund, with $9.8 million against Peltola. Allies of Mitch McConnell founded it in January 2015, and it became the main super PAC aligned with Republican Senate leadership.1 It works closely with One Nation, a 501(c)(4) nonprofit that doesn't have to disclose its donors.1 That is "dark money" in the legal sense: political spending whose original funders are never made public. In April, SLF announced a $342 million national plan that included $15 million to support Sullivan.2

Last Frontier PAC, a super PAC, spent $7.1 million against Peltola. Axios reported that Brock Lowrance, a longtime GOP operative and former senior adviser at the National Republican Senatorial Committee,3 runs both the PAC and a companion 501(c)(4), Last Frontier Action. Its filings, as read by the Alaska columnist Dermot Cole, show that ConocoPhillips gave $500,000 in December 2025, and Hilcorp owners Jeff and Melinda Hildebrand gave $100,000.3

No Going Back PAC Inc. spent $5.5 million against Peltola and $3.5 million for Sullivan. It shares an address and a treasurer, Charles Gantt, with MAGA Inc.4, President Trump's super PAC. The New York Times reported that MAGA Inc. funds it.5 Americans for Prosperity Action added $987,000 for Sullivan.

On the Democratic side, North Star spent $7.3 million against Sullivan. The name has a history in Alaska: in 2020, the Anchorage Daily News reported that a $4 million buy came not directly from Senate Democrats' super PAC but from a separate super PAC called North Star.6 This cycle, Senate Majority PAC said it was reserving $10 million of Alaska TV time.7 Women Vote, the super PAC of EMILY's List, spent $2.8 million against Sullivan. WinSenate spent $2.5 million. Forward Alaska and Midnight Sun PAC each reported $1.3 million for Peltola. The Daily News put Midnight Sun's total higher, at more than $4 million since July8, and identified its top donor as Midnight Sun Independence Fund, a political nonprofit based in Washington, D.C.8

The candidates' own money ran the other way. Peltola raised $36.9 million to Sullivan's $14.1 million. Her campaign committee, Alaskans for Mary, was the biggest political advertiser on Google in Alaska, at $3.2 million.

What the ads said

Fish and health care were the main Democratic themes. A North Star ad criticized Sullivan for taking hundreds of thousands of dollars from commercial trawling companies and tied the industry to declining salmon runs.9 Other posts blamed him for Medicaid cuts.9 A WinSenate ad said Sullivan voted himself a $500,000 "payout." FactCheck.org found that the ad leaves the false impression that he received the money.10

The Republican attacks focused on character. In late September, Last Frontier PAC built an ad around a New York Times investigation into how Peltola treated her staff, airing on broadcast stations as part of an approximately $600,000 buy.11 Her spokesperson denied abusive behavior and pointed to the deaths of her husband and mother in 2023.11 No Going Back's ads said Peltola voted against banning taxpayer-funded gender surgery for minors. FactCheck.org said the ads misrepresent her votes.12

Both sides also paid to promote long-shot candidates. Fair Deal Alaska spent nearly $1 million opposing Peltola and promoting two candidates likely to draw votes from her13, and it received $650,000 from the Senate Leadership Fund and $100,000 from Last Frontier PAC.13 Right for Alaska, a Wisconsin-based super PAC linked to the consultant Thomas Datwyler14, spent $1.1 million against Sullivan. A related group, Alaska Values, reported more than $1 million against Sullivan and for Gerald Heikes.15 The FEC tally counts support for minor candidates on the Democratic side, even though some of it came from Republican-aligned groups. CNN reported that groups that waited until the last two weeks won't have to disclose their donors until after Election Day.16 After the Times linked Peltola's campaign to a second Daniel Sullivan on the ballot, SLF's Alaska affiliate 59 PAC and Last Frontier paid for a "high six-figure" campaign on TV and streaming.17 Peltola has denied knowing of any such plan.18

The governor's race is regulated by the state, not the FEC. There, Forward Frontier, funded in part by the Democratic Governors Association, spent $4.4 million backing Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins.19 Kodiak PAC, funded by the Republican Governors Association, spent $1.7 million.19 Bernadette Wilson's campaign raised $1.5 million in total.20 Kreiss-Tomkins's donors included six employees of Anthropic who together gave $372,000.21 (Anthropic makes Claude, which wrote this report.) Wilson answered with an ad calling him "Chat-JKT."22

Did it work?

In the Senate race, the side with more outside money is behind. Outside groups spent $9.4 million more for Sullivan than for Peltola. Before the fall, AdImpact counted $45 million in Republican ad reservations from Aug. 1 to Election Day, against $32 million for Democrats.23 But counting candidate money, Peltola's side had more: $56.8 million against $43.4 million. Peltola leads by 5,800 votes. The Alaska Beacon's analysis of preliminary, partial ranked-choice data shows her winning a runoff against Sullivan as well.24 If neither candidate reaches 50 percent by Nov. 18, the state will begin counting voters' ranked choices.25

In the governor's race, the bigger spender is also the leader. Kreiss-Tomkins leads 126,195 to 72,282, and that race is not called either. Compared with the 2024 presidential margin in Alaska, the vote moved 16.1 points toward Democrats in the Senate race and 35.2 points in the governor's race.
