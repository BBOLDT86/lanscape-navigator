Alaska: GOP super PACs outspent Peltola's allies, but she outraised everyone

Republican groups spent about $29 million on outside ads in Alaska's Senate race. Democratic groups spent $21 million. Mary Peltola's campaign raised more than twice Dan Sullivan's. She leads an uncalled race heading into the Nov. 18 ranked-choice count.

Alaska's Senate race between Sen. Dan Sullivan, a Republican, and Mary Peltola, a Democrat and former congresswoman, drew $49.8 million in independent expenditures that committees reported to the Federal Election Commission in 2025–26. That works out to about $180 for every vote cast. In the FEC figures, $29.3 million helped the Republican side and $20.6 million the Democratic side. Other tallies run higher: the Anchorage Daily News, citing OpenSecrets, reported that independent groups have spent a combined total of more than $100 million on the race — including $43 million opposing Peltola and $40 million opposing Sullivan1.

Where the money came from

The biggest outside spender in the FEC data was the Senate Leadership Fund, the main Republican super PAC for Senate races, with $9.8 million against Peltola. In April it announced a $342 million national spending plan. It includes $15 million to support Sullivan.2 The fund's donor list is led by Elon Musk. But CNN and NPR reported that his $158 million gift went on to a separate committee working on the Texas Senate race. The Senate Leadership Fund contribution was then used to solely fund the Texas PAC3. Bloomberg reported that the fund's third-quarter receipts also included seven-figure donations from billionaires Stephen Schwarzman, Joe Ricketts and Stephen Wynn.4

Last Frontier PAC, a super PAC run by Sullivan allies, spent $7.1 million against Peltola. The longtime Alaska journalist Dermot Cole, reading its filings, reported that ConocoPhillips gave $500,000 on December 15 last year, while the owners of Hilcorp—Jeff and Melinda Hildebrand—gave $100,0005. A companion group, Last Frontier Action, is a 501(c)(4) nonprofit. Axios noted it can accept unlimited — and anonymous — contributions6; this is "dark money" in the legal sense, because the donors never have to be disclosed. No Going Back PAC spent $5.5 million against Peltola and $3.5 million for Sullivan. It shares the same address and treasurer, Charles Gantt, with the MAGA Inc.7 super PAC that is tied to President Donald Trump.

On the Democratic side, North Star spent $7.3 million against Sullivan, the most of any pro-Peltola group. Women Vote put in $2.8 million and WinSenate $2.5 million. The reporting reviewed for this piece did not name North Star's 2026 donors. In 2020, Alaska Public Media reported that the firm North Star hired to buy TV time is the same one as Senate Majority PAC, the group aligned with Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer.8 Senate Majority PAC said in May that it was reserving $10 million of Alaska TV time9.

Much of the Democratic side's money ran through Peltola's own campaign. She raised $36.9 million, against Sullivan's $14.1 million. Counting campaign money and outside money together, the Democratic side had about $57.5 million and the Republican side about $43.4 million.

The ads

On the Republican side, the ads went after Peltola's record. No Going Back's TV spots said she "wants our money working for they/them."10 According to AdImpact, the PAC has spent more than $3.8 million to air the ads, which have 8.6 million impressions so far10. FactCheck.org found that these ads misrepresent her votes11. Last Frontier PAC built an approximately $600,000 buy12 around a New York Times report on Peltola's treatment of her staff. Her campaign denied that she had been abusive. Late in October the PAC aired an ad accusing Peltola of a scheme to "Put a decoy Dan Sullivan on the ballot"13. Peltola has denied that she knew of any plan13 to put the second Dan Sullivan, a retired teacher from Petersburg, on the ballot.

On the Democratic side, a North Star ad criticizes Sullivan for accepting hundreds of thousands of dollars from commercial trawling companies, linking the industry to declines in salmon runs14. North Star was also the second-largest political advertiser on Google in Alaska, at $2.4 million. Only Peltola's campaign committee, Alaskans for Mary, spent more there, at $3.2 million. A WinSenate ad accused Sullivan of voting himself a $500,000 payout. FactCheck.org said the ad leaves the false impression that Sullivan received that money.15

Both sides also paid to promote long-shot candidates in hopes of pulling votes from the frontrunners. Fair Deal Alaska spent heavily on a fringe Democrat and a Green Party candidate. After the primary, its filings showed $650,000 from Senate Leadership Fund16 and $100,000 from Last Frontier PAC. Right for Alaska, which the Alaska Beacon describes as Democratic-aligned, spent $1.1 million against Sullivan. In the primary it had to disclose only the donor behind a single $100,000 contribution, a retired Massachusetts doctor.16 A related group, Alaska Values PAC, ran YouTube, Facebook and mail ads for Gerald Heikes, a Republican drywall contractor. One told viewers, "Rank one name, leave the rest blank: Only rank Heikes"17.

Did it work?

The side that spent more on outside ads, the Republican side, is trailing. The side that spent more overall is leading. The Associated Press has not called the race. Peltola leads 138,240 to 127,828, a 3.8-point margin, in a state Trump carried by 13.8 points in 2024. No candidate has a majority, so the race will be decided by ranked choice tabulation on Nov. 18.18 Before Election Day, Republicans had the bigger TV commitment: Republicans have reserved $45 million in Alaska advertising from Aug. 1 through Election Day, compared with Democrats' $32 million, according to AdImpact.19

The governor's race was not close. Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins, a Democrat, won with 139,293 votes to Bernadette Wilson's 77,110. Wilson was one of three Republicans on the ballot, and the Republican vote was split among them. According to Alaska Public Offices Commission filings reported by the Daily News, Kreiss-Tomkins raised more than $5 million, more than all his opponents combined. He was also boosted by millions from the Democratic Governors Association, which far outspent the Republican Governors Association in Alaska.20 Kodiak PAC, which is funded by the Republican Governors Association, has spent $1.7 million backing the GOP candidates and opposing Kreiss-Tomkins.21 In that race, the side that spent more won.
