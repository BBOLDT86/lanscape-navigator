In Alaska, a $31.6 Million Senate Air War Ends Almost Even

Outside groups split Alaska's Senate spending almost down the middle, but Mary Peltola's campaign outraised Dan Sullivan's by more than two and a half to one. She leads narrowly in an uncalled race. The Democrat for governor, who had far more outside money behind him, leads by a wide margin.

Federal Election Commission filings read Nov. 10 show that independent groups reported spending $31.6 million in the U.S. Senate race between Democrat Mary Peltola and Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan. Of that, $15.7 million helped the Democratic side and $15.8 million helped the Republican side. That works out to $144.34 for every vote cast. A week after the Nov. 3 election, the race has not been called.

Treat those FEC figures as a floor. The Anchorage Daily News, citing OpenSecrets, reported that independent groups have spent a combined total of more than $100 million on the race — including $43 million opposing Peltola and $40 million opposing Sullivan1. The Watch could not reconcile the two counts. The committee-by-committee figures below are the FEC's.

Where the money came from

The biggest spender was SLF PAC, the Senate Leadership Fund's super PAC, which reported $9.8 million against Peltola. The group publicly put its total commitment in the state2 at $17 million. The Alaska Beacon reported that SLF has drawn much of its cash in recent months from Elon Musk3. Issue One reported that SLF also takes in large sums from unknown contributors giving through their affiliated dark money groups4. That is the term for nonprofits that do not have to disclose their donors. One of them, One Nation, spent $1.3 million on Google ads in Alaska.

Last Frontier PAC, a super PAC backing Sullivan, reported $2.8 million against Peltola. The Alaska journalist Dermot Cole, reading its filings, reported that ConocoPhillips gave $500,000 on December 15 last year, while the owners of Hilcorp—Jeff and Melinda Hildebrand—gave $100,0005. Smaller Republican spenders included Americans for Prosperity Action ($987,000 for Sullivan), Club for Growth Action ($689,000) and America PAC ($390,000 against Peltola).

On the Democratic side, North Star reported $7.3 million against Sullivan. The Anchorage Daily News called it the largest outside spender on Peltola's behalf6. Women Vote!, the super PAC of EMILYs List, reported $2.8 million against Sullivan. The data block shows Midnight Sun PAC at $1.3 million for Peltola, but the Daily News reported that it has spent more than $4 million since July7. Its top donor is Midnight Sun Independence Fund, a political nonprofit based in Washington, D.C.7 Majority Forward, the nonprofit tied to Senate Majority PAC, is not required to disclose its donors8. Its affiliate, Majority Forward Fund, spent $2.2 million on Google ads in Alaska.

The biggest gap was between the candidates' own campaigns. Peltola raised $36.9 million and Sullivan $14.1 million. Peltola's committee, Alaskans for Mary, was the largest political advertiser on Google in the state, at $3.2 million.

Some money went to long-shot candidates. Right for Alaska reported $1.1 million against Sullivan and $700,000 for Republican Gerald Heikes. The Alaska Beacon described the group as Democrat-aligned and reported that another $1.3 million, came from what the group identified as an Alexandria, Virginia-based contributor called "Liberty Force Action Fund."3 Republican money played the same game. A super PAC that promoted Green Party and fringe Democratic candidates disclosed $650,000 from Senate Leadership Fund3.

The ads

SLF's ads said Peltola "sold Alaska out" and criticize her for siding with President Joe Biden6. Others featured veterans from Palmer and Eagle River and attacked her votes on the Permanent Fund Dividend. In the final days, SLF used CNN to announce a spot on TV and streaming platforms9 about a New York Times report linking Peltola's allies to a spoiler candidate. The buy is being paid for by 59 PAC, an SLF affiliate group in Alaska, and Last Frontier PAC9. Peltola's campaign and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee denied recruiting candidates.10

North Star ran an ad that criticizes Sullivan for accepting hundreds of thousands of dollars from commercial trawling companies, linking the industry to declines in salmon runs6. Other North Star ads blamed him for Medicaid cuts. An ad from WinSenate, a super PAC funded by Senate Majority PAC, claimed Sullivan had voted himself a $500,000 "payout." FactCheck.org found that the ad leaves the false impression that Sullivan received that money11.

Alaska Values PAC, another group linked to the same Wisconsin treasurer as Right for Alaska, bought YouTube ads telling voters to "Only rank Heikes"12. CNN reported that it has not disclosed any donors. On broadcast TV, AdImpact's count, reported by Politico, showed both parties posting over $9 million in reservations13 for the final three weeks.

Did it work?

So far, Peltola has 106,321 first-choice votes to Sullivan's 103,804, a lead of 1.2 points. In 2024, the state voted Republican for president by 13.8 points. The AP has not called the race, and Alaska's ranked-choice count will decide it. The Alaska Beacon reported that Peltola gained enough first-choice votes to outweigh Sullivan's runoff advantage14. A memo from Sullivan's campaign argued that the ranked-choice count still favors him. Tens of thousands of ballots are expected to be added to the tally on Tuesday.14

The outside money ended almost even, so it does not explain the result by itself. The clear money advantage was Peltola's own fundraising. The candidates the spoiler groups promoted are running far behind.

The governor's race was more lopsided, in money and in votes. Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins leads Republican Bernadette Wilson 107,166 to 62,500, a margin of 20.7 points, also not called. According to state filings reported by the Daily News, Forward Frontier, a group funded in part by the Democratic Governors Association, has spent $4.4 million backing Kreiss-Tomkins15. On the other side, Kodiak PAC, a group funded by the Republican Governors Association, has spent $1.7 million15. A pro-Wilson group is funded primarily by a Florida-based private prison company called the GEO Group16. Kreiss-Tomkins's top donors included six employees of Anthropic, a California-based artificial intelligence company, who collectively gave Kreiss-Tomkins $372,00017. (Disclosure: Claude, which wrote this report, is made by Anthropic.) In this race, the side that spent more is the side that leads.
