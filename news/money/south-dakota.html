Nearly $10 million on South Dakota's Senate race; Rounds wins by 21

Out-of-state super PACs spent millions for and against Sen. Mike Rounds, who beat independent Brian Bengs by a smaller margin than Trump's in 2024. Gov. Larry Rhoden outspent his Democratic challenger about 18 to 1.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 8, 2026. 17 sources. All the states › · How South Dakota voted ›

South Dakota voters filled two statewide offices on Nov. 3. The money in the two races looked very different. In the Senate race, committees from outside the state put millions into a contest between Republican Sen. Mike Rounds and independent Brian Bengs, with no Democrat on the ballot. The governor's race, the one The Watch tracked on its wave, was lopsided. The Watch has not yet processed the Federal Election Commission's data for South Dakota. The figures below come from filings as reported by the state's press.

Where the money came from

Total spending on the Senate campaign approached $10 million, according to campaign finance reports1 cited by South Dakota Searchlight. Both candidates raised a majority of their campaign contributions from outside the state and spent a combined $6.1 million.2 From July through September, according to their federal reports as summarized by KELO, Bengs raised twice as much money as Rounds did, while Rounds spent nearly double what Bengs spent.3

The outside money ran in both directions. Two federally registered PACs, Retire Career Politicians and Freedom from Politics, spent $1.7 million to promote Bengs or oppose Rounds4. Searchlight found that Retire Career Politicians took in $890,000 from the Western Futures Fund, a nonprofit that is not required to disclose its donors4. Because the original donors don't have to be named, this kind of money is legally known as "dark money." The PAC also got $500,000 from the Way Back PAC, which drew much of its own money from the same nonprofit, and $200,000 from California computer scientist Scott Shenker. Freedom from Politics Inc. received all of its $1.2 million from Stephen Pajcic, a Florida lawyer, Democratic politician and philanthropist.5 South Dakota Public Broadcasting spelled his name Steven Pajcic III6.

On the other side, the South Dakota Conservative PAC spent nearly $1 million while Defend American Jobs spent about $400,000 to support Rounds or oppose Bengs.7 The Conservative PAC received $200,000 from the late South Dakota billionaire-philanthropist T. Denny Sanford before he died7. It also took $521,000 from South Dakota First Action Inc., an entity that divulges little about itself publicly7. Defend American Jobs is a super PAC and FairShake's Republican-facing affiliate, a single-issue committee focused on cryptocurrency8. Separately, Wired magazine reported that AI-focused political groups have put nearly $1 million into supporting Rounds.9

In the governor's race, Rhoden's pre-general report showed his campaign received $2,735,219.69 and spent $2,760,044.38 through the October 14 close of the reporting period10. Part of that money paid for a primary and a runoff. Late filings showed $199,500 from the Free America Fund, based in St. Louis, Missouri, and $48,000 from the Strong Safe & Free PAC that was established to raise money for Rhoden's campaign.11 South Dakota News Watch calls the first group the Free American Fund, a federal PAC; Denny Sanford gave the largest amount to the PAC at $50,000.12 KELO reported that those PACs allowed donors to legally exceed the single-person contribution limit10. Democrat Dan Ahlers received $177,309.31 and spent $155,697.56 during the latest reporting period10. He did not report receiving any money from out-of-state or federal political action committees13.

The ads

The South Dakota Conservative PAC ran broadcast spots, starting in September, that called Bengs a "pro-abortion extremist"14 and put him alongside Joe Biden and Hillary Clinton. Bengs said the ad violated Federal Communications Commission rules. According to The Dakota Scout, the PAC stood by it.

The spending against Rounds attacked him over his ties to the president. Anti-Rounds advertising from outside groups characterized him as complicit in Trump policies, including higher tariffs and the war in Iran15. Bengs went after Rounds' links to the tech industry, including a Rounds senior adviser, Rob Skjonsberg, who has served as a state-level lobbyist for Facebook parent company Meta.15 Rounds answered: "Does anybody see where I would make a vote that would be different based upon individuals participating in funding my campaign15?"

Rounds' campaign focused on his opposition to abortion, his support of military spending, and his A+ rating (compared to Bengs' F) from the National Rifle Association.1 Bengs challenged Rounds to debates, but Rounds declined.1

In the governor's race, Ahlers' biggest expenses from June to October were advertising and salaries, nearly $120,800 combined out of almost $155,40013. The reporting reviewed for this piece did not describe Rhoden's general-election ads or where they ran.

Did it work?

Rounds won 184,589 to 120,509, a margin of 21 points, according to the Associated Press results published by The New York Times. Donald Trump carried the state by 29.2 points in 2024, so Bengs ran 8.2 points closer than the top of that ticket. In Searchlight's late-October count, about 40% of the spending had been in support of Bengs or against Rounds2. By that measure, the side that spent more won. The final margin was wider than the pre-election polls. Bengs trailed Rounds by 8 points in an Emerson College/KELO-TV poll4, while Mason-Dixon Polling and Strategy and South Dakota News Watch showed Rounds with a lead of 19 points4.

Rhoden beat Ahlers 193,333 to 111,157, a 27-point margin, 2.2 points closer than the 2024 presidential race. Ahlers struggled to gain a statewide foothold as his campaign's minimal fundraising allowed him to spend only $164,000 by the Oct. 19 reporting deadline.16 Rhoden's fundraising committee spent more than $3 million throughout the campaign.16 Much of that went to winning the nomination: Rhoden won after three elections in five months17. And he ran in a state where Republicans outnumber Democrats 2-to-116.
