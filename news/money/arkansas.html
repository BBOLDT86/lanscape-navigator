The money · race by race · Arkansas

Arkansas Republicans outspent their challengers many times over, and won by less

Sen. Tom Cotton and the allied super PAC America One far outspent Hallie Shoffner on ads. Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders outspent Fred Love about 95 to 1 on advertising. Both Republicans won easily but finished far below Trump's 2024 margin.

Republicans held both of Arkansas's top races on Nov. 3. According to the Associated Press results published by The New York Times, Sen. Tom Cotton beat Democrat Hallie Shoffner 480,627 to 375,647, an 11.9-point margin. Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders beat Democratic state Sen. Fred Love 477,138 to 341,666, a 15.3-point margin. Donald Trump carried the state by 30.6 points in 2024. In both races the winner spent far more than the loser.

A note on sources: The Watch's own read of Federal Election Commission data for Arkansas isn't finished, and governor's races report to the Arkansas secretary of state, not the FEC. The figures below come from those state filings and from news reports on the federal filings, and each one is credited.

Where the money came from

AdImpact data reported by Punchbowl News in late September showed Cotton and his allies have spent $13.6 million, while Shoffner and Democrats have spent just $208,703.1 That is about 65 to 1. Punchbowl noted that in 2020, Cotton faced Ricky Dale Harrington Jr., a libertarian, and spent roughly $900,000.1 It also reported that much of the spending came from a C4 nonprofit allied with Cotton earlier in the year.1

The largest outside group was America One, a super PAC. The Arkansas Democrat-Gazette reported that America One, a super PAC connected to Cotton, directed $4.5 million on media opposing Shoffner2. Its filings list two joint fundraising representatives in its current statement of organization: the Cotton Victory committee and the Cotton Majority Committee.3 In the third quarter, America One Policies, a 501(c)(4) nonprofit organization, contributed $450,000 to the super PAC. Members of the Stephens family -- including Warren Stephens, who currently serves as the United States ambassador to the United Kingdom -- gave a combined $250,0004. A 501(c)(4) does not have to disclose its donors, so money it passes to a super PAC can't be traced to the original givers. That is what the legal-policy term "dark money" means. Shoffner applied the term to America One Policies and said it was launched in 2019 by Cotton supporters5.

A second super PAC, Arkansans for Democracy and Justice, registered with the FEC last December, listing an address in Mountain Brook, Ala.3 By May it had reported $75,500 in independent expenditures opposing Shoffner as she ran against Lewisville Mayor Ethan Dunbar in the Democratic primary. The committee has spent $70,400 so far on media and texts objecting to Shoffner3.

Shoffner, according to The American Prospect, managed to raise a couple million dollars from an army of small donors and a handful of local Democratic committees, advocacy groups, and unions.6 The national party mostly stayed out.

Sanders's state filings showed she had raised about $8.5 million and spent about $7.6 million as of Sept. 307. Love had raised about $263,200. In September alone, 20 people who listed their place of employment as Entergy or Entergy Arkansas donated to Sanders' campaign7, giving a combined total just under $25,000.

The ads

According to the Arkansas Advocate, Cotton's ads portrayed Shoffner as soft on immigration and too closely allied with national Democrats like former Vice President Kamala Harris.8 In September, Cotton's team began running TV ads quoting Shoffner saying in a podcast interview that Arkansas farmers would "trade five Americans for just one" undocumented immigrant worker.9 She told the Advocate she was relaying another farmer's words rather than speaking for herself or her family.9 Earlier ads used a remark she made in June about defunding Immigration and Customs Enforcement "as it exists right now."10 In one spring America One spot, Marcus Richmond (R-Harvey) stars in an America One PAC ad opposing Hallie Shoffner11, according to the Arkansas Times.

Shoffner's own advertising started late. On Oct. 15 she announced two new ads that her campaign said are part of a $1.1 million buy. They include a 30-second spot featuring former Democratic Gov. Mike Beebe supporting Shoffner's bid.12 An earlier spot features a cattle rancher and former Cotton voter, who bashes the senator for voting down the farm bill twice and abandoning the state.6

In the governor's race, state filings showed that through Oct. 24, Sanders spent a total of $5.7 million on television advertising, direct mail and other advertising, according to the secretary of state's website. In contrast, Love spent $60,181 on other advertising13. In one Sanders ad, she tells viewers that taxpayers are paying for "guys like Brad who just choose not to work."14 Some right-wing commentators objected because the man shown on welfare was white. Her campaign described earlier spots as six-figure buys airing statewide on television and digital platforms.14

Did it work?

The side that spent more won both races. Polling moved toward the Republicans as the ads ran. An August Talk Business & Politics-Hendrix College poll showed Shoffner with a slim lead over Cotton10. By mid-October the same poll had Cotton ahead by 9 points. Its editor, Roby Brock, pointed to the barrage of millions of dollars of ads spent in opposition to Shoffner and in support of Cotton. Clearly, this has had some impact in the numbers15. In the governor's race, the same poll moved from Sanders leading Love 45-to-40%16 in August to 51 to 32 in October. Jay Barth, a Hendrix professor emeritus, said advertisements also had helped move the needle for Sanders.16

Both margins were still far smaller than usual for Arkansas. Cotton's 2020 margin was 33 points. This year, Libertarian Colt Shelby won more than 7.5% of the vote in the governor's race17. Not everyone agrees the spending paid off. Punchbowl suggested that because Shoffner is airing so few of her own ads, Cotton is likely raising voters' awareness of her candidacy.1 An Arkansas Advocate commentary argued that the national wave mostly passed over Arkansas18, but said Shoffner's loss shows the party still faces serious resource gaps.18
