The money · race by race · Oklahoma

Mazzei's $12.8 million in loans and RGA ads held Oklahoma's governorship

The Republican nominee paid for most of his own campaign, and a Washington-based PAC tied to the Republican Governors Association did most of the outside spending. He won by six points in a state Trump carried by 34.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 10, 2026. 17 sources. All the states › · How Oklahoma voted ›

On Nov. 3, Oklahoma's two statewide races had very unequal money, and the votes were closer than usual. Mike Mazzei, a Republican, Tulsa financial planner and former state senator, won the open governor's seat over Democrat Cyndi Munson, the Oklahoma House minority leader. The vote was 581,706 to 514,342, a six-point margin, according to the Associated Press count published by The New York Times. Donald Trump carried the state by 34.3 points in 2024. In the U.S. Senate special election, Republican Kevin Hern beat Democrat N'Kiyla Jasmine Thomas by 17.9 points.

The governor's race is a state contest, so its money is reported to the Oklahoma Ethics Commission, not the Federal Election Commission. The figures below come from those filings as the state's press reported them.

Where the money came from

Mazzei paid for most of his campaign himself. Filings show he loaned himself $1.8 million between August 11 and October 19, bringing his total donations to his own campaign to over $12.8 million, more than any other candidate for statewide office this year.1 Apart from that, he raised about $4 million, for a total of about $16.5 million as of Oct. 19.1 Oklahoma Voice described the money as $11 million in bank-backed loans2 at the time. The day after his August runoff win, The Oklahoman asked how he would repay the loans. Mazzei answered, "I haven't figured all that out yet."3 In a letter to state banking regulators, the Ethics Commission and the FDIC, State Rep. Jared Deck claims Mazzei received the loan with zero interest and plans to pay it back with donations made from his campaign committee.4

Munson hasn't loaned her campaign any money and raised $3.2 million over the course of her entire campaign.1 Her spokesperson said more than 18,000 donations from more 13,000 donors3 paid for the campaign.

The national governors' groups also put money in. According to The Oklahoman, the Republican Governors Association began spending $3 million in early October through an Oklahoma-based political action committee to support Mazzei, while the Democratic Governors Association said it would spend $500,000.3 Oklahoma Watch named the Republican vehicle: Oklahoma Values, a Washington, D.C.-based organization opposing Munson, accounted for more than half of outside spending before the election, and the PAC has organizational ties to the Republican Governors Association.5 Across all state races, outside groups spent more than $5.5 million, nearly matching the $5.6 million candidates themselves reported spending since September.6 Oklahoma Watch found that almost half of that outside money had no traceable donor. The groups must file quarterly reports showing their donors, but funders often conceal their true identity through a chain of nonprofits.7

The Senate race was even more one-sided. Hern raised more than $10.8 million between January and Oct. 14, including about $5 million in personal loans, more than $3 million transferred from other committees and nearly $2.6 million in contributions.8 Thomas, a former nurse, raised about $115,000.8 The FEC outside-spending data The Watch relies on had not yet been read for Oklahoma and shows none. The tracking service Legis1, however, reports that outside groups reported $2,449,305 in independent expenditures supporting Hern, with no outside spending recorded against him or in support of any other candidate9. That figure has not been checked against The Watch's FEC data.

The ads

Mazzei's first general-election spot was a 30-second ad called "Trusted." It highlights his financial-planning career and his proposals to cut property taxes for seniors and veterans, eliminate Oklahoma's income tax and reduce government spending.10 Later, his campaign ran an ad attacking Munson with a selfie that Imad Enchassi, senior imam of the Islamic Society of Greater Oklahoma City, took on a 2024 tour of the mosque.11 According to Enchassi, the ad shows Munson smiling at a board that says "To what extent Cyndi Munson is liberal" in Arabic.12 Mazzei spokesman Jahan Wilcox defended the ad. He said Munson had attended meetings with the Council on American-Islamic Relations, which he said "has documented ties to terrorism."11

Oklahoma Watch reported that the Oklahoma Values PAC has been running ads against Democrat Cyndi Munson in the governor's race6. The Watch could not find a reliable published account of what those ads said or which stations aired them.

Munson's first general-election ad was posted on Facebook. It shows family photographs as she describes her father's responsibilities, and she promises to "stand up to the insurance companies" and calls for higher teacher pay.10 One attack on Mazzei came from neither party. It was about the planned aluminum smelter in Inola, which Mazzei initially opposed, publicly reversing his stance hours before he received an endorsement from Trump.13 The ad began on social media and was later sent by text message. It was paid for by Fairness Corp Pac Inc., an Oklahoma PAC opposed to the smelter.4

Munson complained about the volume of ads: "I think what we're all tired of is big money coming in and flooding our TV screens and streaming services."5

Did it work?

In both races, the side that spent more won. In the governor's race, Mazzei's loans and the Republican Governors Association's $3 million far exceeded Munson's roughly $3.2 million and the Democratic governors' $500,000. Republicans swept every statewide office, and Mazzei carried 73 of 77 counties.14

But his six-point margin was 28.2 points smaller than Trump's in 2024. Hern's margin shrank by 16.3 points even though he raised about 93 times as much as Thomas. In Oklahoma County, Munson defeated Mazzei by nearly 25 percentage points.15 In Tulsa County, she won by about 10 points.16 Democrats also gained in the Legislature. They flipped six Republican-held state House seats in urban and suburban areas.14

Before the election, a Cross Timbers survey of 575 likely voters (Oct. 24 to 28) had Mazzei at 46 and Munson at 4317. The money paid for a victory but did not stop a large shift toward Democrats. Whether Mazzei would have lost without it, the filings cannot say.
