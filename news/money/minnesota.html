Uihlein money evened Minnesota's governor race; Klobuchar won by nearly 13 points

A super PAC funded almost entirely by the Uihlein family spent more than $14 million to help Lisa Demuth and attack Amy Klobuchar. Klobuchar won by more than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin. The Senate race got far less outside money.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read not yet. 18 sources. All the states › · How Minnesota voted ›

Where the money came from

The biggest outside spender in Minnesota this year was a committee formed for a single candidate. Restore Sanity, a GOP group that sprang up early this year to support Demuth's candidacy, is the biggest spending group in the race.1 By the last reports filed before the election, Restore Sanity had raised over $16 million this year — over $15 million from the Uihlein-funded Restoration of America PAC.2 That national committee is primarily funded by Wisconsin billionaire duo Richard and Elizabeth Uihlein3, who own one of North America's largest distributors of shipping and packaging supplies.3 The Minnesota Reformer, citing The Washington Post, reported that the Uihleins have donated $70 million this election cycle in favor of GOP candidates and causes, making them the fifth-largest donors in the midterm election so far4.

The candidates' own money was lopsided. According to state filings reported by the Reformer, Amy Klobuchar, the Democratic-Farmer-Labor (DFL) nominee, has raised over $15.5 million2, and since launching her campaign at the end of January, Klobuchar has spent nearly $13.5 million, with most of her expenses going to advertising.2 House Speaker Lisa Demuth raised $3.6 million as of Oct. 19, about $1.3 million of which came from an August public subsidy payment.2 Klobuchar opted out of the subsidy, which allowed Demuth to receive a larger payment.3

On the Democratic side, the main outside group was the Alliance for a Better Minnesota. The Reformer says it has historically been backed by wealthy benefactors like Rockefeller heiress Alida Messinger and public-sector unions.3 KSTP reported that the group had raised nearly $9 million as of October 19, with about $3 million spent opposing Demuth.5 More of its money went to a different race: it spent over $4.7 million on ads against Republican attorney general candidate Ron Schutz6. The DFL Party spent about $757,000 through mid-September on ads and materials to prop up Klobuchar.1

The U.S. Senate race followed a different pattern. The FEC figures for it have not yet been read for this report, so the numbers below come from the press. MPR News reported that the candidates slogged through a primary race in which more than $20 million dollars was spent by outside groups. That was mostly on the DFL side.7 After the primary, the race has fallen off a cliff financially. As of Friday, post-primary outside spending was in the ballpark of $2 million7. The Star Tribune reported that American Bridge 21st Century PAC accounted for most of that activity, reporting approximately $1.4 million supporting Flanagan, principally for billboard advertising8, and that Minnesota Forward, a Republican-aligned super PAC, reported approximately $207,000 opposing Flanagan8.

The ads

Restore Sanity's ads were mostly about the fraud in state social-services programs. MPR News quoted one: "Somali fraudsters have stolen billions, and Amy Klobuchar made it worse."9 Another said Klobuchar tried to send $1 million to Generation Hope, a Minneapolis nonprofit. The Star Tribune's ad check noted that Generation Hope has not been charged with fraud.10 KSTP gave a 15-second Restore Sanity TV ad about money transfers to Somalia a C-minus, finding no evidence in the ad that Klobuchar was acting to enable fraudsters to send money overseas.11 A third ad paired footage of Klobuchar speaking Somali at a 2017 community event with a narrator asking viewers to remember "who Amy Klobuchar had words for, and who she didn't."12 Over the year, the group spent over $5 million on digital and TV ads and direct mailers in favor of Demuth, and it has spent over $9 million on negative ads and mailers opposing Klobuchar's campaign.2

Demuth's own campaign spent over $2.2 million on digital and TV ads.2 Her first television ad of the general election accuses her Democratic rival of "securing" $1 million for Generation Hope13. In the final week she aired one saying, "These days, it's hard to even recognize Minnesota. Enough of the fraud and Somali gang violence"12.

The Alliance for a Better Minnesota spent mainly online and on streaming services. In August it launched a seven-figure digital ad buy against the Republican candidate for governor, Lisa Demuth. The first spot features Lisa Demuth praising Trump's disastrous agenda14. That description comes from the group's own announcement. Later spots focused on health care and the cost of living.8 A DFL Party TV ad attacking Demuth over health coverage received a B from KSTP's Truth Test.

In the Senate race, Republican Michele Tafoya ran a direct-to-camera ad that began, "I'm a pro-choice Republican. I'm not afraid to stand up to my party.7" FOX 9 reported that one ad links Tafoya to President Trump, while another questions how Flanagan's finances changed between 2018 and 2026.15

Did it work?

Klobuchar beat Demuth 1,569,698 to 1,206,583, a margin of 12.8 points, according to AP results via The New York Times. That is 8.6 points better for Democrats than the state's 2024 presidential margin.

Did the side that spent more win? On the year's state filings, spending was close to even. Demuth's campaign spent about $3.5 million and Restore Sanity spent more than $14 million for her or against Klobuchar. On the other side, Klobuchar's campaign spent nearly $13.5 million and her allies spent roughly $3.8 million more. Political scientist Steven Schier told KSTP that the outside money was "really helping Lisa Demuth be financially competitive with Amy Klobuchar."16 After winning, Klobuchar said voters had shown "that a Packers fan cannot buy our governor's seat, even if that guy is a billionaire."17

Republicans got close to Democrats in money but not in votes. Statewide, the Reformer counted over $36 million in negative ads in statewide and legislative elections since Minnesota's Aug. 11 primary election, compared to the $10 million they've spent on positive ads.6

The Senate race is the comparison. Peggy Flanagan beat Tafoya by 10.1 points with little outside money on either side after the primary. The Star Tribune reported that the Thune-aligned Senate Leadership Fund never funded an ad blitz in Minnesota that rivaled the spending in true swing states.18 The governor's race drew far more Republican outside money, and Democrats gained more there than in the Senate race (8.6 points against 5.9).
