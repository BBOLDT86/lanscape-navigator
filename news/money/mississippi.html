The money · race by race · Mississippi

In Mississippi, Democratic money came early and late; Colom won by 4,669

Outside Democratic groups spent millions attacking Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith, from digital ads and mail in the summer to an Epstein-survivor TV spot in the final week. Republicans answered late. Scott Colom won by 4,669 votes in uncertified results.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 12, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 12, 2026. 23 sources. All the states › · How Mississippi voted ›

Scott Colom, the district attorney from Columbus, finished with 473,336 votes to Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith's 468,667. That is a margin of 4,669 votes, or half a percentage point, according to Associated Press figures carried by The New York Times. Donald Trump carried Mississippi by 22.9 points in 2024. The AP called the race for Colom based on uncertified results1, and Hyde-Smith conceded in a video posted on X Wednesday evening2. Counties must submit their certified results within 10 days of the election to the secretary of state.3

A note on sources: the Federal Election Commission extract The Watch works from showed no independent expenditures in this race. Mississippi elects its governors in odd-numbered years, so there was no governor's race on the ballot. The outside-spending figures below come from FEC filings as tallied by reporters, and from the ad-tracking firm AdImpact as reported by news outlets.

Where the money came from

The candidates raised similar amounts but spent them differently. In the third quarter, Colom raised $1.3 million and had $991,802 in cash on hand. Hyde-Smith raised $642,988 last reporting cycle and has $2.4 million on hand4, according to Mississippi Today. The Commercial Dispatch in Columbus reported that about $138,500 of Hyde-Smith's fundraising came from PACs, including $5,000 from the American Israel Public Affairs Committee5. It also reported that more than 90% of Colom's quarter came from individuals.

Outside money arrived first on the Democratic side. A review of FEC records published Oct. 21 by Derrion Arrington at msindy.org found that seven committees have reported independent spending in Mississippi's U.S. Senate race. Five of them are spending against incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith.6 The largest was AB PAC, the super PAC of American Bridge 21st Century, with 21 expenditures against Hyde-Smith totaling $2,022,930.906. That money went through two vendors, Berni Consulting and K Squared Media. New Southern Majority IE PAC, an affiliate of the Southern Poverty Law Center's action fund, has reported 13 expenditures against Hyde-Smith totaling $710,725.79. That includes the single largest line in the race, $550,000 in digital advertising placed through Chism Strategies6.

The national committees came in during the final two weeks. Senate Majority PAC, the super PAC aligned with Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, announced a $1.2 million campaign on Oct. 20. It ended up spending nearly $2.1 million on the airwaves, according to the ad-tracking firm AdImpact7. The Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, a party committee, spent $500,000 on ads for Colom8. A newer super PAC, New Deal for All, also spent in the race. It was started by Steve Pajcic, a Florida lawyer, and The New York Times reported that Pajcic, 80, and his children are spending at least $7 million through New Deal for All on Senate races in Florida, Kansas, Mississippi and South Carolina.9

On the Republican side, the Senate Leadership Fund (SLF) made an initial reservation of about $160,000 in Mississippi on Thursday, according to AdImpact data10 (Oct. 22). Semafor later reported a buy in the "low seven figures"11. SLF's biggest donor this cycle has been Elon Musk, who gave it $158 million12. It has also received money from One Nation, a so-called "dark money" group that, as a registered 501(c)(4) organization, does not have to disclose its donors13. Senate Majority PAC has a similar affiliate. Its largest contributors include Majority Forward, the George Soros-funded Democracy PAC and retired media executive Fred Eychaner14.

Google's Transparency Report shows spending in Mississippi since 2018 by many of the same groups: AB PAC $309,000; WinSenate, a Schumer15-aligned committee, $302,000; SLF PAC $190,000; and the National Republican Senatorial Committee $171,000. The biggest spender in Google's "political" category in the state was Kalshi, a prediction market, at $964,000. Its ads were not about the campaign.

The ads

American Bridge said it would advertise through a mix of digital advertising, streaming television, streaming audio, social media, direct mail and AM/FM radio16. After the election, the group said its ads featur[ed] working-class voices from Mississippi17 complaining about prices and health care. It also said it attacked Hyde-Smith over donations from fertilizer companies.

Senate Majority PAC's television spot said: "A decade in power, Hyde-Smith hasn't passed one piece of legislation, but she did vote to kick people off Medicaid,"18 and ran statewide alongside digital ads.

New Deal for All's ad featured Jena-Lisa Jones, a survivor of Jeffrey Epstein's abuse. It accused Hyde-Smith of "voting to protect the sex trafficker who abused me."19 The ad referred to a 2025 procedural vote that indefinitely postponed an amendment that would have required the full release of the Epstein files20. By AdImpact's count, reported by Axios, it was the most-aired ad of the closing stretch. It aired more than 1,200 times over the last week, costing the left-leaning New Deal for All super PAC over $329K20. The Mississippi senator dismissed the ad as "partisan propaganda," pointing to her support for measures against human trafficking.21

Hyde-Smith's campaign ran its own attack ad, which called Colom a "transgender defender"20. It played over 2,800 times across Mississippi and Memphis, Tenn., television markets in the 30 days since its Sept. 10 debut, costing the senator's campaign over $854K, per AdImpact.20 SLF's ad tied Colom to Schumer, Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, D-N.Y., and George Soros22 and closed with the line "Scott Colom, too liberal, too radical."22 Colom has accused Republicans of distorting his record.22 Barack Obama also appeared in an ad that the Colom campaign launched on digital platforms.7

Did it work?

The side that spent more won, though only just. Before the national committees arrived, the race had seen only about $8 million in total spending, with Colom outspending Hyde-Smith nearly $4.6 million to $3.2 million, according to AdImpact.18 In the final week, only one of the eight most-aired spots20 portrayed Hyde-Smith positively, according to AdImpact data reported by Axios.

The money was not the only factor. Mississippi voted during a national wave: Colom's win expands Democrats' total number of Senate seats to 552. CNN reported that Hyde-Smith's anemic fundraising and historic midterm headwinds10 added to the pressure on Republicans. An independent candidate, Ty Pinkins, had 2.1% of the vote.23 American Bridge has claimed credit for the result. No outside analysis has yet measured how much difference the ads made in a race decided by less than one vote in 100.
