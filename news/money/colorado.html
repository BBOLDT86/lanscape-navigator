The money · race by race · Colorado

Colorado's biggest money fight came in June, and the bigger spender lost

A super PAC funded by Michael Bloomberg spent about $11 million to make Michael Bennet governor, and Phil Weiser beat him. In the Senate race, the FEC shows only $800,000 in outside money, all of it spent on the Democratic side.

In Colorado, most of the money that could have changed an outcome was spent before summer. Federal Election Commission records show $800,000 in independent expenditures in the U.S. Senate race, about 30 cents per vote cast. All of it went to the Democratic side, and none of it went to Republican Mark Baisley, either for him or against him. The larger sum was spent in the Democratic primary for governor, which was a state race.

Where the money came from

Sen. John Hickenlooper's campaign raised $11.7 million, according to the FEC. Baisley raised about $138,000 through mid-October1, The Denver Gazette reported. The outside money came in June, when state Sen. Julie Gonzales challenged Hickenlooper from the left.

Two super PACs that spent $495,000 for Hickenlooper did not have to name their donors until after the primary. Common Sense Action Fund spent $300,000. Colorado Newsline reported that Kent Thiry, the former CEO of Denver-based DaVita and a longtime backer of centrist causes and candidates in Colorado, was the largest donor2. He gave $250,000. Other donors included hedge fund billionaire Paul Tudor Jones, who contributed $50,000, and Denver private equity investor Jim Kelley, who gave $25,0002. ModSquad Action, a 501(c)(4) "dark money" nonprofit that does not disclose its donors, contributed $100,0003. A 501(c)(4) can spend on politics without disclosing its donors, which is why the money is called "dark." The second super PAC, Our Future Colorado, spent $195,000. Its only money was a single $200,000 contribution from ModSquad Action2, and it filed a termination report ending the group's 36-day existence2.

On Gonzales' side, the FEC lists the Working Families Party PAC at $225,000, Indivisible Action at $46,000 and Young People For A New Era at $33,000. The Working Families group's donors include unions, nonprofits and progressive donors4.

In the governor's primary, Rocky Mountain Way was a state super PAC backing Sen. Michael Bennet. According to state filings reported by The Aspen Times, it raised $11,409,223, more than $5 million from one donor: former New York City Mayor Mike Bloomberg5, and spent $10,912,819. It received $1.25 million from Brighter Future for Colorado, a 501(c)(4)6, and money from Stand For Children, the Campaign for Great Public Schools, the Colorado League of Charter Schools Action and EDF Action Votes6. Fighting for Colorado, the super PAC backing Attorney General Phil Weiser, raised $1,464,191 and spent $1,372,2385. Its top donors were Jay Monroe, Jason Mendelson and Jim Kelley, all venture capitalists who have contributed a total of $100,000 each7.

On the Republican side, the Freedom Independent Expenditure Committee supported Victor Marx. One of its largest donors is Sema Construction, Inc., a construction firm based in Centennial, which has donated $435,0008.

The ads

Common Sense Action Fund ran a $300,000 digital advertising campaign highlighting Hickenlooper's efforts to "fight back" against President Donald Trump and his mass-deportation agenda2. Our Future Colorado spent its money on digital and text advertising2. Gonzales' campaign didn't air ads on television at all; the super PAC support she got went mostly to digital and text advertising and mailers.9

Rocky Mountain Way bought TV and digital ads, as well as mailers10 attacking Weiser. AdImpact logged a Rocky Mountain Way ad called "Colorado's Watchdog" that began airing June 13. It said Weiser failed to join dozens of lawsuits to stop Trump's giveaways to corporate polluters11 and accused him of raising money from companies his office was suing. Earlier, the group ran a TV ad that paints Bennet as a hard-charging opponent of the Trump administration12.

Freedom IEC spent $400,000 in May and June on advertising, including television and YouTube.13 In the general election, Weiser's first ad, "Plan," ran statewide on streaming platforms, in Grand Junction and Colorado Springs on cable and broadcast14. Marx never ran a TV ad. In the last days of the race he sent fundraising emails asking for $100,000 for his first TV ads, but Federal Communications Commission records show Marx's campaign never bought any TV ad time15, The Colorado Sun reported.

National committees also bought ads on Google that Colorado viewers saw. Google's transparency report credits the National Republican Senatorial Committee with $1.1 million in Colorado and LCV Victory Fund with $960,000. Those totals go back to 2018, though, and each group's largest ad this cycle targeted another state, such as Ohio.

Did it work?

In the Senate primary, the side with more outside money won, but by less than many expected. Hickenlooper prevailed over Gonzales by just 5.6 percentage points16, which was a much narrower margin than many observers expected16. In November, little outside money was spent on either major candidate17, according to Colorado Public Radio. Hickenlooper beat Baisley 1,609,163 to 932,741, a margin of 25.4 points. That is 14.4 points better than the Democratic margin in Colorado's 2024 presidential vote.

In the governor's primary, the bigger spender lost. The Associated Press called the race for Weiser when he was leading Bennet by 10 percentage points.18 Weiser's own campaign had raised more than Bennet's: Weiser raised $6,794,306, while Bennet raised $5,018,224, according to filings with the Colorado Secretary of State's Office19. Once Rocky Mountain Way is counted, though, far more was spent for Bennet than for Weiser.

In the general election, Weiser beat Marx 1,618,777 to 881,406, a margin of 27.8 points. Marx's campaign was hit with a $57,647.16 fine for 210 violations of state campaign finance law20. CPR found that more than 36% of Marx's campaign spending — about $1.4 million — is classified as fundraising expenses21.
