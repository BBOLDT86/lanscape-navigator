Georgia: Ossoff's cash advantage won; Rick Jackson's $220 million did not

Sen. Jon Ossoff and his allies outspent Rep. Mike Collins by wide margins and won by 18 points. In the governor's race, the money ran the other way: Rick Jackson spent more than $200 million of his own and lost.

Georgia had two of the most expensive races of 2026, and their results point in opposite directions. Sen. Jon Ossoff, the Democratic incumbent, reported $136.6 million in receipts to the Federal Election Commission. His Republican opponent, Rep. Mike Collins, reported $10.7 million. Ossoff won 2,466,422 votes to 1,712,444, an 18-point margin in a state Donald Trump carried by 2.2 points in 2024. In the governor's race the bigger spender lost. Rick Jackson, the Republican, lost to Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms by 8.7 points.

Where the money came from

The Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported that Ossoff has raised more money this campaign cycle than any other federal candidate nationally1. Outside groups added more on his side. The biggest in the FEC figures was The Georgia Way, which spent $6.1 million against Collins. It is one of two super PACs, along with Georgia Honor, that Senate Majority PAC formed in November 20202. Lower Costs Now spent $1.5 million against Collins and $370,000 for Ossoff. It is a super PAC affiliated with FF PAC, formerly known as Future Forward USA PAC3. FactCheck.org reported that nearly all of its money came from SMP, which contributed $30 million, and FF PAC, which gave $20 million3. Labor and Black voter groups spent smaller amounts for Ossoff: Worker Power PAC ($785,000), Workers Vote ($615,000), Black Voters Matter Action PAC ($560,000) and BlackPAC ($395,000).

On the Republican side, most of the outside money in the FEC figures went into the primary. Hardworking Americans Inc. spent $4.2 million supporting Derek Dooley, the former football coach. WCTV described it as a Super PAC aligned with Kemp4, meaning Gov. Brian Kemp. The station reported that FEC records show Daniel Dooley, Derek Dooley's brother and the founder of the school-safety company Centegix, gave it $100,000 on Nov. 17, 2025. Collins beat Dooley in the June 16 runoff.

Two cautions about the federal numbers. First, the FEC data used for this piece puts $19.3 million on the Democratic side and $1.6 million on the Republican side. That count lists Dooley as an independent and places Hardworking Americans' $4.2 million on the Democratic side. If that money is counted as Republican, the split is about $15.1 million to $5.8 million. Second, the data does not include the Senate Leadership Fund, the Republican super PAC, which committed to spending $44 million in Georgia in hopes of defeating Ossoff1. In late September the Georgia Recorder reported, citing the Collins campaign, that Senate Leadership Fund and the associated One Nation nonprofit have spent $25.9 million in the Georgia race5. One Nation is a 501(c)(4) nonprofit. It is not required to disclose its donors, which makes it a so-called "dark money" group6.

The governor's race was funded mostly by one man. The AJC, reviewing state filings, reported that Jackson has poured more than $200 million of his fortune into Georgia's race for governor7. His fortune comes from Jackson Healthcare, a behemoth company that provides contract nurses and other workers to hospitals and health systems8. Bottoms had raised a little over $5.7 million by late September, according to Capital B Atlanta. Then, in the final days, she received a $5 million boost from the Democratic Governors Association and another $2.5 million from former New York City mayor9 Mike Bloomberg.

The ads

Republican TV ads against Ossoff started early. One Nation's main ad shows a woman shocked by her grocery bill and blames Ossoff for a tax increase. FactCheck.org reported that the group spent more than $9 million on the anti-Ossoff ad, which has been airing on broadcast and cable TV, as well as streaming platforms, since July 3010, citing AdImpact. FactCheck.org said the ad cherry-picks parts of last year's budget reconciliation law10. The Senate Leadership Fund's super PAC ran an AI-generated ad showing Ossoff in a salon chair. WRDW reported that South Carolina and Georgia, Ossoff's home state, have no laws on the books addressing AI-generated political ads11.

Ossoff's first attack ad, "The Vote," features a retired police sergeant who blasts Collins for siding with House Republicans last year to block a Democratic maneuver aimed at forcing the release of the Epstein files12. It ran on broadcast TV, streaming and digital platforms. Another Ossoff ad played a Collins audio clip about Social Security. FactCheck.org said it took the quote out of context and reported that the Ossoff campaign has spent more than $2.8 million on the ad13. Online, Google's transparency report shows Jon Ossoff for Senate as the top political advertiser in Georgia, with $22.4 million. Its single biggest video ran from Sept. 10 to Oct. 12 and cost $1.5 million to $2 million.

Jackson's two campaign accounts have spent nearly $165 million on TV and cable ads, at least $8 million on direct mail7, and another $14.6 million on Google. His ads depicted her record as mayor as catastrophic for Atlanta9. NBC News reported that late in the race Bottoms ran ads that hit Jackson over previous comments supporting the construction of data centers and linked him to President Donald Trump14.

Did it work?

In the Senate race, the side with more money won. Ossoff's campaign outraised Collins's by about 13 to 1, and Democratic-aligned groups outspent Republican ones on every count above. Neither side spent heavily at the end. The Washington Examiner reported that the Senate Leadership Fund paused ad spending on his race to focus elsewhere15, and that Senate Majority PAC also paused spending in the state, signaling its confidence in Ossoff15.

In the governor's race, the side with more money lost. In September an AdImpact analysis found that pro-Jackson groups have spent or reserved nearly $76 million in airtime ahead of the general election, compared with about $8 million16 for Bottoms and her allies. Bottoms overcame an enormous spending disadvantage14, NBC News reported.

The vote counts add one more fact. Jackson won 197,122 more votes than Collins, and the governor's race finished 9.3 points closer than the Senate race. Both Democrats still improved on the 2024 presidential margin by double digits. Readers can decide how much of that the money explains.
