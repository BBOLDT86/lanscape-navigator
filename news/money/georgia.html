In Georgia, Ossoff's War Chest Won; Rick Jackson's Fortune Did Not

Sen. Jon Ossoff raised nearly 13 times as much as Rep. Mike Collins and won by 18 points. Billionaire Rick Jackson outspent Keisha Lance Bottoms with his own money and lost the governor's race by nearly nine.

Money was lopsided in both of Georgia's top races this year, but it ran in opposite directions. In the Senate race, Democratic Sen. Jon Ossoff's campaign reported $136.6 million in receipts to the Federal Election Commission. His Republican opponent, Rep. Mike Collins, reported $10.7 million. In the governor's race, the Republican nominee, Rick Jackson, outspent Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms several times over. Jackson is a billionaire and the founder and CEO of Jackson Healthcare, a healthcare staffing and services company headquartered in Alpharetta, Georgia1. The candidate with more money won one race and lost the other.

Where the money came from

Outside groups reported $36.1 million in independent expenditures in the Senate race, or $8.63 per vote cast. The FEC tally counts $19.8 million as helping Democrats and $16.3 million as helping Republicans. But the Democratic figure includes $4.2 million that Hardworking Americans Inc., the PAC of Republican Gov. Brian Kemp, spent backing Derek Dooley in the Republican primary. It also includes $489,000 that Win It Back PAC spent against Rep. Buddy Carter, who ran only in that primary. If you count only the money aimed at the two nominees, groups that attacked Ossoff or backed Collins reported $16.3 million. Groups that backed Ossoff or attacked Collins reported $15.2 million.

The biggest single spender was Senate Leadership Fund (SLF PAC), which spent $9.9 million against Ossoff. It is the main super PAC backing Republican Senate candidates2. In April it announced an initial $44 million investment in the Georgia U.S. Senate race3. Its large donors include Miriam Adelson, a physician and businesswoman; Paul Elliott Singer, a hedge fund manager; and Elon Musk4. Musk alone gave $158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund2. SLF also received at least $46 million, so far, from an affiliated nonprofit, One Nation4. One Nation is a 501(c)(4) that does not have to disclose its donors, which is what "dark money" means. Google's transparency report shows One Nation, under two advertiser names, has bought $14.5 million in political ads in Georgia since 2018.

No Going Back PAC spent $4.1 million against Ossoff and $1.1 million for Collins. FactCheck.org wrote that according to the New York Times5, the group is funded by MAGA Inc., Trump's main super PAC.

On the Democratic side, The Georgia Way spent $6.1 million against Collins. A super PAC by that name was one of two Senate Majority PAC formed in November 20206 for that year's Senate runoffs. The 2026 committee registered on June 12, 2026. Its FEC filing reports total receipts · $8,500,095.007, almost all of it transfers from affiliated committees. Lower Costs Now spent $1.5 million against Collins. FEC records show SMP, which contributed $30 million, and FF PAC, which gave $20 million8, provided nearly all of its money. Smaller pro-Ossoff groups included Worker Power PAC ($785,000), Workers Vote ($615,000) and Black Voters Matter Action PAC ($560,000).

The governor's race is regulated by state law, not the FEC. GPB reviewed state filings and found that Jackson's two committees raised at least $120 million, about four times as much as Bottoms9. The vast majority of Jackson's campaign money came from his personal wealth.9 GPB also reported that the Democratic Governors Association has given Bottoms' leadership committee more than $14 million.9

What the ads said

Ossoff's television ads used a clip of Collins discussing Social Security. FactCheck.org said the ads take a sound bite from his opponent about Social Security out of context10. According to AdImpact, the campaign spent more than $2.8 million on the ad since it first aired on Aug. 810. The campaign also spent $23.0 million on Google's platforms this cycle. Its biggest Google ad was a video that cost $1.5 million to $2.0 million and ran from Sept. 10 to Oct. 12.

One SLF spot, "Ugly Record," uses AI-generated imagery to depict Democratic Sen. Jon Ossoff sitting in a salon getting his hair styled, his nails done11. Its script accused Ossoff of voting for higher taxes on Social Security recipients and of spending more on makeup than other federal candidates.

In the primary, Kemp's PAC reserved a million dollars in advertising to bolster Dooley in the final stretch12, according to AdImpact. One of those ads showed the governor himself asking voters to support Dooley. The PAC spent more than $3 million on pro-Dooley text messages and ads13, NBC News reported. Dooley lost a June 16 runoff to Collins. J Street Action Fund's ad focused on Republican Rep. Mike Collins' extensive ties to white nationalists through his staff and son-in-law14.

The Associated Press reported that Jackson filled television and digital ads with clips of street protests in Atlanta after the 2020 police killings15 of Rayshard Brooks and George Floyd. In the closing weeks, his line was "Bad mayors don't make good governors"15. On Google, his campaign spent $15.1 million this cycle.

Did it work

Ossoff won 2,468,986 votes to 1,713,656, an 18.1-point margin. Donald Trump carried Georgia by 2.2 points in 2024. By late September, Ossoff had already spent $57 million in advertising and had $13 million reserved16, according to AP. AdImpact counts ad spending rather than FEC filings, and its figures show outside groups aligned with Collins had spent more than $52 million16. That is far more than the FEC tally of independent expenditures. Even so, Collins's own campaign had no ad time reserved. Republicans then pulled back: AdImpact said SLF had recently canceled $7.6 million in ad reservations in Georgia17. In the end, Ossoff and his allies spent nearly twice as much on broadcast ads18 as Collins and his supporters, according to Campaigns & Elections. Outside groups' spending on the two nominees was close. The big gap was in the candidates' own money.

Bottoms won the governor's race 2,273,861 to 1,910,910, an 8.7-point margin. AP reported that Jackson had covered the airwaves with more than $200 million of his own money15. In one race the bigger spender won. In the other, the biggest self-funded campaign in Georgia's history lost.
