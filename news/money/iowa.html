Iowa's record money fight ends with Democrats winning Senate and governor

Republican super PACs and national party groups spent heavily to keep an open Senate seat and the governor's office. Democrats Josh Turek and Rob Sand raised more than their opponents and won both races, by 4.1 and 11 points.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 11, 2026. 18 sources. All the states › · How Iowa voted ›

On Nov. 3, Iowa filled two open statewide offices, and the money in both races was enormous. Josh Turek, a Democratic state representative from Council Bluffs, beat U.S. Rep. Ashley Hinson, a Republican, 686,641 votes to 630,808. That is a 4.1-point margin in a state President Trump won by 13.3 points in 2024. Turek became the first Democrat since Barack Obama to win a federal race in the state.1 He takes the seat of Republican Sen. Joni Ernst, who is retiring. In the governor's race, State Auditor Rob Sand, a Democrat, beat Republican businessman Zach Lahn 757,506 to 607,591, an 11-point margin.

The Watch's own reading of Federal Election Commission data had not come in when this was written. The federal dollar figures below come from published counts of FEC filings, and those counts do not always agree. The governor's race is a state contest, so its figures come from reports filed with the Iowa Ethics and Campaign Disclosure Board.

Where the money came from

The two Senate campaigns raised very different amounts. Through Sept. 30, Turek led in total fundraising for the cycle at $20.15 million2, while Hinson had $13.52 million2, according to the Iowa Capital Dispatch. Hinson ended the reporting period with more cash on hand3, The Gazette reported.

Most of the money came from outside groups. The Des Moines Register counted more than $137 million3 in outside spending by mid-October. Of that, spending against Turek had reached $53.8 million and spending against Hinson $48.3 million3. OpenSecrets came out the other way: it found $59.86 million in outside spending supporting Turek and $51.67 million supporting Hinson2. The Register named the SLF PAC, aligned with the Senate Republicans' Senate Leadership Fund, at $35.53 million and WinSenate, a Democratic super PAC, at $28.27 million2 as the two biggest spenders. The Senate Leadership Fund said it would spend $53 million in all, and that One Nation, the group's sister organization, is spending $11 million on ads praising Hinson's record4.

The donors behind those groups are partly visible. The Senate Leadership Fund's large donors include Miriam Adelson, a physician and businesswoman; Paul Elliott Singer, a hedge fund manager; and Elon Musk5. The super PAC also took at least $46 million from One Nation, a registered 501(c)(4) that does not have to disclose its donors5. That undisclosed money is what "dark money" means in the legal sense. Musk gave the fund $158 million, but The Washington Post reported that the fund immediately transferred an identical sum to a PAC backing Ken Paxton in Texas6. WinSenate is financially tied to the Senate Majority PAC7, whose largest contributors include Majority Forward, the George Soros-funded Democracy PAC and retired media executive Fred Eychaner8. Majority Forward is also a 501(c)(4) and does not disclose its donors.

The parties spent directly, too. After a June Supreme Court ruling, the RNC spent $5 million on a coordinated ad buy with Hinson9.

In the governor's race, state filings show the two candidates raised a combined total of $81 million, breaking the previous $36 million record2. Sand had raised nearly $54.15 million to Lahn's $26.94 million2 as of mid-October. Lahn raised more in the final period, though: RGA Right Direction PAC, affiliated with the Republican Governors Association, provided Lahn $11.8 million — about 70% of his cash donations10 for that stretch. The Republican Party of Iowa supplied another $1.72 million.10 MAHA Action PAC, a group aligned with Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s Make America Healthy Again movement, gave $1 million and later announced $1.5 million more. The Democratic Governors Association gave Sand $5.5 million.10 According to Little Village, Christine Sand and members of her family have donated a total of $12.75 million11 to his campaign since May 2025.

The ads

AdImpact estimated that the candidates and outside groups spent close to $200 million12 on the Senate race. PolitiFact put it at $212 million in ad spending13.

Turek and Democrats ran several ads accusing Hinson of profiting off her office13. WinSenate's $3.7 million closing ad said Hinson had "gotten $8 million richer."14 Hinson says the money came from the sale of her husband's insurance company12. Turek's own ads showed him dragging his wheelchair up steps to talk to voters12.

Ads against Turek focused on his attendance record in the Iowa House, immigration and transgender issues.13 PolitiFact found it True that Turek missed 58% of the votes in the Iowa House this year13. It rated a Senate Leadership Fund ad saying he supports amnesty Half True.

In the governor's race, Sand opened the general election by going after Lahn's family home in Kansas. His ad said "Zach Lahn isn't even a full-time Iowan."15 An early Lahn ad drew criticism from KCCI, a Des Moines TV station whose clip it used: the station said the ad omits "some context included in KCCI's original reporting"16. In late October, Lahn ran an ad using an AI-generated likeness17 of Sand. Lahn says he stands by the ad.17

Did it work?

In both races, the candidate whose own campaign had more money won. In the Senate race's third quarter, Turek's campaign spent roughly $12.8 million3, while Hinson spent nearly $5 million.3 Outside spending was close to even, and the two main counts disagree about which side had more. The Republican side leaned more on super PACs and the national party. Turek won by 4.1 points.

The governor's race was more lopsided. Between mid-July and mid-October, the candidates spent more than $46 million18, and Sand accounted for about $29 million of that spending.18 He won by 11 points.

The money arrived in a strong Democratic year. Democrats won the Senate, ending Republican dominance in Washington1. Compared with the 2024 presidential vote, Iowa moved 17.4 points toward Democrats in the Senate race and 24.2 points in the governor's race. No filing shows how much of that shift the ads caused. On election night, Lahn told supporters he believed their campaign had already shifted the conversation within the Republican Party.18
