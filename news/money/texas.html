Texas: Musk's $224 million and Abbott's $281 million lost to Democrats

Republicans and one billionaire outspent Democrats about two to one in Texas's Senate race and eight to one for governor, according to filings and ad trackers. James Talarico and Gina Hinojosa won anyway, by 12 and 7 points.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 10, 2026. 24 sources. All the states › · How Texas voted ›

Texas voters elected Democrats to the U.S. Senate and the governor's office on Nov. 3. State Rep. James Talarico of Austin beat Attorney General Ken Paxton 5,446,309 to 4,250,166, a 12.1-point margin. State Rep. Gina Hinojosa beat Gov. Greg Abbott 5,231,979 to 4,525,393, a 7.1-point margin. Both results come from AP via The New York Times. Donald Trump carried the state by 13.7 points in 2024. The Republican side had more money in both races. The Watch's Federal Election Commission data for Texas has not been read yet and shows no outside spending, so the federal figures below come from the filings as reported by the press and ad trackers. The governor's race is a state race, and its money is reported to the Texas Ethics Commission.

Where the money came from

Talarico's campaign raised far more than Paxton's: a cumulative $155 million compared to $40.61 million, according to The Texas Tribune. Outside groups closed the gap. According to Senate Leadership Fund's quarterly FEC report, Musk gave the main Senate Republican super PAC $75 million on Sept. 9 and another $83 million2 later that month. The day after each donation, the fund transferred the exact same amount to Texas PAC, a super PAC supporting Paxton. The filings don't prove Musk's specific dollars funded those transfers2. Musk's trust also gave $10 million to Lone Star Liberty PAC, the main outside group backing Paxton.3 In all, Musk put about $224 million in Texas, according to campaign finance records.2

The Tribune's tally shows Texas PAC spent $69.7 million in support of Paxton and more than $110 million opposing Talarico.1 MAGA Inc., Trump's super PAC, spent $7.5 million supporting Paxton and $30.1 million opposing Talarico.1 On the other side, about $23 million was spent to support his candidacy, nearly $6.6 million of which came from Lone Star Rising PAC.1 The biggest donor to that super PAC was LinkedIn cofounder Reid Hoffman4, who gave $11.5 million, according to The Dallas Morning News.

The governor's race was even more lopsided. From Hinojosa's campaign launch in October 2025 through Oct. 24, he spent $281 million, nearly eight times her $36.8 million, according to campaign finance reports.5 Abbott's money ran through Texans for Greg Abbott, a specific-purpose committee6. In its final full report, Musk's donation was the largest Abbott reported, followed by $3 million from the Republican Governors Association on Oct. 20.7 Other large checks included another $2 million from Kenneth Fisher, who heads the financial investment firm Fisher Investments7, and a $2.5 million donation from Nike co-founder Phil Knight.7 Hinojosa reported nearly 437,000 donations at an average of $50 each.8 Her top donors included the Democratic Governors Association and Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker, who gave $1 million each.7

The ads

AdImpact counts the Senate race, primaries included, as the most expensive non-presidential race on record — with ad spending reaching $544.5 million.9 In the general election alone, AdImpact said Republicans spent $274.1 million on political ads for Paxton, while Democrats spent $133.4 million on political ads for Talarico.9 Talarico had the airwaves to himself for much of the summer. One of his ads was a $4.4 million ad buy, according to AdImpact. The 30-second ad features the Democrat speaking about affordability while leaving a Red & White food store.10

That changed in September. KVUE reported that most of the Republican money paid for TV and digital ads attacking Talarico, highlighting his past statements about gender and God.11 A Houston Chronicle analysis of broadcast, cable and streaming ads found that another $17.3 million has funded the "For They/Them" ad12. The same analysis found pro-Talarico ads highlighting Paxton's alleged corruption in almost every spot12. In the final weeks, Talarico also made Musk's spending part of his message.

In the governor's race, Abbott's campaign put $12 million into a statewide TV ad saying Hinojosa voted 40 times to raise property taxes. The $12 million ad buy has been playing since September 22.13 KPRC's fact check rated the claim True, but misleading.14 It found that the Abbott campaign's list accounts for 10 bills between 2017 and 2025.14 Hinojosa's lawyers sent cease-and-desist letters to stations. Abbott's spokeswoman called that a "desperate use of lawfare"15.

Hinojosa had far less to spend. She unveiled her first TV ad buy, a $340,000 campaign16, on cable. After that, Abbott responded with $7 million of ads promoting his campaign during hundreds of commercial breaks in all the biggest markets.17 Hinojosa then ran a cable ad, "Abbott Intelligence," which her campaign says aired in rural markets and accused Abbott of accepting over $20 million in campaign contributions from donors with major stakes in data centers.18

Did it work?

In both races, the side that spent more lost by a wide margin. Reporting does not give money as the reason, and the outcomes had other likely causes. Hinojosa beat Abbott on her own message. In the Senate race, Talarico's own campaign had the larger treasury, and only the outside money tilted the ad totals toward Paxton.

The Houston Chronicle's county maps show that Republicans at the top of the ticket did not net the strong margins they had become accustomed to in West Texas and the Panhandle19. They also show that in Tarrant County, home to Fort Worth and one of the last red, urban areas in the entire country, Talarico won by nearly 20 points.19 In both races, the Democrats focused intensely on pocketbook issues and government corruption, even as their Republican opponents tried to center transgender rights and other culture war issues.20

Talarico treated the result as a verdict on the money. He told supporters "We just proved that all the money in the world is no match for the people of Texas"21.
