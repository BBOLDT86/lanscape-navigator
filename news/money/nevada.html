The money · race by race · Nevada

Nevada's GOP ad edge of $32.6 million didn't save Lombardo

Gov. Joe Lombardo's campaign and allied PACs, funded heavily by the Republican Governors Association and Budget Suites owner Robert Bigelow, spent far more on ads than the other side. Attorney General Aaron Ford still won by 7.7 points.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 10, 2026. 24 sources. All the states › · How Nevada voted ›

The Watch's federal data shows no outside spending in Nevada's congressional races. The money this year went into the governor's race, which is a state contest. Its spending is reported to the state, not the Federal Election Commission, and tracked by ad-monitoring firms. It was the most expensive race in Nevada this cycle by ad spending, with more than $77.2 million spent as of Monday1, the day before the election, according to AdImpact figures reported by The Nevada Independent. Republicans' spending outpaced Democrats' by more than $32.6 million. For every dollar Democrats spent, the GOP spent about $2.47.1 Ford, a Democrat, won 561,367 votes to 477,556. That margin is 7.7 points, in a state President Donald Trump carried by 3.1 points in 2024.

Where the money came from

Lombardo's campaign raised more than $1.5 million from July 1 to September 30, bringing its total to just over $6 million this year2, KTVN 2News reported from state filings. Most of the money on his side went through the Better Nevada PAC, a state political committee aligned with the governor. Transparency USA, which compiles the state filings, lists $30,736,395 in total contributions3 to the PAC. The biggest givers were the federal arm of the Republican Governors Association ($14.5 million), Lombardo's other affiliated committee, the Nevada Way PAC ($7.3 million), the RGA's Nevada PAC ($3 million), Robert Bigelow ($2.5 million), venture capitalist Ben Horowitz and his wife, Felicia ($1 million), and Jessica Roy ($1 million). Roy is the wife of Switch founder Rob Roy4.

Bigelow owns the Budget Suites of America hotel chain and spent more than $50 million supporting Lombardo and other Republican candidates in 20225. This year he also donated $6 million to the Republican Governors Association's (RGA) Nevada PAC6. A Nevada Independent analysis found he has given more than $20 million to Lombardo and affiliated groups since the governor first declared his candidacy in summer 2021.6 Democrats have argued that his support convinced Lombardo to veto an eviction protection law that would have hurt Bigelow's business, a claim Lombardo refutes.7 Better Nevada also received $750,000 from The Revitalization Project, a Virginia-based dark money nonprofit8. "Dark money" here is the term for a nonprofit that does not have to name its donors.

Ford relied more on small donors. In the first quarter he received 13,000 donations, 90 percent of them $80 or less, totaling $1.23 million9. By the third quarter he reported more direct campaign contributions than Lombardo for the first time this election cycle, though the incumbent outspent Ford.8 For most of the Democratic air cover, Ford's side depended on the Democratic Governors Association contributing $7 million8 to A More Affordable Nevada PAC. Illinois Gov. J.B. Pritzker (D) donated $400,0006 to the same committee. As of mid-October, AdImpact counted more than $14.1 million spent or reserved by A More Affordable Nevada, compared with more than $29.3 million by Better Nevada PAC8.

The ads

The Republican side started early. In February, Better Nevada launched a seven-figure ad campaign running on statewide streaming services and the Reno broadcast market10. The ad called Ford "high-flying" for traveling to locations such as France, Singapore, Taiwan and the Yucatán.10 Ford's office told the Las Vegas Review-Journal that of the 137 days Ford was not in Nevada in 2024, 98 were for work and 33 were for events associated with prominent Black leaders.10 Ford called the travel ads racist dog whistles that attacked his work ethic11. The PAC also put up billboards across the Las Vegas Valley showing Ford alongside California Gov.12 Gavin Newsom.

In the fall, Better Nevada's "Clemency" ad went after Ford for sponsoring a pardons board application from Norman Smith. 2News checked the ad and confirmed that in 2015, Smith shot and killed Tinesha Adams, who was pregnant, in a parking lot in North Las Vegas13. MultiState's race summary says a 2News fact check called the ad's early-release claim misleading14. The attorney general's office said that sponsoring an application is standard and doesn't mean a member will vote in favor15 of it.

Lombardo's own campaign ran mostly positive ads. In one week this summer it put out four new ads in the Reno and Las Vegas markets on broadcast and CTV, at a cost of more than $224,00016, in which a parent and a small-business owner thanked him. Lombardo's campaign and affiliated PACs spent more than a million dollars on Spanish-language advertising17. In an AdImpact analysis, The Nevada Independent found that from Aug. 1 on, Democrats spent more than $7 million on governor race ads mentioning Trump, of more than $13 million in total. Republicans spent $0 on governor race ads mentioning Trump.18

The Democratic ads were about prices. Ford's first spot, "Enough," featured "I taught middle school math, so I know the Lombardo-Trump economy doesn't add up"19. A More Affordable Nevada's buy included $1.4 million in Spanish-language ads20. Its spots kept replaying Lombardo's remark that Nevadans should "maybe feel a little pain" from tariffs. Late in the race, a mailer went out promoting nonpartisan candidate Danielle Ford. It was paid for by what The Nevada Independent called a "shadow PAC," because its registrants set it up after Sept. 30, missing Nevada's campaign finance reporting deadline and avoiding donor reporting before Election Day.21 Lombardo's campaign said it had no knowledge of the PAC or mailer.21

Did it work?

The side that spent more lost. The Nevada Independent's reading is that Lombardo significantly outperformed other statewide Republicans in raw vote count, but it wasn't enough to withstand a nationwide wave. Ad spending likely helped narrow the gap11. The Nevada Current argued that the Republican governor's ties with Trump effectively saddled him with Trump's economy.22

In 2022 the money ran the other way. Democrats had a spending advantage of about $3.3 million1, and Lombardo won anyway. This year Democrats won every statewide office. Democrats have not held all constitutional offices since 196223. Rebecca Gill, a political scientist at UNLV, said before the vote that the two sides had "kind of bloodied each other up a little bit"24.
