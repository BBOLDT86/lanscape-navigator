California's record-cost governor's race: labor, Realtors and a tribe backed Becerra

Most of the record $350 million in ads ran before the June primary. Xavier Becerra and the committees backing him outspent Steve Hilton, who relied on small donors and AI-made ads. Becerra won by 30 points.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 7, 2026. 20 sources. All the states › · How California voted ›

AdImpact, which tracks political advertising, counted California's governor's race as the most expensive in U.S. history: roughly $350.3 million1. Most of that money was spent before the fall. Tom Steyer, a liberal billionaire, spent over $215 million of his own money trying to make it into the general election.2 He did not make it. By AdImpact's count, the June primary alone drew $315.8M in ad spending and reservations3.

The general election matched Xavier Becerra, a Democrat, against Steve Hilton, a Republican. According to the Associated Press count published by The New York Times, Becerra won 6,378,410 votes to 3,404,640. The Watch has not finished reading Federal Election Commission data for California's congressional races, and so far that data shows no outside spending in them. This report therefore follows the governor's race. Money in that race is reported to the state, not to the FEC.

Where the money came from

Fundraising totals vary with the reporting period. In mid-October the Los Angeles Times reported that Becerra has raised $40.5 million, as of Friday, while Hilton has raised $29 million4. After the election, CalMatters put Hilton's total at more than $33 million to Becerra's $38 million5.

The bigger gap was in outside money. California's Fair Political Practices Commission lists the top contributors to independent expenditure committees, the state's version of super PACs. These committees can take unlimited sums but may not coordinate with a candidate. For the general election, the commission's list shows Governor - Xavier Becerra6 with Support: $14,050,0006 and Hilton with $2.5 million against him. Most of the pro-Becerra money went to Working Families for Healthy Communities Supporting Becerra for Governor 2026. Its largest donors were California Real Estate Independent Expenditure Committee - California Association of Realtors6 at $2.75 million, the Pechanga Band of Indians6 at $2 million, the California Medical Association's committee at $1.4 million, a committee sponsored by Airbnb, and the California State Assocation of Electrical Workers6 at $1 million. Transparency USA's compilation of the filings shows the Davita Patient Protection Committee7 gave it $500,000. KMPH, the Fresno television station, read the expenditure reports and found that a little over $19 million has been spent in support of Becerra, with the most coming from the PAC group "Working Families for Healthy Communities" at $15.34 million.8

A second committee, Greater Golden State, worked against Hilton. According to CNN, it was an independent expenditure campaign that the Democratic Governors Association helped set up and is backing.9 State records show its donors were Bill Bloomfield of Utah ($1 million), the governors association's Democratic Action ($750,000), the California Nurses Association's PAC ($500,000) and an SEIU state council ($250,000).

No committee supporting Hilton showed up on the state's list. KMPH found that Hilton has none that raised enough to reach the necessary mark10. His money came to his campaign directly. By May he had more than 20,00011 donors, and nearly a quarter of them live outside California.11 KRON4 reported that his larger donors included Rupert Murdoch, Sergey Brin, Buccaneers co-chairman Ed Glazer and golfer Phil Mickelson. Big checks favored Becerra. In the first half of August, no fewer than 17 donors gave Becerra $39,200, the maximum allowed by state law12, compared with six for Hilton.

The ads

KMPH found that Becerra's campaign had spent a total of $26,052,6508, of which nearly $21 million is going to media buys and digital ads.8 Hilton spent 41%8 of his campaign's money on media, or $9.75 million. The AP reported that Becerra kept a low public profile and sought to take advantage of Trump's deep unpopularity in the state by tying Hilton to Trump13. He also ran ads and social media posts in Spanish.13 The Los Angeles Times reported that he began airing television ads as mail ballots reach California voters14.

During the primary, AdImpact credited Working Families with $13.4 million in ads for Becerra. The governors association says it put seven figures15 into Greater Golden State to define Steve Hilton as a MAGA extremist15.

Hilton's ads got more attention than his budget would suggest. In late October he launched a $1 million AI-generated ad accusing Becerra of failing to protect unaccompanied migrant children while Becerra ran the federal health department. The ad was set to run across digital, text, and streaming platforms during the final two weeks16. Becerra's campaign called it "AI slop." Another AI ad attacked Becerra over transgender athletes. It ran in English and Spanish and, according to Metro Weekly, was aimed at socially conservative Latino voters. Hilton's "Just try it" series used real actors to court liberal women. Filmmaker Gabriel Mann made the ads for $125,00017, and two of Hilton's conservative allies who have campaigned with him rebuked the ad.18

Did it work?

The side that spent more won, and by a wide margin. Becerra's 30.4-point win was 10.3 points better than the Democrats' 20.1-point margin in the 2024 presidential race. AP and other outlets called the race, and the count is still going: at NBC's last update, about 71% expected votes in19.

The money may not have decided much. CalMatters noted that for months polls showed Becerra with a double-digit lead20. In October the San Francisco Chronicle concluded that neither approach by Steve Hilton and Xavier Becerra appears to have moved the needle much18. A late-September UC Berkeley poll showed Becerra up 25 points. Hilton's own poll showed a gap of under six, and the result was nowhere near that. Becerra's larger margin also came in a year when nationally Democrats take back congress1. The filings show the money; they cannot say how much of the swing it caused.
