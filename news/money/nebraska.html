The money · race by race · Nebraska

Nebraska's Senate fight drew about $55 million in outside money, and Ricketts held on

A crypto super PAC, a group tied to Senate Republicans and a Democratic-funded PAC behind ads about pardons spent tens of millions on Pete Ricketts and Dan Osborn. In the governor's race, the Republican Governors Association backed Jim Pillen.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 7, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 14, 2026. 19 sources. All the states › · How Nebraska voted ›

Republicans held both of Nebraska's top races on Nov. 3. Sen. Pete Ricketts beat independent Dan Osborn 378,281 to 331,268, a 6.2-point margin. Gov. Jim Pillen beat Democrat Lynne Walz 381,371 to 321,162, a margin of 8 points. Those are the Associated Press counts published by The New York Times. Both races were far closer than Donald Trump's 20.6-point win in Nebraska in 2024. The Watch had not finished reading the Federal Election Commission's data for Nebraska when this was published, so the federal dollar figures below come from the filings as reported by Nebraska news outlets and trackers, and they are credited to those outlets.

Where the money came from

Most of the money went to the Senate race. Ricketts, Osborn and the other candidates on the ballot raised more than $25.6 million combined1, and according to OpenSecrets, the combined outside spending for Ricketts and Osborn is about $55 million.1 Based on third-quarter reports filed with the FEC, Ricketts raised $14.5 million and spent $13.8 million, and Osborn raised $11 million and spent $9.1 million.2 Ricketts, who is independently wealthy, had contributed $5.3 million to his own campaign3 by mid-September, Axios reported.

The biggest outside spender on Osborn's side was Nebraska Values PAC. It is a super PAC, meaning it can raise and spend unlimited amounts but cannot coordinate with a campaign. It had spent over $15.5 million on pro-Osborn and anti-Ricketts ads4 by late October, Nebraska Public Media reported. Most of its funding came from other PACs, like Democracy Matters PAC and Government That Works PAC, and the 501(c)(4) nonprofit Economic Opportunity Fund.4 Nonprofits of that kind do not have to name their donors, which is what the legal term "dark money" means. Ricketts' campaign used the term against Osborn: a spokesman said Osborn was "propped up by $15 million in dark money from Soros-funded groups, climate radicals and a billionaire named in the Epstein files."5 The campaign did not name the billionaire.

On the Republican side, Senate Leadership Fund, the major super PAC for Senate Republicans, spent nearly $15 million on ads opposing Osborn and supporting Ricketts4. Much of that went through Cornhusker Majority, a super PAC that Axios first reported has digital ties to the Senate Leadership Fund3. Defend American Jobs, a cryptocurrency super PAC, spent $13.5 million on ads opposing Osborn starting Oct. 9.4 It is largely funded by another PAC, Fairshake, which backs crypto-friendly candidates from both parties.4 Ricketts has supported crypto-friendly legislation as a member of the Senate Banking Committee.6

The governor's race is a state race, so its money is reported to the Nebraska Accountability and Disclosure Commission. By the late-September reports, Pillen's campaign had spent about $5.8 million, while Walz's had spent $2.6 million.7 Pillen received $2 million from the Republican Governors Association, the single largest contribution to any candidate this year, and Walz received $507,500 from the Democratic Governors Association.8 Union Pacific gave $375,000 to Pillen8. Government That Works PAC in Washington, D.C., gave Walz $800,0008, so the same PAC helped pay for both Walz's campaign and Nebraska Values PAC.

The ads

Nebraska Values PAC's best-known ads were about pardons Ricketts approved as governor. In one 60-second spot, a woman says Ricketts cast the deciding vote to pardon a man convicted of sexual assault without contacting the victim9. Ricketts called it a "terrible crime" and said he "made the best decision" he "could at the time with the information" he had9. In another, a woman named Beverly, who describes herself as a conservative voter from Lincoln10, criticizes a 2017 pardon. The PAC also spent about $130,000 on mailers opposing4 Robin Richards, a minor-party candidate. A separate super PAC, Sandhills Education Fund, spent $385,000 on ads opposing Osborn and supporting Richards4.

Cornhusker Majority's ads called Osborn a "phony 'independent'" who's really a Democrat11. One showed Osborn on a cereal box12. Many of these ads aired during Nebraska football games. Nebraska Public Media tracked 53 ads over seven games13, and across all races, attack ads outnumbered positive ones.13

In the governor's race, Walz's largest expenditure was a nearly $1.2 million television ad buy placed through a D.C. firm7. Pillen's largest single expenditure was with Ohio-based Flexpoint Campaigns.7 The America First Party of Nebraska ran an ad criticizing a $2.5 million no-bid contract Pillen's administration awarded in 202414. The party's main funder was Preserve the Good Life, a PAC that has mostly supported Democrats and Democratic-endorsed nonpartisan candidates15. Late in the race, the attorney general said Pillen's office "was not implicated in any criminal wrongdoing"16 in the contract.

Did it work?

In the Senate race, outside money was roughly even, so it is hard to say one side simply outspent the other. In OpenSecrets' late-October count, Osborn had about $24 million spent against him and $10 million in support, while Ricketts had $18 million spent against him and $1 million for him1. That is about $25 million on Ricketts' side and $28 million on Osborn's. Ricketts' campaign raised and spent more than Osborn's. Ricketts won by 6 points, about the margin of Osborn's 2024 loss to Sen. Deb Fischer17.

The two sides read the result differently. "Unfortunately, money won,"17 Osborn said. Fairshake spokesman Geoff Vetter said his group was "proud to have helped Sen. Ricketts weather the waves of a hard-fought cycle."18

In the governor's race, the candidate who spent more won. Pillen spent about twice what Walz did and won by 8 points. In 2022 he had won by 23 points.19 All of these figures come from filings made before the election. Final totals will come in the post-election reports.
