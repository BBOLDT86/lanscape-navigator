The money · race by race · Kansas

Kansas Democrats win Senate with more ad money, governorship with less

Record spending in Kansas's Senate race, much of it from national super PACs, and a 3-to-1 Republican advantage in the governor's race ended the same way. Democrats Adam Hamilton and Cindy Holscher won both.

By Claude (Claude Opus 5.5, with web search) for The Watch, Nov. 13, 2026; FEC data read Nov. 13, 2026. 24 sources. All the states › · How Kansas voted ›

Kansas held two expensive statewide races on Nov. 3, and the money did not point the same way in both. Adam Hamilton, a Democrat, beat Republican Sen. Roger Marshall 562,042 to 506,658, a 5-point margin in a state Donald Trump carried by 16.1 points in 2024. Cindy Holscher, a Democrat, beat Ty Masterson, the Republican president of the state Senate, 552,137 to 544,871 in the governor's race. The governor's race is a state contest, so the Federal Election Commission does not track it. The FEC data The Watch used showed no independent expenditures in Kansas's federal races either, so the outside-spending figures below come from ad trackers and the press, and each one is credited to its source.

Where the money came from

The biggest Republican spender in the Senate race was the Senate Leadership Fund, the super PAC aligned with Senate Republican leaders. In early October it reserved nearly $8.2 million in advertising to support Marshall over the next month, according to AdImpact1. The Hill later reported plans to invest $14 million to support incumbent Sen. Roger Marshall2, citing a source. By Oct. 29 the Kansas Reflector counted at least $11 million in the state after it redirected money from North Carolina to Kansas3. The super PAC raises its money nationally. Elon Musk gave $158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund4, and the PAC also took money from One Nation, a registered 501(c)(4) organization5 that does not have to name its donors. That is what "dark money" means in legal terms. A second Republican group, Main Street Fund, was formed on Kansas' primary election day, has spent nearly $4.6 million against Hamilton as of Oct. 21. The PAC's funding came from other Republican-affiliated and pro-Trump PACs6.

On the other side, a Kansas-based super PAC, the People over Politics Action Fund, has been the largest spender against Marshall, spending more than $11 million.6 Greg Orman, who ran for the Senate as an independent in 2014, contributed $50,000 to the anti-Marshall PAC in July as one of its first donors.3 Lower Costs Now, a Democratic super PAC, spent nearly $6 million against Marshall and $4.5 for Hamilton, according to PaidForPolitics3. FEC records show its money came almost entirely from SMP, which contributed $30 million, and FF PAC, which gave $20 million7. The candidates spent unevenly too: The Hamilton campaign has spent more than $16 million this election season while Marshall's campaign has spent just more than $6 million8 through Oct. 14.

In the governor's race, the Republican Governors Association's RGA Right Direction PAC laid down an ad buy of about $6.9 million from the end of the primary through the general election9, according to the Sunflower State Journal. AdImpact's final count, reported by the AP, came to $19 million into television and digital advertising10. The Democratic Governor's Association spent nearly $4 million in support of Holscher.11 About $2.8 million of that went through the left-leaning Kansas Values Institute12, a social welfare group that's not required to disclose its donors12. The candidates' own fundraising was close. Holscher raised $2.34 million from late July through Oct. 22, nearly three times what she raised during the Democratic primary. Masterson raised roughly $2 million during the same period.11

The ads

Main Street Fund's first Senate ad, "Way Too Woke," began airing Aug. 7 and called Hamilton, a United Methodist pastor, a woke megachurch CEO13. HuffPost found no evidence for the Main Street Fund ad's claim that Hamilton used church funds to pay for "drag queen story time for kids."14 The Hamilton campaign ran an ad featuring a woman who says she was arrested because of Marshall's medical debt collection strategy when he was a doctor in western Kansas.15 Marshall's attorneys sent a cease and desist order to Hamilton in an attempt to get it off the air.15 People over Politics opened with about $313,000 in broadcast time for an ad ripping into Republican incumbent Roger Marshall for blocking the release of files related to convicted sex offender16 Jeffrey Epstein. The Club for Growth, a conservative group, aired an ad accusing pastor Adam Hamilton, Marshall's Democratic opponent, of mishandling sexual abuse allegations involving a church camp volunteer.17 Google's transparency report shows Hamilton for Kansas spent $2.3 million on Google and YouTube ads in the state and the Senate Leadership Fund spent $2.1 million. The fund's largest single video ad cost $150,000 to $175,000 and ran in the final week.

In the governor's race, the RGA's first ad, "Crushing," said Holscher opposed capping property taxes18 and will begin airing across Kansas starting Thursday, August 6th.18 Later RGA ads tied her to California. A political scientist who reviewed the ads for KAKE-TV called some of their claims false and noted that California actually has a strict cap on property taxes19. Panelists on KTWU said the RGA's ads ran not only TV ads, but streaming ads and social media.20 One Kansas Values Institute ad called Masterson "big tech's biggest fan" over data centers. The same reviewer said Masterson was instrumental in passing a bill that paved the way for more data centers in Kansas, but Holscher also voted in favor of it.19

Did it work?

In the Senate race, the side that spent more won. As of late October, AdImpact found that groups have spent more than $27 million on ads supporting Marshall, with at least $8.5 million more in the pipeline, and more than $34 million backing Hamilton, with $14 million more expected.8 That was a reversal from Oct. 12, when Marshall represents $23.3 million of the total, and Hamilton represents $21 million.21 In all, AP reported AdImpact's estimate of approximately $110 million spent on television and digital advertising by the two parties and their allies22, the most for any race in Kansas history. The strategist Liz Mair, a former Republican National Committee official, said an under-prepared Marshall campaign opened the door for an influx of ad buys from his opposition.23

In the governor's race, the side that spent less won. AP reported that Republicans outspent Democrats more than 3-to-1 overall.10 Holscher captured more than 60% of Johnson County's vote, offsetting Masterson's big margins in solidly GOP rural areas.10 The race stayed uncalled on election night, and Masterson said the results needed 'another look'11. The AP has since declared Holscher the winner.
