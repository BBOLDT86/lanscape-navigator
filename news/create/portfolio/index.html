Section 01

Executive Summary

Create is Scrolls' product line of free, single-file HTML applications that connect peer to peer over WebRTC. There are no servers, no accounts, no databases and no subscriptions. You open one file and work; to work with someone, you share a six-letter code and your two devices talk directly. This edition grows the line from seven work tools to 35 products in 9 lines — Work, Hobby, Relationships, Home & Food, Travel, Money, Garage, Sports & Games and Body — and moves it inside The Watch, where each line sits on the desk its readers already use. 7 are live today (Vision, Together / 90, Date Night and Create · Home), 0 are in build this wave, and 28 are specified to the data model. The reference architecture is no longer a design: it is the shipped Vision, Rev 2.

  • The reference build shipped. Vision went live at vision/ as Rev 2: a stable six-letter device code instead of a random peer id, per-field last-writer-wins stamps instead of whole-record replacement, a hello / put protocol instead of resending the whole state on every change, an accept step for a device the board hasn't seen, automatic reconnection, soft deletes, QR pairing, and a board that travels as a compressed link. Every product now inherits that, not the 1.0 sketch. The build spec (SCR-CRT-ENG-2026-002) documents it as protocol v2.

  • Nine lines, not one. The Hobby and Relationships lines come first because The Watch already had the readers for them — the Hobbies desk's three pursuits and the Lifestyle desk's two-person tools. Home & Food, Travel, Money, Garage, Sports & Games and Body follow the other desks.
  • Three live products folded in. Vision, Together / 90 and Date Night were built before the line existed; they are its first members, listed as live, and their storage keys are left as they were.

  • The Work line moved to Wave 3. It is unchanged in scope; it waits for the two lines readers asked for first.
Section 03

Architecture — How Create Works

Every product is a self-contained HTML file with PeerJS loaded from a CDN. On first run it gives itself a six-letter device code (the same alphabet as Vision: no 0/O, 1/I/L) and that code is its PeerJS id for life — create-<prefix>-<code>. One person shows or sends the code; the other types it or scans the QR. PeerJS's free cloud server introduces the two browsers, and from then on everything travels over a WebRTC data channel between them. The server never sees the data and is not needed again until a device changes networks.

The sync cycle. When two devices connect, the dialer says hello with its whole state; the other side, having let it in, answers with its own. After that every change is a put of the changed items. Each item carries a stamp per field, so when both people edited the same thing offline the newer write of each field wins — no conflict dialogs, no lost edits. Deletes are soft: an item is marked gone and the mark syncs like any other field.

The inbox handoff. Products talk to each other the way The Watch talks to Vision: through a small array in the browser's own storage, create.<id>.inbox. Cookbook leaves ingredients for Pantry, Circle leaves gift ideas for Gift, Trip leaves spending for Split, Date Night and Weekender leave evenings and weekends for Week. The next time the receiving product is open it drains the inbox, saves, and syncs the new items to its partner. No product ever needs another one to be open.

Pictures are base64 data URIs, resized on import and capped at 2 MB; products that carry them split large messages into 128 KB chunks. Multi-person products (Form, Pool, Gift, a Trip with three travelers) use the hub pattern: one device hosts, guests connect to its code, the host merges and re-sends. Vault and Net encrypt every entry with AES-256-GCM before it reaches storage or the wire.

The static home — https://bboldt86.github.io/lanscape-navigator/news/create/ — is a page, not a service: the catalog, the launcher, the downloads, this document and the build spec. The same catalog.json drives The Watch's Create section, so the paper and the launcher never disagree.

Section 08

P2P Sync Patterns

PatternProductsHow it worksConflicts
Items (LWW)Pad, Board, Practice, Scoresheet, Cutlist, Felt, Vision, Circle, Split, Week, Pantry, Points, Budget, Scorebook, Train, StreakOn connect, each device says hello with its whole state. After that, every change travels as a put of the changed items. Each item carries a per-field stamp, so the newer write of each field wins and both people can edit offline.Per field, last writer wins
Chunked itemsDeck, Snap, Collection, Journal, Cookbook, Fix, Inventory, Trip, Garage, RebuildThe items pattern, with items that carry photos (base64 data URIs up to 2 MB) split into 128 KB chunks, sent in order and reassembled before merging.Per field, last writer wins; a photo is one field
Cell deltaCalcOnly changed cells travel, each with a stamp; the whole sheet travels once at hello.Per cell, last writer wins
Encrypted itemsVault, NetEntries are AES-256-GCM blobs before they reach storage or the wire. Peers exchange blobs by id and stamp; only a device that knows the passphrase can read them.Per entry, last writer wins on the blob
HubForm, PoolOne device hosts; any number of guests connect to its code. Guests put their own records; the host merges and puts the merged whole back, so every guest sees everyone.Per field, last writer wins; the host is the relay, not the authority
Hidden claimsGiftThe items pattern, where some fields (who claimed a gift) sync to every device but are never drawn on the device of the person the list is for.Per field, last writer wins
