The House · Florida 18

Franklin keeps redrawn Polk County seat by under four points

Rep. Scott Franklin beat Democrat Curtis Gibson by 10,018 votes in a district now centered primarily in Polk County, with part of Osceola County. Polk moved 14 points toward Democrats from 2024, and Osceola moved 20.

The race

Scott Franklin, the Lakeland Republican, won his fourth term in Congress since first being elected in 20202. With 99% of the expected vote counted, he had 137,597 votes (50.3%). Democrat Curtis Gibson had 127,579 (46.7%), and Deva Simmons, who ran with no party affiliation, had 8,110 (3.0%). The Associated Press called the race at 9:37 p.m. Eastern on election night. LkldNow, a Lakeland news site, called it a closer-than-expected congressional race2 and reported that Gibson held a slim lead as early results began to come in2.

On paper the seat was safe. WUSF reported that the Cook Political Report rated3 the district solid Republican. LkldNow reported that Franklin has nearly $1 million ($960,764) in cash on hand4. As of June 30, Gibson has raised $12,208 and spent $10,756, leaving him with $1,452 in cash on hand4.

Gibson is a former Lake Wales city commissioner5 who works as the property manager for Eagle Ridge Mall5. The Ledger of Lakeland reported that he ran as the "voice for Florida's Heartland"5. He promised to push for a federal reinsurance backstop for property owners5 and also campaigned on health care for veterans5 and property tax relief. Gibson did not respond to requests for an interview.6

Franklin, a U.S. Navy veteran7 and former Lakeland city commissioner, emphasized issues such as federal spending, national security, and lower taxes2, according to LkldNow. He told that outlet the concerns he heard most were cost of living, the rising cost of housing and insurance, and the polarization dividing our country4. The Ledger reported that he drew criticism for being too reliant on virtual town halls5. It also reported that he supports the Iran war but is "not thrilled' with the strategy7 and wants more transparency from the Trump administration. Simmons, a former Polk County Public Schools employee5, wanted the ACA subsidies restored5 and Congress to push back against President Donald Trump on the tariffs and the Iran War5. Her 8,110 votes were fewer than Franklin's 10,018-vote margin.

The district

The district kept its number but changed shape. Gov. Ron DeSantis signed into law on Monday a new congressional map, reworking 21 of Florida's 28 U.S. House districts1. The old 18th used to run from Polk County south, encompassing the state's rural counties of DeSoto, Hendry, Hardee, Highlands, Glades and Okeechobee8. Reports describe the new Polk boundary differently. WUSF says the district takes in all of Polk County and a sliver of Osceola County3, while LkldNow says most of Polk County2. Around Osceola reported that the far northwestern part of Osceola that includes Four Corners, Celebration, Reunion and ChampionsGate is part of District 189, and the rest of the county is in District 9.

The Watch's wave compares each county's 2026 Senate and governor vote with the 2024 Harris–Trump result. Both of the district's counties moved sharply toward Democrats. Trump won Polk by 20.7 points in 2024. This year Republicans carried it by 6.7, a 14-point move, with 257,911 votes cast. Osceola went from R+1.4 to D+18.4, a 20-point move, with 131,845 votes cast. Weighted by votes, the district's counties moved 16 points, more than the state's 10-point move in the Senate race and 12-point move for governor.

Two cautions. The Osceola numbers cover the whole county, most of which is outside this district. They say little about Celebration or ChampionsGate on their own. And a move does not prove that voters changed their minds. A total like Polk's 257,911 ballots shows how the county voted, not whether 2024 voters switched parties or a different mix of people turned out. Statewide reporting points to both. The Florida Phoenix reported that approximately 68% of registered Democrats voted statewide, compared to 63% of Republicans10. Democratic data consultant Matt Isbell told the Phoenix that Democrats overperformed Tuesday, especially with independent voters and even some "soft Republicans."10

What it means

No incumbent lost here. LkldNow pointed out that Franklin won the old 18th by wide margins in 2022 and 2024, a stark contrast2 with this year. His 3.7-point House margin was also smaller than the 6.7-point Republican edge across Polk in the Senate and governor races, though the district and the county do not match exactly.

Republicans won both of Florida's statewide races. Ashley Moody beat Angie Nixon for the Senate by 2.9 points, and Byron Donalds beat David Jolly for governor by 1.3. Trump had carried Florida by 13.1 in 2024. Gibson listed Nixon among his endorsements4. WKMG in Orlando reported that Republicans had 20 members in Florida's congressional delegation before the election and still had 20 afterward11, with two races still too close to call. Nationally, 252 House seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called. That puts Democrats past the 218 needed for a majority. In the 18th, about 1% of the expected vote was still uncounted as of Saturday morning.
