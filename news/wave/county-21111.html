The wave · the biggest counties · Jefferson County, Kentucky

In Louisville, Booker nearly doubles the Democratic margin but Kentucky stays red

Jefferson County gave Charles Booker a 32-point margin on a quarter fewer votes than 2024, a swing in line with Kentucky's other counties. Its suburbs also ousted Republican state lawmakers, though Andy Barr still won the Senate seat.

What Jefferson County did

Jefferson County is Kentucky's largest vote center by far. Since a city-county merger in 2003, the county's territory, population and government have been coextensive with the city of Louisville1. Census Reporter puts the population at 783,0222, and about one resident in ten was born outside the country.

On Nov. 3 the county gave Charles Booker, the Louisville Democrat, 173,234 votes. Republican Rep. Andy Barr got 88,656. With 99% of the expected vote counted, that is a Democratic margin of 31.9 points. Kamala Harris carried the county by 16.6 points in 2024, so the county moved about 15 points toward the Democrats and the margin nearly doubled.

That was not enough statewide. Barr won Kentucky by 12.1 points, though that was down from Donald Trump's 30.6-point win in 2024. Jefferson County supplied about 28% of Booker's statewide vote.

By Kentucky standards, Louisville's move was average. All 120 counties moved left, and Jefferson ranks 62nd. The typical county moved about 16 points. Lexington's Fayette County moved about 14 points and ended at D+31.6, almost the same margin as Louisville. Some Republican counties moved further. Kenton County, across the river from Cincinnati, went from R+21.7 to R+2.3, and Warren County, home to Bowling Green, moved about 21 points. Among big counties elsewhere that Harris won by similar margins, Jefferson's move matched Broward in Florida (15 points) and Pima in Arizona (16). It trailed Gwinnett in Georgia (26) and beat Hamilton in Ohio (10) and San Diego (5).

Turnout fell sharply. The county cast 264,869 votes, 75% of its 2024 total. Booker still won by about 84,600 votes, compared with Harris's 2024 lead of roughly 58,700, even though about 88,700 fewer people voted. County totals can't separate Republicans who stayed home from voters who switched parties, so this move reflects both.

Why it moved

Barr had an endorsement from President Donald Trump and campaigned on priorities aligned with the president's agenda, including border security, tax cuts, domestic energy production and support for law enforcement3, WHAS11 reported. His campaign called Booker a socialist. According to WDRB, it pointed to his positions on abolishing ICE, on policing and on the Green New Deal. Booker argued that affordable health care and the price of gas and groceries were not radical concerns, Louisville Public Media reported. A parade of Democrats rallied for Booker in Louisville on the first two days of early voting4, among them Gov. Andy Beshear. Booker also became the first-ever Senate candidate in Kentucky to receive an endorsement from former President Barack Obama5.

Fox 56 reported that early and absentee voting in Jefferson County ran well ahead of the 2022 and 2023 pace by the Thursday before the election. The county also opened Louisville's first drive-through voting center6 at the Kentucky Expo Center. Before the election, University of Louisville political scientist Dewey Clayton told public radio that he expected Democratic and independents to be highly motivated while Republicans are… less so7. Afterward, Al Cross, a former Courier Journal political writer, wrote in a Kentucky Lantern commentary that Republican turnout seemed depressed in Kentucky, as it was nationally8. He also found evidence of ticket-splitting. Some Barr voters also picked Louisville-area Democrats who defeated Republican state Sens.9 The Watch did not find a published exit poll for the county alone that would show which voters moved.

What it means for Louisville politics

The clearest change was in the legislature. Louisville Public Media reported that Democrats made the largest inroads in suburban Louisville districts where they had lost ground in recent years, knocking off seven GOP incumbents with at least part of their district including Jefferson County.10 Longtime State Senator Julie Raque Adams, R-Louisville, lost her reelection bid to former Jefferson County Public Schools board member Sarah Cole McIntosh11. House Majority Whip Jason Nemes lost to Democrat Jennifer Hardin. Even so, Republicans will keep a supermajority in Frankfort11, according to Spectrum News. Senate President Robert Stivers said the losses were minor in terms of numbers but cost Republicans experience on policy.12

Analysts disagree about how far the shift reached. Republican strategist T.J. Litafik told WKYT that "There was no growth for the Democratic Party in Kentucky last night outside of Lexington and Louisville"13. That is accurate for seats. In vote share, though, every Kentucky county moved toward Booker compared with 2024, and Louisville's move was in the middle of the pack.

Locally, Mayor Craig Greenberg won Louisville's first nonpartisan mayoral race against Metro Council member Shameka Parrish-Wright, a fellow Democrat. A precinct-level analysis by LPM News found that Greenberg performed best with voters on the outer edges of Louisville, but lost support in the city's urban core.6
