Bernalillo County moved 17 points left, less than most of New Mexico

Sen. Ben Ray Luján won Albuquerque's home county by 38 points over a Republican who reached the ballot through a write-in campaign. But the state's biggest county was already strongly Democratic, and it ranked 23rd of 33 in how far it moved.

What Bernalillo County did

Bernalillo County is home to Albuquerque. By the county government's count it is 1,160 square miles and is New Mexico's most populous county with more than 676,000 residents1. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried it by 21.0 points on 310,844 votes. On Nov. 3, Democratic Sen. Ben Ray Luján won it over Republican Larry Marker by 172,306 votes to 77,461. That is a margin of 38.0 points on 249,767 votes, with about 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The county moved 17 points toward the Democrats.

By New Mexico's standards, that was a modest move. Statewide, Luján won by 26.5 points with about 98.5 percent counted. Harris had carried the state by 6.0, so the state moved 20.5 points. All 33 counties moved left, and Bernalillo ranked 23rd. Other counties that were already strongly Democratic, or suburban, also moved less: Santa Fe by 15 points and Sandoval, which takes in Rio Rancho, by 14. Doña Ana, around Las Cruces, moved 26. McKinley, around Gallup, moved 33. Valencia, just south of Albuquerque, moved 23, enough to turn a 16.4-point Trump county into a 6.5-point Luján win.

Among big counties elsewhere that Harris won by similar margins, Bernalillo moved less than Allegheny, Dallas and Montgomery, Pa., which moved about 23 points each, and less than Queens at 20. It moved more than Sacramento at 10, and more than the 14-point move of the typical county nationally.

The Senate race was lopsided from the start. The Albuquerque Journal noted that Marker had to mount a write-in campaign simply to qualify for the November ballot2. The Santa Fe New Mexican reported that Luján had $2.8 million in hand3 in his last federal report, while Marker's war chest contained $88.3 The governor's race between Deb Haaland and Gregg Hull was closer: Haaland won the state by 14.7 points, a move of 8.7 points. That gap suggests the Senate result partly reflects how weak the matchup was. The Journal reported that Haaland decisively won in New Mexico's three most populous counties — Bernalillo, Doña Ana and Santa Fe4.

Who turned out, and why

The 249,767 votes counted in Bernalillo County equal 80 percent of the 2024 total, about 61,000 fewer ballots. The county count cannot show how much of the 17-point move came from voters changing sides and how much came from which voters showed up.

Most of the local reporting points to turnout. Before Election Day, an analysis by The New Mexico News found that 51% of early and absentee ballots cast statewide during early voting were from registered Democrats, even though they only make up 40% of registered voters.5 Bernalillo, Doña Ana, Santa Fe and Sandoval together accounted for 67.40% (more than two-thirds) of all pre-Election Day ballots cast in the state.5 After the vote, Source NM reported that the statewide shortfall from 2022 turnout appears to be a result of relatively low turnout by the state's third major voting bloc: unaffiliated or Decline to State voters.6 It also found that Democratic turnout, predominately in the state's most populous counties, exceeded that of 20226.

Brian Sanderoff, the Albuquerque pollster, told the Journal that "many Democrats in New Mexico were motivated to vote partially due to national politics and their anger or frustration with Donald Trump"7. In Albuquerque on election night, County Clerk Michelle Kavanaugh blamed a delay on "an unusually large number" of voters showing up to the University of New Mexico polling location to register and vote.7

On the issues, a September Journal poll found that about 47% of likely voters said inflation and cost of living was the biggest concern facing their family8. The poll came as the war with Iran pushed gas prices from $2.64 a gallon at the start of the conflict to $3.61 at the end of August.8 KOB 4 reported that Albuquerque voters on Election Day named crime, affordability, education and the country's future9 as the issues that drove them to the polls. Around Albuquerque and Rio Rancho, Source NM reported, voters hoped their votes would counter federal efforts to implement cuts to services such as Medicaid and SNAP.10 None of the reporting so far shows which groups of Bernalillo voters moved most.

What it means for the county

The same pattern held further down the ballot. The Paper reported that Democrats won nearly every contested partisan race, including 22 of 24 state House districts11 in the county. Republicans held on in some places: KUNM reported that Republican Rep. Nicole Chavez narrowly kept her Northeast Heights seat, one of the few Republican represented districts in the Albuquerque area.12

The county's size shaped the statewide result. On election night Hull told supporters to keep waiting because "Bernalillo County hasn't even reported any numbers yet"13. According to the New Mexican, he finally conceded the race after Bernalillo County results came in after 10 p.m.14 Haaland won statewide despite carrying only 15 of the state's 33 counties4, so the Democrats' edge rested on Albuquerque and the state's other big counties.

These results are unofficial. A small share of the vote is still being counted, and the county canvass has not yet taken place.
