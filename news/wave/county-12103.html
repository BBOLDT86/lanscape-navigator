The wave · the biggest counties · Pinellas County, Florida

In Pinellas, a narrow Nixon edge and a smaller swing than Florida's

Pinellas County backed Donald Trump in 2024. This year it gave Democrat Angie Nixon a lead of about 2,600 votes over Sen. Ashley Moody. Its six-point move left was real but smaller than the state's, and fewer people voted.

Pinellas is the crowded peninsula of St. Petersburg, Clearwater, Largo and the Gulf beach towns, and it has been one of Florida's true swing counties. Hillsborough and Pinellas counties around Tampa Bay are key state battlegrounds. Trump narrowly won both counties in 2024, while Democrat Joe Biden narrowly carried both in 2020.1 On Nov. 3 it tipped back by the narrowest of margins. In the U.S. Senate race, state Rep. Angie Nixon, the Democrat, had 200,154 votes to 197,529 for Sen. Ashley Moody, the Republican. That is D+0.7 with 99% of the expected vote counted. Moody won the state by 2.9 points.

What Pinellas did

Pinellas moved from R+5.2 in the 2024 presidential race to D+0.7, a shift of 6 points toward the Democrats. That is a real move, but a modest one by this year's standards. Sixty-six of Florida's 67 counties moved left, and Pinellas ranks 45th by the size of its move. The typical Florida county moved 7 points, and the state as a whole moved 10. Across the bay, Hillsborough went from R+3.1 to D+9.0, a 12-point move. Duval moved 12 points, Miami-Dade 19 and Palm Beach 10.

Pinellas also trails most big counties elsewhere that started in about the same place. Tarrant, Texas, moved 24 points and Maricopa, Arizona, 20. Of the comparable counties, only Nassau, New York, moved less: 4 points, to D+0.2. Across the country the typical county moved 14 points.

The governor's race went the same way at the top. David Jolly, the Democrat, is a former congressman who represented Florida's 13th congressional district, in Pinellas County, from 2014 to 20172. He lost statewide to Byron Donalds by 1.3 points, but WTSP reported that a majority of the Tampa Bay area favored Donalds, but Jolly won the two biggest counties: Hillsborough and Pinellas.3

Who turned out, and what moved them

Pinellas cast 403,626 votes in the Senate race. That is 78% of its 2024 total, or about 113,000 fewer ballots. A county's move mixes two things: which voters showed up and which voters changed sides. The county-level count cannot separate them. Reports on who voted suggest the two parties' turnout ran close. Six days before the election, Fox 13 reported that among Pinellas voters who had already cast ballots, Democrats [held] a much narrower 3,402 voter registration advantage4 than in Hillsborough. On Election Day morning, Florida Politics reported, registered Republicans outpaced Democrats by more than 6,600 ballots Tuesday morning, reversing an early Democratic lead.5 One post-election analysis found that Republicans were a smaller share of the voting electorate in both Hillsborough and Pinellas counties than they were four years ago6. That comparison is with the 2022 midterm, not 2024.

Local campaigns ran mostly on cost of living. In a north Pinellas state House race, Florida Politics found rising insurance premiums, utility bills and housing costs have become central issues7. In the 13th Congressional District, Democrat Leela Gray campaigned on an agenda that included putting an end to the war in Iran8, ending tariffs and cracking down on corporate investors buying homes across Pinellas and Pasco Counties.8 The 2024 hurricanes still shaped local politics. In the St. Petersburg mayor's race, WUSF reported that Charlie Crist criticized Welch's handling of the city's recovery from two hurricanes9.

Nixon herself was an unusual nominee. The AP described her as a democratic socialist who tested the strength of Democrats' left flank10 after she upset establishment Democrats' more moderate favorite in the primary.10 None of the reporting reviewed for this piece explains why Pinellas moved less than Hillsborough or the state.

What it means for the county

Below the top of the ticket, the results were mixed but closer than two years ago. The Tampa Bay Times reported that Tim Hughes, a Democrat and political newcomer, won over incumbent Republican Commissioner Brian Scott11 for a countywide commission seat. Scott's first win in 2022 tipped the commission to a Republican majority for the first time in nearly a decade.11 In the 13th District, Rep. Anna Paulina Luna, a Republican, narrowly held a seat that Florida Politics noted was redrawn to make Luna's race easier, not harder.12 Decision Desk HQ projected her the winner. In a later report, the Associated Press has yet to call the race.13 A Pinellas school board race also appeared headed to a recount14, and Crist unseated Mayor Ken Welch in St. Petersburg.

The Tampa Bay Times summed up the region this way: In Tampa Bay, Republicans won by narrower margins than in 2024.15 In Pinellas, that left the county almost exactly where it has spent most of the past decade: split nearly evenly.
