The wave · the biggest counties · Denver County, Colorado

Denver gives former mayor Hickenlooper 83%, moving 14 points left

Colorado's capital moved toward Democrats about as far as the state did, and it did so with far fewer voters than in 2024. Over the summer, its Democratic primary voters had turned against party veterans.

What Denver did

Denver is a consolidated city and county, with one government serving as both. Its voters gave Sen. John Hickenlooper, a Democrat and former two-term mayor of the city, 254,474 votes. Republican state Sen. Mark Baisley got 39,286. That is about 83% to 13%, a Democratic margin of 70.4 points, with 99% of the expected vote counted. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried Denver over Donald Trump by 56.1 points, so the county moved 14 points toward the Democrats.

Colorado as a whole moved almost exactly as much. With about 97% of the expected vote counted, Hickenlooper led statewide by 25.3 points, compared with Harris's 11.0. That is also a 14-point move. Phil Weiser's race for governor moved 17. All 64 Colorado counties moved left, and Denver ranks 14th by the size of its move. Some suburban neighbors moved further: Adams County moved 23 points and Arapahoe 16. Trump carried El Paso and Douglas counties in 2024, and both went narrowly Democratic this time. Among big, heavily Democratic counties in other states, Denver landed in the middle. Philadelphia moved 20 points and DeKalb, Georgia, moved 13. Alameda, California, moved 8, and Multnomah, Oregon, moved 5.

Denver is a young, growing city. The Census Bureau puts it at 740,613 residents in its July 2025 estimate, 28.0% Hispanic or Latino and 54.0% non-Hispanic white1.

Why it moved

Colorado reporters treated the race as decided early. The Colorado Sun called it a contest that "was never really competitive."2 Colorado Politics reported that Hickenlooper raised a reported $11 million through mid-October, compared with $138,000 for Baisley3. Colorado Public Radio noted that the ballot also included Bob Chew, a self-funded Forward Party candidate4. In Denver, about 11,900 votes went to someone other than the two major-party nominees.

Denver's Democrats had not been solidly behind Hickenlooper. In the June primary, state Sen. Julie Gonzales of Denver ran against him from the left. The Colorado Sun reported that Gonzales was beating Hickenlooper by about 13,000 votes in Denver, while he won each of Colorado's 63 other counties5. Five months later, he took 83% of the city's vote.

Polls pointed to cost of living and the national mood as the main issues. Colorado Public Radio reported that in a Colorado Polling Institute survey, voters across the political spectrum named affordability as the top issue they wanted state government to address6. A statewide exit poll of Latino voters found they backed Democrats overwhelmingly, mainly because of concerns over affordability and immigration enforcement7. That poll was reported in coverage carried by Yahoo News. Nationally, the NBC News Exit Poll found that 57% of voters said Trump's immigration enforcement actions had gone too far8. Axios Denver said the statewide results delivered a stinging rebuke of President Trump9.

Turnout matters to the picture. Denver cast 305,655 votes in the Senate race, 84% of its 2024 presidential total, or about 57,000 fewer ballots. Denverite reported that by Election Day afternoon, about 45% of the county's active voters had returned ballots, the lowest share of any of the 64 counties10. The site added that turnout usually jumps late because younger people tend to vote later10. Statewide, CPR reported that ballot returns were up in every age group, with the biggest increase among voters ages 18-2411.

Even with the smaller electorate, Hickenlooper's net lead was about 215,000 votes, compared with roughly 204,000 for Harris. The county count cannot show whether Trump voters changed sides, stayed home, or were outnumbered by a different group of voters who showed up. Denver's 14-point move is some mix of all three.

What it means for Denver

The left wing of Denver's Democratic Party had a strong year. Melat Kiros, a democratic socialist, unseated 15-term U.S. Rep. Diana DeGette in the June primary12, then won the 1st Congressional District. The Denver Gazette notes that the district nearly coincides with Denver's city limits12. When Kiros takes office, she will be the district's first new representative since January 199713. Axios Denver reported that her win, like those of similar insurgent candidates elsewhere, showed some voters wanted candidates who could more directly combat President Trump's policies14.

The Senate seat is the next open contest. Hickenlooper has said this is his final term, meaning the race to replace him starts now15, according to Axios Denver. Statewide, Colorado Politics noted that for the sixth election in a row, Democrats swept every statewide race16.

The count is not final. Denver's elections office lists Nov. 12 as the deadline to receive overseas and military ballots and to cure ballots17, so the last few ballots could shift the figures slightly.
