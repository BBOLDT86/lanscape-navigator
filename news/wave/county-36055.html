Rochester's Monroe County edges toward Hochul as suburban turnout slips

Kathy Hochul won Monroe County by 23 points, four more than Kamala Harris did in 2024. The county moved only about half as far as New York did, and about a quarter fewer people voted.

Monroe County is the city of Rochester and the towns around it, from Greece and Irondequoit on the lakeshore to Perinton and Penfield on the east side. The Rochester Beacon has found that towns in the western part of Monroe County, such as Parma, Ogden, Clarkson, and Hamlin, are the strongest performers for Republican candidates.1

What Monroe County did

Gov. Kathy Hochul, a Democrat from Buffalo, carried the county over Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman, 167,229 votes to 104,824. That is a margin of 22.9 points with 98 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris won the county by 19.1 points in 2024, so Monroe moved about 4 points toward the Democrats.

That was a real move in a state where half the counties did not move left at all: 31 of New York's 62 counties moved toward Hochul, and the typical county did not move. Monroe ranked 19th. But it lagged the state as a whole, which went from D+12.7 to D+19.6, a 7-point shift driven downstate: Queens moved 20 points, Brooklyn 15 and Suffolk 11. Erie County, Hochul's home, moved only 1 point.

Next to big counties elsewhere that voted like Monroe in 2024, the move was small. Sacramento moved 10 points, Broward 15, Allegheny 23 and Gwinnett 26. Only San Diego, at 5, came close. The typical county nationally moved 14.

The electorate also shrank. About 272,800 votes have been counted so far, 76 percent of the 2024 total. Hochul's lead of roughly 62,400 votes is smaller than Harris's lead of about 69,000. A county's move mixes who turned out with who changed sides, and these counts can't tell the two apart.

Who showed up, and what they heard

Before Election Day, the signs pointed to a big turnout. The county Board of Elections reported record early voting, and officials said turnout has been more in line with a presidential election.2 Stephen DeVay, the Monroe County Democratic chairman, said there was a lot of enthusiasm from voters with record midterm election turnout for early voting3.

The Rochester Beacon's analysis of unofficial returns found something different. It reported that overall voter turnout was down countywide compared to the last midterm election4, and that the suburbs accounted for the drop while turnout in the city held roughly steady. The Beacon said Rep. Joe Morelle's vote total rose in both city and suburbs compared with 2022, "further suggesting that county Republicans failed to show up this midterm."4 It also called Greece's turnout decline particularly damaging4 for Morelle's Republican opponent, Virginia McIntyre. That is the Beacon's reading of the numbers. The board did not yet have turnout broken down by party, and we found no exit or voter poll that reports on Monroe County voters separately.

In Rochester, the campaign was about energy bills. News10NBC reported that the cost of utility bills has become a central issue in the race for New York governor.5 Hochul announced her utility rebate checks in Rochester. Blakeman promised to cut rates in half and said he would sign an executive order "opening up the Marcellus Shale to natural gas extraction"5. Statewide, the New York Public News Network reported that Hochul won by relentlessly linking him to President Donald Trump.6 Gothamist reported that her campaign spent $30 million worth of ads touting her push to send energy rebate checks to New Yorkers, pause data center construction and expand access to childcare.7 Upstate had looked like Blakeman's best ground: Spectrum News reported in late October that two independent polls show Bruce Blakeman leading upstate8.

What it means locally

The bigger change was further down the ballot. In Gates, Democrat Nick Coffee beat the Republican incumbent, Cosmo Giunta, ending a 65-year stretch of Republican leadership in the town supervisor's office9, WHAM reported. Republicans won back Rush, where the Democratic supervisor had served only a year. The Democrat and Chronicle reported that the Democratic wins in town races last year, including several supervisor races which had been filled by Republicans for decades, largely held firm a year later.3

WXXI News described the night as signaling the local Republican party's dwindling influence.10 Peter Elder, who leads the county Republican committee, rejected that. He said the Republican Party in Monroe County is still alive and well, and we will keep fighting10.

The trend is not new. A Beacon analysis of the 2025 results noted that in Monroe County, about 58 percent of Republicans are 55 or older, compared with 46 percent of Democrats.11 All of the figures are still unofficial. The Board of Elections must recheck votes, count absentee ballots, and certify the final results. The process could take up to two weeks12.
