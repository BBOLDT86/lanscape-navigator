Sean McCann unseats Bill Huizenga, flipping southwest Michigan's 4th District

Kalamazoo state senator Sean McCann beat the eight-term Republican by 1.8 points. All six counties in the district moved toward Democrats in the statewide vote, but each moved less than Michigan as a whole.

Sean McCann, a Democratic state senator from Kalamazoo, has unseated Rep. Bill Huizenga in Michigan's 4th Congressional District. With 97.4 percent of the expected vote counted, McCann had 186,195 votes to Huizenga's 179,484. That is a lead of 6,711 votes, or 1.8 percentage points. AP lists Shafina Barnett as an independent; The Herald-Palladium described her as of the Green Party1. She drew 5,695 votes, fewer than the gap between the two leaders. The Associated Press called the race at 1:13 p.m. Eastern on Nov. 4.

This would have been Huizenga's ninth term representing the district.2 The Detroit News called McCann the first Democrat to represent the 4th Congressional District in nearly a century3.

The race

Bridge Michigan reported that McCann benefited from name recognition because he has represented parts of southwest Michigan in the state Legislature on and off since 2011.4 He is a former Kalamazoo city commissioner2 who won the Democratic primary with about two-thirds of the vote over Diop Harris II. National Democrats put him in the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee's "Red to Blue" program5. Huizenga had initially weighed a run for Senate last year before seeking reelection5. He won his primary with about 85% of the vote against former Holland Mayor Phil Tanis.2

The Detroit News reported that McCann ran ads about Huizenga's six votes against war powers resolutions that seek to remove U.S. forces from the fighting3 in Iran. The war slowed the shipping of oil through the region, which drove up the price of both gas and diesel6, which hit hard in what the paper called this heavily agricultural district3. Tariffs were the other fight. They affected three important parts of the region -- manufacturing, tourism and agriculture6, and Huizenga softened his original support for the border taxes6 during the campaign. Huizenga went after McCann's 2025 vote to raise the base price of Michigan's gas tax from 31 to 51 cents per gallon6 and put up billboards calling him "Tax Man McCann."

According to Bridge, McCann had sought to play up Huizenga's ties to Trump while Huizenga has positioned himself as a workhorse focused on affordability.4 John Clark, a political scientist at Western Michigan University, said both campaigns' ads on energy costs and data centers were so alike that "you'd be hard pressed to figure out which campaign was airing them."7

Spending was heavy, though the tallies differ. The Detroit News counted $15 million since Aug. 46 from the candidates and their outside backers. A Gongwer roundup a month before the election put general election spending in MI-048 near $24 million. In the last days, Tanis endorsed McCann; during the primary he had argued that Huizenga puts Donald Trump and the republican party ahead of what's best for the country.9 WGVU reported that the Huizenga campaign answered by accus[ing] him of attacking President Trump for years9.

The district

The district covers all of Allegan and Van Buren counties and parts of Ottawa, Kalamazoo, Calhoun and Berrien.2 The cities of Holland, Battle Creek, Benton Harbor and Kalamazoo all fall in the 4th District.10

The Watch's wave compares each whole county's 2026 Senate and governor vote with its 2024 Harris–Trump margin. On that measure all six counties moved toward Democrats, by a weighted 6.2 points:

• Calhoun moved the most, 8 points, going from R+14.0 to R+6.2.
• Van Buren moved 8 points, from R+15.1 to R+7.5.
• Kalamazoo moved 7 points and is now at D+24.8.
• Allegan and Berrien each moved 6 points.
• Ottawa, the biggest county with 160,391 votes counted, moved the least: 5 points, to R+15.7.

No county switched sides; Berrien came closest, at R+1.6. Every one of the six moved less than the state did in the Senate race (8.5 points).

These are whole-county results from the statewide races, not the House vote, and four of the six counties are split by the district line. On the House race itself, WKZO reported that McCann also won in Berrien and Calhoun counties, while Huizenga won in Allegan, Ottawa and Van Buren counties.11 The counts also can't separate voters who changed their minds from changes in who turned out. Michigan cast 4,796,991 votes in the Senate race, and the Detroit News reported record turnout for a non-presidential year12.

What the result means

Huizenga had won the last eight elections.3 The Hill noted that while he easily won his last race in 2024, the district has become more competitive in recent years5. The Hill also reported that AP rival Decision Desk HQ called the race for McCann. This was one of three Democratic pickups in Michigan. Bridge reported that McCann's win and Will Lawrence's win over Barrett in mid-Michigan's 7th District helped solidify a Democratic majority in the House4. WKZO said Michigan Republicans will hold just four seats in the U.S. House in the next term, their smallest delegation since 1884.11 Across the country, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.

Some votes are still being counted: only 92 percent of Van Buren's expected vote and 95 percent of Berrien's are in. The results stay unofficial until they are certified. Huizenga conceded and said he wanted to congratulate Sean McCann on his victory.2 McCann said his first priorities would be to expand health care, stop the war in Iran and end a tariff policy he described as chaotic.6
