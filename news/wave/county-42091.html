The wave · the biggest counties · Montgomery County, Pennsylvania

Montgomery County doubles its Democratic margin for hometown Gov. Josh Shapiro

Josh Shapiro's home county gave him a 46-point win over Stacy Garrity, twice Kamala Harris's 2024 margin. Turnout fell to 75 percent of 2024's, and a county election official says Republicans staying home likely did much of the work.

What Montgomery did

Pennsylvania had no U.S. Senate race on Nov. 3, so the governor's race is the measure here. In Montgomery County, Gov. Josh Shapiro, a Democrat, beat state Treasurer Stacy Garrity, a Republican, 283,249 votes to 103,298. That is a 46-point margin, roughly 73 percent to 26 percent, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 23 points, so the county moved 23 points toward the Democrats.

This is Shapiro's home county. He grew up in Montgomery County and served in the state House and on the county Board of Commissioners before winning two terms as attorney general.1 He finished his statewide bus tour there the day before the election.2 Even so, the county moved about as much as the rest of the state. Pennsylvania went from a 1.7-point Trump win in 2024 to a 22.1-point Shapiro win, a 24-point move. Montgomery's 23-point move ranks 28th of 67 counties, and all 67 moved left. Bucks County moved 26 points, from a near tie to D+25.7. Delaware County moved 22 and finished at D+45.9, just behind Montgomery. Chester moved 22 and Philadelphia 20.

Montgomery's weight is in raw votes. It cast 7.8 percent of the state's ballots for governor but supplied about 180,000 votes of Shapiro's 1.1 million-vote statewide lead, roughly one-sixth. Big counties elsewhere that started from a similar 2024 margin moved by similar amounts: Dallas County, Texas, moved 23 points, Wake County, N.C., 22 and Queens 20. The typical county nationally moved 14.

It is a wealthy, highly educated suburban county. The Census Bureau estimates 877,643 residents. Fifty-three percent of adults hold a bachelor's degree, and the median household income is $113,915.3

Who showed up, and why

Fewer people voted. The county cast 390,151 votes for governor, 75 percent of its 2024 presidential total and about 131,000 fewer. Turnout always drops in a midterm, but Patch reported this was the lowest turnout for a midterm election year since 20144, even though the number of registered voters has increased by 21,0004. On election night, county officials had predicted "impressive" turnout, pointing to long lines in multiple precincts5. The Philadelphia Inquirer found that in Southeastern Pennsylvania, the blue wave was not fueled by a surge in voter turnout.6 Neil Makhija, a Democratic county commissioner who chairs the elections board, told the paper: "Much of the win is likely from Republicans who didn't have the enthusiasm to turn out."7

That is the limit of county numbers. A 23-point move mixes who stayed home with who switched sides, and the count alone cannot separate them. Statewide polling suggests some of both happened, though those figures are not specific to Montgomery. An Oct. 7 Quinnipiac poll found Shapiro winning 17 percent of Republicans and leading among independents 62 percent to 23 percent.8 WHYY reported that Shapiro benefited from some GOP support and from Trump's sagging favorability1.

On the issues, the Inquirer reported that the cost of living, data centers, ICE and Trump were on voters' minds at Philadelphia-area polling places9. It also reported that Democrats in swing state Senate districts, including the 24th, built their pitch around affordability as voters disapproved of Trump's handling of the economy and gas prices.10 PublicSource reported that Garrity was hampered by weak fundraising, limited GOP support and Shapiro's name recognition, approval ratings and incumbency11. City & State reported that since 2025 Shapiro has reported more than $63.7 million in contributions, against $10.6 million for Garrity.12

What it means in Montgomery

The shift reached down the ballot. Chris Thomas, a former executive director of the Montgomery County Democratic Committee, won the 24th Senatorial District, which covers part of Montgomery County and part of Berks.13 He unseated Republican Tracy Pennycuick in what WHYY had called one of the Senate's most competitive seats14. That win helped Democrats flip the Pennsylvania state Senate for the first time in 48 years15. WHYY also reported that, according to unofficial results, all Republicans were voted out of the legislature in Chester, Delaware and Montgomery counties, the first time that has happened.16

County Commissioner Jamila Winder, a Democrat, credited "a really strong slate of candidates coupled with the national environment"16. She recalled that when she moved to East Norriton Township, the area's seats were mostly held by Republicans, and said the tide had turned over four decades.16 The Montgomery County Republican Committee did not immediately respond to WHYY's request for comment.16

Makhija, for his part, said the turnout numbers are a warning for Democrats as the party takes power7.
