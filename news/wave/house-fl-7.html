Seminole swings, Volusia holds: Florida's 7th sits near a recount line

Republican Ryan Elijah, the former TV anchor who ousted Rep. Cory Mills in August, leads Democrat Bale Dalton by 1,631 votes with 99% counted. AP has not called the race, and the margin sits just outside Florida's recount threshold.

Republican Ryan Elijah ended the week 1,631 votes ahead of Democrat Bale Dalton in Florida's 7th Congressional District. The count stands at 161,300 to 159,669, with 99% of the expected vote counted. Christopher Dennison, the Libertarian candidate1, drew 3,485 votes, more than twice the gap between the two leaders. The Associated Press has not called the race.

The race

Elijah got to November by beating his own party's incumbent. The Washington Post reported that Rep. Cory Mills went into the primary facing allegations of sexual misconduct and domestic abuse2 as well as campaign finance violations. Axios reported that a House Ethics probe was looking into allegations of domestic abuse, stolen valor and financial misconduct, all of which Mills denies3. President Donald Trump had endorsed Mills in February but left him out of the Florida endorsements he posted in the last week before the primary4, and Gov. Ron DeSantis said he did not support Mills3. Elijah is a former news anchor and NFL sideline reporter who hosted Fox 35's weekly "Orlando Matters."4

Dalton is a retired Navy captain and former NASA chief of staff who campaigned on affordability, health care and national security5. The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee put him in its Red to Blue program before any other Florida candidate6. Central Florida Public Media reported that he out-raised Elijah, taking in $2.3 million, according to FEC reports7. Florida Politics reported in September that Elijah had not agreed to a debate, including one proposed by WESH1.

The ads were about money and trust. Elijah's "Bad Bet" spot said Dalton's financial disclosure showed investments in companies controlled by China.8 Dalton's ad said Elijah didn't take people's economic struggles seriously, backed Trump's tariffs and had disclosed that he planned to trade stocks in Congress.8 Elijah replied that he does not intend to trade stocks.8

Outside Democratic money came and went. Citing AdImpact, Florida Politics reported that House Majority PAC canceled its ad reservations9 and then put $507,000 back into the district10. On election night, Elijah pointed to roughly $2 million that Democrats spent in the final week and a half7. NBC Miami reported that across Florida's targeted seats, Democrats seized on disenchantment with Trump amid high gas and grocery prices11.

The district

The district covers all of Seminole County and southern Volusia County12, and this year the two parts ended up on opposite sides. In 2024 Seminole voted R+3.5 for president. This year it went D+6.7 in the statewide vote on The Watch's wave map, a 10-point move. That is close to the state's own shift of 10.2 points in the Senate race and 11.8 in the governor's race, both of which Republicans Ashley Moody and Byron Donalds still won.

Spectrum News 13 reported that Seminole voted Democratic in all but one race, including governor and Senate13. Central Florida Public Media reported that Dalton carried the county7, and WKMG said Dalton beat many predictions with help from the southern part of the district, including Sanford.14

Volusia moved less. The county as a whole went from R+21.8 to R+15.6, a 6-point move, and that figure includes northern Volusia, which is outside the district. Elijah had counted on Volusia: "Seminole carried us through the primary, and I said it's going to be Volusia that carries us through the general,"7 he said. He spent Election Day knocking on Volusia doors.7

The wave map also lists Orange County, home to Orlando, with this seat. Orange swung the most, from D+13.6 to D+28.5, a 15-point move. But local reporting describes the district as Seminole and southern Volusia only. Orange also cast 471,829 votes, which pulls the map's 12-point weighted move for the seat above what either Seminole or Volusia did.

None of these moves proves that voters changed their minds. Seminole cast 195,588 votes in the statewide race, and a county total cannot tell switched votes apart from changes in who turned out. The Seminole County Democratic Party said Democratic turnout rose compared with Republican turnout.13

What the result means

Mills won the seat in 2022 and again in 202415, and Republicans expected to keep it. In August, Rep. Mike Haridopolos called it a "safe Republican seat."3 No incumbent lost here on Tuesday; Mills had already lost in August.

The open question is a recount. State law requires a machine recount when the margin is 0.5 percentage points or less6, and the first unofficial returns, which decide whether one happens, are due Saturday, Nov. 716. On AP's count, half a percent of 324,454 votes is about 1,622. That puts Elijah's lead roughly nine votes outside the line. Earlier this week Florida Politics reported that late-arriving ballots, military and overseas ballots, and provisional ballots were still to be counted or reviewed by canvassing boards6.

The Republican Party of Florida has declared Elijah the winner.6 Dalton has said "it is incumbent on all of us not to pretend otherwise until all ballots are counted."6

Nationally, this is one of 22 House seats AP has not called. Democrats already have 252 called seats, so this one will not decide the majority.
