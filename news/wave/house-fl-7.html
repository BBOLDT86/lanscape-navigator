The House · Florida 7

Ex-anchor Ryan Elijah keeps Florida's 7th Republican by 1,631 votes

Republican Ryan Elijah ousted the scandal-hit Rep. Cory Mills in August, then beat Democrat Bale Dalton by half a point in Seminole and southern Volusia counties. Statewide, Florida's races were far closer than in 2024.

Ryan Elijah, a Republican, won Florida's open 7th Congressional District by 1,631 votes. He had 161,300 votes to 159,669 for Democrat Bale Dalton, or 49.7 percent to 49.2 percent, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. That is a margin of half a point out of 324,454 ballots. Christopher Dennison won 3,485 votes, about twice the gap between the top two. AP's results list him as an independent, but local outlets called him a Libertarian1.

The race went very close to a recount. Under Florida law, mandatory recounts are triggered if the second-place candidate is within 0.5% of the first-place finisher.2 Central Florida Public Media reported that Elijah's race was a mere 9 votes away from triggering a recount.3 Secretary of State Cord Byrd determined that there will be no recount4. WESH reported that the Associated Press called the race on Tuesday, Nov. 10, one week after Election Day.5 The results are not final yet: counties will continue processing outstanding ballots and completing their required canvassing procedures before the state certifies the results.6

The race

The seat was open because the incumbent lost his primary. Elijah, a former news anchor who worked for WOFL-TV in Orlando for 13 years7, beat Rep. Cory Mills by 13 points in August. NPR reported that the House Ethics Committee is investigating Mills over allegations of sexual misconduct and campaign finance violations.8 Mills denies wrongdoing. Trump did throw his support to Elijah after he had won the GOP nomination.9

Dalton was a much stronger opponent than Mills had faced before. He is a captain in the Navy Reserve who served as chief of staff at NASA under former Administrator Bill Nelson.7 The DCCC named Dalton to its Red to Blue program before any other Florida candidate this cycle1, giving him national party backing.

WKMG News 6 reported that for families across Seminole and Volusia counties, the cost of living has become the defining issue of the race.10 Asked how they would bring costs down, Ryan Elijah put energy at the top of his list.10 Bale Dalton pointed first to health care, tying rising costs directly to Congress10, citing the expired Affordable Care Act subsidies. Dalton also ran against corruption. Florida Politics reported that he proposed a ban on stock trading by members of Congress.

The ads were harsh on both sides. Dalton's ads said Elijah had signed a pledge saying he planned to trade stocks while in Congress; Elijah said he signed the form that way but has since said he will not trade stocks.11 Elijah in turn ran an ad tying Dalton to "communist China." The ad apparently referenc[ed] Dalton's investments in the state-owned Bank of China and a Chinese pharmaceutical enterprise as part of his retirement portfolio.9

Dalton had more money. According to WUSF, his campaign account raised triple what his opponent brought in, and spending to advantage Dalton was double spending for Elijah.12 In late October, the Cook Political Report moved the race from Likely to Lean Republican.13

The district

District 7 covers all of Seminole County and parts of southern Volusia County.5 It runs from the Orlando suburbs north and east toward the coast and includes Deltona, Port Orange, and Sanford.14 State GOP lawmakers left the district unchanged in the new congressional map they passed earlier this year.7

The Watch's data for this seat does not include county results. This piece therefore cannot say how far Seminole or Volusia moved from the 2024 Harris–Trump vote, or which county did more to close the gap.

The statewide results show how far Florida moved. Republican Ashley Moody won the Senate race over Democrat Angie Nixon by 2.9 points. Republican Byron Donalds won the governor's race over Democrat David Jolly by 1.3 points. Florida had voted for Trump by 13.1 points in 2024, so the two races moved toward Democrats by about 10 and 12 points. The data only counts votes. With about 8.3 million Senate ballots and 324,454 House ballots, it cannot show whether voters changed their minds or a different group of voters turned out.

What the result means

Republicans will hold the seat for a third consecutive term7. Mills won it in 2022 and 2024. The last Democrat to win the 7th District was Stephanie Murphy in 2020, under different district boundaries.15 The incumbent did lose this year, but he lost to his own party in August, not to Democrats in November.

Nationally, AP has called 254 House seats for Democrats and 167 for Republicans, with 14 still uncalled. Central Florida Public Media wrote that the closeness of the race resembles the widespread losses that Republicans endured nationwide on Tuesday.3 WUSF concluded that Dalton measurably benefited from that sentiment this year, even if he didn't win.12
