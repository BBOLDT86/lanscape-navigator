Bucks County, split evenly in 2024, gives Shapiro a 26-point win

Pennsylvania's most evenly divided big county moved nearly 26 points toward the Democrats in the governor's race. That was more than the state or its suburban neighbors moved. Turnout fell to three-quarters of 2024's, and Republicans lost their congressman.

Two years ago, Bucks County came about as close to a tie as a county of 400,000 voters can. Donald Trump beat Kamala Harris there by 0.1 point. On Nov. 3, Gov. Josh Shapiro, a Democrat, beat the Republican state treasurer, Stacy Garrity, by 25.7 points: 189,221 votes to 111,093, with 98.31 percent of the expected vote counted.

What Bucks did

That is a 26-point move toward the Democrats. Statewide, Shapiro won by 22.1 points, a 23.8-point move from Trump's 1.7-point win in 2024, and all 67 counties moved left. Bucks ranked 14th by the size of its move. It moved more than the other suburban counties around Philadelphia: Montgomery and Delaware each moved 22 to 23 points, and Chester moved 22. Bucks is still the least Democratic of the four, though. Montgomery and Delaware both went for Shapiro by about 46 points, and Chester by 36.5. The typical county nationally moved 14 points. Among big counties that were close to even in 2024, only Fort Bend, Texas, moved more, at 27 points. Palm Beach, Fla., moved about 10, and Riverside, Calif., 17.

Bucks has 647,8281 residents, according to the Census Bureau's 2025 estimate. Its median household income of $114,7642 is far above the national figure of $80,734, and ranked 2nd in median household income among counties in Pennsylvania3. The Inquirer describes Lower Bucks as a populous part of the county4, and most of it voted Democratic this year.

The county counted 303,780 votes, 76 percent of its 2024 total, or about 97,000 fewer. A county's vote count cannot tell voters who changed sides from voters who stayed home or came out, so the Bucks move mixes both. There are hints about who turned out. As of Oct. 31, the county had sent 103,560 mail ballots: 60,040 to Democrats and 30,616 to Republicans5. Republicans, meanwhile, still held a small lead in registration. In 2024, for the first time since 2007, there were more registered Republicans in Bucks County than Democrats6. But the Bucks County Courier Times reported this year that Democratic year-over-year registration changes have outpaced Republican changes for the first time since the 2020 election7.

Why it moved

Garrity's campaign was outmatched from the start. Spotlight PA reported she was hampered by lackluster fundraising, limited support from the GOP and Shapiro's name recognition, approval ratings and incumbency advantage8. City & State reported that Shapiro reported more than $63.7 million in total contributions, while Garrity raised $10.6 million9. The night before the election, Garrity held a rally in Bucks and predicted she would win, despite multiple statewide polls showing her with a double-digit disadvantage10. WHYY reported that Shapiro benefited from some GOP support and President Donald Trump's sagging favorability11.

The Bucks County Democratic chairman, state Sen. Steve Santarsiero, told WHYY that economic concerns were "probably the most important issue as to why we had such a good day yesterday."12 In an August poll by The Inquirer, The New York Times and Siena, only 39% of likely Pennsylvania voters approve of Trump's job performance13. Nationally, the AP Voter Poll found that about two-thirds of U.S. midterm voters say Trump's policies have worsened economic conditions14. In Warminster, Spotlight PA heard voters talk about immigration, healthcare cuts15 and prices. Data centers also came up. Garrity made opposing them a key pillar of her campaign9, and Eileen Hartnett Albillar, the Democrat who won a Bucks state Senate seat, said in an interview that concerns about them were one of the top three issues16.

What it means for Bucks

The biggest change was further down the ballot. County Commissioner Bob Harvie beat U.S. Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick. The Inquirer called it the first time that a Democrat will represent Bucks County in Congress since 201117. In past elections, voters across the political spectrum in Bucks County — one of the swingiest parts of Pennsylvania — routinely decided to keep the Republican around17. Hartnett Albillar beat incumbent Republican Sen. Frank Farry in the 6th Senate District18, and Mark Pinsley ousted Republican incumbent Sen. Jarrett Coleman to flip the 16th Senate District, which includes parts of Bucks County18. Those wins helped give Democrats control of the state Senate.

Local Democrats disagree on how lasting this is. Harvie told reporters on election night, "I don't think we're a purple county anymore. I think we're blue."12 Santarsiero was more careful: "it will be a blue county if Democrats continue to espouse and pass solutions that make a difference in people's lives."12 The results are still unofficial. About 2 percent of the expected vote had not been counted, and the county still has to certify.
