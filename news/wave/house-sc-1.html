Retired admiral Nancy Lacore flips Charleston's coastal House seat by a point

Nancy Lacore, a retired Navy admiral fired by Pete Hegseth, beat Republican Jenny Costa Honeycutt by 3,352 votes in the Charleston-area seat. Berkeley and Dorchester counties swung more than 20 points toward Democrats.

Democrat Nancy Lacore has taken South Carolina's 1st Congressional District from the Republicans by 3,352 votes. The district is a coastal seat built around Charleston. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, she had 162,103 votes, or 49.7 percent. Republican Jenny Costa Honeycutt had 158,751, or 48.6 percent, which leaves a 1.0-point margin out of 326,423 votes cast. Bill Reeside, the Libertarian candidate1, drew 5,351. The Associated Press called the race for Lacore at 6:51 p.m. Friday2, three days after the polls closed.

The race

The seat was open because Rep. Nancy Mace is leaving it after an unsuccessful run for governor3. Lacore, a retired vice admiral, moved to Mount Pleasant in 2025 after being fired as Chief of Navy Reserve by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth4, who fired her without explanation3. Honeycutt, a two-term Charleston County Council member, earned Donald Trump's endorsement after topping the June primary field. She stressed that she grew up and started a business on James Island and cast Lacore as a liberal transplant settling a grudge with Hegseth1.

The Post and Courier reported that Lacore's campaign focused on affordability and the war in Iran, and sought to tie Honeycutt to Trump, local developers and data centers1. Honeycutt argued that Lacore's support for a public health insurance option would drive up taxes, and she blamed inflation on government spending and debt5. Lacore called for repealing Trump's tariffs, some of which Honeycutt also opposed, and reversing GOP cuts to federal welfare programs5. She also broke with her party's progressive wing by opposing calls to abolish ICE6. A lawyer for Honeycutt demanded that Lacore's campaign pull an ad saying Honeycutt had voted for her legal clients on county council, calling the suggestion "categorically false"7.

By mid-October, Lacore had raised more than $4.8 million to Honeycutt's roughly $1.3 million8, the SC Daily Gazette reported. Outside groups spent almost $11.5 million in the general election, including seven-figure sums from each party's main House political action committee9. The Cook Political Report rated the race a toss-up8.

The district

The district runs along the lower South Carolina coast and includes all of Beaufort and Berkeley counties and most of Charleston County10. It also takes in parts of Dorchester, Colleton and Jasper. When Republican state lawmakers redrew the lines in 2022 to make the seat safely red again, they moved all of Berkeley County into it.11

The wave map measures each whole county's result in the statewide top-of-ticket race, not the House race. Together the six counties cast 445,703 votes, well above the district's total. On that measure they moved about 20 points toward Democrats since 2024:

• Berkeley moved the most, 26 points, from R+16.3 to D+9.4.
• Dorchester moved 24 points, from R+14.6 to D+9.6.
• Colleton moved 22 points, from R+18.2 to D+4.3.
• Charleston, already Democratic, went from D+5.7 to D+26.1.
• Jasper and Beaufort moved 12 points each. Jasper tipped to D+2.0, while Beaufort stayed Republican at R+2.7.

The House vote followed the same pattern. The Post and Courier reported that Lacore's win ran through Berkeley and Dorchester counties, typically Republican strongholds11. Berkeley's Republican chairman said party members watched the county turn blue in absolute shock11. In the south, Honeycutt won the majority of votes in Beaufort and Jasper counties12, WTOC reported.

County counts can't tell voters who changed their minds apart from changes in who turned out, and turnout was unusually high. Statewide it was the highest in a non-presidential election since 199413. Among the large counties, Charleston had the highest turnout, and its straight-ticket voters strongly favored Democrats13, the Post and Courier found. Charleston's 175,121 votes in the statewide race were the largest share of the district's vote.

What the result means

Democrats narrowly won the seat in 2018, Mace flipped it back two years later, and she was easily reelected twice after the 2022 redraw.1 This time the seat flipped with no incumbent on the ballot. Statewide, Democrat Annie Andrews unseated Republican Sen. Darline Graham14 by 4.5 points, a 22.4-point move from Trump's 2024 margin in the state. Republican Alan Wilson won the governor's race by 4.5 points. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still open.

The margin sits close to South Carolina's recount line. State law requires a recount when the margin is 1% or less of the total votes cast, unless the losing candidate waives it in writing.15 On AP's count, Lacore's lead is just over 1 percent. Lacore said Honeycutt's campaign sent her an early morning email conceding the race4 on Nov. 4. Honeycutt's campaign did not confirm a concession at the time, telling the SC Daily Gazette it was still seeking more information from election officials16. The count ran late on election night after a technical issue in Dorchester County forced election workers to rescan more than 5,000 ballots1. As of Saturday morning, about 1 percent of the expected vote was still uncounted in AP's tally, including part of Beaufort's. The results are unofficial.
