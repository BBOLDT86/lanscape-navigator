Steve Toth holds Texas' 2nd for Republicans, but by just two points

The state lawmaker who ousted Dan Crenshaw in March beat Democrat Shaun Finnie by about 5,600 votes north of Houston, as Harris and Montgomery counties swung more than 20 points toward Democrats at the top of the ballot.

Steve Toth won Texas' 2nd Congressional District on Nov. 3. He kept the north Houston suburban seat for Republicans, but by a much thinner margin than forecasters expected. With 99% of the expected vote counted, Toth had 144,748 votes to 139,167 for Democrat Shaun Finnie. That is a lead of 5,581 votes, or 2.0 points, out of 283,915 cast. The Associated Press called the race for Toth.1 Before the election, the Cook Political Report and Inside Elections both rated the seat Solid Republican.2

A primary fight, then a narrow November

The race seemed to be settled in March, when Toth beat four-term Rep. Dan Crenshaw in the Republican primary. Houston Public Media reported that Toth, one of the most conservative members of the Texas House, framed his challenge of Crenshaw as a referendum on the ideological future of the Republican Party.3 Crenshaw had clashed with Republican allies over Ukraine aid and over certifying the 2020 election results, and he was the only Texas Republican incumbent in the House running without Trump's endorsement.3 Sen. Ted Cruz endorsed Toth and appeared in a super PAC ad backing him.4 Toth won even though Crenshaw raised $1.3 million more3, and even though the super PAC Fight to Win America spent more than $1.7 million backing Crenshaw and opposing Toth5. CBS News said at the time that the winner of the GOP primary was all but guaranteed victory in November.6

Finnie is an oil and gas investment banker in The Woodlands7. He said concern over the growing federal debt was the main reason he ran.8 The Houston Chronicle's editorial board endorsed him. It described a $2 million-plus campaign chest that's largely self-funded9 and a pitch aimed at voters tired of partisan fighting. Toth's own platform called for permanently securing the southern border through legislation and for prioritizing American graduates and workers over H1-B foreign laborers.7

Flooding came up throughout the year. During the primary, Crenshaw attacked Toth's legislative record, including his votes on property tax reform and flood mitigation funding for Kingwood.5 After the election, the Chronicle's board noted that Toth voted against a bill to facilitate dredging of the San Jacinto River, which frequently floods his district10. The board argued that his narrow win showed weakness as a candidate. That is the paper's opinion, not a finding.

Across the state, Houston Public Media said Latino and rural voters, the Iran war and tariffs likely played a huge role11. Houston-area voters who spoke to KPRC 2 described concerns about affordability, the war in Iran, immigration enforcement and the direction of government.12

Two counties, two big moves

The district covers portions of New Caney, Porter, Conroe, The Woodlands, Spring, Cy-Fair and the Lake Houston area.7 CNN reported that the district changed slightly in Republican-led mid-decade redistricting, and much of Toth's state House seat was drawn into it.13

The district takes in parts of two counties, and on The Watch's wave map both moved sharply toward Democrats in the Senate and governor vote. Harris County went from D+5.5 in the 2024 Harris–Trump race to D+33.0, a move of 27 points. That is slightly more than the statewide Senate move of 25.8 points. Montgomery County went from R+45.5 to R+22.2, a 23-point move, although Woodlands Online wrote that the county maintained its historical red-centric voting tradition.14

Both counties are only partly inside the district, so their totals say more about the region than about this seat. Harris County cast 1,382,875 votes, nearly five times the district's entire House vote, which means its move mostly reflects Houston and other suburbs. Montgomery County cast 258,438.

County results also cannot show whether voters changed their minds or whether different people turned out. The Chronicle reported that Harris County broke its midterm voting record15. Its turnout analysis found that turnout fell in Montgomery County16 compared with 2018. Republican strategist Brendan Steinhauser told Houston Public Media that Republicans "were not as motivated and probably didn't show up in the numbers that they needed"11. Data analyst Steven David pointed to a combination of independents supporting Democrats, people crossing party lines and voters returning after sitting out previous elections.12

What the result means

Crenshaw was first elected in 201817. He lost his seat in the March primary, so no incumbent lost here in November, and the seat stays Republican. The count was close for a time. On election night, the Chronicle's live coverage reported that Toth was losing to Finnie in unofficial returns18. Its later coverage found that other Houston-area Republicans barely survived19 as well.

The narrow result came in a year when Democrats won Texas outright. James Talarico won the Senate race by 12.1 points and Gina Hinojosa won the governor's race by 7.1 points. Nationally, Democrats have been called the winners of 252 House seats, well past the 218 needed for a majority, so Toth will start out in the minority. About 1% of the expected vote is still uncounted, and all results are unofficial until canvassed.20
