Nurse Mitchell Berman ends Republicans' three-decade hold on Wisconsin's 1st District

Outspent and new to politics, the emergency room nurse beat four-term Republican Bryan Steil by 3.9 points. In the governor's race, all five counties in Paul Ryan's old district moved toward Democrats.

Mitchell Berman, an emergency room nurse running for office for the first time, has unseated Republican Rep. Bryan Steil in Wisconsin's 1st Congressional District. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Berman has 158,839 votes to Steil's 146,885, a margin of 3.9 points. The Associated Press called the race at 12:48 a.m. Eastern time. Steil conceded at around 11:30 p.m.1 Central time, speaking to a crowd of a few dozen people at Route 20 in Sturtevant2. The result ends a Republican winning streak for the 1st District seat that began with the 1994 election.3

The race

Berman lives in Racine County. In August he won a four-way Democratic primary4 against Peter Burgelis, Lorenzo Santos and Miguel Aranda. TMJ4 reported that he centered his campaign on his opposition to Medicaid cuts, lowering everyday costs, strengthening Social Security, raising the minimum wage4 and limits on stock trading by members of Congress. His main target was Steil's vote for the 2025 tax and spending bill. The Wisconsin Examiner reported that Berman accused Steil of abandoning commitments to protect Medicaid3. Steil defended the legislation as a measure to lower taxes, strengthen border security and tighten Medicaid eligibility requirements.3

The two met once, on Janesville radio station WCLO. Both Berman and Steil agreed that the cost of living was a top issue.1 Berman said he'd work to repeal Trump administration tariffs, while Steil said he'd work to deliver tax relief for residents.1 Steil said Wisconsin's Medicaid funding had grown by $1 billion. Berman answered that this was "putting a band-aid on a bullet hole"5. Berman also went after data centers. That is a local issue in a district where Microsoft has been expanding a separate AI data center6 in the village of Mount Pleasant. WisPolitics reported that one of his ads ends with him shooting a clay pigeon labelled "data center" out of the sky7.

Steil had the money. Citing October federal filings, Wisconsin Watch reported that Steil spent nearly $6.4 million in the race compared to Berman's more than $1.6 million8, and that outside groups spent more than $17.9 million on the race8. Counts of the outside money differ. WisPolitics found $7.7 million into the race behind Berman9 and $12.9 million on Steil's side. National Democrats put Berman on their "Red to Blue" list of target races in September.

The district

The 1st stretches from Lake Michigan to South Central Wisconsin3. It includes Kenosha and Racine counties, the majority of Walworth County and parts of Rock and Milwaukee counties10, among them the suburbs of Franklin and Oak Creek. WPR noted that at the last redistricting the district added some new Democratic areas around Beloit and Janesville, while losing a portion of Waukesha County, which typically votes Republican.10

The Watch's wave map uses whole-county results from the governor's race, which Democrat David Crowley won statewide by 8.0 points, an 8.9-point move from 2024. On that map, all five counties moved toward Democrats. Kenosha County went from R+6.2 to D+5.7, a 12-point move. Racine County moved 8 points, from R+6.2 to D+2.2. It's the first time that Racine County has voted in a Democratic governor since 2002.11 Rock County, home to Janesville, widened its Democratic margin from D+7.3 to D+17.6. Walworth County, where Berman held his victory party in Elkhorn, stayed Republican, but its margin narrowed from R+22.2 to R+14.2. Milwaukee County moved the most, about 14 points. Its 356,762 votes pull the district's weighted move up to 11.9, even though most of them were cast outside the 1st.

These are net moves, not proof that anyone changed their mind. The 305,724 House votes in the district, or the 82,578 governor's votes in Racine County, show who turned out in a midterm. The counts cannot tell voters who switched parties apart from voters who stayed home or turned out this time.

What it means

Steil grew up in Janesville and worked as an attorney in the manufacturing sector for more than a decade before he was first elected to Congress in 2018.12 He won reelection in 2020, 2022 and 202412, and he chairs the House Administration Committee. WisPolitics noted that he has regularly outrun the top of the ticket, was initially expected to win reelection handily.9 The Cook Political Report rated the seat "likely Republican" for most of the current term13 before moving it to "lean Republican." Peter Barca was the last Democrat to hold the seat, from 1993 to 1995, and he lost a comeback attempt against Steil in 2024.14

Berman won by less than Crowley did statewide. Democrats also took Wisconsin's 3rd District that night, where Democrat Rebecca Cooke beat incumbent Republican Derrick Van Orden15. Nationally, 252 seats had been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. In the 1st, AP's count stood at 99 percent of the expected vote on Saturday morning, and the race has been called. "We gave it our all, but we came up short," Steil said.1
