Josh Green's record win pushes every Hawaii county 20-plus points left

Gov. Josh Green won a second term by 45 points, 22 points better than Kamala Harris did in Hawaii in 2024. Kauai moved the most. A quarter fewer ballots were cast, so lower turnout is part of the story.

Gov. Josh Green, a Democrat, won a second term on Nov. 3 by 45.3 points. He took 278,652 votes to 104,926 for Republican Gary Cordery, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris carried Hawaii by 23.1 points two years ago. Green's 22.2-point improvement on her margin is larger than the national figure on The Watch's map, where the vote-weighted move was about 14 points.

All four of Hawaii's counties moved toward the Democrats, and by similar amounts. Maui and the Big Island moved about 21 points and Kauai about 29. None changed hands. Harris had carried all four, and Green won each by more than 40 points. Honolulu Civil Beat called it the largest margin of victory in a general election gubernatorial race since statehood1. Local papers working from earlier printouts reported bigger gaps than ours: the Star-Advertiser put it at a record-setting 50 percentage points2. On AP's count the margin is 45.3.

A race that was never close

Green previously served as lieutenant governor and as a state lawmaker and also has worked as a physician serving rural areas in Hawaii3, the Associated Press noted. Cordery founded a construction company and co-founded a conservative advocacy group called the Aloha Freedom Coalition3. During the pandemic, Civil Beat reported, that group staged protests outside Green's condominium in downtown Honolulu and elsewhere4.

The Republican asked again and again for a debate. Civil Beat wrote that Green essentially ignored Cordery this year. He rebuffed repeated calls to debate his GOP challenger.1 Cordery raised only $430,800 for the campaign, which was not nearly enough to counter a flood of advertising on behalf of the Democrats.1 Hawaii Public Radio reported that both Green and Cordery listed the cost of living as their top issue. Cordery said he would eliminate the general excise tax on food and medicine.5 Green campaigned on continuing efforts to address Hawaii's high cost of living, housing shortage, homelessness and rising energy costs.6

The year's main political trouble was on the Democratic side, and it did not reach Green's own name on the ballot. In April, Lt. Gov. Sylvia Luke dropped her reelection bid; days later her lawyer acknowledged that she is a target in a bribery investigation7. In July an Oahu grand jury indicted her for criminal conspiracy to commit bribery, bribery and falsifying candidate committee reports8. Kauai Mayor Derek Kawakami took her place as Green's running mate. Going into the general election, Green had a 64% approval rating among registered Hawaii voters, according to a Morning Consult ranking2.

Who moved: the counties are too few to say much

The charts beside this section don't have much to sort. All four counties voted for Harris by 10 to 30 points in 2024. All four cast between 65 and 80 percent as many votes as they did then. The two smaller counties, Kauai and Maui, cast 19 percent of the vote and moved 23.2 points, weighted by votes. Honolulu and Hawaii County, with 81 percent, moved 21.9. Kauai accounts for that whole gap; Maui actually moved the least. The data block includes no Census figures for Hawaii. And counties are not voters: four totals can't show which people changed their minds.

There is one clear pattern: the counties ended up closer together. Kauai was the least Democratic county in 2024, at Harris +19.5. It moved the most and finished at D+48.3, just behind Hawaii County's D+48.9.

Statewide, 383,578 votes were cast for governor, against 516,701 for president in 2024, or 74 percent. With a quarter of the electorate missing, the county counts cannot separate voters who switched sides from voters who stayed home. The University of Hawaii's Colin Moore told the Star-Advertiser that "a lot of Republicans came out to support President Trump"9 two years ago and that many of them stayed home this time. House Minority Leader Lauren Matsumoto blamed "a combination of the national scene and our extremely low, abysmal voter turnout."10

The county turnout figures don't settle the question. Honolulu cast 73 percent of its 2024 vote and Hawaii County 74 percent, while Maui held 78 percent and Kauai 79. The county that kept the most of its 2024 turnout, Kauai, also moved the most. Maui held up almost as well and moved the least. If the swing were only about who stayed home, you might expect the steepest drops to line up with the biggest moves. They don't.

Four islands, four local stories

Honolulu, the city and county that covers Oahu, cast 248,995 votes, about two-thirds of the state's total. It moved 22.2 points, exactly the statewide figure, from Harris +21.6 to Green +43.9. Hawaii County, where Green won a Big Island House seat11 in 2004, was the most Democratic county both years.

Kauai is Kawakami's home island, and it had a busy local ballot. Council member Mel Rapozo was elected mayor after defeating former Mayor Bernard Carvalho Jr.12 The county count can't show whether the hometown running mate or the mayor's race brought out voters who also backed Green.

On Maui, early ballot returns were the lowest turnout in the state13 as of Oct. 29, KHON2 reported. Hawaii News Now described the mayor's race as a pivotal referendum on the county's recovery efforts14 after the 2023 Lahaina fire, and on Mayor Richard Bissen's plan to phase out short-term vacation rentals. Bissen won. Maui's move to D+46.4 was the smallest in the state, though still about 21 points.

Further down the ballot, Republicans lost ground too. Democrats took three House seats from Republicans and a Green Party candidate won a fourth, losses the Star-Advertiser linked to a national "blue tsunami".15 Moore told Hawaii Public Radio that, nationally, "It was an election around affordability, and really it was around fuel prices."16
