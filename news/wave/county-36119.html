The wave · the biggest counties · Westchester County, New York

Westchester moved toward Hochul, but less than New York did

Kathy Hochul carried the county by nearly 32 points, about five more than Kamala Harris did in 2024. A countywide scanner failure on Election Day left about a tenth of the expected vote still uncounted days later.

What Westchester did

In the count so far, Westchester County gave Gov. Kathy Hochul a 31.7-point margin over Bruce Blakeman, the Republican Nassau County executive. She had 220,263 votes and he had 114,080. In 2024, the county voted for Kamala Harris over Donald Trump by 26.3 points, so this year's result is about 5 points more Democratic.

Statewide, Hochul won by 19.6 points. Harris carried New York by 12.7, so the state moved 7 points. Westchester moved less than that and ranks 14th of New York's 62 counties by the size of its move. Most of the state's big shifts came from New York City and Long Island. Queens moved 20 points and Brooklyn 15, while Manhattan barely changed. Suffolk moved 11 points and narrowly flipped to Hochul. Nassau moved 4. Outside New York, big suburban counties that voted for Harris by similar margins moved much further. Montgomery County, Pennsylvania, moved 23 points and Wake County, North Carolina, 22. Nationally, the typical county moved 14.

Westchester is a large, mixed suburb. The Census Bureau's 2025 estimate1 puts its population at about 1.02 million. About 29 percent of residents are Hispanic, 18 percent are Black and 26 percent were born abroad1, and its median household income is about $119,000, compared with about $81,000 nationally1. Yonkers is its largest city and the third-most populous city in New York.2

The count is not final. About 90 percent of the expected vote has been counted, or 334,770 ballots. That is 74 percent of the county's 2024 vote. A midterm electorate is smaller and different from a presidential one. Because of that, the 5-point move mixes changes in who turned out with voters who switched sides, and county totals can't separate the two.

Why, according to the reporting

The biggest local story was the count itself. On Election Day, voting machines would not take ballots at polling places across the county. NYS Focus reported that, according to the State Board of Elections, the machines used on Election Day were missing a necessary update made after they had been tested3. Voters put their ballots into secure orange emergency bags instead.3 News 12 reported that the county Board of Elections said no voters were turned away or told to come back later4, and that bipartisan staff would keep scanning the emergency ballots every day until all of them were counted4. Patch reported that officials apologized for the "frustration, confusion and inconvenience"5. ABC7 reported that both Hochul and Blakeman urged Westchester voters to stay at their polling places and vote6.

Interest was high before Election Day. Tajian Nelson, the county's Democratic elections commissioner, told News 12 that early voting turnout had already passed 2022's7.

CBS News reported that both campaigns centered on affordability8. Polls of the downstate suburbs found a mix of concerns. In an October Quinnipiac poll, reported by Nexstar's Albany bureau, suburban voters in Nassau, Rockland, Suffolk and Westchester ranked crime and immigration tied at 17% behind cost of living at 20% and the economy at 19%9. Across the state, Hochul led 71% to 24% among voters who said cost of living was their top issue9. News 12 reported that Blakeman built his campaign around public safety and quality of life and highlighted President Trump's support10. In Siena's final poll, 35% of voters said neither candidate would make life more affordable11. After the election, a Republican consultant told the Times Union that Blakeman "hammered home the MAGA talking points at the expense of other things that could have excited the suburbs"12.

The reporting reviewed for this piece did not include a voter survey of Westchester alone. Who moved inside the county is not yet known.

What it means

The county's biggest local race was in the House. Democrat Cait Conley defeated Rep. Mike Lawler, a two-term Republican, in the Hudson Valley's 17th District13. CBS News noted that Westchester accounts for about 40% of the district's vote8. Lawler conceded on election night, saying that "When all of the votes are counted tomorrow in Westchester it's going to be pretty clear we're going to be short"14.

Voter registration is shifting too. Nexstar reported that unaffiliated voters now outnumber Republicans in Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester, led by growth in Westchester15, according to NYPIRG.

The scanner failure will also be a local issue. CBS New York reported that County Executive Ken Jenkins said the problem will be looked into and there will be accountability16. NYS Focus found that Hochul improved on her 2022 result in every county, though there aren't enough Westchester votes counted yet to confirm17 it there. The county's final margin will not be known until the emergency ballots are counted and the results are certified.
