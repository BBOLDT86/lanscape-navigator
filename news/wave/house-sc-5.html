Wes Climer holds South Carolina's 5th as its counties swing left

The Rock Hill Republican beat Democrat Mallory Dittmer by 5.7 points to succeed Ralph Norman, even as all ten of the district's counties moved toward Democrats on The Watch's wave map and two of them flipped.

Wes Climer, a Republican state senator from Rock Hill, will succeed Ralph Norman in South Carolina's 5th Congressional District. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Climer had 151,497 votes to 135,203 for Democrat Mallory Dittmer. That is a 5.7-point margin, about 16,300 votes, out of 286,858 cast, including 158 write-ins. The Associated Press called the race at 10:08 p.m. on election night.

Norman elected not to run for reelection in the House but instead ran unsuccessfully for Governor and U.S. Senate.1 On the same night Climer won, Democrat Annie Andrews defeated GOP Sen. Darline Graham in South Carolina's Senate race2, by 4.5 points. Graham was appointed to the seat after the death of her brother, longtime Republican Sen. Lindsey Graham, in July.2 The state elected Republican Alan Wilson governor by the same 4.5 points. Nationally, 252 House seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled, so Climer will join a Republican minority.

A safe seat in a hard year

Climer aligned his bid with President Donald Trump's agenda, promising to be an ally in Congress3. He listed his priorities as cutting the federal budget, sealing the Southern border, overhauling the Department of Justice, and managing South Carolina's growth.3 Norman backed Climer for the 5th District seat4. Climer resigned from the state Senate effective Election Day, as election forecasters rated the contest as "safe" for the GOP.4

Dittmer was a first-time candidate from Fort Mill5. She comes from the corporate world, where she worked in branding. She says her campaign is about lowering costs for families, including childcare.6 WRHI reported she was an outspoken opponent of the proposed mid-decade Congressional redistricting7. In October she backed the Fort Mill School District in York County's fight over a solar panel plant, after two toxic chemical leaks from the plant that shut down nearby Flint Hill Elementary for two days.8

The money was lopsided. Climer raised nearly $1.1 million, about five times the $215,607 raised by5 Dittmer, according to FEC reports. Here Rock Hill reported that about 30% of Climer's haul came from political action committees and other candidate committees, while Dittmer has taken $1,100 from such groups.9

The statewide mood worked against Republicans. NPR reported that Trump's approval ratings hit record lows in the weeks before the midterms, driven largely by opposition to the war in Iran, the president's tariff policies and high energy prices.2 Many voters in this district split their tickets. WSOC reported that the effect showed most clearly in York County, where voters elected both Andrews and Republican gubernatorial candidate Alan Wilson.10 Eric Heberlig, a political scientist at UNC Charlotte, said "Many voters who voted for Ralph Norman, for example, in the primary didn't like the installation of Darline Graham by the president."10

Ten counties, all moving one way

The district stretches from the state's Charlotte suburbs south into Sumter County11. It includes all of Cherokee, Chester, Fairfield, Kershaw, Lancaster, Lee, Union, and York counties and parts of Spartanburg and Sumter counties12.

The Watch's wave map compares each county's 2026 statewide vote with the 2024 presidential result. On that map, the district's counties moved a weighted 24 points toward Democrats, a little more than the state's 22-point move in the Senate race. Every county moved: the smallest shift was 15 points in Fairfield and the largest 29 in Spartanburg. York, the largest county, went from R+19.1 to D+3.5, a 23-point move on 116,035 votes. Chester went from R+17.2 to D+6.2. Kershaw nearly flipped, ending at R+1.1 after a 27-point move, and Lancaster went from R+24.8 to R+5.3. Cherokee stayed solidly Republican at R+28.8. Sumter, Fairfield and Lee were already Democratic, and their margins grew to between 27 and 37 points.

The Post and Courier reported that York County flipped for Democrat Annie Andrews and no other statewide candidate13, and that more of Rock Hill and Fort Mill voted for Andrews in 2026 than voted for Harris in 2024.13

These figures need two cautions. First, they are countywide results from a statewide race, not the House race. The ten counties cast about 396,000 votes in that race, compared with 286,858 for the House seat. Part of that gap is the areas of Spartanburg and Sumter that lie outside the district. The figures also cannot show where Climer ran ahead of or behind the top of the ticket. Second, a move is not persuasion: a county count cannot tell voters who changed their minds from voters who turned out or stayed home. Statewide turnout was the highest turnout in a non-presidential election since 199414. A Post and Courier analysis found both effects: Democrats nearly matched Republicans in the number of "straight ticket" votes cast15, while some Republican voters appeared to split their tickets.

What the result means

For most of its history this was Democratic ground. Spratt's district had been in Democratic hands for more than 100 years until Republican Mick Mulvaney defeated Spratt in a 2010 race for the seat.16 After Mulvaney left for the Trump administration, a special election was held in 2017, in which Republican Ralph Norman defeated Democrat Archie Parnell.17

The seat was open, so no incumbent lost. Forecasters had rated it safe for Republicans, and Climer won it by 5.7 points. The count is at 99 percent, and the race has been called. In York County, results remain unofficial until certified by the York County Board of Voter Registration and Elections after write-in votes and provisional ballots are reviewed.18 Elsewhere in the state, Democrats are also celebrating a flip in the coastal 1st Congressional District and a shockingly close finish in the 2nd District19. Dittmer conceded and, in keeping with the bipartisan spirit of cooperation that was the cornerstone of20 her campaign, offered to help Climer serve the district.
