Arapahoe County, Colorado, swings 16 points toward Hickenlooper on smaller turnout

The suburban county that casts most of Aurora's votes gave John Hickenlooper a 36-point margin. That is 16 points better for Democrats than Kamala Harris did in 2024, with about a fifth fewer votes counted so far.

Arapahoe County takes in Denver's southern and eastern suburbs and the farm country beyond them. Most of it sits in the 6th Congressional District, which covers Centennial, Aurora and Littleton1 and since the last redistricting has been a purely inner suburban district anchored in western Arapahoe County2. The county's eastern end votes in the 4th District, the seat Lauren Boebert holds3. Aurora's voters make up most of the county's electorate. Of the more than 174,000 ballots4 the county had received by the day before the election, the Sentinel reported, most came from Aurora.

What Arapahoe did

Sen. John Hickenlooper, a Democrat, got 170,691 votes in Arapahoe County. His Republican challenger, state Sen. Mark Baisley, got 76,364. That is a margin of D+36.3, with 95.77% of the expected vote counted. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 20.1 points, so the county moved 16 points toward the Democrats. About 5% of the Senate vote went to other candidates, among them self-funded independent challenger Forward Party candidate Bob Chew5.

Statewide, Hickenlooper won by 25.3 points. Colorado as a whole moved 14 points from its 2024 presidential margin of D+11.0. All 64 counties moved left, and Arapahoe's move ranks eighth. Among the state's seven biggest counties, only Adams moved further (23 points). Denver and El Paso each moved 14, Jefferson and Larimer 13, and Douglas 11. Large counties elsewhere that started near Arapahoe's 2024 margin went in the same direction by different amounts. Allegheny and Montgomery in Pennsylvania and Dallas in Texas each moved about 23 points, Broward in Florida 15 and Sacramento 10.

Much of the move comes from who voted. Arapahoe has counted 259,598 votes, 80% of its 2024 total of 325,451. That is about 66,000 fewer. Even so, the Democratic lead grew from about 65,000 votes in 2024 to about 94,000. The county count cannot show how much of that came from Trump voters staying home, how much from Democrats turning out in larger shares and how much from voters switching sides. The last few percent of the vote is still being counted.

Why it moved

The race was lopsided from the start. The Colorado Sun called it a race that was never really competitive6. Hickenlooper is a former two-term governor and two-term Denver mayor7. Colorado Newsline described Baisley as a hardline conservative voice in the state Legislature since 20198 and reported that he made little effort to distance himself from Trump8 at their final debate. Hickenlooper called him a "rubber stamp" for Trump's agenda9. Colorado Politics reported that the Republican nominee for governor, Victor Marx, failed to coalesce the party behind him after squeaking out a primary win with 40% of the vote10. Meanwhile, Democratic candidates traveled the state together on a bus tour.

On issues, Colorado Public Radio cited a bipartisan Colorado Polling Institute survey. It found voters across the spectrum cited affordability as the top issue11, followed by housing, taxes and immigration. Immigration is a local issue in this county. CPR noted that Aurora is home to the GEO Group's U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) detention center12, which has drawn attention over detainee health and a tuberculosis outbreak. The Sentinel reported that Rep. Jason Crow, a Democrat, called for more oversight of ICE detention facilities13 during his campaign. Statewide, voters rejected a ballot measure that would have amended the state constitution to force police and prosecutors to work more closely with federal immigration agents14.

No county-level voter poll has been published. The turnout data that is available points to an electorate that leaned Democratic. In the secretary of state's tally two days before the election, 49.4% of registered Democrats15 had returned ballots, the highest rate of any group. Unaffiliated voters account for 43.1% of ballots that have been returned.15 Arapahoe election officials reported that about 40% of registered voters have already returned ballots as of Monday4.

What it means for the county

Democrats won down the ballot too. The Sentinel reported that Arapahoe County races are so far all going to incumbent Democrats, without exception13, including the reelection of county commissioners Jessica Campbell and Leslie Summey. Crow easily defeated his Republican challenger, former investment adviser Jason Clark of Aurora, to secure a fifth term16. He first won the seat in 2018 by beating Mike Coffman, current Aurora Mayor13.

Voters did not back every proposal Democrats might favor, though. In Aurora, all four Aurora ballot questions appear to be failing by solid margins13, including three sales-tax increases for city infrastructure. In the 4th District, the eastern part of the county is part of a race still being counted, with Colorado Politics reporting that Eileen Laubacher hangs on to slim lead over Lauren Boebert17.

This was a midterm, with fewer voters than a presidential year, and the count is not final. The numbers show where Arapahoe stood on Nov. 3. They do not show whether the Democrats' wider margin came from voters changing their minds or from who turned out.
