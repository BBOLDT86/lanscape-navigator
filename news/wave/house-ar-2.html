French Hill survives the closest race of his career in central Arkansas

Republican French Hill beat Democrat Chris Jones by 4,405 of the 250,469 votes counted, a margin of 1.8 points. Every county in the district moved toward Democrats in this year's statewide races.

A campaign about prices, and about Trump

French Hill is a banker who chairs the House Financial Services Committee1, and he has won a seventh term with the smallest margin of his career. AP's count gives him 127,437 votes to 123,032 for Chris Jones, a lead of 4,405 votes, or 1.8 points, with 99% of the expected vote counted.

Jones is both a physicist and ordained minister, and was the Democratic nominee for governor in 2022.2 The Arkansas Democrat-Gazette reported that his campaign stressed "affordability and accountability for all"3 and blamed Hill for higher prices, arguing that the congressman has refused to confront Trump about rising costs from the Iran war and the White House's tariff policies.3 In their October debate in Conway, Jones said the costs of healthcare, groceries and gasoline have been voters' primary concerns1. Hill defended the Iran policy and said he would instead support waiving the federal diesel fuel tax.1 At times Hill put distance between himself and the president, including opposing the president's plan to allow more beef imports into the U.S.1, and he pointed to his committee's recent review of problems within the Little Rock Housing Authority.3 If Jones had won, he would have been Arkansas' first Black member of Congress.4

Jones slightly outraised5 Hill in the July-to-September quarter, but Hill held the overall money advantage. Fairshake, a pro-cryptocurrency super PAC, spent $1 million on advertisements supporting Hill. The Committee for American Leadership, another super PAC, spent nearly $2.9 million against Jones' campaign, contending the Democrat was "too radical for Arkansas"6. On the other side, Ready for the Fight, a political committee affiliated with Democratic Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker, dropped $2 million on media opposing the congressman.3 In August, a poll by Hendrix College and Talk Business & Politics found Jones with a slim lead over Hill7, within the poll's margin of error.

Little Rock and the counties around it

The 2nd District takes in central Arkansas, including most of Little Rock.8 State legislators drew a new congressional map in 2021 that split Pulaski County into the 1st, 2nd and 4th districts. Opponents of the map have argued these boundaries dilute the influence of Black voters and make the 2nd District more favorable to Republican candidates.6 In the House race, Jones carried Pulaski County. Hill won White, Cleburne, Van Buren, Conway, Faulkner, Perry and Saline counties.6

The Watch's wave map compares each county's 2024 Harris–Trump margin with this year's Senate and governor's vote. On that measure, all eight counties moved toward Democrats, by a weighted 15 points. That is less than the Senate race's statewide move of 18.8 points (from R+30.6 to R+11.9) and close to the governor's race's 15.3. No county flipped. Pulaski went from D+22.1 to D+35.2, although that figure covers the whole county, including precincts outside the 2nd. The largest move came in Faulkner County, home to the city of Conway, which went from R+32.3 to R+12.4, a shift of 20 points. Saline County, southwest of Little Rock, moved 17 points to R+23.2. White and Perry counties each moved 15 points, and Conway County, a separate county west of Faulkner, moved 13. Van Buren moved 12. Cleburne, the most Republican county in the district, moved least: 10 points, to R+58.0.

These moves do not show that voters changed their minds. Pulaski cast 130,223 votes in the statewide races, almost as many as the other seven counties combined. A county count cannot tell persuasion apart from changes in who turned out for a midterm. The Arkansas Times reported that Pulaski's early vote far surpass[ed] its 2022 figure9. Jay Barth, a Hendrix College professor emeritus, told the Arkansas Advocate that rural Republican turnout was somewhat demobilized, while urban and suburban areas saw "a little bit more of a true blue wave"10.

What the result means

Hill has represented Central Arkansas in Congress since January 2015.3 The Arkansas Advocate reported that this is his narrowest margin of victory in his seven congressional elections, even after Republican state lawmakers redrew the 2nd District in 2021 to favor their party.10 The Associated Press called the race Thursday at 11:01 a.m.11, two days after the election. Before the call, KARK reported that the Arkansas Secretary of State reported12 fewer outstanding absentee, provisional and overseas ballots than Hill's lead.

Jones conceded the race to Hill Thursday afternoon.8 In his statement he said the 2021 map had probably decided the outcome, and he urged the next Congress to restore the Voting Rights Act and protect majority-Black districts.6 Hill said in his own statement, "This election was about lowering costs for families, growing the economy and keeping America strong"7.

The result keeps all four of Arkansas' House seats Republican. Democrats have not won a statewide or U.S. House race in Arkansas since 2010.10 Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called. In the 2nd District, 99% of the expected vote is counted. Conway County stands at 98%, and the returns are still unofficial6.
