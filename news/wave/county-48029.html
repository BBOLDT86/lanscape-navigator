In San Antonio's Bexar County, a 10-point Democratic edge becomes 35

San Antonio's home county gave James Talarico a 35.5-point win over Ken Paxton. That is a 26-point move from 2024 that tracked the statewide swing almost exactly, on about 99,000 fewer votes than two years ago.

Two years ago, Bexar County gave Kamala Harris a 9.8-point margin over Donald Trump, a thin showing for a county Democrats have long counted on. On Tuesday, it gave James Talarico, the Democratic state representative who once taught language arts at Rhodes Middle School on San Antonio's West Side1, a 35.5-point win over Republican Attorney General Ken Paxton in the U.S. Senate race. The count stands at 440,271 to 206,838, with 99% of the expected vote counted.

What Bexar did

That is a move of 26 points toward the Democrats, almost exactly the statewide move. Talarico carried Texas by 12.1 points; Trump won the state by 13.7 in 2024. Bexar ranked 50th of Texas's 254 counties by the size of its move. Every county in the state moved left, and the typical county moved about 18 points. Among the big metro counties Bexar sat in the middle: Harris moved 27 points, Tarrant 24, Dallas and Collin 23 each, Denton 22, and Travis, already the most Democratic of them, 19.

Counties elsewhere that started from about the same place moved much less. Erie County, N.Y., moved 1 point, Oakland County, Mich., 4, Orange County, Fla., 15, and DuPage County, Ill., 16. Of that group, only Harris County moved more.

In votes, Talarico's lead of about 233,000 compares with a 2024 Democratic edge of roughly 74,000. Fewer people voted, though: 658,445 ballots in the Senate race, 87% of the 2024 total. Bexar is a county of 2,160,088 people by the Census Bureau's 2025 estimate, 60.7% Hispanic and 24.7% non-Hispanic white2, and University Health describes it as Texas' most populous majority-Hispanic county3.

Why it moved

Turnout was high for a midterm. After the first week of early voting, Elections Administrator Michele Carew said the county would probably see more votes cast in this November's midterm election than there was in the 2024 presidential election4. That did not happen. Texas Public Radio reported that overall turnout was shy of5 the 2024 total, although early voting before Election Day was the largest voting turnout for a midterm election in the county's history5. San Antonio Republican strategist Kelton Morgan told TPR that the clearest lesson of the early numbers was that an unusual amount of money is being spent here right now4.

Turnout matters for reading the swing. With the electorate at 87% of its 2024 size, a county-level count cannot separate voters who changed sides from 2024 Trump voters who stayed home, or from newcomers. The county has added more than 45,000 new voters since 20244. The 26 points are a mix of all three.

The polls point to Latino voters. The AP Voter Poll found roughly six in 10 Hispanic voters in Texas supported Talarico, compared with about three in 10 who backed Paxton.6 The Houston Chronicle tied the statewide sweep partly to drops in turnout in rural areas GOP nominee Ken Paxton needed to win, and by a shift among Latino voters7. In focus groups run by Equis before the election, the Texas Tribune reported, numerous Texas Latino voters said they connected with Talarico's faith and economic message8. Axios San Antonio added a caution: the results alone don't establish why voters shifted9.

The candidates mattered too. The Associated Press called Paxton scandal-plagued10 and reported that his primary defeat of Sen. John Cornyn bolstered Talarico's appeal to Republican and independent voters10. Talarico told KSAT that the rising cost of living was a top priority, and he supports suspending federal gasoline and diesel taxes and reversing tariffs he believes are driving up prices.11 AP's statewide account described voters frustrated over high prices and concerned about government corruption12.

What it means here

The swing reached legislative races. In Texas House District 121, which hadn't elected a Democrat since 198113, first-time candidate Zack Dunn beat Republican Rep. Marc LaHood. The San Antonio Report noted that the Northside district includes some of the area's wealthiest enclaves, and once belonged to Texas House Speaker Joe Straus13. On the Southside, Democrat Kristian Carranza flipped a seat held by Rep. John Lujan, who ran for Congress instead.14 The swing had limits. The San Antonio Report had District 122 still too close to call Wednesday morning, but the Texas Tribune later reported that San Antonio Rep. Mark Dorazio won reelection14. Republican Mark Teixeira will be the only Republican in San Antonio's congressional delegation15, after winning the 21st District.

At the county level, Democrats haven't lost a countywide office in more than a decade16. Former San Antonio mayor Ron Nirenberg will now take over as county judge. Before the vote, Morgan called the county a laboratory of whoever is going to dominate Texas in the future17. A 2024 county won by 9.8 points and a 2026 county won by 35.5 had different electorates. This count can't tell which one shows up next time.
