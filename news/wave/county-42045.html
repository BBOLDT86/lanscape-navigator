In Delaware County, Shapiro nearly doubles Harris's margin; GOP loses last seat

Josh Shapiro carried the inner-ring suburb west of Philadelphia by 46 points, 22 points better than Kamala Harris did. The same night, Democrats won every Harrisburg seat in the county for the first time.

Delaware County, which locals call Delco, voted Democratic before Tuesday. It voted much more heavily Democratic after it. Gov. Josh Shapiro beat Stacy Garrity, the Republican state treasurer, 169,689 votes to 62,219, with 99% of the expected vote counted. That is a margin of D+45.9. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 23.7 points, so the county moved 22 points toward the Democrats.

The county is dense and older-suburban. It has an estimated 580,937 residents as of July 20251 and 3,138.1 people per square mile1 at the 2020 census. It is the fifth-most populous county in Pennsylvania and the third-smallest in area.2

What Delaware County did

The county's move was close to the state's. Shapiro won Pennsylvania by 22.1 points two years after Trump carried it by 1.7, a statewide swing of 24 points. Every one of the 67 counties moved left, and the typical county moved 23 points. Delaware County ranked 40th by size of move, near the middle.

Its suburban neighbors moved by similar amounts. Montgomery County went from D+22.8 to D+46.1, a 23-point move that leaves it almost even with Delaware County. Chester County moved 22 points, to D+36.5. Bucks County, the one collar county that was a toss-up in 2024, moved 26 points, to D+25.7. Philadelphia moved 20.

Outside Pennsylvania, the counties that started from a similar Democratic lead mostly moved by a similar amount. Dallas moved 23 points, Wake County, N.C., 22 and Queens 20. Westchester County, N.Y., moved only 5. Every one of them moved well past the national figure: across 2,775 counties counted, the typical move was 14 points.

Who moved, and who stayed home

The 2026 electorate was smaller. Delaware County cast 234,015 votes, 71% of its 2024 total of 328,172, or about 94,000 fewer ballots. The Democratic lead in raw votes still grew, from about 78,000 to about 107,000. The county totals cannot say how much of the move came from voters changing sides and how much came from which voters turned out. A 22-point move in an electorate less than three-quarters the size of 2024's mixes both.

Reporters did find crossover voters. On Election Day, Spotlight PA interviewed Charles Vogeley, a 75-year-old accountant in Concord Township3 who has been a registered Republican all his voting life. He voted for Shapiro, and he didn't even know the name of Shapiro's GOP opponent4. WHYY reported that Shapiro benefited from some GOP support and President Donald Trump's sagging favorability5. Trump endorsed Garrity, the state's two-term elected treasurer.6

The campaign was lopsided. Shapiro outspent Garrity by a ratio of 7 to 1, per campaign finance reports.4 The Philadelphia Inquirer reported that in the final days Shapiro's bus tour was hitting all four of the collar counties over as many days7. Statewide, Franklin & Marshall College polling in October found Pennsylvania voters are deeply concerned about their personal finances and cite the economy as the state's biggest problem8. A September poll covered by the Inquirer found gas prices were Trump's weakest issue with Pennsylvania voters, half of Pennsylvania voters who cast their ballots for Trump in 20249 included.

Delaware County Democrats were not all enthusiastic early on. At the May primary, Spotlight PA met another Delaware County voter, 38-year-old Danielle Allor10, who was unsure about voting for Shapiro and had left the governor's line blank as a protest vote.

What it means for Delco's politics

Below the governor's race, the result ended the Republicans' last foothold in the county. WHYY reported that Democrat Elizabeth Moro upset Republican state Rep. Craig Williams in a rematch for the 160th District11, which covers parts of southern Delaware County and Chester County. Moro's win gives Democrats control over every legislative seat in Delaware County for the first time ever.11 Patch reported that the 160th voted for a Democratic candidate in the Pennsylvania House of Representatives for the first time since 1976.12

The Republican who voted for Shapiro also voted for Williams, the local Republican representative. Vogeley told Spotlight PA that "The split legislature is OK because it provides checks and balances"3. Statewide, Democrats won both legislative chambers, and Widener University political scientist J. Wesley Leckrone told WHYY that Tuesday's win will significantly change the shape of policy in Harrisburg.13

For decades the county Republican Party dominated Delaware County. WHYY recalled that in 2019, three Democratic women handed the party control of council for the first time since the Civil War.11 The congressional seat stayed Democratic too: Mary Gay Scanlon, a Democrat, defeated Republican Nick Manganaro14 in the district built around the county.

The count is not final. The county says results remain unofficial for about two weeks after Election Day15, and its election board has scheduled a provisional ballot meeting for November 1016.
