The wave · the biggest counties · DeKalb County, Georgia

DeKalb gave Ossoff more votes on a smaller turnout than 2024

Georgia's most Democratic big county moved 13 points left. That is small next to the state's 20-point move, but DeKalb started at D+65, and its vote margin still made up about a third of Jon Ossoff's statewide lead.

DeKalb County went from very Democratic to more so on Tuesday. Sen. Jon Ossoff, the Democratic incumbent, beat Republican U.S. Rep. Mike Collins in the county 285,433 votes to 36,253. That is a margin of D+77.5, up from Kamala Harris's D+64.8 against Donald Trump in 2024. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, the county moved 13 points left.

DeKalb is the state's inner core and part of its suburbs. About 774,400 people lived there in 2025, making it the fourth-most populous county in Georgia.1 Non-Hispanic Black residents make up 51.6% of the population.1 The northern part of the county, above I-285, belongs to what The Atlanta Journal-Constitution calls the northern arc of suburbs, once Republican hotbeds that have seen significant Democratic shifts.2 In the south are Stonecrest and Lithonia, which the Atlanta Regional Commission found rank first and second for the highest percent share3 of Black residents among Georgia's cities.

What DeKalb did

Statewide, Ossoff won by 18.0 points. That is a 20-point move from Trump's 2.2-point win in 2024. Every one of Georgia's 159 counties moved left. DeKalb's move ranks 110th, below the typical Georgia county's 14 points. Fulton moved 14 points, Cobb 19, Henry 23 and Gwinnett 26. Gwinnett went from D+16.5 to D+42.6.

Most of that gap comes from where each county started. A county at D+65 has little room left to move. Manhattan began at exactly the same D+64.8 in 2024 and barely moved, to D+64.6. Philadelphia, which started six points less Democratic, moved 20 points and finished at D+78.9, close to where DeKalb ended. Multnomah County, Ore., moved 5 points, Alameda County, Calif., 8 and Montgomery County, Md., 10.

In raw votes, DeKalb counts for more than its point move suggests. Ossoff's lead there, about 249,000 votes, is roughly a third of his statewide lead of about 754,000.

Who voted, and why

DeKalb cast 321,686 votes, 88 percent of its 2024 total of 366,008. The county count cannot tell voters who switched sides from voters who stayed home or showed up. The move mixes both. Statewide, the Brennan Center found that turnout spiked among Black voters and dipped for white voters4. Over 29 percent of white voters who cast a ballot4 in 2024 sat this election out, compared with 24 percent of Black voters. In a majority-Black county, a gap like that could move the margin left even if nobody changed their mind.

There were signs of this before Election Day. An AJC analysis found that in the 27 counties Harris carried, the number of ballots cast has jumped 9%5 over the same point in early voting in 2022. The AJC also reported a Democratic firm's finding that Black and younger voters are heading to the polls at a significantly faster clip6 than in the last midterm. In NBC News' exit poll, Black voters backed Ossoff 93%7 to 6 percent, and the poll's Atlanta metro region backed him 84%7 to 15 percent.

Voters said money was the main issue. WABE reported that the Senate candidates clashed over the causes of the higher cost of living, the war in Iran, access to healthcare, and immigration8. Ossoff attributes the increased prices to tariffs implemented by the Trump administration8. Bloomberg reported that Ossoff gained from Trump's falling popularity in Georgia, where high fuel and food prices were top issues for voters.9

Collins was also a weak candidate. NPR reported that his personal baggage alienated independents and some Republican-leaning voters.10 The Associated Press noted that he was dogged by questions about an unresolved House ethics investigation and his ties to white nationalists11, and that Republican groups supporting Collins pulled back their spending in mid-October11. CBS News Atlanta reported that an AP poll of Georgia Senate voters found Ossoff seen as less extreme than Collins12.

What it means for DeKalb

DeKalb's own government was already Democratic, and this vote kept it that way. FOX 5 Atlanta reported that Michelle Spears defeated Republican Ben Pepper13 for the District 2 county commission seat, and that Keyanna Moore secured victory over Republican Andrea Smith13 in District 3. Decaturish reported that voters renewed the school sales tax, approving the seventh iteration of the Education Special Purpose Local Option Sales Tax14. These counts are not final yet. On Nov. 5, the county elections office was still describing its results as Unofficial and Incomplete15.

In statewide races, DeKalb's role is to supply votes, not to swing. The biggest point moves came in Gwinnett and Cobb, while DeKalb's job was to deliver a large lead on a big midterm turnout, and it did. Ossoff also did better than the rest of his ticket. Keisha Lance Bottoms won the governor's race statewide by 8.7 points, half his 18.0. The 2026 count shows how many votes DeKalb can cast in a midterm. It does not show whether those voters will come back without Ossoff on the ballot.
