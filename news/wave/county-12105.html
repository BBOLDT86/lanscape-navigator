Polk County, Florida's I-4 Republican anchor, swings 14 points toward Democrats

Ashley Moody still carried Polk, but by 6.7 points, down from Donald Trump's 20.7-point win in 2024. A schoolteacher also became the first Democrat in more than a decade to win a Polk seat in the Legislature.

Ashley Moody, the Republican appointed to the U.S. Senate, carried Polk County on Nov. 3. Republicans usually do. But her margin was much smaller than her party is used to here. With 99% of the expected vote counted, Moody led Democrat Angie Nixon 136,056 to 118,889, a margin of 6.7 points. Two years ago Donald Trump beat Kamala Harris in Polk by 20.7.

What Polk did

That is a 14-point move toward the Democrat. Florida as a whole moved 10.2 points: Moody won statewide by 2.9 after Trump's 13.1-point win, and Polk's move ranked seventh among the state's 67 counties. Polk sits between Tampa and Orlando, and it moved about as much as Orange County (14.9 points). It moved more than Hillsborough (12.1) and far more than Pinellas (5.9). The difference is that Orange and Hillsborough went Democratic outright, while Polk stayed Republican.

The county is home to Lakeland, Winter Haven and the county seat, Bartow, and it is growing fast. Its population is nearly 850,0001, Bay News 9 reported, and Census data show the Lakeland–Winter Haven area grew by 2.7% from 2024 to 2025 — ranking fourth in the nation.1 Experts told the station that more people are moving from nearby coastal areas like Pinellas County1. About 23.5%2 of residents are Hispanic, according to Ballotpedia's demographic profile.

Among large counties elsewhere that leaned Republican in 2024, Polk started out more Republican than any. Its 14-point move was bigger than Macomb County's in Michigan (11.9) or Suffolk County's in New York (10.7), but much smaller than the moves in Denton (21.6) and Collin (23.2) counties in Texas. It was close to the national typical move of 14.1.

Fewer people voted, too. Polk cast 257,911 votes for Senate, 74% of its 2024 presidential total of 348,922. Trump won here by roughly 72,000 votes; Moody won by about 17,000. County totals cannot tell us how much of that gap came from Trump voters staying home and how much from voters switching parties.

Why it moved

Both campaigns treated Polk as contested ground. Fox 13 called the county a crucial battleground3 as Byron Donalds, the Republican candidate for governor, and his Democratic opponent, David Jolly, spent part of the final day there. Jolly held a rally in Haines City. Donalds appeared in Lakeland with Polk County Sheriff Grady Judd and U.S. Senate candidate Ashley Moody.4 Jolly's campaign believed the county offered an opportunity to gain votes5 by drawing disaffected Republicans. Spectrum News reported that Frostproof City Council member Austin Gravely has joined Jolly for 10 campaign stops and said he voted for a Democrat for the first time in his life.6

The issues were mostly about money. In Haines City, WFLA reported, Jolly promised a moratorium on data centers7 and talked about health care. Donalds pledged to lower insurance, cut property taxes, and protect parental rights.7 In the Senate race, News4Jax described a contrast between Moody's economic policies, law enforcement and her record in office, and Nixon's focus on the cost of living, health care and support for families.8

Turnout may also have helped Democrats. Spectrum News reported before Election Day that Florida Democrats surpassed their very own turnout records from the 2022 midterms6. Statewide, NBC's exit poll found Puerto Rican voters backing Nixon by roughly two to one. Nixon 32% / 67%9 Yes, Puerto Rican (4%)9 A Florida Politics poll in October found Latino voters10 uneasy about immigration enforcement they considered too aggressive. Neither poll looked at Polk on its own.

What it means

The clearest local sign of the shift came in Florida House District 51, which covers much of northern Polk County, including Auburndale, Haines City, Davenport, Lake Alfred, Polk City and the Four Corners area11. There, Democrat Octavio Hernandez, an algebra teacher from Davenport, beat Republican Rep. Hilary Holley. LkldNow reported that he raised $4,922.45, compared with $135,500 raised by Holley.11 The Ledger reported it appears that for the first time in more than 10 years12 a Democrat will represent Polk in the Legislature.

Republicans still held most of the county's offices. The Ledger noted that most Polk County incumbents held their seats12, and Rep. Scott Franklin won another term in Congress. LkldNow called his win a closer-than-expected congressional race13. Voters also passed a school property tax that adds a 1-mill property tax for school operations.14

Polk is still a Republican county, only by a much smaller margin than two years ago. Whether that holds, and how much of it came from who stayed home in a midterm, will only become clear in later elections.
