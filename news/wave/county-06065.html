Riverside County swings 18 points, from Trump to Becerra

The Inland Empire county Donald Trump narrowly carried in 2024 gave Democrat Xavier Becerra a 17-point lead for governor, one of the larger moves in California, with about a fifth of the expected vote still uncounted.

Two years ago, Riverside County was one of the few big California counties Donald Trump carried. He won it by 1.3 points out of 940,405 votes. In the Nov. 3 race for governor it went the other way. With 79.65% of the expected vote counted, Democrat Xavier Becerra leads Republican Steve Hilton 390,385 to 278,471, a margin of 16.7 points. That is an 18-point move toward the Democrats.

What Riverside did

Statewide, Becerra won by 31.3 points, 11.1 points better than Kamala Harris's 20.1-point margin in 2024. Riverside moved further than the state did. It ranks seventh of California's 58 counties by size of move, and the typical county moved about 6 points. Among the state's biggest counties, only neighboring San Bernardino moved more, going from R+2.1 to D+19.1, a 21-point move. Los Angeles moved 14 points, and San Diego and Orange moved 8 each. Riverside is still about 15 points more Republican than California as a whole. Outside the state, Maricopa County, Arizona, made a similar jump, from R+3.5 to D+16.3. Florida's Duval and Hillsborough counties moved about 12 points each. Nationally, the typical county moved 14.

Riverside is a big, heavily Latino county with a lot of immigrants. The Census Bureau puts its population at 2,544,9161. It reports that 53% of residents are Hispanic or Latino, 29% are non-Hispanic white, and about 22% were born abroad.

The count is not finished. Mail ballots had to be postmarked on or before Election Day and received no later than seven days after, by November 10, 20262. The registrar has 30 days to certify results.3 The numbers above will change.

Why it moved

Turnout is part of the story. The 668,856 votes counted so far equal 71% of the 2024 total. The final figure will be higher, but a midterm electorate is usually smaller and different. A county's move mixes who turned out with who changed sides, and the county count cannot tell them apart.

The Watch found no published voter poll covering Riverside County alone, so statewide surveys are the best guide. According to The AP Voter Poll, most voters who said the economy was the top issue facing the country supported Becerra4. He also won a majority of voters who said the cost of living was "crucial"4 to their vote. The Associated Press reported that Becerra repeatedly tied his opponent to the president and promised to defend California against Trump's policies4.

Affordability matters a lot in this region. A September survey by the Public Policy Institute of California found that 79% of Inland Empire residents said the candidates' positions on affordability were very important5 to their vote. Becerra made appealing to Latino voters a key part of his strategy, including ads and social media posts in Spanish6. The AP Voter Poll found that about 7 in 10 Latino Catholics voted for Becerra7. He became the state's first elected Latino governor7.

Nationally, the AP Voter Poll found approximately six in ten Hispanic voters supported Democratic House candidates8. The Latino News Network cautioned that it remains uncertain how much of either shift reflected changing political attachments, short-term candidate preferences, or differences in who participated8.

The race itself drew limited attention. Patch, the local news site, called it somewhat anticlimactic following a chaotic primary9.

What it means for the county

Riverside's own sheriff hung over this election. KQED reported that Sheriff Chad Bianco, a Republican, confiscated the ballots in February as part of an investigation into purported voter fraud that he launched during his failed bid for governor10. Those were more than 650,000 ballots from the November 2025 special election on Proposition 50. In September, CalMatters reported, the state Supreme Court ruled unanimously that Bianco broke the law and created "a substantial risk to election integrity"11. He has since returned the ballots and election materials to the registrar.12

Republicans still have strong ground in the county. Under the Proposition 50 lines, CalMatters noted, the 40th Congressional District became a safe Republican district whose center of gravity shifted from Orange County to Riverside County13. That race was between two Republicans, and AP called it on Nov. 9 for Rep. Ken Calvert14 over Rep. Young Kim. The district takes in the Temescal Valley, most of Lake Elsinore, Canyon Lake, Corona and Menifee15.

Becerra's margin in Riverside is about half his statewide margin, and the county is still well to the right of California. What is new is the gap since 2024: the county Trump won narrowly two years ago gave the Democrat a clear lead.
