Lee County, Florida, stays deep red, but Moody's margin shrinks by seven

Ashley Moody carried Southwest Florida's biggest county by 21 points. That was 7 points less than Donald Trump's 2024 margin there, and fewer voters turned out. Lee moved left about as much as a typical Florida county, but well short of the state's big metros.

Lee County ranks eighth in Florida by votes cast this year, and it stayed firmly Republican in the Nov. 3 U.S. Senate race. With 99% of the expected vote counted, Sen. Ashley Moody, a Republican, had 182,895 votes to 117,928 for Democrat Angie Nixon. That is a margin of 21.4 points. Donald Trump carried the county by 28.4 points in 2024, so the county moved 7 points toward the Democrats.

The county includes Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Bonita Springs, Lehigh Acres, Sanibel and Fort Myers Beach. A recent News-Press opinion piece described it as home to approximately 900,000 residents1. Republicans hold a large lead in registration. On the eve of the election, the elections office counted Democrats: 110,710 Republicans: 251,9062 among active voters.

What Lee County did

Statewide, Moody won by 2.9 points. Trump had won Florida by 13.1, so the state moved 10.2 points. Lee moved less than the state as a whole. It ranked 33rd of 67 counties by size of move, almost exactly at the typical Florida county's 7 points. Sixty-six of the 67 counties moved left. Among Florida's other big counties, only Pinellas moved less, at 6 points. Miami-Dade moved 19 points and Hillsborough 12, and both went from Trump to the Democrat.

The big counties elsewhere that were closest to Lee's 2024 margin were all much less Republican to begin with, and all of them moved more. Suffolk County in New York moved 11 points. Macomb County in Michigan moved 12, and the Texas suburbs of Denton and Collin moved 22 and 23. Across the country, the typical county moved 14 points, twice as much as Lee.

Turnout fell. The 304,021 votes counted were 77% of the 392,519 cast in 2024. Trump won the county by about 111,000 votes. Moody's lead was 64,967. When that many fewer people vote, a county's shift mixes two things: who turned out and who changed sides. The totals cannot tell 2024 Trump voters who skipped this election apart from voters who switched.

Why it moved

The two candidates were very different. The Associated Press described Nixon as a state lawmaker and a democratic socialist who tested the strength of Democrats' left flank3. She had upset establishment Democrats' more moderate favorite in the primary.3 WJXT's News4Jax reported that she described affordability as a central concern for Floridians, pointing to health care costs, home insurance and child care.4

Statewide exit polls published by NBC News showed how much costs mattered. Among voters very worried about grocery costs, Very concerned (59%) 35% 64%5. The poll lists Moody's share first and Nixon's second. Before the election, a University of North Florida poll found Nixon led Moody by 15 percentage points4 among independents. That matters in Lee. Nixon's vote total was higher than the county's number of active registered Democrats, so at least some of her votes came from independents or Republicans.

Both parties also pointed to turnout. CBS News Miami reported that before Election Day, Democrats had a higher participation rate among their registered voters6. Florida Politics reported on a post-election memo from Byron Donalds's campaign. The memo said Donalds, the governor-elect, won by counteracting the biggest Democratic turnout advantage since the 1980s.7 That is the campaign's own account. Donalds is a congressman from Naples, just south of Lee, and his bus tour ended at Sugarshack Downtown in Bonita Springs8 the day before the election.

Property costs also came up. WINK News found Cape Coral homeowners split on Amendment 39, which would raise the homestead tax exemption. Some told the station they wanted relief from rising bills9. The amendment passed statewide, Amendment 3 passes10, according to WKMG's News 6 in Orlando. The local reporting reviewed for this piece did not include a neighborhood-by-neighborhood breakdown of where Lee's vote shifted.

What it means for the county

Lee's Republican officeholders kept their seats. The AP projected that Republican Jim Schwartzel has been elected to take over the U.S. House seat being vacated by Byron Donalds.10 Supervisor of Elections Jenna Persons-Mulicka also won, according to WINK's results11.

The bigger local change may be a charter question. Voters were asked whether to elect county commissioners from single-member districts beginning with the 2028 general election12. The county's unofficial results show the "yes" side winning. Supporters argued that among Florida's eight largest counties, Lee stands alone as the only one that continues to elect its county commissioners entirely on an at-large basis.1 The Cape Coral Breeze's editorial board had urged a no vote.

Nonpartisan races also brought change. WGCU reported that Armor Persons, chair of the Lee County School Board, was defeated by Crystal Johnson13. In August, the station noted that the Republican establishment's pick for a commission seat lost a primary in which she outraised Cochran by at least $175,00014.
