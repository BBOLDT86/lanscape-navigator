Yarbrough wins Texas' redrawn 32nd, the GOP's lone gain from its new map

Republican Jace Yarbrough beat Democrat Dan Barrios by 2.9 points in a district that now stretches from Dallas County into rural East Texas. Every county in the seat moved toward Democrats in the statewide races, but the rural east held.

In 2025, Texas Republicans redrew five Democratic-held U.S. House seats, hoping to take all of them. The 32nd District is the only one they won. Republican Jace Yarbrough received 142,834 votes and Democrat Dan Barrios received 134,781, a margin of 2.9 points, or 8,053 votes, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race for Yarbrough. CBS19 in Tyler reported that Barrios acknowledged his campaign fell short1.

Elsewhere in Texas, the night went against Republicans. Democrat James Talarico beat Republican Ken Paxton for the U.S. Senate by 12.1 points, and Democrat Gina Hinojosa beat Gov. Greg Abbott by 7.1. The Texas Tribune reported that GOP candidates lost four of five seats their new congressional map was designed to flip and lost two of their own.2

The race

No incumbent ran. Axios Dallas reported that Rep. Julie Johnson, the Democrat who held the seat, decided to run in the 33rd after her district was redrawn to stretch into East Texas3, and she lost to former Rep. Colin Allred4 in that primary.

Yarbrough is a constitutional lawyer and Air Force veteran5 who also ran for state Senate District 30 in 20246. He notched support from several prominent Republicans, including President Trump and Texas Gov. Greg Abbott4, and his runoff opponent, businessman Ryan Binkley, withdrew in March4. Barrios has served as a member of the Richardson City Council since 20237.

Yarbrough had far more money. The Texan reported that from July through September he brought in over $345,000 while Barrios brought in about $54,0006 and ended that stretch with $293,000 on hand to Barrios's $28,000. The Texas Tribune reported that American Mission has spent almost $130,000 supporting Yarbrough8. American Mission is a super PAC aligned with the AI industry.

Barrios ran on the cost of living. In a pre-election interview, he said rising costs are putting pressure on families and farmers across the district9 and that some longtime Republicans told him they would vote Democratic to keep family farms open. Yarbrough argued that "the biggest tax on the American people right now is fraud."9 On immigration, Barrios called for a major overhaul of the system and Yarbrough emphasized enforcement of existing laws.9

Matthew Wilson is a Southern Methodist University professor who lives in the district. He told Al Jazeera that Yarbrough moved from the social and cultural issues of his primary to talk a lot about lowering prices.10 Wilson's other explanation was the map itself: "his district was drawn to be so heavily Republican."11

The district

The old 32nd was a compact urban district in Dallas12. CBS19 reported that the new one encompasses Camp, Rains, Rockwall, Upshur and Wood counties, along with portions of Collin, Dallas and Hunt counties13. The Texan described more of those counties as split. According to The Texan, Yarbrough won the more rural East Texas counties of Camp, Hunt, Rains, Upshur, and Wood and the suburban Rockwall county. Barrios carried the votes in the Dallas and Collin county portions of the district.12

On The Watch's wave map, every county in the district moved toward Democrats in the statewide races, by 22.7 points when weighted by votes. The Dallas end moved most. Dallas County went from D+22.2 in 2024 to D+45.6, and Collin County flipped from R+11.2 to D+12.1. Hunt County moved 22 points, Camp 19 and Rockwall 18. The counties farther east moved least and stayed lopsided: Wood moved 11 points to R+59.0, Rains 11 points to R+61.6 and Upshur 15 points to R+56.0.

Those figures cover whole counties in the Senate and governor races, not the House race inside the district lines. Dallas County cast 783,308 votes, nearly three times the district's entire House vote, so most of its swing happened outside the 32nd. A move is also not proof that voters changed their minds. Whether the ballots number 783,308, as in Dallas County, or 4,278, as in Camp County, the count cannot separate voters who switched sides from changes in who turned out. KLTV, citing the Texas Tribune, reported record early turnout for a non-presidential election year14.

What the result means

Allred flipped the 32nd District in 2018 after winning 52% of the vote over the incumbent, Republican Pete Sessions. The former Republican stronghold was redrawn in 2022, making it more strongly Democratic.15 Johnson succeeded him in 2024. Yarbrough's win puts the seat back in Republican hands, but on mostly different ground, and no incumbent lost here.

Wilson told Al Jazeera that "the race was a lot closer than he would have expected."16 Yarbrough called his win "one of only a handful of Republican pickups on a tough night across the country."13

He will be in the minority. Democrats have been called the winners of 252 House seats, more than the 218 needed for a majority. Republicans have 161, and 22 races have not been called. In the 32nd, about 1 percent of the expected vote was still out as of Saturday morning, against an 8,053-vote margin, and the results remain unofficial until counties finish their canvass.
