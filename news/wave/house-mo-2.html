Fred Wellman unseats Ann Wagner in Missouri's suburban 2nd District

A retired Army officer and podcaster, outspent by the seven-term Republican, won the St. Louis suburban seat by 4.9 points on district lines Republicans had tried to replace, as Democrats won a House majority.

Fred Wellman, a newcomer Democrat1, unseated seven-term Republican Rep. Ann Wagner in Missouri's 2nd Congressional District on Tuesday. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Wellman had 169,477 votes to Wagner's 153,132. That is 51.1 percent to 46.2 percent, a margin of 4.9 points or 16,345 votes. AP called the race at 11:57 p.m. Eastern, and St. Louis Public Radio reported that Wagner called Wellman to concede late Tuesday night.2

Brandon Daugherty drew 8,782 votes. AP's count lists him as an independent, but the Leader newspaper called him the Libertarian candidate1. Even if his votes were added to Wagner's, she would still trail Wellman. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still open. KOMU noted that the victory gives Democrats three of Missouri's eight seats in the U.S. House3.

The race: a veteran and podcaster against a seven-term incumbent

Wellman, 61, of Wildwood, served 22 years in the Army as an aviator, including four combat deployments1. He then founded Scout Comms, a communications and advocacy firm focused on veterans and military families.1 He hosts the "On Democracy" podcast and was executive director of the Lincoln Project, a group that sought to steer Republicans disillusioned with President Donald Trump toward Democratic candidates.4 Wagner, 64, a Republican from Ballwin1, had represented the district since 20133.

St. Louis Public Radio reported that Wellman's campaign focused on workers' rights, a higher federal minimum wage, paid family leave and equal pay, and that he criticized Wagner and the Trump administration over the Iran war2. Wagner said Trump made the right decision to attack Iran because he needed to stop it from getting a nuclear weapon.5 KSDK reported that she voted against all three Iran war powers resolutions6. The Webster-Kirkwood Times reported that her campaign focused on affordability and public safety and on her lifelong roots in the district7. KCUR, running St. Louis Public Radio's story, said her loss came amid a growing backlash against Republicans over high gas prices, tariffs and the Iran war8.

Wagner had more money. The Missouri Independent reported that she spent nearly $6 million and the Congressional Leadership Fund bought $628,500 in broadcast ads attacking Wellman9. Wellman raised $2.2 million, and VoteVets spent more than $500,000 in the district to support him.9 He also outraised her in the final months.10 St. Louis Public Radio reported that her ads attacked his past statements as a podcaster and his financial problems5. He has declared bankruptcy twice.11 St. Louis Magazine described the ads differently, saying they focused very little on his money troubles and more on casting him as too liberal for the district12. Wellman's ads said Wagner had quadrupled her net worth13. KSDK reported that her campaign called him "a liar" and said she does not trade individual stocks13. St. Louis Magazine concluded that Wagner's focus on culture wars didn't work, while Wellman's strategy of tying her to Trump did12.

The district: the 2022 lines, not the 2025 ones

According to First Alert 4, the district covers west and south St. Louis County, parts of St. Charles and Warren counties, and all of Franklin County14. Missouri adopted a new congressional map in 2025, encouraged by Trump.15 St. Louis Public Radio said it would have made Wagner's district more Republican, but not overwhelmingly so4. The 2025 map was used for the August primary.16 The Missouri Supreme Court then required the 2022 map for the general election.17 On Tuesday, voters rejected the new map16. Wellman had said running under the 2022 map helped him because that version of the district is more Democratic18.

The Watch's data for this seat include only the district-wide count, with no county-by-county figures. So this piece cannot yet compare each county with its 2024 Harris–Trump vote. Local reporting gives a rough picture. The Leader reported that Wagner beat Wellman in Franklin, St. Charles and Warren counties1, and the Missouri Independent said Wellman won in St. Louis County10. Before the election, St. Louis Public Radio had noted that Wagner usually ran ahead of Trump in St. Louis County, the district's population center and a part of the state that has swung toward Democrats5.

The 331,391 votes cast here came from a midterm electorate. Neither a district total nor a county's move can show how many voters changed their minds and how much came from a different group of voters showing up. St. Louis Public Radio reported that in most places, early voting was second only to 202419. St. Louis County elections director Eric Fey said the numbers were hard to compare because the county had 21 early-voting sites this time, up from seven19.

What the result means

According to First Alert 4, Republicans had held the seat since the early 1990s14. KCUR reported that Democrats had tried for a decade to oust Wagner by highlighting her support of Trump, without success8. The Missouri Independent reported that at the start of 2025, the 2nd was the only Missouri district rated less than solidly safe for the party holding it10. St. Louis Public Radio reported that Republicans also lost two seats in the Missouri Senate and 15 in the Missouri House16.

About 1 percent of the expected vote is still out, far less than Wellman's 16,345-vote lead, and the results are unofficial until certified. In his statement after the win, Wellman pointed to his Army career, saying "I never once asked a soldier how they voted."1
