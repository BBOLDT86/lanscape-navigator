Alameda County's Becerra vote outruns Harris but trails California's swing

Xavier Becerra won the East Bay county by 61 points with about 70 percent of the vote counted, 8 points better than Kamala Harris did in 2024. That gain was smaller than California's statewide move and came on far fewer ballots.

Alameda County is the East Bay: Oakland, Berkeley, Fremont, Hayward, and the suburbs of the Tri-Valley. Census estimates put its population at 1,636,630 in July 2025.1 Asian residents make up 36.3%, Hispanic or Latino residents 23.8%, and non-Hispanic white residents 25.9%.1 No racial or ethnic group is a majority.2 It was one of California's most Democratic big counties before Tuesday, and it is even more Democratic now.

What the county did

In the Associated Press count, Democrat Xavier Becerra led Republican Steve Hilton 342,879 to 82,195. That is a margin of 61.3 points, with 69.8% of the expected vote counted. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 53.6 points, so the county moved about 8 points toward the Democrats.

Statewide, though, the swing was bigger. Becerra leads California by 30.4 points, compared with Harris's 20.1, a move of 10.3. Alameda ranks 26th of the state's 58 counties by size of move. That is ahead of the typical county's gain of about 6 points but behind the vote-weighted average of about 9. The biggest moves came in the Inland Empire. Riverside County shifted 17 points and San Bernardino County 19, and both went from Trump in 2024 to Becerra. Los Angeles moved 12 points. Orange and San Diego moved 5 each, and neighboring Santa Clara barely moved at all, about 1 point.

Big Democratic counties elsewhere mostly moved further. Montgomery County, Maryland, which started almost exactly where Alameda did, moved roughly 10 points. Philadelphia moved 20 points, Brooklyn (Kings County) 15 and Fulton County, Georgia, 14. Only Multnomah County, Oregon, which started even further left, moved less, at 5 points. Across the 2,775 counties counted nationwide, the typical move was 14 points.

So far 425,074 ballots have been counted in Alameda, 63% of the 2024 total. The count is not finished. Mail ballots postmarked by Election Day can arrive as late as Nov. 10 and still count.3 The registrar will keep posting updates on Mondays and Fridays until every vote is counted4, and the county must certify its results by Dec. 3.5 A county's move mixes two things: who turned out and who changed sides. With far fewer ballots cast than in 2024, the county count cannot tell those apart.

Why it moved

The statewide campaign was about Washington. The Associated Press reported that Becerra repeatedly tied Hilton to the president and promised to defend California against Trump's policies6. Hilton, in turn, argued that one-party rule was to blame for the state's high cost of living and homelessness crisis.6 KQED noted that Hilton's approach "made little difference in a state where 3 in 4 voters disapprove of Trump."7 In the CNN debate, KQED reported, Becerra kept reminding viewers that Trump had endorsed Hilton, and Hilton did little to distance himself from the president.7

The polls point the same way. Emerson College's final survey found that the share of voters blaming Trump for the state of California's economy rose 17 points in a year, from 27% to 44%, while the share blaming Newsom fell from 51% to 38%.8 None of the reporting reviewed for this piece included a poll of Alameda County alone that shows who switched sides.

Locally, the governor's race got little attention. After the election, KQED Forum host Alexis Madrigal asked on air whether the race had "even happened."9 East Bay ballots were crowded with other contests. Barbara Lee won a full four-year term as Oakland mayor, and the regional sales tax for BART and other transit systems passed.10 Oakland voters also overwhelmingly approved Measure EE, which gives the mayor more authority over city government.11 KALW reported that most voters mailed their ballots11, and that only a small share voted in person on Election Day.

What it means

Alameda's real political fights now happen among Democrats. In the 14th Congressional District, which covers Fremont, Hayward and the Tri-Valley, both finalists were Democrats. The San Francisco Chronicle reported that progressive former state senator Aisha Wahab beat BART Board President Melissa Hernandez, who ran as the moderate and tried to court Republican voters who saw Wahab as too progressive.12 KTVU reported that the state Democratic Party endorsed Wahab, while super PACs, including two with ties to the American Israel Public Affairs Committee, backed Hernandez.13

The county's Democrats have not moved uniformly left on every question. In June's race for district attorney, CBS News reported, voters strongly backed the more traditional approach of Ursula Jones Dickson and rejected a return to Pamela Price's progressive prosecution reforms.14 The district attorney's job and Oakland's new strong-mayor charter are where questions of public safety and city management will be argued. Republicans are mostly not part of that argument.

Hilton won 82,195 votes in the count so far, about one ballot in five. All results are unofficial until certification, and the final margin may change as the rest of the ballots are counted.
