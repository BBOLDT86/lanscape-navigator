The House · California 7

Matsui leads Vang in Sacramento's all-Democratic House race as counting continues

With two-thirds of the expected vote counted, Rep. Doris Matsui leads fellow Democrat Mai Vang by about 11 points and 19,871 votes in California's 7th District. The Associated Press has not called the race.

Rep. Doris Matsui leads Sacramento City Council member Mai Vang, also a Democrat, 97,765 votes to 77,894 in California's 7th Congressional District. That is 55.7 percent to 44.3 percent, with 66.99 percent of the expected vote counted as of Saturday morning. The Associated Press has not called the race. Because both candidates are Democrats, the party margin on the results page reads D+100. The real contest is the 19,871-vote gap between them.

The seat has stayed with one family for a long time. AsAmNews noted that Matsui has been reelected every time since she replaced her late husband in 2005, and that a Matsui has held the seat every year since Bob Matsui held the same office in 1979.1

The race

Matsui, 82, faces an opponent who is half her age: 41-year-old Sacramento City Council Member Mai Vang. A progressive, Vang finished first in the primary2, which set up the all-Democratic general election. CalMatters called it her first competitive reelection challenge3. Vang is the daughter of Hmong refugees from Laos. If elected, she would be the first Hmong American in Congress.4

CBS Sacramento reported that Matsui ran on her work securing federal funding for Sacramento-area flood protection and transportation projects5. Vang ran on lowering costs for working families, housing affordability and Medicare for All5, refused corporate PAC money and made Matsui's PAC donations a central line of attack. CalMatters reported that Vang excoriated Matsui for accepting campaign contributions from companies that contract with ICE and the Department of Homeland Security3. Vang supports completely abolishing ICE.3

The campaign was harsh. Abridged, a Sacramento newsroom, reported that pro-Matsui ads have accused Vang of frequent absences from her local government positions6. Matsui's campaign also went after Vang for appearing on a livestream with the left-wing influencer Hasan Piker. Elk Grove News reported that a Texas-based super PAC raised $500,000 and financed television advertising attacking Vang7. On Vang's side, Justice Democrats PAC spends $304,000 backing Mai Vang7. The Sacramento Bee reported that Matsui had spent $2.9 million of her own money on her campaign8.

Voters with no candidate of their own may have mattered most. A late independent poll reported by the Bee found leads for incumbent Rep. Doris Matsui among undecided and Republican voters9. Earlier Bee reporting found that several Republicans in the district will reluctantly support Matsui to prevent Vang from winning the seat. Their opposition is largely rooted in concerns that Vang is part of a growing socialist movement.9

The district

Proposition 50, the 2025 measure that redrew California's House map, reshaped the seat. CBS Sacramento reported that the new district includes downtown and South Sacramento, Elk Grove, Galt and Lodi, and stretches east into El Dorado County, including El Dorado Hills and Placerville.5 On election night Matsui said 40% of her district is new10. CapRadio reported that her early margins over Vang were substantially larger than in Sacramento County11 in the San Joaquin and El Dorado portions, while Sacramento County, where the majority of the district lives11, is close.

On The Watch's wave map, which tracks the governor's race, the three counties moved very differently. Sacramento County went from D+19.7 for Kamala Harris in 2024 to D+29.5 for Xavier Becerra, a 10-point move toward Democrats. San Joaquin County, which Donald Trump carried by 0.9 points, flipped to D+16.4, a 17-point move. El Dorado County barely changed, going from R+12.0 to R+12.3. Weighted for the district, the move is +10.5. That is close to Becerra's 10.3-point gain on Harris statewide, where he leads Republican Steve Hilton by 30.4 points. These figures cover whole counties, and the district includes only part of each one.

The counts are also unfinished. Sacramento County has reported 355,971 votes, 59 percent of its expected total, compared with 78 percent in San Joaquin and 85 percent in El Dorado. County totals cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from changes in who turned out. These moves describe the electorate counted so far. They do not measure persuasion.

What the result means

With 66.99 percent of the expected vote in, about 86,000 votes remain by that estimate. To pull even, Vang would need about 61.5 percent of them. CapRadio reported that late-arriving ballots tend to favor the more progressive candidate, in this case Vang.12 Wesley Hussey of Sacramento State told the station that Vang could potentially catch Matsui in the Sacramento County portion of the district, but it is unlikely that she'll overtake Matsui altogether.12

Outlets described Friday's update in different ways. CapRadio said Vang has cut into11 Matsui's advantage. Elk Grove News said Matsui has widened her lead13 overall, even as the gap narrowed in the vote-rich Sacramento County portion13. Matsui has not declared victory13, and Vang has not conceded.

The count will go on for weeks. According to the California Voter Foundation, voters with signature problems have until November 25 to fix them. Counties must certify results by December 314. Either way, the seat stays Democratic, so the outcome does not affect control of the House. Democrats have 252 called seats and Republicans 161, with 22 not yet called, this one among them.
