The wave · the biggest counties · Hillsborough County, Florida

In Ashley Moody's home county, Hillsborough swings to Angie Nixon by nine points

Hillsborough County, home to Tampa, went from a narrow Trump win in 2024 to a 9-point win for Angie Nixon in the Senate race. Even so, Senator Ashley Moody, who grew up there, held the seat statewide.

What Hillsborough did

Moody spent Election Day in Hillsborough, her home county.1 She told supporters gathered at Hotel Haya2 in Tampa that Florida had chosen freedom. The county she spoke in voted the other way. Angie Nixon carried Hillsborough County 278,704 votes to 232,111, a margin of 9.0 points, with 99% of the expected vote counted. Nixon is the Democrat who has represented portions of the Jacksonville area in the Florida Statehouse since 2020.3 In 2024, Donald Trump beat Kamala Harris in the county by 3.1 points. That makes a 12-point move toward the Democrats, and it flipped the county.

Hillsborough was not just any county for the Republican. Moody, a Plant City native, entered the Senate in January 2025 following her appointment4 by Gov. Ron DeSantis, and her legal career includes service as a federal prosecutor and a Hillsborough County circuit judge.4 Statewide she won by 2.9 points in a state Trump carried by 13.1, a 10-point move. Hillsborough moved 2 points more than the state and ranked 12th of Florida's 67 counties. Sixty-six of those counties moved left, and the typical one moved 7 points.

Among Florida's biggest counties, Hillsborough landed in the middle. Miami-Dade moved 19 points, Broward and Orange 15 each, and Duval, Nixon's home, 12. Palm Beach moved 10 and neighboring Pinellas 6. Compared with big counties elsewhere that were about as close in 2024, Hillsborough's move was smaller. Maricopa in Arizona moved 20 points, San Bernardino in California 19 and Riverside 17. Only Nassau on Long Island moved less, at 4. The typical county nationally moved 14. Hillsborough also went the same way in the governor's race: Democrat David Jolly carried Hillsborough and Pinellas counties, while Republican Byron Donalds won Pasco and Polk.5

Who voted, and what drove it

Fewer people voted than in 2024: 517,016 Senate votes, 77% of the presidential total. A county count cannot tell voters who switched sides from voters who stayed home or turned out, so a 12-point move in a midterm electorate three-quarters the size of a presidential one mixes both. A week before the election, Supervisor of Elections Craig Latimer told WTSP that the numbers for registered Democrats and Republicans have remained relatively close, with Democrats slightly ahead in the county's early voting and mail ballot totals.6 He added that the balance has historically shifted as more people vote in person. Statewide, the Tampa Bay Times reported that DeSantis credited the proposal with driving out just enough of the GOP's voter base to keep all statewide elected officials Republican7. He meant Amendment 3, the property-tax cut.

The two campaigns made different cases. News4JAX reported that Nixon had described affordability as a central concern for Floridians, pointing to health care costs, home insurance and child care.8 Moody's campaign emphasized public safety, immigration enforcement, support for law enforcement and loyalty to President Donald Trump's agenda.9 Nixon also attacked Moody over Hope Florida. A leaked grand jury report, which was later ordered expunged by an appellate court, named Moody, who was state attorney general at the time.2 An October University of North Florida poll found independent voters favor Jolly by 16 points and Nixon by 15 points10. In the same poll, among independent voters, 38% said the messaging made them less likely to vote for Moody.10 In the other direction, twenty-five percent of respondents said Republican messaging about Nixon's ties to the Democratic Socialists of America made them less likely to vote for her10. These were statewide numbers, not Hillsborough's.

Local voters named their own concerns. On the first day of early voting, one Hillsborough voter listed them for WTSP: "There's healthcare, there's immigration, there's the AI data centers"11. The Tampa Bay Times primary guide asked candidates about data centers, property taxes, immigration, Tropicana Field and more.12

What it means for the county

The change reached down the ballot. The Times reported that Democrats Neil Manimala and Aileen Rodriguez took over two countywide seats held by Republicans in the fourth most-populated county in Florida13, which leaves four Democrats and three Republicans13 on the commission. That board will not last long in its current form. Voters also passed a referendum that would expand the number of seats on the commission to nine and eliminate countywide seats.13 Without countywide races, the commission's makeup will depend on how the new district lines are drawn.

Congressional results were mixed. U.S. Rep. Kathy Castor held District 14. WUSF reported that the district was shifted to absorb conservative-leaning voters in the southern and eastern parts of the county.14 Republicans Gus Bilirakis and Laurel Lee kept the other two House seats on Hillsborough ballots, and Voters also made the school superintendent an elected post.

The next test is local. Tampa Mayor Jane Castor is term-limited and cannot run for a third term15, and the city votes in March 2027. Columnist Stephanie Hayes's view in the Times was that Florida is closer than it has been in a long time.16 In Hillsborough, a Republican senator who grew up there lost her home county by 9 points.
