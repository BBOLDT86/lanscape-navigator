In redrawn California 40th, Ken Calvert leads Young Kim as count continues

Proposition 50 put two Republican incumbents in one Orange and Riverside County seat. With about 63 percent of the expected vote counted, Calvert leads by nearly eight points and has declared victory. AP has not called the race.

The new 40th District was drawn so that one of two Republican members of Congress would have to go. With about 63 percent of the expected vote counted, Rep. Young Kim is behind. Rep. Ken Calvert leads her 88,111 votes to 75,282, or 53.9 percent to 46.1 percent. The Associated Press has not called the race. Both finalists are Republicans, so the party margin on our map reads R+100. The real contest is the gap between them: 12,829 votes, or nearly eight points. By The Watch's arithmetic, Kim would need about 57 percent of the uncounted ballots to catch him.

Calvert declared victory on Friday, HeySoCal reported. He said "Election officials are still counting ballots, but the outcome of this race is clear."1 MyNewsLA reported that Kim had not released a statement as of Friday morning.2

Two incumbents, one seat

The race grew out of Proposition 50, which a majority of voters approved in a November 2025 special election1 and which cost Calvert his longtime 41st District. Calvert, of Corona, has been in Congress since 19933. He is the longest-serving Republican member of California's congressional delegation4 and chairs the Defense Appropriations Subcommittee4. Kim lives in Anaheim Hills, immigrated from South Korea as a teenager5, and has represented the 40th congressional district since 2023.5

The two came through an eight-candidate field in the June primary, with Calvert well out front.6 NBC Los Angeles reported that the two candidates drew similar levels of support in Orange County, but voters in the Riverside County portion of the district overwhelmingly backed Calvert.7 The Democrat in that field, Esther Kim-Varet, finished third8 and was eliminated.

Spectrum News reported that in the primary, each candidate cast the other as not 'MAGA' enough9 to win over Trump voters. By fall, the reporting diverged. KVCR, the Inland Empire's public station, said that Kim has tried to distance herself from President Trump's MAGA politics, while Calvert has gone all in.10 NBC Los Angeles found that both Republicans had been distancing themselves from President Trump in recent months7, and that Calvert has also reduced the prominence of Trump in his campaign messaging.7 Kim's case was that a Republican would hold the seat whatever happened, so voters should ask "what type of Republican do you want representing you?"9

After the primary, reporting showed that Kim raised nearly $8 million over the course of the primary, far more than Calvert's roughly $5.2 million.11 But Punchbowl News reported that defense contractors are funding a super PAC that is spending millions of dollars against12 Kim. The group is Americans 4 Security. Its filings show more than $3 million to oppose Kim and another $250,000 to support Calvert8. Households with registered Democratic and no party preference voters reported receiving the mailers tying Kim to Trump.8 Late in the race, a Calvert TV ad went after Kim for taking campaign contributions from a person who the FBI later identified as a registered agent of China. Kim ultimately returned the campaign funds.1 Kim called Calvert a "do-nothing" representative since beginning his congressional career in 19932. She also accused him of steering taxpayer-funded projects to within miles of his own rental properties1.

The district on the wave

The new district takes in Orange, Rancho Santa Margarita, Villa Park, Anaheim Hills and Mission Viejo in Orange County and Corona, Lake Elsinore, Canyon Lake, Wildomar, Menifee and Murrieta in Riverside County.4 Spectrum News called it the last Republican stronghold in Southern California following redistricting.9 Calvert's campaign said the district includes 51% of the current district he represents.3

Both counties moved toward Democrats in the governor's race. Statewide, Democrat Xavier Becerra beat Republican Steve Hilton by 30.4 points, 10.3 points better for his party than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin. Orange County went from Harris by 2.6 points to Becerra by 7.9, a 5-point move. Riverside County, which Donald Trump carried by 1.3 points, has gone to Becerra by 15.5, a 17-point move. Taken together and weighted, the district's two counties moved 8.9 points.

Two cautions apply. First, these are countywide figures, and the 40th covers only part of each county. They describe the area around the seat, not the seat itself. Second, a move is not persuasion. The county count cannot separate voters who changed their minds from changes in who turned out. Riverside County's number rests on 443,297 ballots, only 53 percent of its expected vote. Orange County has 940,690 ballots counted, 79 percent of its expected vote. And in this House race, Democrats and independents had only two Republicans to choose from.

What the result means

Whatever the final count, the seat stays Republican and one incumbent leaves Congress. If Calvert's lead holds, he would begin his 18th term in Congress2. Kim, who was first elected to Congress in 2020, would be out. Nationally, this is one of 22 House races AP had not called. Democrats have 252 seats called and Republicans 161, and this race cannot change that balance. Counting goes on in both counties, and the secretary of state's office says results will be certified by December 11, 2026.13
