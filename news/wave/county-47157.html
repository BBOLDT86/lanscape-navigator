Memphis's Shelby County moves left, but less than the rest of Tennessee

Marquita Bradshaw won Shelby County by 37 points, 12 points better than Kamala Harris did. Smaller counties in Tennessee moved further, and Memphis voted with its congressional district newly split three ways.

What Shelby County did

Shelby County, home to Memphis, cast more votes than any other county in Tennessee on Nov. 3. Its Democratic margin was wider than the one Kamala Harris won there in 2024. Marquita Bradshaw, the Democratic nominee for U.S. Senate, carried the county 167,623 to 75,272 over Republican Sen. Bill Hagerty. That is a margin of 37.2 points with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Harris won Shelby by 25.4 points, so the county moved 12 points toward the Democrats.

For Tennessee, that was a small move. All 95 counties moved left, and Shelby ranks 71st. Hagerty won the state by 12.2 points in a state Donald Trump carried by 29.7, a move of about 18 points. The typical county moved 14. Among the state's big counties, Davidson (Nashville) moved 17 points and Knox (Knoxville) 18. Hamilton (Chattanooga) moved 16 and flipped to D+2.6. Rutherford moved 22 points to a near-tie at D+0.2. Of the big counties, only suburban Williamson, at 9 points, moved less than Shelby. In 2024 Shelby and Davidson voted Democratic by similar margins. Now Davidson is about 8 points more Democratic.

Large counties elsewhere that were about as Democratic in 2024 mostly moved further: Wake, N.C., 22 points; Montgomery, Pa., 23; Queens, N.Y., 20; Franklin, Ohio, 14. Only Westchester, N.Y., at 5, moved less. One possible reason is that a county already voting Democratic by 25 points has fewer Republican voters left to win over. The county count alone can't confirm that.

Who turned out

Shelby cast 248,486 votes for Senate, 76 percent of its 2024 total. The Daily Memphian reported that Shelby County posted the highest early voting turnout total of the state's 95 counties1. That early vote outpaces 2022 but trails the 2018 early voter turnout for the same election cycle.1 On election night, the paper reported that the bluest county in the state came through for Democrats in the early and absentee2 count.

Turnout and changed minds can't be separated here. Shelby cast about 78,000 fewer votes than in 2024, yet the Democratic lead in raw votes grew from roughly 83,000 to about 92,000. County totals can't show whether that came from Trump voters switching, Republicans staying home or Democrats turning out at a higher rate.

Bradshaw is a tenth-generation South Memphis resident3 who lost to Hagerty in 2020. She did it with very little money: the Nashville Banner reported she had reported raising just $2,257.07 as of July 174. Hagerty ran with more than $13 million in contributions and a Trump endorsement5. Statewide exit polls reported by NBC News found that 47 percent of voters called the cost of living crucial to their vote, and Crucial (47%) 35% 62%6 split for Hagerty and Bradshaw respectively. That poll covers all of Tennessee, not Shelby County.

A county split three ways

Shelby voted under a new congressional map. In May, the Republican-controlled legislature split Memphis and Shelby County into three separate districts: Districts 5, 8, and 97. That followed a Supreme Court ruling that dealt a serious blow to the Voting Rights Act of 1965.8 Rep. Steve Cohen, a Democrat, decided not to run for reelection.9 A federal panel let the map stand, writing that "The road to a reliably 9-0 map runs through Memphis"10.

Under that map, the Daily Memphian reported, Republicans David Kustoff and Brent Taylor won razor-thin margins in gerrymandered congressional districts President Donald J. Trump won comfortably in 2024.11 In the 9th District, Taylor defeated state Rep. Justin Pearson. The Tennessee Lookout reported the day after the election that Pearson had not conceded, and the Associated Press declared Taylor the winner at 2:40 p.m. EST12 on Thursday.

The Memphis Safe Task Force, the law-enforcement effort Trump ordered into the city last year, was a running local issue. Taylor has also taken credit for helping the federal government bring the Memphis Safe Task Force to West Tennessee.13 In October, the Memphis City Council voted in favor of a nonbinding resolution calling for the removal of Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the Tennessee National Guard from the city.14 The two sides disagree about crime: U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche credited the task force with bringing crime to a 25-year low, while the council's resolution cited police figures showing crime in Memphis during the months before the task force deployment had already reached a 25-year low.14

Memphis also supplied the Democratic candidate for governor. The Commercial Appeal reported that Jerri Green, a Memphis City Council member, carries governor vote in Shelby County, falls to Blackburn overall15. Statewide, Republican Marsha Blackburn won by 2.5 points.

Shelby County still gives Tennessee Democrats a lot of votes. Its 167,623 votes for Bradshaw were about a fifth of her statewide total. But Shelby's Democratic voters are now split among three Republican-leaning districts. And this year, more of Tennessee's movement toward Democrats came from Nashville and the mid-sized and suburban counties than from Memphis.
