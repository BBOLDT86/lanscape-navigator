Paul LePage holds narrow lead in Maine's 2nd; Dunlap concedes

The former governor leads Democrat Matt Dunlap by 5,420 votes in AP's uncalled count but falls just short of a majority of all ballots, sending Maine's 2nd District to a ranked-choice count as Democrats sweep statewide.

On the night Democrats won Maine's races for Senate and governor, the state's 2nd Congressional District went the other way. Former Gov. Paul LePage, a Republican, leads state Auditor Matt Dunlap, a Democrat, 170,265 votes to 164,845 in the Associated Press count. That is a margin of 5,420 votes, or 1.6 points, with about 98 percent of the expected vote counted. The AP has not called the race. Dunlap conceded on Thursday: "This isn't the result we were hoping for, and I'm disappointed that we came short."1

The count is not formally finished. LePage's total is 363 votes short of half of the 341,256 ballots counted. That total includes 6,021 ballots left blank in this race and 125 votes for a write-in candidate, Gregory Ward. The one declared write-in brought ranked-choice voting into play even though only two names were on the ballot.2 That means a candidate needs more than 50% of all votes to win outright.3 Among ballots that marked a candidate, LePage has about 50.8 percent. The secretary of state's office says the ranked-choice count begins Monday, Nov. 9, in Augusta.4

The race

The seat opened when Jared Golden, a centrist Democrat who won four terms in the district by winning over Republicans willing to split their tickets, announced last November that he would not seek reelection.5 LePage, Maine's governor from 2011 to 20196, had already announced his campaign and faced no Republican primary challengers.3 He lost the 2022 governor's race to Janet Mills, although he carried the 2nd District in that race.5

Dunlap, 61, of Old Town, is a former secretary of state and onetime head of the Sportsman's Alliance of Maine.5 His primary was bitter. Maine Public reported that Joe Baldacci had the support of a super PAC tied to Democratic leaders in Washington7, while the secretive Real Change PAC spent more than $400,000, most of it opposing Baldacci.7 Dunlap said he did "not want super PAC support in this primary"8 and won the four-way race in a ranked-choice runoff in June.3

In the fall, Dunlap ran a progressive campaign, backing Medicare for All and a war powers resolution to end the Iran war.5 The Bangor Daily News reported that he attacked LePage over his age, his alignment with Trump and his support for parts of the Republican tax-and-spending law that cut federal Medicaid funding.5 He also questioned LePage's residency, prompting LePage to call himself a "snowbird" who spends winters in Florida.5 LePage emphasized his experience in government and his record of lowering taxes and creating jobs.9 The Kennebec Journal reported that he stuck with the president even on the Iran war, which has led to a spike in gas and heating oil prices.10

House Speaker Mike Johnson campaigned twice in Maine for LePage.5 In September, the Bangor Daily News counted $8.2 million in ad reservations from House Majority PAC backing Dunlap and $5.4 million from the Congressional Leadership Fund backing LePage.11 A group tied to Trump's political operation reserved at least $4.4 million more.11 LePage led in final polls from The New York Times/Siena College and the University of New Hampshire, but the margin on Election Day was slimmer.5

The district

The 2nd is a mostly rural district, the largest east of the Mississippi River12. It takes in ten whole counties and part of Kennebec, the only county split between Maine's two districts: 15 of Maine's 16 counties remain entirely within one district or the other.13 Augusta is on the 2nd District side. Kennebec's numbers below therefore include some towns in the 1st District.

Maine Public reported that Dunlap did best in the towns around Bangor, Augusta and Lewiston-Auburn and in the midcoast, while LePage won the vast majority of rural towns in northern, central and downeast Maine.14 LePage trailed early Wednesday before late returns from small, Republican-leaning rural towns put him ahead.5

The Watch's wave map compares each county's Senate vote this year with the 2024 Harris–Trump result, and it shows the same lean. Statewide, Troy Jackson's 6.3-point win over Susan Collins was 0.6 points less Democratic than Harris's margin. Across the 2nd District's counties, the weighted move was about 3 points toward Republicans. Ten of the eleven counties moved right, and none changed sides. Piscataquis moved most, from R+29.2 to R+36.2. Somerset, Aroostook, Kennebec and Franklin each moved about 4 points. Waldo County was the only one to move toward Democrats, by less than a point, and Hancock stayed Democratic at D+9.6. In Aroostook, The County reported that every part of the county went for Collins, including Jackson's native Allagash.15

Two cautions apply. First, the rural counts are incomplete: 54 percent of the expected vote is counted in Washington County, 68 percent in Somerset and 71 percent in Piscataquis. Second, a move is not persuasion. Totals like Penobscot's 71,245 votes so far cannot show whether people who voted for Harris chose Collins this time or whether a different group of voters turned out.

What the result means

If the tabulation confirms the lead, LePage takes a seat in a district President Donald Trump carried three times5. Ranked-choice runoffs have decided the race three times since 2018, and Golden won all three.14 This is the fourth time since 2018 that the 2nd District has needed one.16 LePage would be the only Republican in Maine's congressional delegation and in New England's House delegation.5

He would join the minority. The AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority. It has called 161 for Republicans, and 22 races are still uncalled. The Maine Morning Star noted that the 2nd District could be the only district in the country where Republicans flipped a seat17, and LePage said he is ready to work in a Congress controlled by Democrats.17 Dunlap said the ranked-choice count will not change the outcome.18
