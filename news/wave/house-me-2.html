LePage flips Maine's 2nd District as Democrats sweep the rest of Maine

Former Gov. Paul LePage beat state auditor Matt Dunlap by 1.6 points after a ranked-choice count, taking the seat Jared Golden is leaving and handing Republicans their lone major win in a state where Democrats otherwise ran well ahead.

Paul LePage, the Republican former two-term governor, will represent Maine's 2nd Congressional District. In AP's count, he finished with 170,890 votes to 165,432 for Democrat Matt Dunlap, a margin of 1.6 points on 336,322 votes. Those are the totals after the Elections division completed ranked choice tabulations1 on Thursday.

The rest of Maine went the other way. Troy Jackson unseated Republican Sen. Susan Collins by 6.2 points, close to Kamala Harris's 6.9-point statewide margin in 2024. Hannah Pingree won the governor's race by 17.0 points, a 10.1-point improvement on that baseline. LePage will be the only Republican in Maine's congressional delegation and the only one in New England's House delegation.2

The race

The seat opened when Democratic Rep. Jared Golden announced in November 2025 that he would not run again. He cited partisan gridlock and recent acts of political violence3 as his reasons. Dunlap had already been challenging Golden in the primary. He then won a four-way Democratic primary in June over Joe Baldacci, a state senator and son of a former governor. Baldacci was the establishment Democratic pick4. LePage did not face any primary opponents.5

Health care was at the center of the general election. Dunlap campaigned on Medicare for All. In the final debate, LePage defended his record as governor, when he vetoed five bills that would have expanded MaineCare, the state's Medicaid program.6 According to the Bangor Daily News, Dunlap attacked LePage over his age, his alignment with Trump and his support for parts of the Republican tax-and-spending law that cut federal Medicaid funding.2 LePage went after Dunlap's spending positions and, in debates, his record as state auditor. He was also pressed on the months he spends in Florida and answered, "Yes, I'm a snowbird."6

Republicans had more money on the air. The Daily News reported in mid-October that Republicans and their allies hold a $13.5 million to $10.1 million edge in general-election ads, according to AdImpact.7 Most of that came from the Congressional Leadership Fund and a group tied to Trump's political operation, with House Majority PAC the main spender for Dunlap. LePage led Dunlap by 5 points in final polls from The New York Times/Siena College and the University of New Hampshire, but the margin on Election Day was slimmer.2

The district

At more than 27,000 square miles, the 2nd District is the largest congressional district by area east of the Mississippi River.8 It takes in all of Androscoggin, Aroostook, Franklin, Hancock, Oxford, Penobscot, Piscataquis, Somerset, Waldo and Washington counties, plus 18 municipalities in Kennebec County, Augusta among them. Golden has held since 2019, in a district President Donald Trump carried three times.9

Reporting on the town results shows a split between city and country. Maine Public found that Dunlap performed best in the towns around Bangor, Augusta and Lewiston-Auburn as well as in the midcoast. LePage, by comparison, won the vast majority of rural towns in northern, central and downeast Maine10. The Maine Morning Star reported that in Lewiston, Auburn, Bangor, Orono and Augusta, Dunlap not only beat LePage — he outperformed other winning Democratic candidates Troy Jackson and Hannah Pingree.11 The Portland Press Herald found that LePage is slightly outperforming Theriault in these small, rural, Republican towns12. Austin Theriault was the Republican Golden beat in 2024.

The Watch's data for this seat has no county breakdown, so the wave map can't yet show how Aroostook, Penobscot or Androscoggin moved in this House race. Even county numbers would not show whether voters changed their minds or a different set of voters turned out, and the data block has no 2024 district turnout to compare with this year's 336,322 votes.

What the result means

This is a flip. The 2nd District race has been decided by a ranked-choice runoff three times since 2018, all of which were won by outgoing Democratic Congressman Jared Golden.10 The Republican win came in a year when Democrats have been called the winners in 254 House seats, against 169 for Republicans, with 12 still uncalled.

The outcome had been clear for a week. Dunlap conceded Nov. 5, a week before the result was confirmed.13 Secretary of State Shenna Bellows said the state was legally obligated to begin ranked-choice tabulation, despite Dunlap appearing to concede.14 Blank ballots and write-ins had kept LePage just short of a first-round majority. The Associated Press called the race Thursday, nine days after the Nov. 3 election.13

The results are still unofficial results from the secretary of state's office9. AP's results file lists 97.83 percent of the expected vote as counted, but the state's tabulation is finished. LePage spent the week in Washington throughout the week for new member orientation8. Asked how he would work in a Democratic-controlled House, he said, "If it helps Maine, I'm all-in. If it hurts Maine, I'm all-out."10
