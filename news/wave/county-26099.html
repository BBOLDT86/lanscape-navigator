Macomb County stays Republican for Mike Rogers, but by under two points

Michigan's big blue-collar suburb north of Detroit went from a 13.7-point Trump county in 2024 to a 1.8-point Rogers win, the second-largest swing toward Democrats among Michigan's 83 counties.

Macomb County was the Republican Senate nominee's to lose this year, and he did not lose it. Mike Rogers carried the county 209,003 votes to 201,477 for Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, a margin of 1.8 points. Two years ago Donald Trump won it by 13.7. Statewide, El-Sayed beat Rogers by 7.1 points to become, as Bridge Michigan and others reported, the first Muslim to be elected to the Senate1.

What Macomb did

With 99% of the expected vote counted, Macomb moved 12 points toward the Democrats. Every one of Michigan's 83 counties moved left. Macomb's swing was second only to Wayne County's 14 points, and well above the typical Michigan county's 6 and the state's own 8.5. Oakland County, Macomb's wealthier neighbor across Dequindre Road, moved only 4 points. Kent and Genesee each moved about 8 or 9.

By national standards the swing was middling. The typical county in the country moved 14 points. Big suburban counties that were about as Republican as Macomb in 2024 mostly moved further and flipped. Denton and Collin counties in Texas swung 22 and 23 points, Miami-Dade 19, and El Paso County, Colorado, 14. The closest match was Suffolk County on Long Island, which moved 11 points and went narrowly Democratic. Macomb moved about as far but started further to the right, so it stayed Republican.

Turnout fell sharply, as it usually does in a midterm. Macomb cast 418,419 votes, 82% of its 2024 total of 509,056, or about 90,600 fewer ballots. Statewide, Bridge Michigan reported, turnout appears to be a midterm record, topping the 4.5 million Michiganders who voted in 20222. Because of that drop, the county count cannot show how much of Macomb's swing came from Trump voters switching sides and how much came from who showed up and who stayed home. The result reflects both.

Why it moved

Local reporting described a county that moved without flipping. WXYZ-TV in Detroit reported that the county's own precinct map shows more blue/democratic leaning precincts in the 2026 Gubernatorial election compared to results from the 2024 Presidential election3. The station named Sterling Heights as a place where Democrats narrowed the gap, and it read this as suggesting a growing willingness among some suburban voters to support Democratic candidates3. Those findings come from the governor's race, not the Senate contest. Grand Valley State University political scientist Donald Zinman told WWMT that Rogers needed to really perform well in Macomb County. Rogers won Macomb, but by an underwhelming margin.4

The issues that reporting points to were economic. Bridge Michigan reported that a majority of voters chose El-Sayed to try to address high costs of living and put a check on the Trump administration5. Nationally, according to the AP Voter Poll, the majority said that Trump's policies drove costs higher6. In a county built around the auto industry, tariffs were close to home. The CBC reported that Michigan's automakers had been hit hard and quoted Republican strategist Jason Roe saying 'there is no question the president has hurt himself' politically in Michigan with the tariffs on Canadian goods7.

The open 10th Congressional District, which takes in Warren, Sterling Heights and Shelby Township, showed the same arguments. Democrat Christina Hines had campaigned on eliminating tariffs, lowering health care costs and getting corporate money out of politics8. Republican Michael Bouchard focused his campaign on supporting domestic manufacturing and auto jobs in the district, securing the southern border and addressing inflation9. Hines won, and The Detroit News reported that her victory flips the Macomb County-centered district from Republican to Democratic control10.

Rogers had advantages. The Detroit News reported that he and outside groups backing him outspent El-Sayed and his allies by roughly 3:211. According to Bridge, his campaign argued that El-Sayed's progressive views on law enforcement, healthcare and other issues were too extreme for Michigan5. Former Democratic rival Haley Stevens endorsed Rogers two days before the election5. None of that was enough to hold Macomb's 2024 margin.

What it means for Macomb

Macomb is still a Republican-leaning county at the top of the ticket. Its local politics also remain split. Democratic County Executive Mark Hackel won a fifth term in the Nov. 3 election12, and a countywide transportation millage also passed with solid support12. Two Macomb Republicans who ran statewide both lost. John James, who ran for governor instead of seeking re-election in his Macomb County district13, lost to Jocelyn Benson. NBC News reported that Garlin Gilchrist bested Macomb County Clerk Anthony Forlini, a Republican14, in the race for secretary of state.

Axios reported that El-Sayed won a larger share of the vote in Wayne, Oakland and Macomb counties than both Harris and Sen. Elissa Slotkin15. A final count, with about 1% of Macomb's expected vote still to come, will settle the exact margin. It will not settle how much of the swing came from voters changing sides.
