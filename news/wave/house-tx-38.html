Jon Bonck keeps Texas 38 Republican as Harris County moves left

Jon Bonck, a mortgage banker endorsed by Donald Trump, beat Democrat Melissa McDonough by 4.2 points in the west and northwest Houston suburbs. Harris County as a whole moved 27 points toward Democrats.

Republican Jon Bonck will succeed Wesley Hunt in Texas' 38th Congressional District. The Houston Chronicle's editorial board had called this west Harris County seat "among the Houston region's reddest districts."1 With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Bonck had 157,261 votes to 144,397 for Democrat Melissa McDonough. That is a lead of 4.2 points, or 12,864 votes, out of 305,101 cast. Alex McMenemy, the Green Party candidate2, had 3,443.

The Associated Press called the race for Bonck3 on the afternoon after Election Day. The Chronicle counted him among Houston-area Republicans who "barely survived the Democratic onslaught,"4 and noted that his district had voted for Trump by a wide margin two years ago4.

The race: a newcomer with Trump's backing

Bonck is a mortgage banker and a deacon and financial steward for Second Baptist Church5. The Chronicle reported that he had little experience in politics but won the support of many Republicans in Washington, including Trump, U.S. Sen. Ted Cruz and House Speaker Mike Johnson6. He easily beat Shelly deZevallos, a pilot and former vice chair of the Harris County Republican Party,6 in the May runoff. Hunt did not endorse anyone in the race.6 Houston Public Media reported that Bonck's campaign raised more than $1 million before the March primary and another $1.08 million between early April and early May, including $330,000 in loans from Bonck himself2. McDonough's campaign reported about $125,000 in total receipts through mid-October7, according to the Federal Election Commission.

The Hill reported that Bonck ran on tighter border security, gun rights, cutting spending on foreign wars and support for Trump's agenda8. McDonough is a Realtor from Tomball who also ran for this seat in 2024. Her campaign centered on restoring Congress as a check on the executive branch.9 The Chronicle's editorial board endorsed her. It wrote that her message appealed to voters in both parties, and that the administration's decision to go to war with Iran without congressional approval made it more pointed9. After the election, the Chronicle reported that voters across Texas had grown unhappy with the Trump administration over inflation and the war in Iran10.

The district: one slice of a county that moved 27 points

The 38th lies entirely inside Harris County. The Chronicle said it runs from the Galleria area out toward Katy and north through Jersey Village and Tomball9. It is a district of wealthier suburbs that also includes Cypress and Spring Branch6. Its current lines come from the map that Texas House Republicans pushed through in the summer of 2025, a mid-decade redistricting11.

The district has only one county on The Watch's wave map. Harris County voted D+5.5 for Kamala Harris in 2024 and D+33.0 in this year's Senate and governor vote. That is a 27-point move, on 1,382,875 votes with 99 percent counted. It is a little larger than the swing across the state. James Talarico beat Ken Paxton for the Senate seat by 12.1 points, and Gina Hinojosa beat Gov. Greg Abbott by 7.1, in a state Trump carried by 13.7. Houston Public Media reported that Democrats won the county's top offices, and that Letitia Plummer was elected county judge12.

Two cautions apply. First, the county figure covers all of Harris County, including central Houston. The 305,101 votes in this House race are only a little more than a fifth of the county's total, so the map cannot show how far the district's own precincts moved. What it does show is the gap: the county went D+33.0 while this part of it elected a Republican by 4.2 points. Second, a county's move is not proof that voters changed their minds. The clerk's office said the county broke its midterm turnout record13. A bigger or different set of voters can shift a margin without anyone switching sides, and the vote count alone cannot tell the two apart.

What the result means

The district was drawn after the 2020 census5. Hunt won it in 2022 and was its only representative until he left the seat open for a failed Senate run against John Cornyn6. No incumbent lost here, and the seat stays Republican. That matches the rest of the region. The Chronicle reported that Democrats held all four of their Houston-area seats and Republicans kept the other six14. One of the Democratic holds was the redrawn 9th District, where Leticia Gutierrez defeated Republican Alex Mealer4. Across Texas, Democrats won four of the five seats Republicans had targeted with the new map10.

Nationally, as of Saturday morning, 252 House seats had been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. Democrats are past the 218 needed for a majority.

The count in the 38th is almost finished. Bonck's 12,864-vote lead is far bigger than the share of the expected vote still outstanding. McMenemy's total equals about a quarter of the gap. Harris County's results are not final until they are sent to the state and county commissioners accept them, which the clerk's office expected around Nov. 1213.
