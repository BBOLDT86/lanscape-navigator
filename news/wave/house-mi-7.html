The House · Michigan 7

Will Lawrence unseats Tom Barrett in mid-Michigan's record-spending House race

The Sunrise Movement co-founder won Michigan's 7th District by 3.3 points. The Lansing-area seat returns to Democrats after one Republican term, with Ingham County moving furthest toward Democrats and Eaton County changing sides.

William Lawrence, a Lansing organizer who co-founded the Sunrise Movement climate group, unseated first-term Republican Rep. Tom Barrett in Michigan's 7th Congressional District. With about 98 percent of the expected vote counted, Lawrence had 202,657 votes to Barrett's 189,482. That is a margin of 3.3 points, or about 13,200 votes. Green Party candidate Shane Dedrick totaled 4,974 votes1.

WKAR reported that Barrett led through much of the night Tuesday, with Lawrence taking the lead for the first time around 2 a.m. after Ingham County began reporting results.2 The Associated Press called the race at 2:06 p.m. Wednesday.

The race

Lawrence also advocated for housing reforms as a founder of the MI Rent is Too Damn High Coalition.3 In the August primary he beat out former U.S. Ambassador to Ukraine Bridget Brink and former U.S. Navy SEAL Matt Maasdam4. Senator Elissa Slotkin, a more centrist Democrat, who previously represented the district, endorsed Maasdam.5 Lawrence let his membership with Democratic Socialists of America lapse in July6.

Barrett, an Army veteran and first-term congressman who previously served in the state Legislature, had portrayed Lawrence as too far left for the swing district.7 CBS News noted that Barrett was one of the first House Republicans to break with President Trump and vote on war powers resolutions to end the war with Iran8. He still campaigned with Trump over the summer and had his endorsement.

Data centers came up more than any other issue in local coverage. WKAR reported that Barrett introduced legislation to prohibit public officials from signing nondisclosure agreements related to data centers, prevent the federal government from overriding local land-use decisions and require developers to cover the cost of increased electric grid capacity.9 Lawrence wanted a federal moratorium on all data center development. The fight had local roots. The Livingston County Planning Commission voted against recommending the rezoning of 1,000 acres of farmland in Howell Township for a proposed data center.10

Bridge Michigan reported that Lawrence hammered Barrett on high prices under Trump, the Iran War and Barrett's changing stance on data centers.11 Barrett, for his part, described Lawrence's past support for defunding the police and loosening immigration restrictions as "extreme"12. Leaders from the Police Officers Association of Michigan and Fraternal Order of Police13 appeared with Barrett and House Speaker Mike Johnson at an October event. Lawrence said he had changed his mind since those now-deleted posts and now supports funding police along with community violence intervention and more mental health resources.13

Most of the money came from outside groups. According to AdImpact, spending on the race was the most in the country for any U.S. House seat14, at about $66 million. The Congressional Leadership Fund spent nearly $14 million supporting Barrett, House Majority PAC about $13 million for Lawrence, and the Trump-affiliated Safety and Affordability PAC $7 million on Barrett15. Lawrence's campaign spent almost $3.7 million, about $100,000 more than the Barrett campaign.15 In door-knocking, Lawrence's campaign said it had passed 200,000 doors, the most of any Democratic House campaign nationwide16. Barrett's campaign put its volunteers at about 64,000 doors, not counting outside groups' paid canvassers.16

The district

The district encompasses Lansing and East Lansing, which are considered Democratic strongholds, as well as Howell, a Republican stronghold.17 It covers all of Clinton, Ingham, Livingston and Shiawassee counties, most of Eaton County and parts of Genesee and Oakland counties.18

The Watch's wave map compares each county's vote this year for Senate and governor with its 2024 Harris–Trump vote. Every county here moved toward Democrats. Weighted by each county's share of the district, the move was about 5 points. Statewide the shifts were larger: Abdul El-Sayed won the Senate race by 7.1 points and Jocelyn Benson won the governorship by 10.8, gains of 8.5 and 12.2 points on the 2024 presidential margin. Ingham moved the most, 9 points, from a 30-point Democratic margin to 39. Eaton, which includes Barrett's hometown of Charlotte, was the only county to change sides, going from R+3.1 to D+3.5. Shiawassee moved 7 points and Clinton 6, and both stayed Republican. Livingston moved the least, about 4 points, and is still Republican by 20. The Oakland and Genesee figures cover each whole county, and only slices of those counties are in the district. Most of Oakland's 671,858 votes were cast outside it.

These are net shifts. The county counts cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from changes in who voted. Ingham cast 131,602 votes in this year's statewide races. WKAR found that Ingham County sees a notable increase, while Clinton County records a decline19 in turnout compared with the 2022 midterm. The station's figures put every county it examined below its 2024 presidential turnout.19

What the result means

Before Barrett won the seat in 2024, the district was represented by Democrat Elissa Slotkin, who ran for and won a U.S. Senate seat that same year.3 Barrett had also run against Slotkin in 202220 and lost. The win returns the Lansing-area seat to Democrats after one Republican term.18 The Detroit News reported that it was Michigan's third GOP-held U.S. House seat to fall6. Rep. Bill Huizenga was also ousted.12 Nationally, AP has called 252 seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.

Barrett said he had conceded to Lawrence shortly after 2 a.m.12 In a statement, he called this "the worst year for Republicans in decades"2 and said his race was closer than any other competitive district's.

About 2 percent of the expected vote was still outside AP's count on Saturday morning. All totals stay unofficial until county and state canvassers certify them.
