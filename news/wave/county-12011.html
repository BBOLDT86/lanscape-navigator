The wave · the biggest counties · Broward County, Florida

Broward gives Nixon a much wider margin on far fewer votes

Florida's most Democratic large county moved 15 points toward Angie Nixon from 2024, the state's fifth-largest swing, though about a quarter fewer people voted. It was not enough to overcome Ashley Moody's lead elsewhere.

What Broward did

Broward County is anchored by Fort Lauderdale and has a population estimated at 2,013,317 as of July 20251. In the Nov. 3 Senate race it gave Democrat Angie Nixon 433,399 votes to Republican Sen. Ashley Moody's 220,254, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. That is a margin of D+32.3. Kamala Harris carried the county by 17.0 points in 2024, so Broward moved 15 points toward the Democrats.

The swing did not change the statewide result. Moody, a former state attorney general, got the seat when Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis tapped her to succeed Marco Rubio when he became U.S. secretary of state in 20252. She won Florida by 2.9 points, 4,210,242 to 3,972,095. Nixon's lead of about 213,000 votes in Broward offset roughly nine-tenths of Moody's statewide lead of about 238,000.

Broward's move ranked fifth of Florida's 67 counties. That beat both the typical county (+7) and the state as a whole (+10), and 66 of the 67 counties moved left. Among the state's big counties, Miami-Dade swung harder: it went from R+11.4 to D+7.1, a 19-point move. Orange moved about as much as Broward (+15), while Palm Beach (+10) and Pinellas (+6) moved less. Elsewhere in the country, Broward's move was close to Pima County, Ariz. (+16), and to the typical county nationally (+14). It trailed Georgia's Gwinnett (+26) and Cobb (+19) and was far ahead of San Diego (+5), which started from almost the same 2024 margin.

Who voted, and who moved

Read the raw numbers with care. About 660,000 people voted in Broward's Senate race. That is 76 percent of the 2024 total, or roughly 214,000 fewer voters. Even so, Nixon's lead was about 64,000 votes bigger than Harris's. A county count can't separate Democrats who turned out at higher rates from Trump voters who switched sides. Broward's move is a mix of both.

Statewide evidence points both ways. WTSP reported that an analysis by the University of Florida's Fresh Take Florida project found registered Democrats voted at a higher rate statewide than registered Republicans3. Vice President JD Vance, defending his party's turnout operation, argued the bigger problem was that "many people who voted for Trump/Vance in 2024 voted for Democrats in 2026."4

Broward is one of Florida's most diverse counties. Census estimates put it at 35.0% Hispanic, 29.1% Black and 31.4% non-Hispanic white1. NBC News exit polling found that Puerto Rican and other Hispanic voters statewide favored Nixon, while Cuban voters favored Moody5. Next door in Miami-Dade, a Miami Herald precinct analysis cited by the Miami Times found that Nixon won overwhelmingly in majority-Black precincts6, while Hispanic-majority precincts continued to favor Republicans6. The Watch found no comparable precinct analysis for Broward.

Cost of living was the theme. WPLG Local 10 reported that the candidates for governor made campaign stops across South Florida addressing issues including affordability, the economy and property insurance7. In an October Sun Sentinel report on a poll from an organization seen as leaning Democratic8, Jolly led Donalds 2:1 in Broward County8. The same poll found Broward voters favored the property-tax amendment, but support drops when they are told about possible funding cuts to local services8.

Broward's election officials pushed to get mail ballots back. Supervisor of Elections Joe Scott told Local 10 that "as soon as we started having the those intake stations available at 30 locations across Broward County, we saw the number of returns in our vote by mail jump dramatically."9

What it means for Broward

Broward has long been where Democrats bank their statewide votes. David Jolly, the party's candidate for governor, has called it the county with the largest number of registered Democrats in the state10, and said "In many ways the governorship runs through Broward County."10 In 2022 the county pointed the other way. An analysis carried by the Orlando Sentinel called DeSantis's gains in Broward an ominous sign for any Democrat who thinks their party has a shot at winning statewide elections11. In the same piece, Florida Atlantic University political scientist Kevin Wagner called Democratic turnout "miserable."11

This year the county's margin grew sharply, but Democrats still lost both statewide races. Donalds won the governorship by 1.3 points and Moody the Senate seat by 2.9. NBC News reported that Moody largely ran a careful, traditional Florida Republican campaign12. Nixon, a democratic socialist from Jacksonville, had beaten the party establishment's pick in the primary. PBS NewsHour reported that her general election loss is unlikely to help heal the struggling state party's internal rift2.

Down the ballot, Broward's Democratic members of Congress held their seats. WLRN reported that Jared Moskowitz and Debbie Wasserman Schultz were among the winners13. Counting is not quite finished: the data behind this map shows 99 percent of Broward's expected vote counted.
