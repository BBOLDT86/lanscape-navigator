Oregon's Washington County moves left, but less than the state

Sen. Jeff Merkley widened the Democratic margin in the Silicon Forest suburbs west of Portland by about 3 points. That was a smaller gain than he made statewide, and a fraction of the national swing.

What the county did

Washington County is the suburban and farm county west of Portland that includes Beaverton and Hillsboro. In the Nov. 3 Senate race it gave Jeff Merkley, the Democratic incumbent, 161,201 votes to 71,337 for Republican David Brock Smith. With about 85 percent of the expected vote counted, that is a margin of 37.4 points. Kamala Harris carried the county by 34.0 points over Donald Trump in 2024, so the county moved about 3 points toward the Democrats.

Statewide, Merkley leads by 19.4 points with about 89 percent of the vote counted. Harris won Oregon by 14.4, so the state moved 5 points. All 36 Oregon counties moved left, and the typical move was nearly 5 points. Washington County ranks 25th. It moved about as much as its large Democratic neighbors: roughly 3 points in Clackamas and Lane counties and 5 in Multnomah. The biggest moves were in more Republican places. Marion County, which includes Salem, went from R+2.0 to D+5.4. Jackson County went from R+6.4 to R+0.2.

Next to similar big counties elsewhere, the move is small. Fairfax County, Va., moved 17 points; Mecklenburg County, N.C., 21; and Cuyahoga County, Ohio, and Los Angeles County 12 each. Only Contra Costa County, Calif., at 5 points, came close. Nationally, 2,673 of the 2,775 counties counted so far moved left, and the typical move was 14 points.

The Senate race was not close. Merkley has been Oregon's junior senator since 2009, previously served as Oregon House speaker, and was cross-nominated by the Working Families Party1. Smith is a state senator from Port Orford who represents much of the South Coast in the Legislature2. The Washington Post noted the seat was rated as solidly Democratic by the nonpartisan Cook Political Report3. The governor's race went differently. Gov. Tina Kotek beat Christine Drazan by 9.6 points statewide, which is 4.8 points worse for Democrats than Harris's 2024 margin. OPB said voters "spooked by President Donald Trump's overreach opted for the certainty of an unpopular governor"4.

Why: chips, data centers and immigration arrests

OPB calls Washington County the state's most racially diverse county5. It is also the center of the state's chip industry. The Hillsboro News-Times reported that though headquartered in California, Intel's largest presence is in Washington County6, and Intel has been cutting jobs there for two years. OPB reported that there are 29 data centers in Washington County, more than any other county in the state7. Opposition to them became a major campaign issue.

Federal immigration enforcement shaped the year. OPB reported, citing an analysis by the Portland Immigrant Rights Coalition, that the county had the largest share of immigration arrests in Oregon from July 2025 through the end of the year, accounting for 33.5% of statewide ICE arrests7. Hillsboro and the county both declared emergencies. Arrests rose again in October, when immigration enforcement stirred up again with reports of activity in Washington and Marion counties8. The day before the election, the Oregon Capital Chronicle reported that the county district attorney would review an incident in which a masked federal immigration agent appeared to slash the tire of a car belonging to two U.S. citizens9.

No county-level exit poll was published. In NBC News' national exit poll, more than half of voters (57%) said Trump's immigration enforcement actions have gone too far10, and voters blamed Trump for high prices10. Smith campaigned on costs, writing in the Capital Chronicle's voter guide that rising costs for groceries, fuel, housing, healthcare and energy are straining household budgets1.

The county numbers cannot show how much of the 3-point move came from voters changing sides and how much from who voted. So far the county has counted 240,561 votes, 82 percent of its 2024 total. More ballots are coming: Oregon counts ballots postmarked by Election Day that arrive within seven days11. A smaller, more Democratic midterm electorate could produce the same margin as voters switching parties.

What it means here

The county's main political fight this year was among Democrats and progressives, not between the parties. OPB described the county commission races as a broader test of the strength of a growing progressive movement in Washington County12. The chair race pits Commissioner Nafisa Fai against Commissioner Pam Treece. Fai has called for a moratorium on data centers. Treece, who is more moderate and business-friendly, has emphasized public safety and jobs. The seat is open because Kathryn Harrington resigned from her seat this fall after she made an obscene gesture toward Fai during a public work session13.

KGW reported that two key races remain too close to call as Pam Treece and Kipperlyn Sinclair hold leads14. Sinclair, a Hillsboro city councilor in the progressive camp, is running for the District 4 seat. The county must certify results by Nov. 30, according to its elections office. Until then, it is not known whether the county will be led by its moderates or its progressive wing.
