Baltimore County edges right as Maryland and the nation swing left

Wes Moore won Maryland's second-biggest county by votes cast comfortably. But he ran a point behind Kamala Harris's 2024 margin there, one of nine Maryland jurisdictions that did not move left. Many mail ballots are still uncounted.

Baltimore County wraps around Baltimore city, from the inner-ring suburbs of Dundalk and Catonsville to farmland near the Pennsylvania line. In 2025, there were about 847,700 people living in Baltimore County1. Census figures show 32.4%2 of residents are Black and 48.9%2 are non-Hispanic white. County planners describe a west side that is Majority Black, especially along Liberty Road corridor3, a predominantly white3 north and an east side growing increasingly diverse, especially in Parkville, Rosedale, and Rossville.3

What the county did

In AP's count, Gov. Wes Moore, the Democrat, led Republican Dan Cox 154,051 to 93,118 in Baltimore County. That is a margin of 23.5 points, with 81.51 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris carried the county by 24.5 points over Donald Trump in 2024, so the county moved 1 point toward Republicans. The move is small, but it runs against the rest of the map. Statewide, Moore won by 31.5 points, 2.7 points better than Harris. Fifteen of Maryland's 24 jurisdictions moved left, and Baltimore County ranked 18th. Nationally, 2,673 of the 2,775 counties counted moved left, by a typical 14 points.

The county's neighbors split. The Washington suburbs moved sharply left: Montgomery by 9 points, Prince George's by 7, Frederick by 8 and Howard by 5. Anne Arundel moved half a point and Baltimore city 1. Big counties elsewhere that Harris won by similar margins moved much further: Montgomery County, Pa., by 23 points, Wake County, N.C., by 22 and Queens by 20. Westchester County, N.Y., at 5 points, was the closest.

The comparison has limits. The 2024 baseline was a presidential race. This was a governor's race, and about one county ballot in 22 so far went to neither major-party candidate. The 258,935 votes counted equal 63 percent of the 2024 total. A county's move mixes who turned out with who changed sides, and these counts cannot tell the two apart. Counting also continues. Lavoie said about 22,000 mail-in ballots were canvassed before Tuesday's general election. Officials will resume counting the remaining mail-in ballots Thursday morning at the elections board headquarters in Owings Mills4, the Baltimore Sun reported, with counting likely to run into this week. WYPR reported that Democratic council candidates all lead in the mail-in ballots that have been counted so far.5

Why it moved

Capital News Service measured against a different benchmark, the 2022 Moore–Cox race, and found the same pattern. While Moore's margins narrowed in parts of the Baltimore area and the Eastern Shore, he matched or improved on his 2022 performance in the populous Washington suburbs.6 Baltimore County was where his margin fell furthest among the places he carried.

So far, local reporting has documented the slip more than explained it, and there is no county-level exit poll. Cox's campaign focused on costs. Patch reported that Cox pledged not to raise taxes or fees and criticized Moore over utility bills and projected budget deficits.7 At the debate, the Daily Record reported, Moore did not make that commitment Tuesday, nor did he pledge to serve all four years of his second gubernatorial term if reelected.8 Maryland Matters noted that in an April poll by the University of Maryland, Baltimore County, Moore's job approval fell below 50% for the first time, and 6 in 10 respondents indicated that they believed the state was heading in the wrong direction.9 Before the election, that poll's director, Mileah Kromer, said: "Our poll suggests that Dan Cox is not rebuilding the Larry Hogan coalition"10. In the statewide exit poll published by NBC News, the economy was the top issue for 40%11 of voters.

What it means in Towson

Further down the ballot, Democrats kept the top county job. Julian Jones made history Tuesday night when voters elected him Baltimore County's first Black county executive.12 WYPR noted that a Republican has not won a race for Baltimore County Executive since 1990.12

The new County Council is less settled. It is growing from seven seats to nine, and Republicans led in Districts 5, 8 and 9 on election night. WYPR reported that if the Republicans hang on, the Democrats will have only a slim 5-4 majority on the county council.5 Democrats were hoping that the national blue wave would reach down to the local level and they could pick up at least one additional seat.5 The Banner called District 5 the county's northernmost district13 and its most evenly divided, with registered Democrats and Republicans close to even.

On Question 3, the redistricting amendment Moore campaigned for, the county again sat in the middle. WYPR found that every county that falls within District 1 had more votes in opposition to Question 3 than in favor, except for Baltimore County14. These results remain unofficial until the county board certifies them after the final canvass.
