The wave · state by state · Louisiana

Every Louisiana parish moved left, but Julia Letlow held the Senate seat

Democrat Jamie Davis, a Tensas Parish farmer, cut Donald Trump's 22-point margin to 1.8 and flipped eight parishes. Some of the biggest swings came in the Republican suburbs of New Orleans and Baton Rouge.

Julia Letlow is going to Washington as the first Republican woman elected to the Senate from the state1. But the map under her win moved further than the nation's did. The Republican congresswoman beat Democrat Jamie Davis 745,963 to 720,106, a margin of 1.8 points, in a state Donald Trump carried by 22.0 in 2024. All 64 parishes, Louisiana's word for counties, moved toward the Democrats. The typical parish moved 18.6 points. The typical county nationally moved 14.1.

The race was close enough that the Associated Press did not call the race for Letlow until 99% of votes had been counted.2 About 99 percent of the expected vote is in. A few rural parishes are still short, including Grant at about 94 percent. Davis conceded on Wednesday3.

A closed primary, a farmer and a data center

Letlow had first beaten her own party's senator. Trump endorsed her against Sen. Bill Cassidy, and CNN reported that Cassidy's primary loss showed Trump's ability to exact revenge against Republicans who cross him4, since Cassidy had voted to convict Trump in 2021. Axios New Orleans noted that the state's new closed party primaries also gave Davis more time to build name recognition5.

Davis, a third-generation farmer and former Tensas Parish police juror, campaigned on lowering costs, expanding access to health care and addressing economic challenges1. He also kept returning to a story from the Gulf States Newsroom. Its investigation found that Letlow signed an NDA with Meta in June 2024 over its $50 billion data center in Richland Parish. She later bought Meta and Nvidia stock and did not disclose the trades for more than a year6. Letlow's spokesperson said she had no role in directing her trades and that the NDA played no part in them. Letlow declined repeated interview requests from the Gulf States Newsroom and did not debate Davis.6 WWNO reported that Davis raised $5 million and had more cash on hand than Letlow heading into the final weeks6. The Louisiana Illuminator reported that Trump took no part in the race until a "tele-rally" for Letlow on the eve of the election2.

Where the map moved

The biggest swing was in St. Bernard Parish, just downriver from New Orleans. It went from Trump by 29.3 points to Davis by 0.1, a 29-point move. Next door, Jefferson Parish flipped from Trump by 12.9 to Davis by 7.4. On Fox 8, state House Democratic leader Kyle Green Jr. said President Trump's policies, the price of gas and the elimination of a majority-Black Congressional seat all fueled Davis' success.7 A Republican parish council member, Jennifer Van Vrancken, called it a "one-off."7

Outside Baton Rouge, Ascension Parish moved 25 points, to a Republican margin of 8.9. Livingston moved 23 points but still gave Letlow a 45.1-point margin, among the cushions her 25,857-vote margin of victory8 rested on. In the Cajun prairie, Evangeline moved 28 points. Around Davis's home, Tensas went from Trump by 4.3 to Davis by 22.3, and neighboring Concordia and Catahoula moved 24 and 28 points. Richland Parish, where the Meta data center is going, moved 21 points. The count cannot say why.

The smallest moves came in the parishes that were already Democratic and in the northwest. Orleans moved 10 points, to a Democratic margin of 77.2, and East Carroll also moved 10. Caddo (Shreveport) moved 13, and Bossier, across the Red River, moved 14.

Who moved: the red parishes, and who showed up

Parishes are not voters, and the parish count cannot tell people who changed their minds from people who stayed home. About 1.47 million votes were cast, 73 percent of the 2024 total.

The charts point one way. The 40 parishes Trump won by 30 points or more cast 53 percent of the vote and moved 19.6 points when weighted by votes. The 14 he won by 10 to 30 moved 19.9. Orleans, the one parish Kamala Harris won by more than 30, moved 10.2. Mid-sized parishes, with 10,000 to 50,000 votes, moved 19.8. The seven largest moved 17.0, and the smallest moved 17.9.

Turnout tells a similar story. The four parishes that kept 80 to 95 percent of their 2024 turnout moved just 10.4 points. Three of them, Orleans, East Carroll and Claiborne, were among the state's smallest movers. Tensas, Davis's home, is the exception: it kept 85 percent of its 2024 vote and moved 27 points. The parishes in the broad middle, keeping 65 to 80 percent, cast 89 percent of the vote and moved 19.1. St. Bernard and Livingston kept about two-thirds of their 2024 vote and were among the biggest movers. Across all parishes, the link between turnout change and the move is modest but negative (r = -0.23): where turnout fell more, the parish tended to move more.

Louisiana reporting fits a turnout story. KPLC compared six southwest parishes and found that Letlow received about 60 votes for every 100 that Donald Trump got in 2024. Over the same period, Democratic votes in the area barely moved.9 LSU's Robert Hogan told WBRZ that turnout in African American communities and parishes was higher than in previous elections.10 There is also some evidence of persuasion. Before the election, pollster John Couvillon estimated that 10% to 20% of Republican early voters defected to Davis11. The Advocate reported that Davis could pull off a major upset by winning at least 28% of the White vote. He apparently fell short of that.12 Both things may have happened. The parish numbers fit a sagging Republican turnout more than mass defections, but they cannot rule either out.

Davis won't rule out run for Louisiana governor13. Former New Orleans Mayor Mitch Landrieu told Axios that Gov. Jeff Landry's 2027 race "is in play."5
