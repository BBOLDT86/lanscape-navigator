The wave · the biggest counties · Dane County, Wisconsin

Dane County hands Crowley 80 percent, most of his statewide margin

Madison's county cast about 54,000 fewer votes than in 2024 yet gave David Crowley a raw margin slightly bigger than Kamala Harris's, a 9-point move left that tracked the state's swing almost exactly.

What Dane County did

Dane County, home to Madison, the state Capitol and the University of Wisconsin's flagship campus, gave Democrat David Crowley 250,376 votes in the Nov. 3 governor's race. Republican Tom Tiffany got 60,840. That is about 80 percent to 20, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Crowley's margin came to 60.9 points. In 2024 Kamala Harris beat Donald Trump in the county by 51.7, so the county moved 9 points toward the Democrats.

That is close to the state's own swing. Crowley won Wisconsin by 8.0 points, an 8.9-point move from Trump's narrow 2024 win. Dane ranked 15th of 72 counties by move, and 71 of the 72 moved left. Milwaukee County moved further, 14 points, but its D+52.0 margin still trails Dane's. Waukesha County, the big Republican suburb west of Milwaukee, moved 5.

Compared with similarly Democratic big counties elsewhere, Dane's move was on the small side. It was close to Montgomery County, Md. (10) and Alameda County, Calif. (8), and well behind Fulton County, Ga. (14) and Philadelphia (20). The typical county nationally moved 14 points, though the most lopsided places had less room to move in points.

The points hide the raw arithmetic. Dane cast 311,216 votes, 85 percent of its 2024 total, yet Crowley's lead of about 189,500 votes was a little larger than Harris's. Dane alone supplied close to nine-tenths of Crowley's statewide margin of about 213,300. The returns are unofficial until the canvass.

Why it moved

It is a fast-changing place. The Wisconsin Department of Administration put Dane County's population at 621,365 residents as of January 1, 2026, an increase of nearly 60,000 people since the 2020 Census1, and approximately 84% of Dane County's population growth since 2020 has come from people moving into the county.1

Local reporting points to turnout first. Isthmus reported that more people voted than in the 2022 governor's race, and that Tiffany received 1,460 fewer votes in Dane County than Michels did in 20222. WPR noted that even though overall voter turnout is much lower in a midterm than a presidential election, Crowley's margin over Tiffany in both counties eclipsed Harris'.3 Still, a county's move mixes who turned out with who changed sides. With 85 percent of the 2024 electorate voting, the county count cannot separate the two.

Dane was not Crowley's natural base. The Cap Times reported that Madison state Rep. Francesca Hong, a democratic socialist, carried the county in the August primary. Crowley, who was painted as the moderate candidate in the race, picked up wins in 44 of Dane County's 60 municipalities, largely in more rural parts such as Black Earth and Cambridge.4 Data centers were the local sore point. A county supervisor in the rural northeast told the paper constituents don't yet see a clear distinction between Crowley or Tiffany on data centers5, and that neither backed the moratorium the County Board had passed. Hong's voters came around anyway. On Election Day Hong repeated her call to Democratic voters who were upset over her loss2 not to write her in, and Isthmus profiled a DeForest organizer who had worked for Hong and took Hong's advice and backed Crowley.2

Statewide forces pushed the same way. The Associated Press reported that Tiffany was weighed down by the president's record-low approval numbers in Wisconsin6. In the exit poll published by NBC News, voters split evenly between the economy (33%)7 and the state of democracy (33%)7 as their top issue. Crowley won the democracy voters by a wide margin, and Tiffany narrowly won those who picked the economy. Crowley ran a campaign on making Wisconsin work for every family and tackling housing, childcare and access to healthcare8, the Cap Times reported.

What it means

The gains reached past Madison. According to the Cap Times, two of the seats Democrats managed to flip in the Nov. 3 election were in Dane County9. In the 17th Senate District, state Rep. Jenna Jacobson of Oregon beat Sen. Howard Marklein of Spring Green. In the 51st Assembly District, Ben Gruber of Arena beat Rep. Todd Novak of Dodgeville. Those seats helped give Democrats an 18-15 majority in the state Senate and a 53-46 majority in the Assembly, according to unofficial results10.

Some Republicans are already arguing over the lesson. On WPR, former Gov. Tommy Thompson said his party keeps fighting against Madison11, even as the county grows faster than anywhere else in the state and votes about 80 percent Democratic.
