The wave · the biggest counties · Chester County, Pennsylvania

Chester County gives Shapiro a 36-point margin, ousts its last GOP legislators

Gov. Josh Shapiro won Philadelphia's western suburb by 36.5 points, 22 more than Kamala Harris did in 2024. That was slightly less than Pennsylvania's swing, and about a quarter fewer voters cast ballots.

Chester County is the westernmost of Philadelphia's suburban counties, and it is one of the richest and best-educated places in the state. According to the Census Bureau, 57.1% of residents 25 and older hold a bachelor's degree or higher, and the median household income is $127,2081. USAFacts reports that Chester County ranked 1st in median household income among counties in Pennsylvania from 2020–20242. By votes cast in 2026 it is the fifth-biggest county in Pennsylvania and the 87th-biggest in the country.

What Chester County did

Democratic Gov. Josh Shapiro beat Republican state Treasurer Stacy Garrity 164,083 to 75,744 here, a margin of 36.5 points, with 98.95% of the expected vote counted. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county by 14.5 points over Donald Trump, so the move was 22 points. Statewide, Shapiro won by 22.1 points in a state Trump had carried by 1.7, a move of 23.8. Chester County therefore voted about 14 points more Democratic than Pennsylvania as a whole but moved slightly less than the state did. It ranks 43rd of 67 counties by move. All 67 counties moved left, and the typical move was 23 points.

Among the other suburban counties around Philadelphia, Bucks moved 26 points, Montgomery 23 and Delaware 22. Philadelphia itself moved 20 points. So did Lancaster, Chester's western neighbor, which went from R+15.9 to D+4.4. Outside Pennsylvania, big counties that Harris won by similar margins moved less: Cobb in Georgia moved 19 points, Pima in Arizona and DuPage in Illinois 16 each, Orange in Florida 15 and Hamilton in Ohio 10. Nationally, the typical move was 14 points.

The electorate was also much smaller. The county cast 242,354 votes, 75% of its 2024 total of 324,964, or roughly 82,600 fewer. County-level returns can't separate voters who changed sides from voters who skipped a midterm, so the 22 points reflect both. The count is also not final: the county scheduled the official computation of the votes cast & the provisional votes cast3 to begin Nov. 6.

Why, according to the reporting

The Watch found no poll that covered Chester County on its own. Statewide surveys showed Shapiro winning support well beyond his own party. In Quinnipiac's October poll, he carries 95% of Democrats, 17% of Republicans, and leads among independents 62% to 23%4.

The Philadelphia Inquirer described a very lopsided campaign. Garrity ended the race with around $11 million — a fraction of Shapiro's more than $60 million war chest5. The paper reported that for months, she was unable to purchase TV ads, while Shapiro was spending millions defining her6 as the data centers' biggest fan. An Inquirer/New York Times/Siena poll found a quarter of GOP voters in the final weeks of the campaign still did not know enough about Garrity or her platform to form an opinion of her6.

At a polling place in East Bradford Township, Inquirer reporters found voters were split on their views7 of the governor. One lifelong Republican said "Shapiro hasn't done a god darn thing but make everything worse"7. Another East Bradford voter, Doug Swartz, said he cast a vote for "future President Shapiro."6

Republicans who talked to WHYY explained the losses in longer-term terms. Valentino DiGiorgio, a former state party chair, said the result followed a trend "accelerated by not only demographic changes but also the presidency of Donald Trump."8 Former U.S. Rep. Ryan Costello said that swimming against the currents of voter registration trends and changing demographics is not something that can be accomplished in one election cycle8.

What it means for the county's politics

The results down the ballot went the same way. WHYY reported that Democrats on Tuesday ousted all Republicans from the Pennsylvania legislature in Chester, Delaware and Montgomery counties for the first time ever, according to unofficial results8. In Chester County, businesswoman and former public school teacher Madeelyn Alvarino defeated Rep. John Lawrence, R-Chester, who has represented his district since 20119. Elizabeth Moro beat Republican Craig Williams in the 160th District, which crosses into Delaware County. U.S. Rep. Chrissy Houlahan earned another term, according to the AP, in Pennsylvania's 6th Congressional District, which includes all of Chester County into southern Berks County up through Reading. She beat Republican challenger Marty Young.10 Across the state, Democrats flipped the Pennsylvania state Senate, achieving their elusive "trifecta"11 for the first time in 48 years.

The local news site MyChesCo published an editorial blaming the county Republican Party. It said Republicans failed to win a single contested partisan race in Chester County's election-night report12 and noted that they ran no candidate at all in two state House districts. Peter Urscheler, the Democratic county chair, said his party would bring that same commitment to next year's county, municipal, and school board elections13. Those off-year races usually draw an even smaller electorate, and it is not yet known which party that will help.
