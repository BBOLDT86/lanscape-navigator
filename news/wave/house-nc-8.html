The House · North Carolina 8

Mark Harris keeps North Carolina's 8th by 5,179 votes as Cooper surges

In a district seen as safe for Republicans, a first-time Democrat with little money held the incumbent to 50.4 percent, as Roy Cooper's Senate run moved every county from southeast Mecklenburg to Lumberton toward Democrats.

The race

Rep. Mark Harris, the Republican from Indian Trail, located in Union County1, has won a second term by a narrow margin. He got 147,094 votes to 141,915 for Democrat Colby Watson. That is a lead of 5,179 votes, or 1.8 points, with 98.86 percent of the expected vote counted. Bo Whitehead, whom Axios Charlotte described as a Green Party member2 (AP lists him as an independent), drew 2,890. AP has called the race for Harris. The North State Journal reported Harris saying that he "received a call from Colby Watson conceding the race"1.

Few people expected a close race. In late October, Axios Charlotte wrote that Harris faces a potentially easier path to a second term in the heavily Republican district.2 The morning after the election, it called the race unexpectedly competitive3. Watson had never held office. WFAE described him as a small business owner of about 37 whose key issues are to regulate corporate ownership of homes, prevent medical debt from bankrupting people, improve education for children and help the state's farmers4. He defeated Kevin Clark and Jesse Oppenheim in the Democratic primary5 in March. Harris is a member of Freedom Caucus6, and he ran on strengthening border security, reducing federal spending, supporting agriculture and expanding parental rights in education.4 He told Axios that if his district and his party ever conflicted, he would "stand with my district over my party."2

The two campaigns had very different amounts of money. Federal filings through mid-October show Watson raised $57,5907, while Harris raised $1,019,1348.

The Senate race at the top of the ballot looks like the bigger force. NPR reported that Cooper focused his campaign on affordability issues.9 AP's voter survey found that most voters in North Carolina thought Whatley was "too supportive" of Trump.10 AP also found Cooper won a larger share of conservative rural and small-town voters than Democrats typically muster.11

The district

The 8th District runs from southeast Mecklenburg County to Lumberton12. It includes all of Anson, Montgomery, Richmond, Scotland, Stanly and Union counties, as well as parts of Cabarrus, Mecklenburg and Robeson counties.1 Charlotte suburbs sit at one end, and the small towns of the Sandhills and the Lumbee country of Robeson County at the other.

In the Senate race, all nine counties moved toward Democrats compared with Trump's 2024 margins, by about 22 points on a weighted average. Union is home to both nominees: Watson is a Union County native4. It went from R+25.3 to R+2.8 on 100,583 votes. Cabarrus moved 25 points, from R+7.7 to D+17.4. Richmond, Scotland and Anson also flipped to Cooper. Robeson's 27-point move was the largest, from R+27.7 to R+0.6. The Border Belt Independent noted that generations of Robeson County voters, including members of the Lumbee Tribe of North Carolina, reliably picked Democratic candidates13 before the county turned toward Republicans. This year, Democrats there saw less success13 than statewide but narrowed the margins in many races.13 Stanly, the most Republican county, still went R+31.6 after a 19-point move.

Two cautions. First, these are whole-county Senate results. Our data does not break the House vote down by county. Mecklenburg alone cast 439,955 Senate votes, more than the 291,899 cast in this whole House race, so its D+53.6 mostly reflects Charlotte, not the district's part of the county. Second, a move is not the same as voters changing their minds. A total like Union's 100,583 cannot tell voters who switched sides apart from new voters or Republicans who stayed home. Statewide, both seem to have happened. WUNC reported that Republicans made up 26% of early voters this year, compared with 31% four years ago14, while unaffiliated voters made up a larger share.

What the result means

Harris's road to this seat ran through the 2018 fraud case. That year, an election panel declined to certify these results following allegations of ballot fraud involving McCrae Dowless, a Republican political operative employed by the Harris campaign.15 A new election was held without Harris. Dan Bishop1 won it, and Harris was not charged.15 He came back in 2024 after Bishop chose to run for attorney general of North Carolina15.

He held one of the more heavily leaning Republican districts in the state1 on a night when WFAE reported that a Democratic challenger beats Tim Moore12 in the 14th District and Democratic challenger Jamie Ager flipped a Republican-held district in western North Carolina.16 In WSOC's live coverage, Harris caught up to challenger Colby Watson after the first wave of early voting results.17 Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.

The count is not quite finished. AP's 98.86 percent suggests about 3,400 votes are still out, fewer than Harris's lead. WRAL reported that election officials in Robeson County, Hoke County and Scotland County18 are reviewing provisional ballots in a close state Senate race. All race results are unofficial1 until they are certified.
