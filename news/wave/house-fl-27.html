Salazar Leads by 1,090 Votes in Miami's 27th as Florida Recount Looms

Rep. Maria Elvira Salazar, the Republican who broke with Trump on immigration, is inside Florida's automatic-recount margin against former CBS Miami anchor Eliott Rodriguez. Her district sits in a county that moved 19 points toward Democrats from 2024.

Four days after Florida's polls closed, the Miami seat held by Republican Rep. Maria Elvira Salazar still has no declared winner. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, AP has Salazar at 124,528 votes and Democrat Eliott Rodriguez at 123,438. That is a gap of 1,090 votes out of 247,966, or 0.4 points, and AP has not called the race. NBC 6 reported that the final count by the Miami-Dade Canvassing Board on Friday morning had Salazar ahead of Rodriguez by 1,090 votes1. Florida law requires a machine recount when the margin in the unofficial returns is half a percentage point or less.2

Two anchors, one ad

The candidates have similar backgrounds. Florida Politics noted that both are American-born children of Cuban expatriates who built award-winning journalism careers and became familiar TV anchors3. Rodriguez spent 25 years at CBS Miami3. Where they split was immigration. In September, NPR reported, Salazar stood outside the White House in an ad and told President Donald Trump that "some of your immigration enforcement efforts have gone too far."4 Trump hit back by sharing a post in which another user called her a "back stabber."5 NBC News reported that he eventually endorsed her before Election Day6, and that her district has been particularly impacted by Trump's harsh immigration enforcement, including a record number of deportations of Cubans.7

Rodriguez argued that the ad can't erase her funding votes8 for ICE. His other main issue was affordability: he told CBS Miami that the cost of living crisis9 was the issue he heard about most. Rodriguez accepted invitations to three televised debates, but Salazar's campaign did not respond.3

Salazar started with far more money. Newsweek reported that as of the July 29 filing, she had about $2.26 million in cash on hand, against roughly $325,000 for Rodriguez10, though Rodriguez outraised her by more than $300K last quarter11. The DCCC added Rodriguez to its Red to Blue program, and House Majority PAC reported plans to spend nearly $2 million on TV and $800,000 on digital ads.3 Florida Politics later reported that the PAC pulled $750,000 in spending out of12 the district. On election night, Salazar declared victory at a Key Biscayne restaurant13, and WLRN reported that she called the race a "warning" to her party14.

A county that moved

In WLRN's description, the district runs from Little Havana down the coast to Cutler Bay and westward into Kendall15. It also covers parts of Miami, Coral Gables, Kendall, Key Biscayne and Pinecrest and is roughly 74 percent Hispanic10. The 27th sits entirely in Miami-Dade County.16

The Watch's wave map measures each county by its 2026 Senate and governor vote. On that measure, Miami-Dade went from R+11.4 in the 2024 presidential race to D+7.1, a 19-point move. The state moved much less. Republican Ashley Moody won the Senate race against Angie Nixon by 2.9 points, and Byron Donalds beat David Jolly for governor by 1.3. Those are moves of 10.2 and 11.8 points from Trump's 13.1-point Florida margin in 2024.

But the county's number is not the district's. Newsweek reported that the Miami Herald credited majority-Black and majority-white neighborhoods for powering the Democratic wins, while Hispanic-majority precincts stayed Republican16. Axios Miami added that they did so by smaller margins than in previous elections17. In the 27th, almost three in four district residents is Latino.18

The county count also cannot separate persuasion from turnout. Miami-Dade's statewide races drew 784,728 votes with 99 percent counted. The Miami Times reported that participation was higher this year than in any Miami-Dade midterm election in more than two decades.19 The totals cannot show how much of the 19-point move came from voters switching sides and how much from who turned out.

What's still undecided

Salazar won the seat in 2020 by defeating incumbent Democratic Rep. Donna Shalala20, and NBC News noted that she and Trump comfortably won the district in 2024.7 Now she leads by less than half a point. NBC 6 reported that Florida's secretary of state will now order a recount that could happen as early as Monday or Tuesday morning.1 The state's timetable requires machine-recount results by 3 p.m. Thursday, Nov. 12.21 A manual recount would be required if the margin narrows to 0.25 percentage points or less.22

The count is also in court. WLRN reported that Miami-Dade elections chief Alina Garcia refused Friday to recuse herself, and hours later Rodriguez filed a federal lawsuit asking a judge to force her to step down23. The suit says her son-in-law, attorney Robert Fernandez, is the lawyer representing Salazar.23 It also cites Garcia telling the Miami Herald on election night that "Maria Elvira is the winner, with or without a recount"24. Salazar accused Rodriguez of "behaving like an election denier"1 and said she welcomes a recount.

Control of the House does not depend on this seat: AP has called 252 seats for Democrats, more than the 218 needed. Reporting since the election finds Democrats divided over what happened. Some think Salazar's saving grace may have been distancing herself from Trump.25 Others feel like victory was just an investment away.25 The race stays uncalled until the recount is finished and the state certifies the result.
