Salazar holds her Miami seat by 1,086 votes after Florida recount

Republican Maria Elvira Salazar broke with President Trump on immigration and narrowly beat former TV anchor Eliott Rodriguez. The race went to a recount. Statewide Republicans also won by much less than Trump did in 2024.

Republican Maria Elvira Salazar has kept Florida's 27th Congressional District. She won 124,519 votes to 123,433 for Democrat Eliott Rodriguez, a margin of 1,086 votes out of 247,952. That is 50.2 percent to 49.8 percent, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. WLRN reported that the result was close enough to trigger a mandatory recount1, and that the Associated Press declared Salazar the winner at 5:47 p.m. EST Tuesday1, Nov. 10, a week after Election Day.

The race

The race between Salazar and Rodriguez, two former TV anchors with Cuban roots, landed amid a nationwide immigration crackdown that's sent shockwaves throughout South Florida2, the Florida Tribune wrote. Rodriguez worked as an anchor at CBS4 in Miami1 and was running for office for the first time.

Immigration shaped the campaign. In September, Salazar ran an ad in which she told President Donald Trump that some of his immigration enforcement had "gone too far." She also said Hispanic voters who helped elect him "feel betrayed."3 Florida Politics reported that the ad drew attacks from the President and conservative commentators, some of whom called for her deportation4. Rodriguez questioned whether she meant it, pointing to her vote for legislation that substantially increased funding for U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement5.

Rodriguez also campaigned on the cost of living. He made Miami's high housing costs a campaign issue, proposing to build workforce housing on underused federal land6. He backed restoring Affordable Care Act subsidies, a pressing concern in a district with unusually high Obamacare enrollment7. National Democrats put money behind him. National Democrats added Rodriguez to their Red to Blue program, and the House Majority PAC booked more than $2.7 million in TV and digital advertising to support him6. His own campaign amassed more than $1.56 million4. In October, Cook Political Report, Fox News and Sabato's Crystal Ball shifted CD 27 from "Likely Republican" to "Lean Republican."5

The district

The 27th encompasses Coral Gables, Cutler Bay, Key Biscayne, Palmetto Bay, Pinecrest, South Miami, West Miami and Miami neighborhoods south of the Dolphin Expressway in Miami-Dade County6. The Washington Post reported that the district is 74 percent Hispanic8.

The whole seat lies inside one county, and the results behind this page do not split it by neighborhood. The county map therefore cannot show where in the district Rodriguez gained. Around it, the shift was large. Axios Miami reported that Miami-Dade voters favored Democratic candidates for U.S. Senate and governor9. It also found that Hispanic voters in the county sided with the GOP — though by smaller margins than in previous elections9. Statewide, Republican Ashley Moody won the Senate race by 2.9 points and Byron Donalds won the governor's race by 1.3. Trump carried Florida by 13.1 points in 2024, so Democrats gained about 10 and 12 points.

That gain does not prove that Trump voters changed sides. A count of 247,952 votes in this district cannot separate people who changed their minds from changes in who voted. The Miami Times reported that countywide turnout was higher this year than in any Miami-Dade midterm election in more than two decades10.

What the result means

The incumbent survived. Salazar took the seat in 2020 by claiming victory over Democratic incumbent Donna Shalala11 and kept it in 2022 and 2024. WLRN noted that this year's race is a far cry from 20241, when she won comfortably. On election night, WLRN reported, Salazar said voters were telling her party it needs to course-correct on the economy and on immigration12.

The recount was disputed. Rodriguez's lawyers argued that Miami-Dade's elections supervisor, Alina Garcia, could not be neutral. They cited Garcia's statement to the Miami Herald on election night that Salazar had won "with or without a recount."13 They also pointed to Garcia's daughter and son-in-law serving as attorneys for Salazar's campaign.13 U.S. District Judge David Leibowitz denied Rodriguez's emergency request6 to keep her out of the process. Salazar accused Rodriguez of "behaving like an election denier"14 but said she welcomed a recount.

The count is not quite final. Recount returns must be completed and submitted by 3 p.m. Thursday, Nov. 1215. Florida requires a hand recount only if the recount leaves the margin within 0.25% of the total vote15. The current margin is about 0.4 percent. Rodriguez said he would have no further comment on the election or its outcome until all the votes are counted and the final results are certified.16 Nationally, 254 seats have been called for Democrats and 167 for Republicans, with 14 still uncalled. That puts Democrats past the 218 seats needed for a majority. Salazar's seat stays on the Republican side.
