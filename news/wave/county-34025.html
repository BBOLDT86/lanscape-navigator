Monmouth County, a Shore GOP anchor, votes for a Democratic senator

Trump won the Jersey Shore county by 11 points in 2024. Two years later Cory Booker carried it by 6, on a turnout about two-thirds the size of 2024's.

Monmouth County voted for Donald Trump by 11.4 points in 2024. On Nov. 3 it chose Democratic Sen. Cory Booker over Republican Justin Murphy, 122,670 votes to 107,876. That is a margin of 6.3 points, with 97.44 percent of the expected vote counted. The Asbury Park Press reported that for the first time since 1988, a Democratic candidate for U.S. Senate won Monmouth County1. Booker had never done it himself. Before the election, the New Jersey Globe noted that Booker has never won Monmouth County in any of his Senate contests.2

What Monmouth did

The 17.7-point move to the left is bigger than the typical county's nationally, about 14 points. In New Jersey, though, it was middling. All 21 counties moved left, and Monmouth ranked 13th. The state as a whole went from D+5.9 to D+27.3, a move of 21.4 points. Ocean County, just to the south, moved 27.4 points but stayed Republican, going from R+36.0 to R+8.6. Middlesex, to the north, moved 27.7. Morris, the other big Trump-won suburban county, moved less: 14.9 points, from R+2.7 to D+12.2. Outside the state, Miami-Dade started from the same R+11.4 and ended at D+7.1. Suffolk County on Long Island moved 10.7 points.

Monmouth is a wealthy county. Census estimates put its population at 651,0353, with a median household income of $124,8453, against about $103,556 for the state. Before the vote, the Globe described the county as a sprawling political anchor stretching from deeply Republican Shore communities to conservative inland towns2.

The Press's town-by-town review found that Murphy still carried Middletown, Howell, Manalapan and Freehold Township1, while Marlboro, the county's third-most populous town, flipped to Booker. Among towns with more than 10,000 registered voters, the biggest shift came in Wall1, which Murphy still won.

Who voted, and why

About 235,000 votes were cast, roughly 65 percent of the 360,143 cast in 2024. A county's move mixes two things the count cannot separate: voters who changed sides, and voters who stayed home or turned up. Here about 125,000 fewer ballots were cast. The Press's reading was that independent swing voters and motivated suburban Democrats turned Monmouth County purple-to-blue overnight4. Looking at the national results, Rutgers' Ashley Koning said Democrats appear to have run several points ahead of their final polls, likely due to both stronger Democratic turnout5 and weaker Republican turnout.

Pocketbook issues dominated. The Asbury Park Press found affordability was the most common concern among voters it interviewed in Toms River and Freehold.6 In NBC News' exit poll of New Jersey voters, the economy was named the most important issue more often than any other, ahead of the state of democracy7.

The two campaigns were far apart in money and visibility. Booker focused his campaign on the cost of living, pushing a proposal called the Keep Your Pay Act8, and tied economic concerns and other issues to President Donald Trump.9 Murphy is a former Tabernacle Township official. After the June primary, The Hill reported that his campaign has negative $24 in cash on hand10, while Booker had more than $22 million. ABC7 reported that Koning called Murphy's low visibility a factor, but she also said national political dynamics could play a larger role in determining voter turnout than candidate name recognition.11

What it means at the county hall

The change reached county government. Democrats Chelsey Tanner and Sofia Ugarte defeated Republican Commissioner Erik Anderson and Middletown Mayor Tony Perry12, and Democrat Anthony Cherry, a criminal defense attorney, defeated Republican Surrogate Maureen Raisch12. The Globe reported these were the party's first countywide victories in Monmouth since 200812, ending a Republican hold on all five commissioner seats that dated to 2011. Republicans keep a narrow 3-2 majority12. The results will remain unofficial until certified by the Monmouth County Clerk's Office.13

Local Republicans held up better than Murphy did. The Press found that in Middletown, the Republican county slate outperformed Murphy1, and it pointed to name recognition. U.S. Rep. Chris Smith, whose 4th District takes in part of the county, was the only victorious Republican14 in New Jersey's congressional races. The Red Bank-based Two River Times noted that most races for federal offices and municipal and school board positions maintained the status quo15.

The result also cuts against what local observers had seen until recently. In August, the Globe wrote that Democratic local gains have not translated into stronger performances at the top of the ticket. In many respects, Monmouth has become redder.2 About 2.5 percent of the expected vote was still uncounted when these figures were compiled.
