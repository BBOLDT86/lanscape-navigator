The wave · the biggest counties · Gwinnett County, Georgia

Gwinnett swings 26 points to Ossoff, outpacing Atlanta's other big suburbs

Georgia's second-biggest county gave Jon Ossoff a 43-point margin over Mike Collins, up from Kamala Harris's 16.5 in 2024. Fewer people voted, and Democrats took every seat on the county commission.

What Gwinnett did

On Nov. 3, Gwinnett County gave Sen. Jon Ossoff 245,140 votes and Rep. Mike Collins 98,695. That is a Democratic margin of 42.6 points with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Two years earlier, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 16.5. The 26-point move was the fourth-largest of Georgia's 159 counties, and every one of those counties moved toward the Democrats. It was also about six points bigger than the state's own move, which took Georgia from Trump by 2.2 to Ossoff by 18.0.

Gwinnett also moved more than the other big metro counties. Cobb started almost exactly where Gwinnett did and moved 19 points. Henry moved 23. Fulton moved 14 and DeKalb 13, though both began far more Democratic. Outside Georgia, counties that voted about the same way in 2024 moved less: San Diego by 5 points, Broward by 15 and Pima by 16.

Gwinnett is now a county of roughly a million people, and many of them were born abroad. Census Bureau figures put the population at 1,018,0991 in 2025, with 28.4 percent foreign-born and 27.5 percent non-Hispanic white. Before the election, CNN put Gwinnett among the "suburban melting pots," noting its large Black, Asian and Latino population northeast of Atlanta2.

Who turned out, and why

Fewer people voted this time. The Senate race drew 343,835 votes, 82 percent of the 2024 total and about 77,000 fewer. Even so, Ossoff's margin of about 146,000 votes was roughly double the 69,000 or so by which Harris won. The county totals can't tell Trump voters who stayed home apart from voters who switched sides, so the move mixes the two.

Turnout reporting before Election Day pointed in different directions. During early voting, Gwinnett's acting elections supervisor, Kelvin Williams, told WSB Radio "It's record breaking for this midterm"3. But the Gwinnett Daily Post reported that the county was trailing nearly all of its metro Atlanta neighbors4 in the share of voters who had already cast ballots. An Atlanta Journal-Constitution analysis found that early voting in Cobb and Gwinnett counties are running about 15% ahead of the same stage of the last midterm election. The GOP bastions of Cherokee and Forsyth counties are experiencing even greater spikes.5 Advancing Justice-Atlanta, an Asian American advocacy group, cited an Emory University political scientist's analysis showing early voting rose 63% among Asian American and Pacific Islander (AAPI) voters and 105% among Latine voters6 statewide compared with 2022.

On issues, Bloomberg reported that Ossoff benefited from Trump's shrinking popularity in the state where, as elsewhere, high fuel and food prices were top issues for voters7. The ABC News Voter Poll found that roughly 6 in 10 independents voted for Ossoff8, and that 39 percent of white voters backed him, compared with 29 percent for Raphael Warnock in 2022. NPR reported that Collins carried personal baggage9 that turned away independents and some Republican-leaning voters. In the CBS News exit poll, more voters view Colllins' views as extreme than Ossoff's views.10 Collins was not a stranger to the county: his 10th District covers eastern portions of Gwinnett County11, according to the Gwinnett Chamber.

The Georgia Recorder spoke to one Gwinnett voter, Conrad Gill, who backed Ossoff and Keisha Lance Bottoms, fearing that Jackson would be too closely tied to Trump and his policies.12 Emory's Andra Gillespie told the Recorder that the data shows Democrats overperforming among independent voters in this election cycle.13

What it means for Gwinnett's politics

The Democratic gains reached far down the ballot. The Gwinnett Daily Post reported that five Republican lawmakers from Gwinnett's legislative delegations lost their re-election bis on Tuesday and a sixth GOP lawmaker is trailing by a small enough margin that a recount could be requested.14 That sixth race is not settled.

On the county commission, WSB-TV reported that a 22-year-old Democrat, Benjamin Culberson, flipped the last Republican seat on Gwinnett commission15 by unseating Matthew Holtkamp. CNN, citing WSB-TV, said the commission will now be made up of all Democrats.16 Culberson said the national wave helped him. He also said local issues mattered, too. Republicans in north Gwinnett County often invited him to talk about taxes, traffic and data centers.15

That leaves the county's whole commission in Democratic hands, in a place the AJC's Patricia Murphy described as part of the suburbs once considered the cradle of the state's modern Republican Party17.
