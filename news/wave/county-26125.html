Oakland County Gave El-Sayed a Wider Win but Moved Less Than Michigan

Michigan's second-biggest county voted Democratic by a larger margin than in 2024. But it shifted only half as much as the state, and local reporting traced much of the gap to a few heavily Jewish suburbs.

What Oakland County did

Oakland County covers the suburbs north and west of Detroit. It voted for Democrat Abdul El-Sayed over Republican Mike Rogers by 378,723 votes to 282,488 in Michigan's open U.S. Senate race, with 99% of the expected vote counted. That is a margin of D+14.3. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by D+10.6, so the county moved about 4 points toward the Democrats.

In another year that would count as a solid gain. This year it ranked Oakland 74th of Michigan's 83 counties by move. All 83 counties moved left. The typical county moved about 6 points, and the average weighted by votes was about 8. El-Sayed won the state by 7.1 points, an 8.5-point swing from Trump's 1.4-point win in 2024. Jocelyn Benson's race for governor against John James moved the state 12.2 points. Next door, Wayne County moved 14 points. Macomb County moved 12, cutting a Republican margin of 13.7 points to 1.8.

Big suburban counties elsewhere that were about as Democratic in 2024 mostly moved much further. DuPage County outside Chicago moved 16 points, and Cobb County outside Atlanta moved 19. Erie County, New York, was the exception at about 1 point.

Oakland is one of the country's wealthier big counties. The Census Bureau estimates its population at 1,288,3371, with a median household income of $97,7601, compared with $72,8751 for Michigan as a whole.

Why it moved less

Local reporting kept returning to one community. In March, a man drove a truck into Temple Israel in West Bloomfield Township and exchanged gunfire with security personnel before killing himself, authorities said.2 El-Sayed condemned the attack, but he was criticized after saying "Hurt people, hurt people" about the attacker's relatives, who had been killed in an Israeli airstrike in Lebanon.2 He apologized in August3.

Bridge Michigan reported that Rogers heavily courted Jewish voters turned off by El-Sayed's opposition to US support for Israel4 and by those comments. According to Bridge, unofficial results showed Rogers did improve his vote share in some Oakland County communities with heavy Jewish populations, but he did not gain enough ground to make up for Democratic shifts4 elsewhere. Politico's precinct-level analysis found El-Sayed ran well behind other Michigan Democrats in places with more Jewish voters5, including Huntington Woods and West Bloomfield. The Forward reported that the Republican Jewish Coalition spent $2 million targeting about 50,000 Jewish voters, primarily in Oakland County6.

A Mercury Public Affairs survey of Jewish early voters, reported by the Forward, found El-Sayed received 60% of the early Jewish vote in the Senate race, compared with 79% for Democratic gubernatorial nominee Jocelyn Benson.6 El-Sayed's campaign disputes how large the effect was. Its post-election memo touted an outside analysis finding that he only ran slightly behind Slotkin's 2024 performance in predominantly Jewish precincts.5 The Forward also noted that Jewish voters make up an estimated 1.4% of Michigan's electorate6, so they cannot account for the whole county's result on their own. Axios found El-Sayed's gains over Harris and Elissa Slotkin were smallest in Oakland County7 among the Detroit-area places it compared.

The campaign's last weekend also ran through Oakland. Rep. Haley Stevens, who lost the Democratic primary to El-Sayed, announced she was endorsing Rogers, who she once slammed as a rubber stamp for President Trump.8 Her district includes part of Oakland County and Wayne County.9 WDIV found mixed reactions in Birmingham: El-Sayed supporters called her a traitor, and some Rogers backers were surprised but pleased.

These numbers cannot say how many voters changed sides. Oakland cast 671,858 votes in the Senate race, 87% of its 2024 total and about 100,000 fewer votes. The Clarkston News, citing the county clerk's office, reported that turnout was above average for Oakland County10 but down from November 2024. Statewide, NPR reported that El-Sayed motivated young and infrequent voters11. A county's move mixes who turned out with who switched parties, and the totals here cannot separate the two.

What it means for the county

Oakland remains reliably Democratic. Fox 2 noted that the county has voted for the Democratic candidate in the previous two Senate elections as well12. Democrat Jeremy Moss won the 11th District and will succeed Democratic U.S. Rep. Haley Stevens in the Oakland County district.13

The county's place in the Democratic coalition did change this year. Speaking to WWMT, the analyst Zinman said El-Sayed made up for weaker results in Oakland by outperforming Rogers in Wayne, which is not only Michigan's largest county, but also home to Dearborn.14 Statewide, Benson won by nearly 4 points more than El-Sayed, which suggests some voters split their tickets rather than turning toward Republicans. The Forward's survey points the same way: Among Jewish voters who voted for Rogers, 61% said they voted mainly against El-Sayed rather than for the Republican.6 In an interview with Politico, El-Sayed acknowledged Israel was a source of disagreement, but said he's not changing his position.15
