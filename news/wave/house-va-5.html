McGuire holds Virginia's 5th by two points as Perriello comeback falls short

Every county and city in the district moved toward Democrats in the Senate race, by 12 points on average. That was not enough for Tom Perriello, who lost to Rep. John McGuire by 7,714 votes in a seat Democrats last won in 2008.

Tom Perriello is the last Democrat to represent the 5th Congressional District, having served one term from 2009 until 20111, and he spent this year trying to win it back. He lost by 7,714 votes. Rep. John McGuire, the Republican incumbent, had 181,418 votes to Perriello's 173,704, or 50.2 percent to 48.0. That is a margin of 2.1 points, with 98.12 percent of the expected vote counted. Cooke Harvey, an independent, drew 5,781. VPM reported that The Associated Press declared Republican incumbent John McGuire the winner in Virginia's 5th Congressional District just before noon on Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026.2 In a statement Thursday afternoon, Perriello said he had called McGuire to concede.2

The race

McGuire is a former Navy SEAL and state legislator from Goochland County3. He won the seat last cycle after defeating Good in a contentious Republican primary3. The Washington Post noted that Perriello is from a prominent local family4 and lost the seat in 2010 after voting for the Affordable Care Act4.

Most of the campaign was about prices. VPM reported that Perriello said ending the Iran war and the president's tariff powers5 would bring costs down, while McGuire blamed high fuel costs on regulations that slow capacity.5 Farmers who organized for Perriello cited rising fuel costs, rural healthcare cuts and the impact of tariffs6. McGuire has criticized Perriello's support of the Affordable Care Act7, and he also painted Perriello as too far left8.

The race also got personal. Perriello said McGuire profited from stock trades in companies that had business before the committees he serves on9. McGuire told CBS19 that his investments are managed through retirement accounts and that he does not direct the trades10, and he would debate only if Perriello apologized. McGuire previously told ABC13 on Aug. 27 he would only agree to a debate if Perriello apologized for accusing him of insider trading.9 One Perriello ad pointed out that McGuire was the sole no vote in the state senate against raising the state's minimum marriage age from 16 to 1811. WWBT checked that claim against records on the General Assembly website11 and found it true.

Perriello had more money. Cardinal News reported that he outraised the incumbent $3.4 million to $1.9 million as of Sept. 3012. According to Roll Call, the top House super PACs haven't engaged in the race so far, the outside group Our Virginia has spent $1.8 million opposing McGuire13. The Virginia Public Access Project counts $7,271,63014 in independent expenditures in the race. A Washington Post–George Mason University poll put McGuire 6 points ahead of Perriello, just outside the margin of error.12

The district

The district covers a vast stretch of Central and Southside Virginia, running from Charlottesville and Albemarle County through Lynchburg and Danville and south toward the North Carolina border.15 It also extends east into parts of Goochland, Hanover and Powhatan counties near Richmond.15 Bedford County is mostly in the 9th District next door16. Charlottesville, Lynchburg and Danville are independent cities and are not part of any county. Perriello ran up big margins in Albemarle County and Charlottesville. But those numbers were canceled out by far more conservative areas in other parts of the district.17

On The Watch's wave map, all 24 of the district's counties and cities moved toward Democrats between the 2024 presidential race and this year's Senate race. The average move was 12 points. Statewide the move was 16.9 points, as Sen. Mark Warner won by 22.7. Danville moved the most, 16 points, from D+21.1 to D+37.0, with Fluvanna close behind. Four places that Donald Trump carried went for Warner: Lynchburg, Fluvanna, Nelson and Prince Edward. The smallest moves came in Charlotte (8 points) and in Amelia and Appomattox (9 each). Appomattox and Bedford still voted Republican by more than 40 points.

Two cautions apply. First, these are Senate figures for whole counties. The 24 places cast about 436,000 Senate votes, compared with 361,612 in the House race, partly because several of them are split with other districts. Second, a move is not the same as persuasion. The count cannot tell voters who switched parties apart from changes in who turned out. In October, Cardinal News reported early voting up strongly in Democratic areas while it's flat or down in many Republican areas18. On election night, Perriello cited surging support in rural areas and among African-American voters19.

What the result means

Perriello won the seat in a 2008 upset but lost his reelection bid two years later. Republicans have held it since, although the district's boundaries have changed considerably.20 The lines could have changed again this year. Under the redistricting amendment invalidated last month by the Supreme Court of Virginia — a ruling later left in place by the U.S. Supreme Court — Democrats had expected the district to become friendlier to their party. Instead, the race will move forward under the current court-drawn congressional map from 2021.15

In other Virginia districts, Cardinal News reported, two Republican incumbents21 lost. McGuire held on even though his father, John McGuire Jr., passed away just days before election day.8 Rick Buchanan, the outgoing 5th District Republican Committee chair and an outspoken McGuire critic, blamed the close result on the congressman's failure to energize local Republicans20. A Cardinal News analysis reached a different conclusion: the 5th is simply a hard district for a Democrat to win16.

Counting is not finished. Perriello's campaign said provisional and absentee ballots were still out and that it expected McGuire's margin to narrow, but it no longer sees "a path for changing the outcome."22 About 93 percent of Charlottesville's expected vote is counted. The city's registrar told Cville Right Now that counting of provisional ballots, by law, will begin Tuesday.23 Nationally, 252 seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.
