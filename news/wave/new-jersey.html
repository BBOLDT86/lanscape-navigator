The wave · state by state · New Jersey

Booker's 27-point win moved all 21 New Jersey counties left

Cory Booker turned Kamala Harris's 5.9-point 2024 margin into a 27-point win and carried seven counties Donald Trump had won. The biggest moves came where turnout fell furthest: Hudson, Passaic and Ocean.

Cory Booker, the Democrat, won a third full term in the U.S. Senate on Tuesday. With 96.36% of the expected vote counted, he led Republican Justin Murphy by 27.3 points, 1,819,769 votes to 1,023,549. The Associated Press declared Booker the winner at 8:00 p.m.1, as soon as polls closed.

Two years ago Kamala Harris carried New Jersey by 5.9 points. The New Jersey Globe called that the narrowest Dem presidential margin since 19922. Booker's margin is 21.4 points better for his party. Every one of the state's 21 counties moved toward the Democrats. The typical county moved 18.5 points, ahead of the typical county nationally at 14.1.

Where the map moved

Hudson County moved the most. Harris carried it by 28.1 points; Booker carried it by 59.7, a 32-point move. The Globe had noted that in 2024 Hudson recorded the largest swing toward Donald Trump of any county in New Jersey3.

Passaic County, next door, came close behind. Trump won it by 2.9 points in 2024, and CBS News reported that this made him the first Republican to win the presidential vote there in over 30 years4. Booker won it by 27.9, a 31-point move. Middlesex moved 28 points. So did Ocean, the state's most Republican county. Trump won Ocean by 36.0 points, and Murphy won it by only 8.6.

Seven Trump counties went to Booker: Passaic, Morris, Monmouth, Hunterdon, Gloucester, Atlantic and Cumberland. Monmouth went from Republican by 11.4 points to Democratic by 6.3. Murphy carried five counties: Ocean, Cape May, Sussex, Salem and Warren. He won Warren by just 1.3 points, after Trump won it by 20.7. Cape May moved the least, 11 points.

Down the ballot, Rebecca Bennett defeated Rep. Tom Kean Jr. in the 7th District, and Cape May Mayor Zack Mullock defeated Rep. Jeff Van Drew in the 2nd District.5 WHYY reported that in Monmouth County, Democrats marked their first countywide victories since 2008.6

The campaign and the mood

It was never a close race. Murphy is a little-known attorney who emerged from a crowded field in his party's June primary1, and a former committeeman in Tabernacle, a small town in the Pine Barrens of Burlington County.7 Federal Election Commission filings show his committee raised $68,188.878 in total. NJ.com described him as more openly conservative and Trump-supporting than recent Jersey Senate candidates.9

The mood around the race mattered more than the race itself. A Fairleigh Dickinson University poll released six days before the election put Trump's approval in New Jersey at 29%, with 66% of voters disapproving of his job performance10. NJ Spotlight News reported that rising gas prices during the Iran war emerged as their biggest pressure point.11 In the same poll, 90% of voters surveyed for the FDU poll said affordability was important to their vote this year11. During the campaign, Murphy said he supported the war because he believed Iran would use a nuclear weapon if it had one.12

Republicans largely agree on why they lost. State chair Christine Hanlon told the New Jersey Monitor that on top of the usual midterm backlash, "you have a war, you have high gas prices"13. State Sen. Jon Bramnick, a longtime Trump critic, said Trumpism "just doesn't work in New Jersey"13.

Who moved, and why the count can't fully say

Counties are not voters. A county's margin can move because people changed their minds, or because different people showed up. These counts cannot tell the two apart. Turnout fell a long way: 2,918,975 votes were cast, about 68% of the 4,272,725 cast in 2024.

Where turnout fell the most, the move was biggest. Three counties cast fewer than 65% of their 2024 vote: Hudson, Passaic and Ocean. Together they moved 30 points, weighted by votes. The other 18 counties moved 19. Across all 21, the correlation between turnout change and move is -0.70.

That pattern fits Trump's 2024 voters staying home at higher rates than Democrats. But it does not rule out people switching. In Hudson, Booker's lead was roughly 89,000 votes, compared with Harris's roughly 65,000, even though about 83,000 fewer people voted. CBS News studied Passaic County after the 2025 governor's race and concluded that many of Trump's Latino voters stayed home, while others switched sides, and new voters broke heavily Democratic.14

By 2024 result, the moves fall at both ends of the map. Ocean, Trump's best county, moved 27 points. Bergen and Middlesex, the two big counties Harris won by under 10 points, moved 24. The bluest, Essex and Mercer, moved 18, and they had the least room to grow. Monmouth, Cape May, Sussex, Salem and Warren, which Trump won by 10 to 30 points, moved 17. By size, Bergen alone, the one county with more than 250,000 votes, moved 21. The 16 mid-sized counties moved 21 as well. The four smallest, with 5% of the vote, moved 16.

The data block has no census breakdown for New Jersey. Reporting points to Hispanic communities. Hudson and Passaic have the state's largest Latino shares, and CNN reported that Latinos make up roughly 43% of the population15 in Passaic. NJ.com quoted a Democratic official who said Latino voters "have abandoned President Trump, and that's largely over ICE."16 That is one explanation, not something the county counts can show.

Counting is not finished. Somerset (93.37%), Mercer (93%) and Passaic (94%) have the most expected votes still to report, so these figures may still shift slightly.
