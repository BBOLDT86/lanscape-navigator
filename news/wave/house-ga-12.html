Rick Allen holds east Georgia seat as Columbia County moves toward Democrats

Rick Allen, the Augusta Republican, beat Democrat Ceretta Smith by 4.2 points while the 24 counties of the 12th District moved about 16 points toward Democrats in Georgia's Senate and governor's races.

Rick Allen has held Georgia's 12th District since 20151, and on Tuesday he held it again. WJBF in Augusta said this win was much closer than his previous races2. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, the Republican had 140,869 votes to 129,582 for Democrat Ceretta Smith. That is a lead of 4.2 points, or about 11,300 votes. The Associated Press called the race for Allen at 10:57 p.m. Eastern on election night.

The race

Smith is a former Grovetown city council member, veteran, and retired federal worker3. She represented Grovetown for six years, ran on access to health care and better schools, and beat fellow Democrat Traci George in a June runoff4. The Georgia AFL-CIO and the American Federation of Government Employees endorsed her.5 She told WJBF that on the campaign trail she kept hearing concerns about the cost of living2.

Allen's business is an Augusta construction company.6 He spent much of the year on the future of Eisenhower Army Medical Center at Fort Gordon. The Defense Health Agency confirmed it planned to turn the hospital into an outpatient-only facility.7 Allen and colleagues from the region then asked the war secretary to stop the plan.7 He also backed language in the House defense bill meant to block such changes8. Democratic Sens. Jon Ossoff and Raphael Warnock pushed for the same thing8, so the hospital did not divide the parties. Allen has been criticized for not holding in-person town halls. He told WRDW his office does them digitally9. In 2025, more than 100 people held a sit-in at his Augusta office to demand an in-person town hall10, The Augusta Press reported.

The money was heavily on Allen's side. He had raised $1.2 million through September, according to federal filings compiled by VoteSee11. Smith's reported totals were in the tens of thousands of dollars.5, 11

Further up the ballot, Democrats swept Georgia's statewide races. Jon Ossoff beat Republican Mike Collins for the Senate by 18.0 points. Keisha Lance Bottoms beat Rick Jackson for governor by 8.7 points. NPR reported that Collins's personal baggage alienated independents and some Republican-leaning voters12. Prices came up again and again in the coverage. ABC News' voter poll found that about three-quarters of Georgia Senate voters rated the economy negatively.13 A Columbia County barber who voted Republican told WRDW that diesel and grocery prices had climbed since the Iran war started14. Allen's own victory statement promised to "curtail rising costs for hardworking Georgians"15.

The district

The 12th covers eastern and southeastern Georgia, including Augusta, Dublin and Statesboro.16 The Watch's wave maps 24 counties in the district. Weighted by size, they moved 16 points toward Democrats between the 2024 presidential vote and this year's Senate and governor's races. These are countywide results from the statewide races, not from the House race.

Columbia County, next to Augusta, cast the most votes in the district. It went from R+25.6 to R+7.1, a 19-point move. Further down the ballot, WRDW reported, Democrat Samantha Rockwood flipped a county commission seat, ending a decades-long Republican hold14. The seat covers the Grovetown and Harlem areas.17 Richmond County, which is Augusta, went from D+36.1 to D+51.8. Together the two counties cast about 46 percent of the votes counted in the 24 counties.

Effingham County, on Savannah's edge, moved nearly 20 points, to R+29.6. Bulloch County, home to Statesboro, moved 19 points, to R+10.2. Three counties that Donald Trump carried in 2024 voted Democratic this year: Burke (from R+9.3 to D+7.9), Jefferson (from R+1.2 to D+10.2) and Washington (from R+1.9 to D+8.0). The smallest moves, under 9 points, came in Glascock and Johnson counties, which stayed strongly Republican.

The county count cannot show whether voters changed sides or a different set of voters turned out. Columbia's 67,577 votes and Richmond's 66,628 are midterm totals compared against a presidential-year baseline. WRDW reported that turnout as a share of active registered voters was lower in both counties than in the 2022 midterm18. But Columbia's elections director told WJBF that the county's turnout was among the top 25 in Georgia19. Statewide, pollster Mark Rountree told WRDW that early voting was heavy in metro Atlanta and slower in rural conservative strongholds20.

What the result means

Allen won the seat in 2014 by defeating the Democratic incumbent, John Barrow.6 WRDW noted that the district heavily favors Republicans1, and Allen held it despite a strong blue wave across Georgia1. No incumbent lost here, and the seat stays Republican. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called. That puts Democrats past the 218 needed for a majority.

About 1 percent of the expected vote is still out, and the results are unofficial until certified. WJBF asked Smith about running again in 2028. She said she was taking time to reflect on the election2, and local Democratic organizers said they plan to build on relationships formed during the campaign2.
