The wave · state by state · Iowa

Iowa's 99 counties all moved left as Turek took the Senate seat

Josh Turek won by 4.1 points in a state Donald Trump carried by 13.3 two years ago. Every county shifted toward the Democrats, most in Crawford and Wapello counties and least in the state's far northwest corner.

Josh Turek, a Democratic state representative, beat Republican Rep. Ashley Hinson for Iowa's open U.S. Senate seat by 686,641 votes to 630,808. About 99 percent of the expected vote has been counted. Turek won by 4.1 points in a state Donald Trump carried by 13.3 in 2024, a move of 17.4 points. He is the first Democrat elected to the U.S. Senate from Iowa since 20081. The results stay unofficial until the state canvass.

Not one of Iowa's 99 counties moved toward the Republicans. The typical county moved 16.7 points toward the Democrats. Ten counties that Trump won went for Turek, and none went the other way. On the same ballot, Rob Sand, the state auditor, beat Republican Zach Lahn for governor by 11.0 points, a move of 24.2. Sand is the first Democrat elected Iowa governor since Chet Culver in 20062. The map below uses the Senate race.

From Denison to the river towns

The biggest move came in Crawford County, where Trump's 43.5-point margin fell to 17.2 for Hinson, a 26-point move. The county is a deeply agricultural county in the rural Midwest that is one-third Hispanic or Latino3. It is also home to Sand's running mate, Crawford County Supervisor Dave Muhlbauer4. The count cannot say whether that helped Turek there. Next came Wapello County (Ottumwa), which went from Trump by 31.4 to Hinson by 6.4. Iowa Capital Dispatch reported that Turek was joined Sunday in Ottumwa by former U.S. Sen. Tom Harkin5 to knock on doors in the campaign's last weeks.

Turek's home county, Pottawattamie (Council Bluffs), went from Trump by 20.0 to Turek by 0.2. Speaking to the farm news service DTN, Tim Gannon, a former Agriculture Department official, said winning there countywide was always a much more difficult thing to do for those top-of-the-ticket Democrats6. Woodbury County (Sioux City) moved 21 points, from Trump by 23.0 to Hinson by just 2.2. Trump had held a rally in Sioux City to support Ashley Hinson for Senate and Zach Lahn for governor7.

Most of the flips were old Democratic factory and river counties: Muscatine, which went from Trump by 14.9 to Turek by 6.6, plus Des Moines (Burlington), Dubuque, Cerro Gordo (Mason City) and Marshall. The Des Moines suburbs of Dallas County and Scott County (Davenport) flipped by wider margins. The smallest moves came in the far northwest. Lyon and Sioux counties each moved about 10 points and still went for Hinson by roughly 60. Winneshiek County is home to Decorah, the town Sand comes from: a Decorah native, Sand has served as state auditor since 20198. It flipped to Turek by 3.6, but its 13-point move was among the smallest in the state.

Who moved: counties, not voters

Counties are not voters, but the patterns still say something. The move was remarkably even across the state. The 21 mid-sized counties, with 10,000 to 50,000 votes, cast 30 percent of the vote and moved the most: 17.4 points, weighted by votes. The six largest counties cast 43 percent of the vote and moved 15.5. The 72 smallest moved 16.5. Lean made little difference either. Counties Trump won by 30 or more moved 16.7 points, and those he won by 10 to 30 moved 17.7. Johnson County (Iowa City), the only county Harris won by more than 30, moved 14.5.

Turnout tells a clearer story. The Senate race drew 1,359,539 votes, 82 percent of the 2024 presidential total. Secretary of State Paul Pate still said this was the largest amount of voter turnout for a midterm election in over three decades9. In the 68 counties where votes fell to between 65 and 80 percent of 2024, the move was 17.9 points. In the 31 counties that kept 80 to 95 percent, it was 15.6. Across all counties, the correlation between turnout loss and the move was -0.36: where more people stayed home, the move was usually bigger.

That fits the idea that some 2024 Trump voters skipped this election. But a county count cannot tell voters who changed their minds from voters who did not show up. Some evidence points to persuasion as well. The counties with the strongest turnout, including Polk and Dallas, still moved about 16 points. Split tickets were common too: Axios reported that in counties such as Buchanan, Bremer and Boone, Sand prevailed while Turek lost10.

Tariffs, diesel and a war

NBC News reported that Turek hammered Hinson on her repeated votes backing Trump's trade war11 and the war with Iran, which drove up fuel prices across the state. Hinson pointed to rising farm costs and said she had delivered for agriculture in the House. Turek says the pressure on farmers comes from the war in Iran and from tariffs, and he ties both to Hinson's record.12 Farm groups split: the Iowa Farmers Union endorsed Democratic candidates across the U.S. Senate and House races, the Iowa Farm Bureau endorsed Republicans13. DTN noted that whether the rural gains reflect a change among farmers themselves remains an open question6.

Hinson, according to CBS News, initially vowed to be President Trump's "top ally" in the Senate14. The AP reported that she criticized the president on some issues late in the race but still appeared beside him when he made two visits to Iowa in the last two weeks of the campaign15. Turek, by contrast, ran on what his campaign called a bipartisan record in the legislature.

In her concession, Hinson said she hoped "the campaign is behind us and the work of serving the people of Iowa remains ahead"16.
