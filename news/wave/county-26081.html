In Grand Rapids' Kent County, El-Sayed widens Democrats' West Michigan edge

Abdul El-Sayed carried Kent County by nearly 14 points, more than double Kamala Harris's 2024 margin, as a smaller midterm electorate in Gerald Ford's old Republican base kept moving toward Democrats.

What Kent County did

Abdul El-Sayed, the Democrat, beat Republican Mike Rogers in Kent County 181,447 votes to 136,948 in the race for U.S. Senate. That is a margin of 13.7 points, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Two years earlier Kamala Harris carried the county by 5.4 points over Donald Trump, so Kent moved 8.3 points toward Democrats.

The county used to be reliably Republican. It is Michigan's fourth-most-populous county, with 661,000 residents, and for decades it voted Republican; it was the home of President Gerald Ford and is the site of his presidential library.1 U.S. News described it as the longtime base of office furniture makers Steelcase and Herman Miller, with median incomes and a poverty rate similar to national levels.1 Before the vote, the Detroit News pointed to suburbs such as Kentwood and Wyoming as key for both parties and noted that the county had been growing friendlier to Democrats for years.2

Kent's move came close to the state's. El-Sayed won Michigan by 7.1 points, a shift of 8.5 points from Trump's 1.4-point win in 2024. All 83 counties moved left. Kent ranked 14th by size of move, ahead of the typical county's 6.4 points. Wayne County moved 14 points and Macomb 12, which cut Macomb's Republican margin from 13.7 points to 1.8. Oakland moved only 4 points, and neighboring Ottawa moved 5 while staying Republican by 15.7 points. Nationally the typical county moved 14 points, so Kent shifted less than most. Big counties elsewhere that started near Kent's 2024 margin moved much further: Harris County, Texas, moved 27 points and Bergen County, New Jersey, 21. Orange County, California, was the exception at 5.

Why it moved

Kent cast 324,306 votes in the Senate race, 87 percent of its 2024 presidential total, or roughly 47,000 fewer ballots. The county numbers cannot show how much of the shift came from voters switching sides and how much from who did or did not vote. Both are part of the 8 points. Kent County Clerk Lisa Posthumus Lyons told WOOD-TV on Election Day that she expected turnout to hit 60%, and that anything above 61% would be a high-water mark for Kent County in a governor's election.3 Statewide, the Detroit News reported that turnout set a record for a non-presidential year, according to unofficial results.4

The Democratic campaign in Kent started with a strong primary. The Michigan Advance reported that El-Sayed won the county handily in August, when he narrowly beat U.S. Rep. Haley Stevens statewide. In the general election he was not backed by every Democrat. Stevens crossed party lines to endorse Rogers on the Sunday before the vote5, and the Detroit News reported that Grand Rapids' Democratic congresswoman, Hillary Scholten, declined to endorse El-Sayed after he campaigned with far-left social media influencer Hasan Piker6. Even so, NBC News reported on election night that El-Sayed made a particularly strong stride in Kent County7, running ahead of Harris's 2024 result there. NPR reported that statewide he motivated young and infrequent voters by focusing on passing single-payer healthcare and curbing corporate and special interest money in politics.8

The cost of living was a theme in local coverage. WOOD-TV's election coverage listed cost of living, gov't corruption9 as voters' top issues. Kent voters also turned down a 20-year jail millage, with about 60 percent voting no, WOOD reported. Afterward the sheriff's office said in a statement, "We understand how expensive everyday life has become for families across Kent County."10 Before the election, a Marist poll found that 43% of likely voters say their personal family finances have gotten worse during the last year.11

What it means for Kent

Democrats also gained further down the ballot in the county. Scholten won a third term, WZZM reported, in a seat that once belonged to Gerald R. Ford and stayed Republican for decades until 2022.12 Bridge Michigan reported that Republican state Sen. Mark Huizenga of Walker lost to state Rep. Carol Glanville13 in a district that includes parts of Kent and Ottawa counties.13 That result helped Democrats win full control of state government in Lansing.

Kent's move in this race was steady, not exceptional. It roughly tracked the state, it was smaller than the big moves in Detroit and Macomb, and it was well short of the national pattern. On the governor's ballot, Democrat Jocelyn Benson won statewide by 10.8 points, a 12.2-point move from 2024. Kent's next result will show whether the trend Michigan State University's Dante Chinni described before the vote, that "Kent has kind of moved more to the left,"14 holds with a presidential-year electorate. The 2026 figures remain unofficial until the county canvass is complete.
