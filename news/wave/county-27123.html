Ramsey County gives Peggy Flanagan her widest Minnesota margin

St. Paul's county went 8 points further toward Democrats than it did for Kamala Harris. Local reporting ties the shift to last winter's federal immigration crackdown, which also brought out an unusually large midterm vote.

What Ramsey County did

Ramsey County covers St. Paul, the state capital, and a ring of older suburbs that runs from Roseville and Shoreview to Maplewood and White Bear Lake. It was already Minnesota's most Democratic county, and on Nov. 3 it went further. Peggy Flanagan, the Democratic-Farmer-Labor (DFL) lieutenant governor, won 185,997 votes in the U.S. Senate race. Republican Michele Tafoya won 57,335. That is a 51.5-point margin with 98.42% of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris carried the county by 43.3 points in 2024, so it moved 8 points toward Democrats.

Statewide, Flanagan won by 10.1 points in a state Harris carried by 4.3, a move of 5.9. In the governor's race, Amy Klobuchar beat Lisa Demuth by 12.8. Ramsey ranked 8th of Minnesota's 87 counties by move. Eighty-four counties moved left, and the typical move was 4 points. Hennepin County, which includes Minneapolis and started 2024 at almost the same margin as Ramsey, moved only 3 points. St. Louis County, around Duluth, moved 9. Anoka flipped from R+4.4 to D+1.9.

Big Democratic counties in other states moved much more. Cook County in Illinois moved 18 points, Brooklyn's Kings County 15 and Atlanta's Fulton County 14. The typical county nationwide moved 14. Santa Clara County in California moved 1.

Ramsey cast 249,770 ballots, 90% of its 2024 presidential total and a high figure for a midterm. Flanagan's lead of about 128,700 votes was roughly 9,000 larger than Harris's, even though about 27,000 fewer ballots were cast. A county count cannot tell how much of that came from people changing sides and how much from which voters showed up. A county's move always mixes the two.

Why it moved

Local reporting points first to Operation Metro Surge, last winter's federal immigration crackdown. The Star Tribune reported that in Hennepin and Ramsey counties — where the federal agents were most active during Operation Metro Surge — voters turned out in force, casting roughly 122,000 more ballots combined than in the last midterm election.1 State Republican chair Alex Plechash conceded that the surge and the war in Iran had mattered more than he anticipated1, after a campaign in which his party put much of its messaging into fraud. The DFL, the paper found, used the 10,000 observers it trained during the crackdown as a source for recruiting campaign volunteers.1 Republican analyst Preya Samsundar told MPR News that the operation's "excess aggressiveness" played a huge role in voter turnout in the Twin Cities.2

The surge affected St. Paul's schools as well. Sahan Journal reported that the district saw 542 fewer English learners than projected and attributed the drop to Operation Metro Surge.3

Voters gave other reasons too. At a St. Paul polling place, WCCO heard voters cite the high cost of living, including the high cost of gas, along with the war in Iran, fraud and crime.4 The head election judge there said they were seeing a pre-2020 level of turnout.4

The candidates mattered as well. The Star Tribune described Flanagan as a progressive who ran as a foil to President Donald Trump and campaigned on lowering prices and establishing universal health coverage5. In the primary she beat U.S. Rep. Angie Craig by rallying a Democratic base that was furious with Trump's Operation Metro Surge.5 MPR News described Tafoya as more moderate than past Minnesota Republican nominees6. CBS reported that outside groups attacked Tafoya for past praise of Mr. Trump and Flanagan over Minnesota's fraud scandals.4 Fox 9 noted that Flanagan won despite polling and analysis that suggested the race had been tightening in recent weeks.7

What it means for Ramsey's politics

Democrats already dominated Ramsey County, so the closest fights this year were local ones. For an open seat on the county board, St. Paul council president Rebecca Noecker edged past Pérez-Vega by just 119 votes8, which was close enough for a recount, but María Isa Pérez-Vega conceded. In Maplewood, Sahan Journal reported that Chonburi Lee defeated incumbent Mayor Marylee Abrams to become the city's first Hmong mayor.9

Statewide, the Star Tribune found that turnout was up significantly in the state's most populous counties compared to the last midterm, and the DFL's increasing dominance in those counties has made it harder for Republicans to win statewide office.10 Klobuchar's win and DFL gains in the Legislature mean the party can pass budgets without Republican votes, if Democrats can hold their own caucuses together11, the paper noted.

The results are unofficial. The State Canvassing Board will certify statewide results on November 19, 2026.12
