Anna Paulina Luna holds redrawn Pinellas seat by 2,491 votes

Leela Gray, a retired Army general, came within 0.8 points of unseating the Trump-endorsed Republican. Florida had redrawn the Tampa Bay district this spring to favor Republicans, and both Pinellas and Pasco counties moved toward Democrats.

Republican Anna Paulina Luna will return to Congress for a third term from Florida's 13th District after winning by less than a point. With 99% of the expected vote counted, she had 155,189 votes to 152,698 for Democrat Leela Gray, a gap of 2,491 votes, or 0.8 points. Tony D'Arrigo, a self-described "constitution purist" who is running with no party affiliation1, drew 5,782 votes, more than twice the margin.

10 Tampa Bay reported that Luna declared victory Tuesday night, but the Associated Press had yet to call the race2. The AP call, logged in our results feed at 2:39 p.m., came on Thursday, according to Bay News 9, after nearly two days3 of counting. WUSF quoted Luna: "All that matters in politics is if you win by one vote, and so we're very happy."4

Two veterans, two arguments

Gray, a retired Army brigadier general who served 30 years in the military, is making her first run for elected office5. She ran on affordability, highlighting rising housing, insurance, healthcare, and everyday living costs1. Fox 13 reported that she argued that tariffs and military actions in Iran have raised gas prices for local consumers6. Luna, an Air Force veteran endorsed by President Donald Trump7, points to her record in Congress, including her efforts to restrict stock trading by members of Congress and her push to cap credit card interest rates5, along with beach renourishment efforts in Pinellas County8.

The sharpest fight was over corruption. Gray's first general-election TV ad accused Luna of passing insider information to a donor. The ad drew on allegations from anonymous sources, first reported by the Wall Street Journal, that Luna gave insider information on Trump's vice president pick to GOP influencer Rogan O'Handley so he could place a winning bet9 on a prediction market. Luna denied the allegation and sent Gray's campaign a cease-and-desist letter10. She also publicized a Department of Justice letter that said she is not under investigation by that agency for insider trading9. One of Luna's own ads said "Liberal Leela Gray is lying,"9 and Luna criticized Gray's stock ownership in big tech companies like Alphabet and Nvidia11.

Luna raised more money, but Gray had more help from outside groups. The Tampa Bay Times reported that as of Oct. 14, Luna reported raising more than $4.3 million to Gray's more than $2.9 million11. Democrats added outside spending worth over $4.5 million11 for Gray, including nearly $3 million from House Majority PAC in October. On the other side, GOP groups such as the Freedom Caucus Fund and Maha (Make America Healthy Again) PAC have contributed a total of $1.6 million11. Late in the race, Florida Politics reported, a Trump-aligned super PAC shuffled at least $750,000 in the Tampa television12 market out of Rep. Kathy Castor's district and into Luna's. After winning, she also vowed to stop taking corporate PAC money8.

North Pinellas and a slice of Pasco

This was not the district Luna won two years ago. The Florida House and Senate both voted to approve a new map on April 29, 2026. DeSantis signed the map into law on May 4, 2026.13 The new map cuts off the southern part of Pinellas, including portions of blue-leaning St. Petersburg and Gulfport, and pushes north into Pasco County8, taking in 62,000 residents there14 in Holiday and other towns near Palm Harbor15. The district still covers the bulk of Pinellas County — including communities like Clearwater and Largo15. The Tampa Bay Times described its voters as skewing older, white and Republican9.

On The Watch's map, both counties moved toward Democrats in this year's Senate and governor voting. Those figures cover each whole county, not just the part inside the district. Pinellas went from R+5.2 for Trump in 2024 to D+0.7, a 6-point move that flipped it. Pasco, far redder, moved 11 points, from R+25.2 to R+14.2. Weighted together, the district's two counties moved 8 points, less than the state's 10.2-point move in the Senate race and 11.8 points in the governor's race. Florida Politics reported that Pinellas County voters backed Democrats in all statewide races16, and that Gray lost to Republican U.S. Rep. Anna Paulina Luna by less than 0.2 percentage points in Pinellas16.

None of this shows voters changing their minds. The county counts (403,626 votes in Pinellas, 240,319 in Pasco) cannot separate voters who switched sides from changes in who turned out. Florida Politics also reported that registered Republicans took the lead over Democrats in ballots cast in Pinellas County by 10 a.m. Tuesday, reversing the Democratic advantage heading into Election Day17.

A seat redrawn again and again

David Jolly, an aide to Young, a Republican, for 19 years18, won the Pinellas seat in a 2014 special election after C.W. Bill Young died. Jolly lost to Democrat Charlie Crist in 2016 after the district was redrawn, making it more favorable for a Democrat19. In 2022, the recent redistricting process pushed the boundaries north20, and Luna has represented the district since 20234. This fall Jolly, now a Democrat and his party's nominee for governor, appeared with21 Gray at a rally in St. Petersburg. He lost statewide to Byron Donalds by 1.3 points, and Republican Ashley Moody beat Democrat Angie Nixon for the Senate seat by 2.9.

No incumbent lost here, but the race was far closer than two years ago. Then, CD 13 Democratic nominee Whitney Fox lost the district by nearly 10 points. And that was before mid-decade redistricting carved out more liberal parts of the district16. Cook Political Report and other analysts consider the race "lean Republican"22. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats, enough for a majority, and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. In this district, about 1% of the expected vote is still to be counted, and the results are unofficial until certified.
