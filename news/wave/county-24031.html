Montgomery County makes Maryland's biggest move toward Democrats in governor's race

In Maryland's largest county, Wes Moore beat Dan Cox by about 63 points. That is nearly 10 points better than Kamala Harris did in 2024 and the biggest move in the state, with about a fifth of the expected vote still uncounted.

Montgomery County stretches from Silver Spring, Takoma Park and Bethesda on the District of Columbia line up to Gaithersburg and Germantown. It is Maryland's most populous jurisdiction1, and federal work shapes it more than any other place in the state. Brookings counted 54,729 federal employees, or 10% of total employment2 there, and the department's National Institutes of Health (NIH) and Food and Drug Administration (FDA)2 are headquartered in the county.

On Nov. 3 the county gave Gov. Wes Moore, the Democrat, 262,549 votes to 55,992 for Republican Dan Cox in the count so far. That is a margin of about 63 points. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by about 53 points, so the county moved nearly 10 points toward the Democrats. It also cast more votes than any other county in Maryland.

Maryland's biggest move

No other Maryland jurisdiction moved as far. Statewide, Moore led 1,278,118 to 647,110, or D+31.5. That is only 2.7 points better than Harris's D+28.8. Fifteen of the state's 24 jurisdictions moved left, and the typical one moved less than a point. Next door, Prince George's County went from D+75 to D+82, a 7-point move. Frederick County moved 8 points and Howard 5. Anne Arundel barely changed, Baltimore City moved 1 point left, and Baltimore County moved 1 point toward the Republicans.

Next to the rest of the country, the move looks smaller. Across the 2,775 counties counted nationally, the typical move was 14 points to the left. Among big Democratic counties with 2024 margins like Montgomery's, Philadelphia moved 20 points, Kings County (Brooklyn) 15 and Fulton County, Georgia, 14. Alameda County, California, moved 8 points and Multnomah County, Oregon, 5. Montgomery's gain came on top of a 53-point Harris margin.

The count is not finished. About 79% of the county's expected vote was in, and the 328,738 ballots counted so far equal 64% of the 2024 total. The county elections board has canvass sessions scheduled through Wednesday, November 18, 20263. Maryland Matters reported that the mail-in ballots still being counted lean heavily toward Democrats4.

Who showed up, and why

That turnout gap is the first caution. Roughly a third fewer votes have been counted than in 2024. The county's move therefore mixes who turned out with who changed sides, and county totals cannot tell the two apart. The Watch found no published exit poll or voter survey broken out for Montgomery County. Bethesda Today reported that 93,190 people cast ballots in person5 during early voting. That was more than in 2022 but fewer than in 2018, the last time Montgomery County voters had a chance to elect a new county executive during a Trump presidency5.

Local reporting keeps returning to the federal workforce. In a VoicesDMV survey reported by MCM News, 57% of county households said federal job cuts or furloughs hurt them over the past year6. Carolyn Coleman of the Montgomery County NAACP told MCM News the cuts have hit Black residents especially hard7. Statewide, a pre-election UMBC poll found that 81% of likely voters say economic conditions over the past year have been poor or fair8, WYPR reported. Among unaffiliated voters, 37% approve of Moore's work and 49% disapprove.8

The campaign was lopsided. Maryland Matters reported that Cox reported raising $371,822 in his campaign finance report last month with the Maryland State Board of Elections, to just under $17 million reported by Moore9. In their only debate, Cox argued that Moore was spending too much time outside the state on national ambitions, NBC Washington reported. The Washington Post noted Moore won a second term that he has not committed to finishing10. Further down the ballot, Republican county executive nominee Esther Wells challenged Jawando in the general election on issues ranging from taxes to policing11, The Daily Record reported. She was running in a county where registered Democrats outnumber Republicans 4-112, according to Bethesda Today.

What it means here

The county executive's office changes hands. Will Jawando will succeed outgoing County Executive Marc Elrich, who is barred from seeking another term because of term limits13. WJLA reported that Jawando's campaign emphasized public education, affordable housing, economic opportunity and policies aimed at helping working families13.

Question 3, the redistricting amendment, showed how much the county weighs in state politics. Montgomery voters backed it by a wide margin after court battles and confusion over the Maryland redistricting measure14, MCM News reported. WMDT reported that only Anne Arundel, Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Charles, Frederick, Howard, Montgomery, and Prince George's county had majorities voting in favor15. Capital News Service found that in Montgomery, as in Baltimore City, Howard and Prince George's, voters backed Question 3 less strongly than they backed Moore16. All results remain unofficial until the canvass is complete.
