Montgomery County leads Maryland's leftward move in Moore's rematch with Cox

Wes Moore carried Maryland's biggest county by 64 points, 11 more than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin. That was the largest move of any Maryland county, on a midterm vote about 70 percent the size of 2024's, with ballots still being counted.

Montgomery County sits north and west of Washington and takes in Bethesda, Silver Spring, Rockville and Gaithersburg. It casts more votes than any other county in Maryland, and Democrats have long won it easily. On Nov. 3 they won it by more. Gov. Wes Moore, in a rematch with Republican Dan Cox, carried the county 290,184 votes to 59,250 in the Associated Press count. That is about 80 percent of the vote and a margin of 64.0 points. Kamala Harris beat Donald Trump here by 53.3 points in 2024. The move of 11 points toward the Democrats is the largest of Maryland's 24 local jurisdictions, a list that counts Baltimore City, an independent city, as a county. Most of the roughly 11,000 remaining votes went to the Green Party ticket, the Working Class Party ticket and write-ins1.

The county is home to about 1.07 million people2. According to Census Bureau figures, 33.6% of residents were born abroad and 60.6% of adults hold at least a bachelor's degree3, compared with 43.4 percent statewide.

A bigger move than the state's

Statewide, Moore beat Cox by 34.3 points, 1,438,312 to 679,978. Harris won Maryland by 28.8 points, so the state moved 5.6 points. Twenty-one of the 24 jurisdictions moved left, and the typical one moved about 4 points. The other Washington suburbs moved less than Montgomery: Prince George's County went from D+75.2 to D+82.8, a move of 8 points, and Howard County moved 5. Frederick County, up Interstate 270, came closest, moving 10 points from D+8.8 to D+19.2. Baltimore County moved 3 points and Baltimore City 2.

Compared with other big Democratic counties, Montgomery falls in the middle. Alameda County, Calif., moved 8 points and Multnomah County, Ore., 5. Fulton County, Ga., moved 14, Brooklyn's Kings County 15 and Philadelphia 20. The typical move across the 2,775 counties counted nationally was 14 points. So the county that led Maryland still moved less than the typical American county.

The count is not finished. These figures cover 86 percent of the expected vote. Bethesda Magazine reported Monday that thousands of mail-in and provisional ballots were still left to count4. David Naimon, president of the county elections board, said Montgomery has not certified its results on the first possible day for at least the last 20 years5.

Who voted, and why

The 360,695 votes counted so far are 70 percent of the 513,919 cast in 2024. A county's move mixes changes in who turned out with voters who changed sides, and these totals cannot tell a Trump voter who backed Moore from one who stayed home. Capital News Service found that Maryland turnout held nearly flat from 2022 and fell in 13 of 24 counties6. In Montgomery, Bethesda Magazine reported that early voting topped 2022 but stayed below 20187, while far more mail ballots went out than in 20187.

No county-level exit poll was available. The best evidence on who moved is a pre-election survey by the University of Maryland, Baltimore County. Maryland Matters reported it found Cox winning 4% of Democrats and 14% of unaffiliated voters8. The institute's director, Mileah Kromer, said it suggested "Dan Cox is not rebuilding the Larry Hogan coalition"8, a reference to the Republican who governed from 2015 to 2023.

Federal job cuts were a constant local story. WUSA9 reported that the county is home to more than 70,000 federal employees9. The Baltimore Banner reported in mid-2025 that federal downsizing had cost the county over 4,000 jobs since January10. The county opened what it called the first center of its kind in the country for displaced federal workers11, and Moore, touring it, told WJLA that the pain was real across Montgomery County12. In his victory speech, Moore blamed Donald Trump and JD Vance for the firing of more than 31,000 Marylanders13. No poll has measured how much this changed votes in the county.

In the campaign's only debate, WYPR reported, Cox criticized Moore for not ruling out a tax increase to close the budget gap and promised tax cuts14.

What it means locally

The same ballot chose a new county leader. Democrat Will Jawando, a member of the county council, defeated Republican Esther Wells15. He will succeed Marc Elrich, who is term-limited15 and was widely expected to win an at-large council seat16. Jawando's campaign focused on public education, housing affordability and economic opportunity17. WJLA noted he inherits significant budget decisions and housing affordability concerns18.

The county's Republican vote was thin in the governor's race but not absent. In the nonpartisan at-large school board race, Brenda Diaz had the county GOP's support19. Her opponent, Omar Lazo, was backed by Moore and the teachers' union19. Bethesda Today called it the closest race for local seats4 this year. As of Monday, Diaz was still waiting for every vote to be counted4.
