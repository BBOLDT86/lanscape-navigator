In Plymouth County, Massachusetts moves left, but less than the state

Sen. Edward Markey won the county between Boston and Cape Cod by about 13 points, 4 points better than Kamala Harris did in 2024. The state moved further, and only about three-quarters as many people voted.

What Plymouth County did

Sen. Edward Markey, the Democrat, carried Plymouth County over Republican John Deaton by 12.9 points, 125,445 votes to 95,829, with all of the expected vote counted. Two years earlier, Kamala Harris won the county over Donald Trump by 8.8 points. That is a 4-point move toward the Democrats. It is smaller than the move in Massachusetts as a whole, where Markey won by 31.2 points with about 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Harris won the state by 25.3, so the statewide move was 5.9 points.

All 14 Massachusetts counties moved left, and Plymouth ranked 10th by the size of its move. Next door, Bristol County moved 11 points, from D+12.6 to D+1.3 in reverse order: it went from D+1.3 to D+12.6, so it is now almost level with Plymouth. Norfolk County, to the north, moved 3 points and Worcester County 6. Among big counties elsewhere that Harris won by similar margins, Plymouth's move looks like Oakland County, Michigan (4 points) or Erie County, New York (1). It looks nothing like Harris County, Texas (27). Nationally, the typical county moved 14.1 points, and 2,673 of the 2,775 counties counted moved left.

Plymouth County runs from Hingham and Hull, which face onto Massachusetts Bay1, south through the city of Brockton to the cranberry towns around Carver and Wareham. Census estimates put the 2025 population at 546,8292. The county ranked 5th in median household income among Massachusetts counties for 2020–20243, at $114,2003.

Turnout matters as much as the margin here. The county cast 229,390 votes for Senate, 77 percent of its 2024 presidential total. Markey's lead was 29,616 votes. Harris's 2024 lead was roughly 26,000, on almost 70,000 more ballots. County totals cannot show how much of the move came from voters switching sides and how much came from which voters stayed home. It was almost certainly some of both. About 8,100 votes went to other candidates.

What the campaign and the local press found

Deaton is a Bolton Marine veteran and attorney4 who lost to Sen. Elizabeth Warren in 2024. Boston.com reported that his campaign focused on lowering the cost of living, improving economic affordability, and addressing the housing crisis.5 GBH News reported that he tried to distance himself from President Trump and establish his bona fides as an independent6. According to Boston.com, Markey's campaign stressed lowering living costs, affordable health care, strengthening gun laws, protecting immigrant rights and marginalized communities5, along with clean energy. On election night Deaton spoke to supporters on the South Shore, in Weymouth, where he called the result "disappointing"6.

The best estimates of who voted cover the whole state, not the county. Boston.com, summarizing New York Times estimates, reported that nearly every demographic group swung more blue this cycle, with the exception of the 65-and-older group.7 Under-30 voters were the most Democratic group, but they made up a lower percentage of the electorate compared to 2024 and 20207. No reporting found for this piece explains why Plymouth moved less than the state.

Local officials described busy polls. WATD reported a heavy turnout in most communities8. The Plymouth Independent reported that in the town of Plymouth, results went up at 1:30 a.m., an unusually late hour for the town clerk's office.9 Town Clerk Kelly McElreath said a large election day turnout delayed the process of scanning early votes during the day.9 The paper also found that turnout beat the town's turnout in the last midterm election, in 2022.9 Plymouth voters backed the Democratic incumbents, but unlike the rest of the state, Plymouth voters supported an effort to limit taxes and voted against initiatives for same-day registration and to make it easier to build single-family homes on small lots.9

What it means for the county's politics

Below the top of the ticket, the results were mixed. Democrat Rhonda Nyman beat Republican Anthony O'Brien for Plymouth County commissioner8. Republican Representative David DeCoste8 held the Fifth Plymouth District. In the Second Plymouth District, Wareham Week reported that Democrat Sarah Hewins went to bed trailing Rep. John Gaskey, a Republican from Carver, and woke up ahead once Wareham's results came in. The race is not settled. Assuming certified results confirm her victory, Hewins would be the first Democrat elected to the State House from the district since Ruth Provost10. Gaskey did not say whether he would petition for a recount.10

The bigger picture has not changed. Markey won Plymouth by about 13 points while winning the state by more than 31, and the county remains one of the more competitive places in Massachusetts.
