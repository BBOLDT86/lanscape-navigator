Yakym holds Indiana's 2nd District, in his closest race yet

Republican Rudy Yakym beat Democrat Jamee Decio by 5.2 points across South Bend, Elkhart and the smaller counties to the south. Republicans kept the seat even as Democrats flipped Indiana's 5th District and won control of the House.

Rep. Rudy Yakym, the Republican from Granger2, won a second full term in Indiana's 2nd District, beating Democrat Jamee Decio by 99,148 votes to 89,173. That is a margin of 5.2 points, just under 10,000 votes, with 99% of the expected vote counted. William Henry drew 4,908 votes. The Associated Press called the race at 2:54 a.m. Eastern, early Wednesday.

Nationally the night went the other way. As of Saturday morning, Democrats had been called the winners of 252 House seats and Republicans 161, with 22 still uncalled. In Indiana, Democrat J.D. Ford defeated Republican Victoria Spartz in the 5th District1, and the Indiana Capital Chronicle reported that the delegation will include six Republicans and three Democrats when the new Congress convenes1.

The race

Decio grew up in Elkhart. She is the daughter of the late businessman and philanthropist Art Decio. WVPE reported that she earned a law degree from Notre Dame and practiced law in Elkhart, including a stint as a deputy prosecutor, before moving to Los Angeles in her 30s to become a screenwriter3. She moved back to Elkhart in April to run3 in 2025. In the May primary she beat Shaun Maeyens, South Bend Middle School teacher, veteran and small business owner4.

Her campaign was mostly about living costs. In the last week she told WSBT that eliminating tariffs and increasing the minimum wage would help Hoosiers manage expenses5. She told WNDU that 25,000 to 26,000 Hoosiers6 in the district would lose Medicaid after the midterms. Yakym pushed back on claims of cuts and highlighted federal funding for rural medical facilities.6 The Capital Chronicle reported that Yakym campaigned on lower taxes, limited government, reduced regulation and border security7. Decio, for her part, pressed for requiring data center developers to pay for their own infrastructure needs7. That is a live issue here. Amazon's campus near New Carlisle, in St. Joseph County8, sits on a 1,200-acre site9 that was farmland until recently, and Yakym welcomed Amazon's plans to invest $15 billion in northern Indiana to build new data center campuses10.

The two never debated. ABC57 held a debate with Decio and Libertarian candidate William Henry11, who was making his third run for the seat5. The station said it reached out to Yakym several times to invite him to participate11 and got no response. (AP's tally lists Henry as an independent.) Decio said she has held 18 town halls while the incumbent has held none11. Yakym told WNDU that his outreach and record spoke for themselves: "I believe that I've got a record to run on."6

Yakym had far more money. Federal Election Commission pre-general reports compiled by Decision Desk HQ showed Yakym had raised $6,577,079 and Decio $1,455,23212, and DDHQ rated the seat safe Republican.

The district

The district extends south from Michiana and includes areas in Elkhart, Fulton, Marshall, Miami, Pulaski, St. Joseph, Starke, Wabash, Cass, Kosciusko and LaPorte counties.13 Its population centers are South Bend, Mishawaka, Elkhart, Goshen and Warsaw14. It has not always been Republican. Democrat Joe Donnelly held it until he ran for the Senate in 201215, and Jackie Walorski won it that year15. WVPE has reported that the district hasn't been competitive since the Republican-controlled General Assembly redrew lines after 2010 to remove Democratic-leaning parts of Michigan City and LaPorte County16. The lines stayed the same this year because the Senate voted 31-19 against17 a mid-decade map in December 2025.

The results file behind this report gives only the district-wide total. It has no county-by-county breakdown and no 2024 baseline for the seat, so it cannot say which of these counties moved toward Decio or by how much. Even with those numbers, a change in margin would not show that voters changed their minds, because county counts cannot tell persuasion apart from changes in who turned out. The only turnout figure here is the 193,229 votes counted so far.

What the result means

The South Bend Tribune reported that Yakym won with closer margins than any of his previous two congressional campaigns18. It also found that Decio lost by the narrowest margin of the six Democrats who didn't win18 Indiana House races. Yakym first won the seat in a 2022 special election following the death of Rep. Jackie Walorski in an Elkhart County car crash, and was reelected in 2024.19

Decio told the Tribune she "knew it was a very tough race because it's the Republican district."20 She credited the closeness to the 18 town hall events she hosted20. Henry's 4,908 votes were about half the gap between the top two candidates.

No incumbent lost here. Yakym was among eight incumbent21 Indiana House members who kept their seats. AP has called the race with 99% of the expected vote counted, but results remain unofficial until certified by state election officials.14
