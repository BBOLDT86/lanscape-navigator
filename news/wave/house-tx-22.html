Trever Nehls succeeds his twin in Texas' 22nd, by 3.6 points

The retired Army colonel beat Democrat Marquette Greene-Scott southwest of Houston, even as Harris, Fort Bend and Brazoria counties all voted Democratic at the top of the ticket, swinging 25 to 27 points toward Democrats.

Trever Nehls, a Republican and retired U.S. Army colonel1, will take the seat now held by Rep. Troy E. Nehls (R), Trever Nehls's identical twin brother2, in Texas' 22nd Congressional District. He beat Democrat Marquette Greene-Scott 150,166 votes to 139,611, or 51.8 percent to 48.2 percent. That is a margin of 3.6 points, or 10,555 votes, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race at 5:12 p.m. Eastern. AP's bulletin on the result is dated November 43, the day after the polls closed.

The race

Troy Nehls served as the sheriff of Fort Bend County4 before he went to Congress. He announced he was not running for reelection in late 20255. His brother, a former Fort Bend County constable, campaigned as the only candidate in the race endorsed by President Donald Trump1 and won the March primary. The Hill noted that he first enlisted in 1988 and later commanded service members in Iraq and Afghanistan6.

Greene-Scott is an attorney and former teacher7 and the mayor pro tem of Iowa Colony in Brazoria County8. This was her second run: she lost to U.S. Rep. Troy Nehls in 20247. The Houston Chronicle's editorial board endorsed her. It wrote that she was campaigning on improving healthcare access and fixing a broken immigration system9, and that she argued the redrawn district still had notable Latino, Asian and Black communities8. She also referenced Troy Nehls' infamous comment in July dismissing voters' concerns about affordability8. The Chronicle's newsroom reported that when Troy Nehls was asked about affordability, he pivoted to his own Fourth of July plans10 and then said, "Maybe the 60% of Americans don't work as hard as I do."10

Trever Nehls faced questions of his own. In May, Houston Public Media reported that he will be paid $13,380 per month to work for fellow Republican Daniel Wong11, who had been appointed interim county judge. Commissioners approved the payments 3-1 along party lines12. On Tuesday Wong lost, and Democrats won the majority on Fort Bend County Commissioners Court13.

Nehls also had more money. He had raised $327K and Greene-Scott $68K, according to FEC reports through Oct 14, 2026.14 Before the vote, the seat was rated as solidly Republican by the nonpartisan Cook Political Report2. The Chronicle's board described its endorsement as something of a protest vote against our broken system of partisan gerrymandering8. In the final days of the campaign, Houston Public Media heard Fort Bend voters name priorities from health care access and property taxes to cultural and ideological issues15.

The district

Fox 26 described the district as covering a portion of southwest Harris County, much of Fort Bend County, and the majority of Brazoria County5, including Sugar Land, Katy, Pearland and parts of southwest Houston1. The lines are new. Greg Abbott signed the new map into law on August 29, 202516, and The Facts, the newspaper in Brazoria County, reported that it strips most of Brazoria County from District 1417. The old 22nd also took in parts of Brazoria, Harris, Matagorda and Wharton counties11. When the lines were redrawn in 2021, Republicans solidified their control over the district, splitting the Asian American vote in Fort Bend County7.

On The Watch's wave map, all three of the district's counties voted Democratic in the Senate and governor races. Harris County went from D+5.5 in the 2024 presidential race to D+33.0, a 27-point move. Fort Bend went from D+1.6 to D+28.6, also 27 points. Brazoria, which voted R+19.7 in 2024, flipped to D+5.4, a 25-point move. Taken together, weighted by votes, the three counties moved 27.2 points, a little more than the 25.8-point statewide move in the Senate race. Statewide, James Talarico beat Ken Paxton by 12.1 points and Gina Hinojosa beat Abbott by 7.1.

So how did a Republican win here? These figures cover whole counties, and the 22nd holds only part of each one. One explanation is that the district's share of these counties is far more Republican than the counties overall. Another is that Nehls ran well ahead of Paxton and Abbott inside the district. It could be both, and the county figures can't tell us which. They also can't tell voters who switched parties apart from changes in who turned out. Harris cast 1,382,875 votes, Fort Bend 331,775 and Brazoria 141,824. That is a midterm electorate, not the one that voted in 2024.

What the result means

The 22nd has been close before. The Chronicle's board recalled that as recently as six years ago, the 22nd Congressional District nearly flipped from red to blue8, but that was two congressional maps ago8. Troy Nehls first won the seat in 2020 in a closely-watched race that analysts had rated as a toss-up18.

The seat was open, so no incumbent lost, and Republicans keep it. The Chronicle listed it among the other Republican seats19 in the Houston area that came out close. KVUE in Austin reported that across Texas, Republicans lost two U.S. House seats they already controlled and flipped only one of their five gerrymander targets20.

Nehls will join a Democratic House: AP has called 252 seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. In the 22nd, 99 percent of the expected vote is counted, and all results are unofficial until canvassed21.
