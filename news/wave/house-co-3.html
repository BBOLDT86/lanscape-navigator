Jeff Hurd holds Colorado's 3rd as its counties swing toward Democrats

The Grand Junction Republican beat Democrat Dwayne Romero by 6,132 votes, even as Pueblo, the San Luis Valley and the Western Slope moved sharply toward Democrats at the top of the ticket. He may be Colorado's only House Republican.

Jeff Hurd, the first-term Republican from Grand Junction, held Colorado's 3rd Congressional District while the counties around him moved hard the other way. With 95.33 percent of the expected vote counted, Hurd had 163,655 votes and Democrat Dwayne Romero had 157,523. That is a lead of 6,132 votes, or about 1.9 points. Two other candidates, Cory Robertson and Clifton Brown, drew 10,866 votes between them.

Colorado Newsline called Hurd's win a lone bright spot for the Colorado GOP in the midst of a "blue tsunami" that saw Democrats triumphant across the state and country.1 Democrats unseated Reps. Jeff Crank and Gabe Evans2 in the 5th and 8th districts. With Lauren Boebert still slightly trailing Democratic challenger Eileen Laubacher3 in the 4th, Hurd could end up as the only Republican the state elected to the House this year.

The race

Romero lives in Snowmass Village. He is a former Aspen City Council member and an Army combat veteran, a West Point graduate and a small business owner4, and CBS Colorado noted that he served as the state's director of economic development under Gov. John Hickenlooper's administration.5 He launched his campaign relatively late in the 2026 election cycle, and won a head-to-head Democratic primary over financial executive Alex Kelloff.1

Hurd campaigned as a "voice of independence," highlighting disagreements with Trump over his denial of disaster aid and a February vote against some of the president's tariffs.1 That vote cost him Trump's endorsement for a while. The president briefly pulled his endorsement from Hurd and back a primary challenger, before he reversed himself a month later1. Hurd told CNN that manufacturers and agricultural producers in his district were "absolutely" hurting from tariffs6.

Romero argued that Hurd's independence was mostly talk. He tied Hurd to Trump's agenda, especially Republicans' 2025 tax and spending law, which included steep cuts to Medicaid that are expected to hit particularly hard in rural areas like the Western Slope.1 The Aspen Times reported that Hurd, whose district has the most Medicaid recipients in Colorado7, stood by those changes and called them "common sense" reforms7.

Most of the money favored Hurd. By late October, the Colorado Sun counted more than $3 million8 spent to help him, much of it from a pair of super PACs tied to President Donald Trump that are primarily attacking Romero.8 Those ads called Romero liberal and corrupt and brought up how he resigned from his position leading a state economic development agency in 2011 after being arrested in Denver for driving while ability impaired, to which he pleaded guilty.9 Late in the race, House Majority PAC committed an additional $2.1 million on TV ads10. The Sun also reported that Romero has not been as visible in the district as Frisch was during his two campaigns.8

Romero was ahead on election night, but the incumbent took the lead the next day and held onto it as counties continued to report vote totals.11 According to Aspen Public Radio, the Associated Press called the race Friday morning.12 Romero conceded the race on Friday morning.13

The district

The 3rd is Colorado's largest congressional district by area, stretching from the greater Pueblo area across the San Luis Valley and much of the Western Slope.1 In the Senate and governor races, its counties moved 13.5 points toward Democrats, weighted by votes. Statewide, the moves were a little larger: 14.3 points in John Hickenlooper's Senate win over Mark Baisley and 16.7 points in Phil Weiser's win for governor over Victor Marx. Every one of the district's 27 counties moved toward Democrats.

Pueblo County voted for Trump by 5.1 points in 2024. This time it went Democratic by 10.9 in the statewide races, a 16-point move. Four more counties that Trump carried in 2024 also went Democratic: Alamosa, Huerfano, Las Animas and Archuleta. The biggest moves came in the San Luis Valley: 22 points in Costilla County, 20 in Alamosa, 18 in Saguache and 15 in Rio Grande. Mesa County, where Hurd lives, moved 13 points but stayed Republican, at R+11.2. The smallest moves came in counties that were already lopsided: 5 points in Rio Blanco, 6 in Dolores and 7 in heavily Democratic San Miguel.

In the last weeks of the campaign, Gov. Jared Polis declared a disaster emergency in response to flooding in La Plata, Archuleta, Montezuma, Dolores, Hinsdale, San Miguel, Ouray, San Juan, Mineral, Alamosa, Conejos and Rio Grande counties.14 The Durango Herald reported heavy Democratic and unaffiliated turnout in La Plata County15. County totals cannot show whether these moves came from people changing their minds or from changes in who voted. Mesa cast 75,999 votes in the statewide races and Pueblo cast 68,092, and those numbers alone can't separate the two.

What the count does show is this: taken together, the district's counties favored Hickenlooper and Weiser, but Hurd still won the House vote. He ran well ahead of Baisley and Marx, the Republicans at the top of the ticket.

What the result means

The district has been represented by Republicans since 2011.4 The last Democrat to hold it was former Rep. John Salazar of the San Luis Valley, who served three terms before losing reelection in 2010.16 In 2022, Boebert kept the seat by 546 votes following a mandatory recount17. In 2024, Hurd defeated Frisch in the 2024 general election by 5 percentage points.18 This year's margin is narrower.

As of Saturday morning, about 5 percent of the expected vote was still uncounted. The Denver Gazette reported that Hurd's lead is outside the margin that would trigger an automatic recount.11 Nationally, 252 House seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. That means Hurd will return to Congress in the minority. He told Colorado Public Radio that Republicans need to focus on "making life better for families in western and southern Colorado."19
