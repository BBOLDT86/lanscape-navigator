Los Angeles County gives Becerra a 45-point margin, 12 points past Harris

With about 83 percent of the expected vote counted, Xavier Becerra led Steve Hilton by 45 points in the country's biggest county. That is a 12-point move left from 2024, a little more than California's move and much less than the Inland Empire's.

No county in the country cast more votes for Senate or governor on Nov. 3 than Los Angeles County. With 83.28 percent of the expected vote counted, Democrat Xavier Becerra had 1,959,047 votes to Republican Steve Hilton's 741,614. That is about 72.5 percent to 27.5 percent, a margin of 45.1 points. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county by 32.9 points over Donald Trump, so the county has moved about 12 points toward the Democrats.

What Los Angeles County did

Statewide, Becerra led by 30.4 points with about 71 percent of the expected vote counted. Harris won California by 20.1 points, so the state moved about 10 points. Los Angeles County moved a little more than the state and ranks 15th of California's 58 counties by size of move. Fifty-four of the 58 counties moved left, and the typical county moved about 6 points. Los Angeles supplied almost 31 percent of Becerra's statewide votes so far.

The biggest moves in Southern California were inland. Riverside County went from R+1.3 to D+15.5, a 17-point move, and San Bernardino County went from R+2.1 to D+16.8, a 19-point move. Both counties flipped. Orange and San Diego counties each moved about 5 points, and Santa Clara County moved about 1. Outside California, Los Angeles' move was smaller than the typical county's (14 points) and smaller than the moves in Cook County, Ill. (18) and Maricopa County, Ariz. (20). Among big counties that started from a similar Democratic margin, Cuyahoga County, Ohio, moved exactly the same 12 points. Wayne County, Mich., moved 14, Fairfax County, Va., 17 and Mecklenburg County, N.C., 21.

Why: Trump, the raids and who voted

Becerra had represented Los Angeles in Congress. CalMatters, in a report published by LAist, wrote that he reminded voters of Hilton's link to Trump at every turn, and cast himself as a fighter who would, like Newsom, challenge decisions in Washington such as Trump's aggressive deportation campaign.1 Hilton, a former Fox News host with Trump's endorsement, argued one-party rule was to blame for the state's high cost of living and homelessness crisis2, the Associated Press reported. Republican consultant Mike Madrid told Axios that Becerra's win is largely because of anti-Republican, anti-Trump feelings in California3.

Immigration enforcement weighed more heavily here than almost anywhere else. Capital & Main called Los Angeles the county with the largest population of foreign-born residents in the country and ground zero for the Trump administration's ICE crackdown last year.4 Citing the data firm Political Data Inc., it reported that turnout ran ahead of 2022, with some of the biggest increases in areas with large immigrant populations4. In Paramount, where there was a large militarized presence during the 2025 enforcement surge, turnout more than doubled4. Earlier, CalMatters found that in the 2025 vote on Proposition 50, voters in Latino-majority precincts had the largest shift toward supporting Prop. 50 compared to 2024 election results5. NPR reported a national swing back to Democrats among Latino voters this year but cautioned that not all the votes are counted yet, so statistical trends are still emerging.6

Pocketbook issues mattered as well. In a September NBCUniversal Local and Telemundo poll of likely voters statewide, 25% of voters said cost of living and inflation was the top issue when deciding their vote, followed by jobs and the economy at 23%.7 Among likely Latino voters, immigration and enforcement7 ranked third, at 19 percent.

The county's numbers cannot show whether voters changed their minds. Registrar Dean Logan said that by early afternoon on Election Day, turnout had already surpassed the overall voter turnout from the 2022 gubernatorial election.8 Even so, the ballots counted so far equal only 72 percent of the county's 2024 total. The 12-point move is a mix of people who switched parties, Trump voters who stayed home and new or irregular voters who showed up. County totals cannot separate those groups.

What it means for Los Angeles

The same ballot showed that a Democratic county is not the same as a satisfied one. Nithya Raman, a City Council member backed by the Democratic Socialists of America, unseated Mayor Karen Bass. That made Bass the first Los Angeles mayor in two decades to lose reelection9, Axios reported. The Washington Post reported that Raman criticized Bass's response to California wildfires and to the deployment of National Guard troops10 in the city. Voters sided with the party at the top of the ticket and with a challenger to its local leadership.

Conceding, Hilton said "We're going to have a Republican governor in 2030."11 For now, the margin is not final. Mail ballots postmarked by Election Day count if they arrive by the statutory deadline of Tuesday, Nov. 1012, and the county is scheduled to certify on Friday, November 27.13
