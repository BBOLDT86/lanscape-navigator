Los Angeles County gives Becerra a 46-point margin, up 13 from 2024

The nation's biggest county moved 13 points toward the Democrats in the race for California governor, slightly more than the state did, with about four-fifths as many votes cast as in 2024.

What Los Angeles County did

Los Angeles County cast more votes this year than any other county in the states with a Senate or governor's race: 3,046,835 in the race for governor, with 97 percent of the expected vote counted. Democrat Xavier Becerra won 2,229,512 of those votes and Republican Steve Hilton won 817,323. That is a margin of 46.4 points, about 73 percent to 27. Two years ago Kamala Harris beat Donald Trump in the county by 32.9 points.

The 13-point move left is a little larger than California's. Becerra leads statewide by 31.2 points, compared with Harris's 20.1, a move of 11 points. But only about three-quarters of the state's expected vote had been counted.

Los Angeles ranks 14th of California's 58 counties by the size of its move. Fifty-four counties moved left, and the typical county moved about 6 points. The biggest moves came inland. San Bernardino County, which Trump carried by 2.1 points, went for Becerra by 17.4, a 20-point move. Neighboring Riverside County moved 17 points. Counties nearer the coast moved less: Orange 6 points, San Diego 5 and Santa Clara 2. Across the country, Los Angeles moved a bit less than the typical county, which moved about 14 points. Mecklenburg County, N.C., which had the same 32.9-point Harris margin, moved 21 points. Wayne County, Mich., moved 14 and Cuyahoga County, Ohio, 12.

Fewer people voted. The county cast 82 percent as many votes for governor as it did for president in 2024. A county's move mixes who turned out with who changed sides, and the count cannot tell the two apart. A wider margin from a smaller electorate could come from 2024 Trump voters staying home as much as from voters switching parties.

Why: Trump, the raids and turnout

Turnout was high for a midterm. The county registrar's office said that by 1:15 p.m. Tuesday, voter turnout in the county had already surpassed the overall voter turnout from the 2022 gubernatorial election.1 LAist reported that turnout officially passed the 2022 level, though it was likely short of recent presidential elections.2

Becerra made Washington the subject of the race. NBC News reported that he ran on a platform of battling the Trump administration and painted Hilton as a candidate who would rubber-stamp Trump's policies.3 Hilton, a former Fox News commentator who was endorsed by President Trump4, argued that one-party rule was to blame for the state's high cost of living and homelessness crisis5, the Associated Press reported. The political strategist Mike Madrid told Axios that Becerra's win is largely because of anti-Republican, anti-Trump feelings in California6.

Immigration enforcement weighed on the county more than on most places. The Los Angeles Times reported that federal immigration raids in and around the city spiked in June, creating both fear and outrage in Latino communities7, and that the administration sent the National Guard into the streets. CalóNews reported that 19 days before the election Becerra came to Los Angeles to urge Latinos to vote despite increased immigration enforcement actions in recent weeks.8 In a September poll by NBCUniversal Local and Telemundo, 64 percent of the state's likely voters and 68 percent of likely Latino voters disapproved of Trump's performance. Cost of living still ranked as the top issue for both groups, and immigration came third among Latinos at 19 percent.

NPR's national voter poll found that Latinos swung 21 points toward Democratic House candidates, compared to two years ago9, and that Asian Americans moved by a similar amount. Both groups are large in Los Angeles. But county totals cannot show which neighborhoods moved, so any local link is not confirmed.

What it means for the county's politics

Voting against a Republican did not mean voters were happy with the Democrats running Los Angeles. In the city, Nithya Raman, a council member backed by the Democratic Socialists of America, beat Mayor Karen Bass. The AP called it an upset that will alter the trajectory of the nation's second-largest city.10 The Hollywood Reporter tied Bass's loss to anger over housing and affordability, the entertainment production exodus and her response to the Palisades wildfire.11 Raman won even though Bass had support from Gov. Gavin Newsom, former Vice President Kamala Harris and former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi10. At the county level, voters saw Robert Luna re-elected as county sheriff.12

The result leaves the county's Republicans further behind than in 2024, and the main contest in its politics now happens among Democrats. The count is not finished. LAist reported that the registrar is scheduled to certify election results by Friday, Nov. 27.13 The final count will change these figures, though probably only a little, since about 3 percent of the county's expected vote is left.
