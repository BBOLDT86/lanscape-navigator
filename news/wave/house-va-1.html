Shannon Taylor unseats Rob Wittman, ending Republican hold on Virginia's 1st

Shannon Taylor, Henrico County's top prosecutor, won a seat Republicans had held since 1977 by 2.5 points. All 18 localities from Richmond's suburbs to the Northern Neck moved toward Democrats in the Senate race.

Democrat Shannon Taylor, Henrico County's top prosecutor, defeated US Rep. Rob Wittman in the race for Virginia's 1st Congressional District, The Associated Press said Thursday.1 Taylor won 208,885 votes to Wittman's 198,766, or 51.1 percent to 48.6 percent. That is a margin of 10,119 votes, with 98.76 percent of the expected vote counted. Wittman has represented the district since winning a 2007 special election2 and was seeking a 10th full term. He conceded Thursday, saying in a statement that the outcome was "not what we had hoped for"1.

The race

Taylor centered her campaign on affordability, government corruption and criticism of Wittman's record in Washington2. She told CBS 6 that the rising cost of living and corruption in Washington were the two main complaints she heard from people on the campaign trail.3 VPM reported that ads from her side accused Wittman of profiting from his office. They pointed to the growth in his net worth and his individual stocks, including shares in NextEra Energy, the Florida-based giant attempting to buy Virginia-based Dominion Energy1. Wittman went on camera to say Taylor was lying, attributing his increase in wealth4 to inheritances after the deaths of his parents and in-laws.

Wittman attacked her record as a prosecutor, including her role in co-founding Virginia Progressive Prosecutors for Justice in 2020. WAVY's fact-check found it to be TRUE that Taylor separated from the group over the issue5 of defunding police. Taylor also campaigned on restoring Medicaid funding and enhanced ACA subsidies while criticizing Wittman's support for the Republicans' sweeping federal tax-and-spending law6, the Daily News-Record reported.

Wittman's campaign raised more money, but outside spending favored Taylor. According to VPAP figures cited by VPM, Wittman's $5.4 million in fundraising edged out Taylor's $4.6 million, but outside groups spent $16 million, $11 million of which were anti-Wittman advertisements.1 AdImpact data showed that almost one-third of cable and broadcast advertisement spending in the 1st District race has come from House Majority PAC4. Before the election, a Washington Post-Schar School poll showed Taylor leading Wittman by four percentage points.2

The district

The district includes portions of the Richmond suburbs, including Henrico, Chesterfield and Hanover counties, as well as Williamsburg, James City County and communities across the Middle Peninsula and Northern Neck.2 The University of Virginia's Center for Politics called it the country's "median district"7. Donald Trump carried it in 2024, but Democratic Gov. Abigail Spanberger won 51% of the district's vote last year.6

The Watch's map compares each county's 2024 presidential vote with this year's Senate race. Mark Warner won that race over Bert Mizusawa by 22.7 points statewide. All 18 of the district's localities moved toward Democrats: 14 points weighted by votes, a little less than the 16.9-point move statewide. Three of them flipped. York County went from R+6.1 to D+5.9, Essex County from R+7.8 to D+3.8, and Lancaster County from R+7.0 to D+0.6, even though Lancaster's 8-point move was the smallest in the district. Westmoreland County, on the Northern Neck, went from R+14.3 to R+0.8. Chesterfield, Henrico and rural King William counties each moved about 16 points. The most Republican places stayed Republican: the independent city of Poquoson at R+32.7, Mathews County at R+27.5 and Gloucester County at R+25.0. Hanover narrowed from R+25.8 to R+13.4. Richmond County, a Northern Neck county separate from the city of Richmond, moved 15 points.

Two cautions apply. First, Chesterfield, Henrico and Hanover are split with neighboring districts, so their totals include voters outside the 1st. The 18 localities cast 564,901 Senate votes, compared with 408,645 in this House race. The Henrico Citizen reported that Taylor won by overcoming the incumbent's support in more conservative portions of the district with an overwhelming performance in Henrico County8.

Second, the county counts cannot separate voters who switched parties from changes in who turned out. Henrico and Chesterfield alone cast 328,490 of those Senate votes. The Virginia Mercury reported a record early voting turnout9 statewide. Axios Richmond found that compared with Spanberger's 2025 race, voting patterns were largely unchanged. And Richmond-area turnout this year was virtually identical to 202510.

What it means

Taylor's win flips a district Republicans have held since 1977.11 Turnout helped Democrats flip two House seats in Virginia12, experts told WTOP. Nationally, AP has called 252 seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.

The House race was much closer than the Senate race on the same ballot, where Warner won by more than 20 points statewide. Counting is not quite finished. In the independent city of Williamsburg, 91.25 percent of the expected vote is in. VPM reported that Taylor's margin will not be subject to a recount1. On election night, Wittman said he didn't see anything that would lead him to question the results of the election.1 Taylor told CBS 6 she wants to serve on agriculture, because of the large amount of farmland that sits in the 1st District, and judicial, because of her background.3
