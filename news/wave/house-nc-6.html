High Point's mayor flips a Piedmont House seat drawn for Republicans

Cyril Jefferson unseated Rep. Addison McDowell by 6,264 votes on a fraction of his opponent's budget, as the six counties around North Carolina's 6th District moved about 22 points toward Democrats in the Senate race.

Cyril Jefferson, the mayor of High Point, won North Carolina's 6th Congressional District by 140,001 votes to 133,737. He defeated the first-term Republican1 Addison McDowell by 6,264 votes, or 2.3 points, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race on election night.

The seat was drawn for a Republican. The General Assembly redrew the map in 2023 to favor GOP candidates2, WFDD reported, and the seat was rated as solidly Republican by the nonpartisan Cook Political Report.3 The High Point Enterprise reported that Kathy Manning, the Democrat who held the seat then, declined to seek reelection in the redrawn 6th District, saying that no Democrat could win4. McDowell did not face a Democratic opponent in the 2024 general election5.

The race

Jefferson was elected High Point mayor in 2023, the first Black man to hold the position6, and was formerly a music teacher and high school band director.7 He told WGHP that the campaign began in summer 2025, when he saw the defunding of projects in our city, the tariffs that were hurting our furniture market.8 In a city that depends on its furniture market, he told PBS NC that the tariffs, if anything, have been prohibitive.9 WFAE's guide listed affordability and health care among his top issues, and he promised to "oppose reckless tariffs"7.

McDowell, a Lexington native who lives in Davie County, took office in January 2025. He previously worked for U.S. Rep. Richard Hudson and U.S. Sen. Ted Budd, and was endorsed by President Donald Trump10. WFMY reported that he made border security and stopping the flow of illegal drugs central to his campaign, saying the issue was personal because his younger brother died of a fentanyl overdose. He also backed voter ID requirements and tighter absentee voting rules.1 He did not fill out candidate questionnaires from WSOC11 or The Charlotte Observer12.

McDowell had far more money. Axios reported that Jefferson spent just $225,000 to McDowell's $1.3 million, per FEC filings. Neither national party invested in this race. The only ads were from McDowell, who launched a $230,000 campaign ad buy and spent another $40,000 on franked ads, according to AdImpact.13 John Dinan, a political scientist at Wake Forest University, told WFDD that challengers in a seat like this usually need heavy TV spending, but "there wasn't a whole lot of money spent"2. He credited Jefferson's name recognition and record as High Point mayor2, his campaigning across the whole district, McDowell's short time in office and the blue wave that began at the top of the ticket.2

The district

The 6th District covers High Point and Davidson County as well as western Greensboro, eastern and southern Forsyth County, all of Rowan and Davie counties and the western tip of Cabarrus County.4 High Point is the 6th District's largest city.14

On The Watch's map, the six counties moved a weighted 22 points toward Democrats between the 2024 presidential vote and Roy Cooper's Senate race against Michael Whatley. The statewide move was 23 points. The biggest moves came in Republican territory. Davidson County went from Trump by 47.1 points to Whatley by 22.1, a 25-point move. Cabarrus County also moved 25 points and flipped, from R+7.7 to D+17.4. Rowan moved 23 points, to R+13.3, and Davie moved 21, to R+25.2. The two urban counties moved less: Guilford 20 points, to D+42.1, and Forsyth 22 points, to D+35.5.

These numbers need two warnings. First, they cover whole counties in the Senate race, and three of the six counties are only partly in the district. Together the six cast 586,660 Senate votes, more than twice the House race's 273,738. Second, a move does not mean voters changed their minds. The county count cannot tell Trump voters who switched sides from Republicans who stayed home or Democrats who showed up. WUNC reported that Democrats viewed high early voting turnout15 as a sign that Republican-leaning seats could flip. It also reported that Trump's visits likely helped improve base turnout for GOP voters15.

What it means

McDowell becomes a one-term congressman. When he conceded on Wednesday, he said he respected the voters' decision and recognized "the frustration many Americans feel with the current political climate"1. The Enterprise had noted that a win would make Jefferson the first Black leader to hold what's evolved into the modern 6th District post and the first High Pointer to take the seat.16

The result was part of a statewide pattern. WRAL noted that Republicans would be expected to win 11 of those 14 seats in a typical year, due to the way Republican state legislators drew the districts. But Democrats won at least nine of the 14 seats on Tuesday17. Cooper carried the state by 19.6 points. Nationally, 252 House races had been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.

The count is not quite finished. About 99 percent of the expected vote is in, and all race results are unofficial until the Nov. 13 canvass is complete.18 Jefferson will resign from the mayor's office before taking his seat in Congress in January2, and the council would appoint his replacement.5 Jefferson will be sworn in Jan. 3, 2027.10
