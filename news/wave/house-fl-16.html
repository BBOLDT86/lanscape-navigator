The House · Florida 16

Sydney Gruters holds Buchanan's redrawn Florida seat by four points

The Trump-endorsed Republican, wife of the RNC chairman, beat former Sarasota mayor Kelly Kirschner 51 to 47 percent, while the rural inland counties in the 16th swung toward Democrats far harder than its Gulf Coast core.

Sydney Gruters, a Republican who was endorsed by President Trump even before she officially entered the race1, will succeed Vern Buchanan in Florida's 16th Congressional District. With 99% of the expected vote counted, she had 175,643 votes. Democrat Kelly Kirschner had 161,108, and Mark Davis, who ran with no party, had 6,282. That works out to 51.2% for Gruters and 47.0% for Kirschner, a margin of 4.2 points, or 14,535 votes out of 343,033. The Associated Press called the race at 8:39 p.m. on election night.

The race

Buchanan, 10-term Republican Congressman2, announced back in January that he would be retiring at the end of his term.3 Gruters worked for him for more than a decade and was later district director for Rep. Greg Steube. Most recently she led the New College Foundation. 4 She is married to Joe Gruters, chairman of the Republican National Committee.4 WFLA reported that her priorities were lowering the cost of living, securing the border, lowering inflation, ensuring election security, lowering housing costs, and strengthening benefits for seniors and veterans.5

Kirschner spent four years with the Peace Corps in Guatemala. He served on the Sarasota City Commission and as mayor, 5 a job his father, the late Kerry Kirschner, also6 held. The Tampa Bay Times called his message affordability-centered7. Florida Politics reported that he painted Gruters as a likely rubber stamp for Trump's policies, and particularly stressed an unpopular and ongoing military conflict in Iran.6 He set up a "Republicans for Kirschner" coalition8. The Bradenton Journal reported that its supporters included Manatee County Commissioner George Kruse9.

The two met in a September virtual forum that the Tampa Bay Times said wasn't technically a debate10. WUSF reported that Gruters did not debate Kirschner or respond to voter guide questions11. She had more money. The Times reported that she raised more than $1.6 million and that outside GOP groups funneled millions more into the race.7 Florida Politics counted $238,000 on ad buys6 from the National Republican Congressional Committee and $450,000 in Tampa-market ads from the Trump-connected No Going Back PAC. WWSB in Sarasota reported that Kirschner was outspent about two-to-one12, though the first two weeks of October marked the first time all cycle Kirschner outraised Gruters.6 One Gruters ad used AI imagery to show Kirscher doing a "perp walk"11. Kirschner has never been arrested.

The district

Gov. Ron DeSantis signed the new congressional map in May. Under the old map, the 16th was Manatee County plus part of Hillsborough. The new district reaches into St. Petersburg in Pinellas County, takes in parts of Polk and Sarasota counties, and includes all of DeSoto, Hardee and Manatee. 13 Accounts of the new district differ. The Tampa Bay Times says it now spans slices of seven counties7, which matches the seven on The Watch's map. WTSP lists six, without Hillsborough. 14 WWSB noted that the seat combines Democratic-leaning southern St. Petersburg with more conservative rural communities in DeSoto and Hardee counties.12

The wave map shows whole-county results in the Senate and governor races. For Pinellas, Polk, Sarasota and Hillsborough, those numbers mostly describe places outside the 16th. Across the seven counties, the weighted move toward Democrats since 2024 was 8.5 points. The state moved 10.2 points in the Senate race and 11.8 in the governor's race.

The coast barely moved. Manatee, which lies entirely inside the district, went from R+23.5 to R+19.3, a 4-point shift. It cast 182,260 votes in the statewide races, more than half the House total. Sarasota moved 2 points, from R+18.2 to R+16.5. The inland counties moved much more:

• Hardee went from R+56.3 to R+37.2, a 19-point move.
• DeSoto went from R+43.0 to R+29.6, a 13-point move.
• Polk went from R+20.7 to R+6.7, a 14-point move. LkldNow reported that the neighboring 18th District covers the city of Lakeland and most of Polk County15.

Pinellas and Hillsborough both flipped. Pinellas went to D+0.7 and Hillsborough to D+9.0, but most of each county lies outside the 16th.

These counts can't separate voters who changed their minds from changes in who turned out. Hardee's 19-point move came on 6,032 votes and DeSoto's on 9,120. The Florida Phoenix reported that, statewide, a larger share of registered Democrats voted than of Republicans, according to the Fresh Take Florida Project.16

What the result means

The seat stays Republican. It was an open seat, so no incumbent lost. Inside Elections and the Cook Political Report had rated it "solid Republican"17. The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee put it on its "Districts in Play" list, but Kirschner was never added to the high-profile "Red to Blue" program.6 In WWSB's coverage, Kirschner kept it pretty close for a district that had voted for Donald Trump in 2024 by double digits12.

Even so, Gruters won by more than either statewide Republican. Ashley Moody beat Angie Nixon for Senate by 2.9 points, and Byron Donalds beat David Jolly for governor by 1.3. NBC Miami reported that Democrats in targeted seats were able to seize on voter disenchantment with Trump amid high gas and grocery prices18. Republicans, meanwhile, held on narrowly in several seats that were supposedly safe GOP seats.18

On election night Gruters told constituents, "Nobody will work harder to earn your trust"6. She will join a House where the AP has called 252 seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called. As of Saturday morning, about 1% of the district's expected vote was still uncounted.
