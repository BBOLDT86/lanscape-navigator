Garbarino holds Long Island's South Shore as Suffolk swings toward Democrats

Rep. Andrew Garbarino, the House Homeland Security chairman, beat former Suffolk County Executive Patrick Halpin by 3.2 points with 97 percent of the vote counted. In the governor's race, Suffolk County swung 11 points toward Democrats.

Rep. Andrew Garbarino, the Republican who has represented Long Island's South Shore since 2021, won a fourth term on Nov. 3 in a year that went badly for his party nationally. With 97 percent of the expected vote counted, Garbarino had 128,184 votes to 120,263 for Democrat Patrick Halpin. That is a lead of 7,921 votes, or 3.2 percentage points. The Associated Press has called the race for Garbarino. Halpin has not conceded.

The race

Garbarino, of Bayport, chairs the House Homeland Security Committee1. News 12 reported that the post has placed him at the forefront of debates over immigration and national security.2 He campaigned on what he had delivered for the district. He told News 12 that in Washington he makes sure he is "Long Island first"2 and pointed to the increase in the state and local tax deduction and full funding for the 9/11 World Trade Center Health Program2. The Long Island Herald noted that he is a member of the moderate Republican Governance Group and has broken from his party on high-profile issues.3

Halpin, of Oak Beach4, served as Suffolk County executive from 1988 to 19911 and later chaired the Suffolk County Water Authority5. He told News 12 that frustration with President Donald Trump's administration and congressional Republicans motivated his campaign2. He also links affordability concerns to the ongoing war in Iran and argues Congress, and Garbarino, should have done more to stop it.2

Garbarino had far more money. The South Shore Press reported that he had nearly $3.4 million in cash on hand as of June 306, while Halpin reported just $89,408 on hand.6

The governor's race was on the same ballot. Gov. Kathy Hochul's opponent was Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman. Greater Long Island reported that Hochul linked Blakeman to Trump, who endorsed him7, and that Blakeman ran on cutting taxes and utility bills, fighting crime and cooperating with federal immigration enforcement.7 CBS New York reported that analysts think Blakeman's close ties to President Trump likely hurt him, and dragged down members of the GOP across the island.8

The district

The Long Island Press describes the 2nd District as running along the South Shore from Mastic Beach to Massapequa9. Most of it is in Suffolk County, in parts of Babylon, Islip and Brookhaven1. Its Nassau portion covers East Massapequa, Massapequa, Massapequa Park and North Massapequa.1 According to an analysis by Greater Long Island, Halpin carried the Suffolk portion of the 2nd Congressional District1, and Garbarino's much bigger margin in the Massapequas kept him ahead.

The Watch's county figures cover each whole county in the governor's race, not just the parts inside the 2nd District. Both counties flipped. Suffolk voted for Trump by 10.0 points in 2024 and went for Hochul by 0.8 points this year, an 11-point move toward Democrats. That is bigger than the statewide move of 7 points. Nassau moved 4 points, from R+4.2 to D+0.2. That is less than the state as a whole, though Hochul still narrowly carried the county Blakeman runs. Weighted for the district, the two moves come to about 8 points toward Democrats.

These moves show where the vote landed, not why it moved. Suffolk cast 538,227 votes for governor and Nassau 532,336, and counting is not finished in either. County totals cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from differences in who turned out for a midterm compared with a presidential election.

What the result means

Garbarino replaced U.S. Rep. Peter King10, who retired after 28 years in office10. WSHU noted that Garbarino took office in 2021 amid a red wave of Republican support on Long Island.5 This year he held on as Democrats won 252 House seats nationally to 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. A majority is 218. He acknowledged the margin, telling supporters in Patchogue that the race "was closer than we would have liked," Newsday reported.1

Halpin says he will wait for every ballot. "The Associated Press does not elect members of Congress,"9 he said in a statement reported by the Long Island Press. The paper also reported that Suffolk Democratic Chairman Rich Schaffer does not think there are enough Democratic votes in the paper ballots for the challenger to unseat the incumbent.9 Based on AP's estimate of the expected vote, about 7,100 ballots are left to count. That is fewer than Garbarino's 7,921-vote lead.
