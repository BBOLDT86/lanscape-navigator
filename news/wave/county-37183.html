Wake County nearly doubles the Democratic margin as Cooper wins Senate seat

North Carolina's largest county gave Roy Cooper a 47-point win, up from Kamala Harris's 26. That one county supplied about three in 10 of his statewide margin, and the last Republican legislators from Wake lost their seats.

Wake County, home to Raleigh, cast more votes than any other county in North Carolina on Nov. 3. It also gave Roy Cooper, the Democratic former governor, the largest single share of his Senate win. Cooper beat Republican Michael Whatley in Wake 380,365 to 132,965, a 47.3-point margin with 99% of the expected vote counted. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 25.6 points.

What Wake did

Wake moved 22 points toward the Democrats. Statewide, Cooper won by 19.6 points in a state Trump carried by 3.2, a move of 22.8 points. Wake's move was close to the state's and slightly above the typical North Carolina county. It ranked 36th of 100 counties, and every county moved left. Wake cast about one in eight of the state's ballots. Its margin of roughly 247,400 votes was about 30% of Cooper's statewide lead of about 825,000.

The state's other big counties moved in much the same way. Mecklenburg moved 21 points to D+53.6, Guilford 20 and Forsyth 22. Durham was already the most Democratic of the group and moved 14. Elsewhere, Wake's move matched Montgomery County, Pennsylvania (23) and Dallas County (23). It was well above Franklin County, Ohio (14), Westchester County, New York (5) and the national typical county (14).

Wake is a large, highly educated county that is growing fast. The Census Bureau counts 1,257,235 residents in 20251, and 57.2% of adults hold at least a bachelor's degree, against 35.7% nationally. The county grew by 27,800 between 2024 and 2025, the fifth-most among all counties in the US.2

Who turned out, and who moved

Wake cast 523,237 votes, 80% of its 2024 total, which is a normal midterm drop-off. Early voting was heavy: WCNC reported that Wake County leads North Carolina for early voting with over 326,000 ballots.3 The early electorate also leaned Democratic. INDY Week found that registered Democrats represented 41.3% of in-person early voting ballots, a notably higher percentage than the 32.9% of registered voters who are Democrats4 in the county.

That matters for reading the move. Wake cast about 127,600 fewer votes than in 2024, yet Cooper's raw margin was roughly 80,000 votes larger than Harris's. A smaller electorate that leans more Democratic can produce that result even if nobody changes sides. A county count cannot separate voters who changed their minds from voters who stayed home.

Statewide polling suggests some voters did switch, though none of it is specific to Wake. Early ABC News Voter Polls found that Cooper improved among college-educated voters, winning this group by over 30 percentage points5. They also found about 6 in 105 independents backing him. According to the AP Voter Poll, most voters in North Carolina thought Whatley was "too supportive" of Trump.6

WUNC reported that Cooper ran on affordability under the slogan "Make Stuff Cost Less." He criticized Republican policies such as tariffs that raised prices for consumers7, while Whatley's campaign focused heavily on crime.7 The station noted that polls indicated that the economy — not crime — was the top concern for North Carolina voters this year.8 NBC News reported that as voters soured on the economy and the Iran war, Whatley could not escape or distance himself from an increasingly unpopular president9.

What it means for Wake

The clearest local result came in the legislature. In southern Wake, Democrat Winn Decker beat Rep. Erin Paré. WRAL described her district as one that covers Fuquay-Varina and parts of Holly Springs10, and Paré had become one of the House's three main budget writers. In northern Wake, Democrat Evonne Hopkins beat Rep. Mike Schietzelt in a race to represent a district that stretches from Wake Forest to Knightdale.10 WRAL noted that the two wins mean there will soon be no Republicans representing Wake County, the state's wealthiest and most populous county.10 INDY Week reported that both winners finished far outperforming predictions that their races would be some of the closest in the state.11

The results fit a pattern that started a year earlier. After the 2025 town elections, WRAL reported that in suburbs from Wake Forest to Garner to Fuquay-Varina, Holly Springs and Cary — Republicans lost power in areas that Republican President Donald Trump carried just a year ago.12 This year INDY Week called the county commission's southern District 2 historically Wake's reddest district13, and said the Democratic incumbent's win there was part of a Democratic shutout in southern Wake this year, across local and state races.13 Democrats also won both new at-large commission seats. Axios Raleigh noted that those seats were created by a 2023 law filed by Republican state House Rep.14

The numbers are not final. Wake's board of elections says official results reports for the November 3, 2026 General Election will be available following county canvass on Friday, November 13, 2026.15
