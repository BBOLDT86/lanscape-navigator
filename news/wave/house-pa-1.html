In Bucks County, Bob Harvie unseats Brian Fitzpatrick by 5.5 points

Democratic county commissioner Bob Harvie beat a five-term Republican who had long outrun his party. Brian Fitzpatrick ran far ahead of the GOP's candidate for governor, but Bucks County's 26-point swing toward Democrats was too large.

For a decade, Brian Fitzpatrick was the Republican that Bucks County Democrats could not beat. On Tuesday, Bob Harvie beat him. The Bucks County commissioner won Pennsylvania's 1st District 188,087 votes to 168,531, a margin of 5.5 points, with about 98.5 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race at 12:38 a.m. Wednesday. The Philadelphia Inquirer reported that Harvie's victory marks the first time that a Democrat will represent Bucks County in Congress since 2011.1 "Truth wins," he told supporters in Newtown2, WHYY reported.

The race

Harvie was born in Bristol Borough and spent more than two decades as a high school social studies teacher before serving on Falls Township's planning commission and then as a supervisor3. He won a seat on the county commission in 2019 and 2023, making him the first Fitzpatrick challenger to have won countywide office.1 He focused his message largely on affordability and achieving the "American Dream"1, and one of his TV ads called for a moratorium on data centers4.

Fitzpatrick, a former FBI agent seeking a sixth term, cast himself as an independent, citing the bipartisan Problem Solvers Caucus and his vote as one of two House Republicans against Trump's Big Beautiful Bill5. Harvie answered that Fitzpatrick had backed the bill's first House version, calling the later no vote "too little, too late"6.

The sharpest fight was over the FBI. Fitzpatrick's TV ads said Harvie was under FBI investigation7. The claim stemmed from Harvie's 2022 testimony, along with other local officials, before a federal grand jury looking into political donations and union contracts connected to the Pennsbury School District8. Harvie says he testified as a witness and has never been under criminal investigation.8 Fitzpatrick refused to debate unless Harvie released the "files"; Harvie said there are none.8 Pennsylvania legal experts told the Inquirer it was unlikely such files exist9, though Fitzpatrick kept saying Harvie was not being up-front with voters7. Harvie sued Defending America PAC, a pro-Fitzpatrick super PAC, for defamation.7 In the last week, Fitzpatrick sued to remove Democratic Commissioner Diane Ellis-Marseglia from the county Board of Elections; a judge struck down the petition2.

Fitzpatrick spent more on this campaign than on his previous three combined10, the Inquirer found. Outside money followed. CNBC, citing AdImpact data, reported that House Democrats' main super PAC committed more than $7.5 million and the DCCC more than $1.5 million, while the Congressional Leadership Fund and the NRCC committed about $5 million and $1 million11. Gov. Josh Shapiro endorsed Harvie in May12, and the DCCC's closing ad promoted that endorsement while tying Fitzpatrick to Trump13. A late Inquirer/New York Times/Siena poll found that 48% of district voters thought Fitzpatrick supports Trump too much13. Voters told WHYY that Fitzpatrick's attack ads backfired10.

The district

The district covers all of Bucks County and part of Montgomery County, including Hatfield, Montgomery, Franconia and Salford townships and boroughs such as Souderton and Telford.14 Spotlight PA reported that of nearly 575,000 registered voters, about 40% are Democrats, 41% Republicans and 18% unaffiliated15.

On The Watch's wave map, Bucks went from a 0.1-point Trump edge in 2024 to a 25.7-point Shapiro win, a 26-point move. That is slightly more than the state's 24-point move. Montgomery County as a whole, most of which lies in other districts, moved 23 points, to D+46.1. Weighted by votes, the district's counties moved 24.4 points.

Harvie ran well behind Shapiro. Shapiro won Bucks by about 26 points, while Harvie won the district by 5.5. That gap suggests many Shapiro voters either backed Fitzpatrick or skipped the House race, the kind of ticket-splitting the Inquirer had expected13. Even so, Harvie won most of Lower and Central Bucks as well as the Montgomery County portion16.

A shift in the county totals is not the same thing as persuasion. About 304,000 votes were cast for governor in Bucks, and the county counts cannot show how much of the move came from voters changing their minds and how much from changes in who turned out. Before Election Day, 6abc's ballot tracker showed Bucks with the highest turnout in Pennsylvania17. WHYY reported that Democratic registrations surged over the past year, after Republicans had overtaken Democrats in the county in July 202410.

What it means

Fitzpatrick had held the seat since 2016, succeeding his brother Mike Fitzpatrick18. He conceded Wednesday, wishing Harvie success in a responsibility "both extraordinary and humbling."1

The 1st was one of four Pennsylvania congressional races heavily targeted by both parties1, and the Northeast Times reported that Democrats flipped all four19. Nationally, 252 seats had been called for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority, and 22 had not been called.

The count is not finished. About 1.5 percent of the expected vote, roughly 5,500 ballots, remains to be counted. Harvie leads by about 19,600 votes, and all totals are unofficial.
