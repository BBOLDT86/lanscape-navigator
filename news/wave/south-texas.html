The wave · part 1 of 5 · South Texas

The border counties that swung hardest

Starr County moved 72 points. The Rio Grande Valley and the counties along the border, which went for Trump two years ago, came back to the Democrats harder than anywhere else in the country.

No county in America moved as far as Starr County, on the Rio Grande below Laredo. In 2024 it gave Donald Trump a 16-point win, the first time a Republican presidential candidate had carried it in more than a century.10 On Tuesday it went for James Talarico by 56 points: a move of 72 points on 13,082 votes.1, 2

It was not alone. Every one of Texas’s 254 counties moved toward the Democrats, and the ten biggest moves in the country are mostly in Texas or across the Kansas line.1, 2 Zapata moved 63 points, Maverick 60, Webb (Laredo) 50 and Hidalgo (McAllen), with 202,520 votes the Valley’s biggest county, 49. Cameron (Brownsville) moved 44, El Paso 33.

The picture above draws every Texas county as a block whose height is that move. The Valley stands up like a wall; the Panhandle and the Hill Country, which also moved, mostly by 15 to 25 points, sit lower but still blue. Statewide Talarico beat Ken Paxton by 12.1 points, in a state Trump had won by 13.7; Gina Hinojosa beat Gov. Greg Abbott by 7.1, the first Democrat elected governor since Ann Richards.1, 15 Talarico’s 5,447,080 votes are the most a Democrat has ever won in Texas.5

What moved it

The border counties are overwhelmingly Hispanic, and the reporting from the Valley points at the same things voters named everywhere else, sharpened by where they live: prices, and the immigration raids of the past year. Politico quoted a Democratic strategist calling the result “an earthquake,” a Republican one saying his party had “rented” the Hispanic vote, and Rep. Vicente Gonzalez calling the ICE raids a dark blemish that voters punished.10 A CNN voter poll found about 11 percent of Texas Republicans and Republican-leaning independents voted for Talarico, a fifth of those under 45.17 Talarico’s campaign spent about $2.6 million on Spanish-language advertising through September, nearly twice what Paxton and his allies spent.9

Turnout matters to the arithmetic. Starr County cast 13,082 votes this year against 16,419 in the presidential race; across the state the counties counted so far cast 9,888,109 votes, 87 percent of 2024’s total.1, 2 A move of 72 points does not mean a third of the county changed its mind: some of it is Trump voters who stayed home, some is Democrats who came back, and the county-level count cannot tell those apart.

The cities and the suburbs

The metro counties moved less in points but far more in votes. Harris County (Houston) went from D+5.5 to D+33.0, Bexar (San Antonio) from D+9.8 to D+35.5, Tarrant (Fort Worth), which Trump carried, to D+19.2.1 Collin and Denton, the fast-growing counties north of Dallas that voted for Trump by 11 and 13, both flipped.1, 9 In all, 28 Texas counties that Trump won in 2024 voted for Talarico.
