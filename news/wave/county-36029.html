Hochul's home county barely moved while New York swung left

Kathy Hochul carried Erie County by a little more than Kamala Harris did in 2024. Early voting rose sharply, but the county's 1-point move toward Democrats was far smaller than the state's 7 points.

Gov. Kathy Hochul is a Buffalo-born Democrat1, and in the last week of the campaign she was rallying supporters in Buffalo, where she launched her political career.2 On Nov. 3, Erie County backed her over Bruce Blakeman, the Republican Nassau County executive. Her margin there grew only a little compared with the vote two years earlier.

With about 92 percent of the expected vote counted, Hochul led Erie County 193,405 to 154,612, a margin of 11.1 points. Kamala Harris won the county by 9.7 points over Donald Trump in 2024. That makes a move of about 1 point toward the Democrats.

What Erie County did

Statewide, Hochul led by 19.6 points, compared with Harris's 12.7. That is a move of 7 points. Most of that gain came from downstate. Queens moved 20 points toward the Democrats, Brooklyn 15 and Suffolk County 11, where Trump's 10-point win became a narrow Hochul lead. Nassau, Blakeman's own county, moved 4 points and also tipped to her. Westchester moved 5, and Manhattan didn't move. Erie ranks 27th of New York's 62 counties by move. The state split almost evenly: 31 counties moved left, and the typical county didn't move at all.

Erie also lagged the big counties elsewhere that voted the way it did in 2024. Oakland County, Michigan, moved 4 points toward the Democrats. Orange County, Florida, moved 15, DuPage County, Illinois, 16 and Bexar County, Texas, 26. Nationally, 2,673 of the 2,775 counties counted moved left, and the typical move was 14 points.

The county includes the city of Buffalo, suburbs such as Amherst, Cheektowaga and West Seneca, and rural towns farther south. Democrats have the registration edge. In September the Board of Elections listed DEM: · 270,949 · REP: · 161,0473, along with about 174,000 voters in the "other" category.

Why the margin held where it did

Before the election, the question in Erie County was whether Hochul could lose her home county. Spectrum News reported that she won it despite questions leading up to Election Day about whether she could lose there.4 In late October, two independent polls show Bruce Blakeman leading upstate, while a Republican-commissioned survey puts him ahead 56% to 42% in the Buffalo region.2 At a rally in Lancaster, Republican Rep. Nick Langworthy said Hochul "uses Buffalo like a costume, because she's walked away from us years ago"5.

The Buffalo News summed up the campaign as a race that focused on costs, crime and Trump6. In Quinnipiac's polling, cost of living, the economy and immigration topped the list of most important issues in the race7. Hochul kept tying her opponent to the president. When she was asked about her standing at home, she said, "Republicans are running scared from Donald Trump"2. A political observer quoted by Spectrum News, identified only as O'Donnell, said the result was less a referendum on Gov. Kathy Hochul as much as it was a referendum on President Donald Trump and Republicans in Congress8.

Early voting shaped the result. Investigative Post reported that nearly 100,000 Erie County voters went to early polling sites in the week-and-a-half before election day — an increase of more than 40 percent over 2022.9 It found that Hochul's countywide lead came entirely from those early ballots, and that voters who went to the polls Tuesday split their votes evenly between the two candidates.9 County Executive Mark Poloncarz noted that Hochul received more than 20,000 additional votes in the county compared with the previous gubernatorial election in 2022.4

The county's own numbers can't show whether anyone switched sides. So far 349,232 ballots have been counted in the governor's race, about 77 percent of Erie County's 2024 total. A 1-point move in a much smaller midterm electorate could come from who stayed home as easily as from voters changing their minds. Ballots are still being counted, so the final figures will change.

What it means for the county's politics

Investigative Post called the result more of a blue ripple9 than the wave in battleground states. The clearest local change was the county clerk's race. Erie County Clerk Mickey Kearns, a registered Democrat running on the Republican line, has lost his bid for reelection to Democrat Steve Meyer.10 The Buffalo News reported that Meyer's win exposed the limits of that formula amid office controversies, a spending disadvantage and shifting voter sentiment.11 Investigative Post found the same early-vote pattern there: Meyer lost the Election Day vote but won the early ballots by a wide margin.

The clerk's race also affects next year. City & State reported that Kearns had been expected to quickly pivot to a campaign for Erie County executive next year. The county executive's office will see its first open race since 2007, with incumbent Democrat Mark Poloncarz stepping down after four terms.10 Republicans still kept ground in the suburbs. According to WGRZ's results, Scott Honer, Frank Todaro and Edward Rath III won their County Legislature districts. Hochul's margin in Erie County is also still well below her margin statewide.
