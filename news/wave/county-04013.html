In Maricopa County, Hobbs turns a Trump edge into a 16-point win

Arizona's biggest county, which Donald Trump carried by 3.5 points in 2024, gave Gov. Katie Hobbs a 16-point margin over Andy Biggs. That is a 20-point move, on an electorate about three-quarters the size of 2024's.

The county that holds Phoenix moved with the rest of Arizona, and slightly further. In 2024 Donald Trump carried Maricopa County by 3.5 points out of 2,053,945 votes. On Nov. 3 Gov. Katie Hobbs, a Democrat, led Republican Rep. Andy Biggs 874,335 to 626,744, a margin of 16.2 points, with 98.61% of the expected vote counted. That is a move of 20 points toward the Democrats.

What Maricopa did

Census figures put Maricopa at 4,689,558 residents in 2025, up 6.0% since 2020. It is 32.3% Hispanic and 50.8% non-Hispanic white, with a median household income of $89,3001. That income is the highest in the state2, according to Arizona State University's Morrison Institute. The county cast about six in ten of Arizona's votes for governor. Hobbs's 247,591-vote lead there makes up roughly three-quarters of her 333,913-vote lead statewide.

Statewide, Hobbs won by 13.1 points, a move of 18.7 from Trump's 5.5-point win. All 15 Arizona counties moved left, and Maricopa ranks fourth. Pinal County, its neighbor to the southeast, moved a bit more, 19 points, cutting a 22.1-point Republican margin to 2.9. Pima County (Tucson) moved 16 points. The Republican strongholds of Yavapai and Mohave each moved about 12.

Compared with big counties elsewhere that were about as close in 2024, Maricopa falls in the middle. Tarrant County, Texas, moved 24 points and San Bernardino County, California, 19. Hillsborough County, Florida, moved 12, while Pinellas, Florida, and Nassau, New York, moved 6 and 4. The typical county nationwide moved 14.

The count isn't finished. Maricopa County Elections said voters have until 5 p.m. Sunday, Nov. 8, to cure a signature issue or provide identification for a conditional provisional ballot3.

Why it moved

Before the election, county elections director Scott Jarrett projected turnout of 65%4. He said that would be one of the highest rates since the 1970s. Even so, the 1,526,333 votes counted so far come to 74% of the 2024 total, about 528,000 fewer ballots. So the 20-point move combines two things a county total can't separate: people who switched parties, and a different, smaller midterm electorate.

Paul Bentz, a pollster with the firm HighGround, said turnout alone doesn't explain the results5, ABC15's Garrett Archer reported. Archer pointed to The New York Times' voter-trends analysis, which found that statewide, Hispanic voters and voters under 30 — two groups that leaned toward Trump in 2024 — swung back 25 to 30 points.5 Voters without a college degree swung 16 points5, and voters over 65 moved least, still swinging 8 points toward Democrats.5

On the campaign itself, NBC News reported that Hobbs highlighted her differences from Trump's policies, including the unpopular Iran war and tariffs, and enacted a three-year pause on tax incentives for data centers.6 The Associated Press found that Biggs's campaign was hampered by questions about his past election denialism7. Fox 10 Phoenix reported that Hobbs outspent Biggs by $12 million8. She ran on repealing the 1864 abortion ban, sending the National Guard to the border and creating a fentanyl task force. Biggs had outside help: Turning Point USA spent more than $18 million in ads9, the Copper Courier reported. He campaigned on affordability, border security, and water protection10. Axios Phoenix noted that Hobbs won despite two years of pay-to-play allegations involving a state contract awarded to a group home provider11.

Part of the contest was in Maricopa's own East Valley. Biggs lives in Gilbert. Hobbs's running mate, former Mesa Mayor John Giles, is a lifelong Republican who re-registered as an independent this year11. The Deseret News reported that Biggs lost Gilbert, Mesa and the East Valley as a whole, a sprawling metro area that includes Gilbert, Mesa, Chandler and Queen Creek12.

What it means

The result reaches past the governor's office. The AP reported that Democrats won control of both state legislative chambers and retained the governor's office, giving the party its first trifecta in the state since 196613. KTAR reported that if the results hold, Democrats will finish with a 20-10 supermajority in the Senate and a 37-23 advantage in the House.14 In her first term, Hobbs vetoed more than 540 measures passed by the Republican-led Legislature15. She has said she's looking to address water issues and affordability, and take on fraud and abuse in the state's school voucher program.15

The shift showed up in county offices too. ABC15 reported that Republican Jeff Dial conceded the Maricopa County Clerk of Superior Court race to Democrat Sebastian Matta.16 Whether the county stays this far left once a presidential-year electorate returns is a question the 2026 count can't answer.
