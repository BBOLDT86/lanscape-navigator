Don Davis outlasts a North Carolina map drawn to unseat him

Republicans redrew North Carolina's 1st District in 2025 to unseat Rep. Don Davis. He beat Laurie Buckhout by 3.3 points in the costliest House general election on record, as all 25 counties moved toward Democrats in the Senate race.

Rep. Don Davis has won a third term in North Carolina's 1st Congressional District. He beat Republican Laurie Buckhout 156,572 votes to 146,287, or 50.1 percent to 46.8 percent, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Tom Bailey, the Libertarian candidate1, drew 9,881 votes, or 3.2 percent. Davis won by 10,285 votes, or 3.3 points. In 2025, at the urging of President Donald Trump, Republican state lawmakers redrew2 the district to unseat him.

The race

This was a rematch. Davis, an Air Force veteran and former six-term state senator, was first elected to Congress in 20222, and he beat Buckhout in 2024. Buckhout is a retired Army colonel and defense consultant2 and former Trump administration official3. The Daily Advance reported that at an October rally at Pitt-Greenville Airport, Trump invited Buckhout4 onto the stage. House Speaker Mike Johnson rallied with her in New Bern on the first day of early in-person voting5.

The spending broke records. NC Newsline reported a mammoth $52.9 million6 in ad spending and reservations, making it the most expensive U.S. House general election in history6. Democrats have spent $29.6 million to support Davis, and Republicans have deployed $23.3 million to back Buckhout.6

Buckhout tried to tie Davis to other prominent Democrats in Washington7. Republican ads said Davis was "for they/them, not us."7 WUNC reported that Davis did not vote either way on the House bill in question and that he ran his own ads supporting a North Carolina law that bans transgender players from girls' and women's sports teams7. One Buckhout ad was filmed in front of the shuttered Martin General Hospital7, accusing Davis of voting against funding for rural health care. Davis's ads went after her support for The Big Beautiful Bill (H.R. 1), which Davis voted against alongside fellow Democrats8. They also went after her roots: she moved to Edenton about five years ago7, while Davis talked about his childhood growing up on a tobacco farm in eastern North Carolina3.

Davis ran as a moderate. A CQ Roll Call analysis found he sided with the president nearly 70 percent of the time, second among Democrats only to Texas' Henry Cuellar5. WUNC found Williamston officials crediting him with a $1 million federal grant9 that helped pay for a new fire truck.

The district

The district covers the northeastern portion of the state and several Black Belt counties10 along with the Outer Banks1. The 2025 map moved Beaufort, Carteret, Craven, Dare, Hyde and Pamlico11 counties and a sliver of Onslow into the 1st District. It moved Greene, Lenoir, Wayne and Wilson counties out, which means that Davis' Greene County home12 is no longer in the district.

The Watch's county map follows the U.S. Senate race. Democrat Roy Cooper won it statewide by 19.6 points, two years after Trump carried North Carolina by 3.2. All 25 counties in the 1st District moved toward the Democrats, by 17 points combined, compared with 23 points statewide. Four counties Trump carried in 2024 flipped:

• Nash: R+1.8 to D+15.9
• Granville: R+9.4 to D+10.0
• Pasquotank: R+4.9 to D+12.9
• Martin: R+10.3 to D+7.0

The six added counties moved 15 to 18 points, but all of them still voted Republican. Craven narrowed to R+3.7 and Dare to R+1.1, while Carteret stayed at R+28.0. The smallest moves, 11 to 13 points, came in Chowan, Perquimans and four counties that were already Democratic: Bertie, Warren, Northampton and Hertford. Onslow had the biggest move, 23 points, but these are whole-county results and only a small part of Onslow is in the district.

The Daily Advance in Elizabeth City reported unofficial results showing Davis winning Bertie, Edgecombe, Granville, Halifax, Hertford, Martin, Nash, Northampton, Pasquotank, Vance, Warren and Washington counties13. Those are the same 12 counties Cooper carried. Buckhout won the rest, including all six new counties.

A shift in the margin does not mean voters were persuaded. The count shows 50,348 Senate votes in Onslow and 41,878 in Craven, but it can't tell voters who switched sides apart from changes in who turned out. Some Beaufort County residents who talked to WTKR said that even with Buckhout winning the redistricted counties, there was still high turnout14 that helped Davis.

What the result means

The Daily Advance wrote that Davis continues a century-long tradition of Democrats representing the 1st District13. AP reported that he also continues a line of Black representatives elected from the 1st District going back more than 30 years15. Buckhout conceded on election night, saying the voters of eastern North Carolina "made their choice, and I respect it"16. The Watch's results file says AP called the race on election night, but AP's own race-call story says it declared Davis the winner at 11:58 a.m.17 on Thursday.

Bailey's 9,881 votes came to slightly less than the margin. In 2024, NC Newsline noted, his total was more than the margin separating Davis and Buckhout18. About 1 percent of the expected vote, a few thousand ballots, has not been counted yet. Results are unofficial until the counties finish their canvass.

Nationally, 252 seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. WRAL reported that Democrats appeared poised to take the majority of the state's congressional delegation.3
