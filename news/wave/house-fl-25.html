Moskowitz wins Florida's redrawn 25th, from Delray Beach to Miami Beach

Democrat Jared Moskowitz beat Republican Scott Singer, a former Boca Raton mayor, by 4.7 points in a coastal seat drawn to lean Republican. All three of its counties moved 10 to 19 points toward Democrats in the statewide races.

Under a new map drawn in Gov. Ron DeSantis's office, Moskowitz's congressional district was completely dismantled1 this spring. He moved down the coast to the new 25th District and won it. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Moskowitz had 143,919 votes to 130,880 for Scott Singer, the Republican former mayor of Boca Raton. That works out to 51.3 percent to 46.6 percent, a margin of 4.7 points and about 13,000 votes. Peter Jassenoff took 5,882. AP's results list him as an independent; WSVN described him as a Libertarian2. AP called the race at 8:08 p.m. on election night.

The Florida Phoenix reported that the map was designed to block as many as four Democratic incumbents3, and that Moskowitz's old 23rd District was one of the seats it dramatically reconfigured. The lines were drawn so that Moskowitz's home is just outside District 25.4

The race

Singer, the former mayor of Boca Raton, won a five-way Republican primary5 and was endorsed by President Donald Trump2. Florida Politics reported that he stressed spending restraint and tax cuts and backed Trump's immigration enforcement6. In his ads he pointed to lowering Boca's millage rate seven times in eight years6. He frequently attacked Moskowitz for not living in the district4. A super PAC tied to Trump ran ads criticizing Moskowitz for trading stocks while in office and for filing the required trade reports late7.

Moskowitz, a former state emergency management director appointed by DeSantis8, campaigned on restoring Affordable Care Act tax credits, lowering homeowners insurance costs and addressing the broader cost of living5, CBS News Miami reported. In one ad, he played a clip from a TV interview in which Singer said Trump "absolutely" was doing a good job on the economy.4 About one in four district residents is Jewish, and both candidates are Jewish.9 WLRN reported that Moskowitz pointed to his record fighting antisemitism, which was a major issue in the district10.

Both sides spent heavily. WLRN reported that Democrats and Republicans each spent nearly $25 million9. Florida Politics, citing FECTracker, said outside spending exceeded $46.26 million by late October, the most of any federal race in the state6. In October the Congressional Leadership Fund, the House Republican leadership super PAC, canceled $6 million in planned advertising11, and Trump's No Going Back PAC stepped in, reserving $6.3 million12. The Cook Political Report moved the race from Toss Up to Lean Democratic on Oct. 16.13

After the call, Moskowitz said the key to his victory was focusing on the cost of living14. He also said, "I think voters reject gerrymandering."14

The district

The 25th runs along part of Florida's southeast coast through portions of Palm Beach, Broward and Miami-Dade counties, linking Delray Beach, Boca Raton, Deerfield Beach, portions of Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood and Miami Beach5. As WLRN noted, Trump carried these lines in 2024 and Joe Biden carried them in 20209. Cook analyst Erin Covey said Jewish voters make up about a quarter of the electorate, and so do Hispanic voters.15

On The Watch's wave map, all three counties moved toward Democrats in the 2026 Senate and governor vote, by 15 points on a vote-weighted average. Miami-Dade moved the most, 19 points. It went from an 11-point Trump margin in 2024 to a 7-point Democratic edge. Broward's Democratic margin grew from 17 points to 32. Palm Beach, home to Singer's Boca Raton, moved the least: about 10 points, from a near-tie to a 10-point Democratic lead. These are figures for the whole counties, and the 25th takes in only a coastal slice of each. WUSF reported that Moskowitz appeared to outperform Singer on his home turf, leading the Palm Beach County section of the district14.

The moves do not show that voters were persuaded. The 2026 counts come from a midterm electorate: 784,728 votes in Miami-Dade, 660,314 in Broward and 570,658 in Palm Beach. County totals cannot separate voters who changed their minds from changes in who turned out. Axios reported that Democrats led early turnout in both Miami-Dade and Broward counties16, even though statewide, GOP turnout outpaced the Democrats16. The Miami Times reported that participation was higher than in any Miami-Dade midterm election in more than two decades17.

What the result means

Moskowitz was first elected to Congress in 20225 and will now serve a third term. The Sun Sentinel has noted that he won his first two elections by the smallest margins of any congressional victory in the state18. This time he won on ground drawn to be harder for him.

Republicans still won both statewide races. Ashley Moody beat Angie Nixon for the Senate by 2.9 points, and Byron Donalds beat David Jolly for governor by 1.3 points. Those margins were 10.2 and 11.8 points narrower than Trump's 13.1-point Florida win in 2024. WKMG in Orlando reported the day after the election that the changes did not produce the additional seats the party sought19, although some races were still too close to call.

Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called. In the 25th, 99 percent of the expected vote is counted. Singer said he spoke briefly with Moskowitz to congratulate him on the victory20, CBS12 reported.
