The wave · state by state · Florida

Miami-Dade and Osceola led Florida's swing; The Villages went the other way

Ashley Moody held Florida's Senate seat by 2.9 points in a state Donald Trump carried by 13.1. Sixty-six of 67 counties moved toward Angie Nixon, and seven Trump counties flipped, from Jacksonville to Kissimmee.

Florida's Senate race ended the way the state's recent statewide races have, with a Republican winner. The margin was much smaller this time. Ashley Moody, whom Gov. Ron DeSantis tapped to succeed Marco Rubio when he became U.S. secretary of state in 20251, beat Angie Nixon 4,210,242 votes to 3,972,095, with 99% of the expected vote counted. That works out to 2.9 points. Nixon is a Jacksonville-area state representative and democratic socialist who upset a more moderate opponent in the primary2. Trump won the state by 13.1 points in 2024, so the Senate margin moved 10.2 points toward the Democrats.

The governor's race moved further and was closer. Byron Donalds beat David Jolly by 1.3 points, a move of 11.8. PBS NewsHour reported that Donalds was elected Florida's first Black governor3. Jolly is a former Republican congressman who switched parties and ran a centrist campaign3.

Florida still moved less than most of the country. The typical Florida county moved 6.9 points, about half the national median of 14.1.

Where the map moved

Seven counties that Trump carried voted for Nixon. Miami-Dade, the state's largest, went from R+11.4 to D+7.1, a 19-point move. Osceola, south of Orlando, went from R+1.4 to D+18.4, a 20-point move and the largest among the state's populous counties. Duval, Nixon's home county, went from R+1.5 to D+10.8. Jacksonville Today noted that since 2023 Democrats have been making countywide gains in local elections4 there. Hillsborough (Tampa) went from R+3.1 to D+9.0, and Pinellas went from R+5.2 to a narrow D+0.7. Seminole and St. Lucie flipped too.

The biggest move of all came in a small county. Hendry, a farm county on the southwest edge of Lake Okeechobee, moved 27 points: Trump won it by 38.3, Moody by 11.6. Its neighbors in the interior moved a lot as well: Hardee 19 points, Okeechobee and DeSoto 13 each, and Polk 14, which narrowed from R+20.7 to R+6.7. None of the reporting reviewed for this piece explains why these counties moved so much, and the county totals can't explain it either.

Sumter County was the only county that moved right. It went from R+37.6 to R+43.2. The Villages sprawls across three counties, but the bulk of its booming population is in Sumter County5, and Trump endorsed Donalds at a rally in The Villages6 in May. Nearby Gulf Coast retirement counties barely moved: Charlotte by 1 point, Sarasota by 2 and Citrus by 2.

Who moved

The charts beside this section show that most of the movement came from the biggest counties. The 10 counties that cast more than 250,000 votes each made up 56% of the statewide vote, and together they moved 12.5 points. Counties in every smaller size group moved between 6.9 and 8.1.

Movement also tracked how a county voted in 2024. The 40 counties Trump won by 30 points or more moved only 5.2 points, weighted by votes. The six he won by less than 10 moved 11.0. Four Harris counties, Broward, Orange, Alachua and Leon, moved 14.4 and became even more Democratic.

Turnout is the other half of the story. Floridians cast 8,276,945 votes for Senate, 76% of the 2024 total. The 61 counties where turnout fell to between 65% and 80% of 2024 levels cast 93% of the vote and moved 10.7 points. In six counties turnout held above 80%, and they moved just 2.2. Most of their votes came from Sumter, Charlotte and Sarasota. Across all counties, steeper turnout drops went with bigger moves toward Democrats, but the relationship was modest (r = -0.25).

County totals can't tell voters who changed their minds apart from voters who stayed home. Counties are not voters. There is evidence of both. Fresh Take Florida, a University of Florida project, found that registered Democrats7 voted at a higher rate than registered Republicans. Before Election Day, Axios reported that Republican turnout is weakest in parts of the conservative Panhandle and rural North Florida8. In Santa Rosa County, this year's vote came to 66% of the 2024 total.

Persuasion probably played a part too, especially among Hispanic voters. Hispanic or Latino residents make up 70.6% of Miami-Dade County's population9. In Osceola, Puerto Ricans are the largest Hispanic community10. The AP Voter Poll found that less than half of Hispanic voters supported Moody, a notable change from 2024, when Trump won the Hispanic vote in Florida.11 But Axios Miami reported that Miami-Dade's Hispanic voters sided with the GOP — though by smaller margins than in previous elections.12

What the campaigns argued

The two Senate candidates ran very different campaigns. Nixon campaigned for a national rent freeze, a 5% annual wealth tax on billionaires, universal child care and abolishing ICE13. She also kept attacking Moody over Hope Florida, the case in which DeSantis' administration diverted $10 million from a Medicaid settlement14 to a foundation tied to Casey DeSantis. WLRN noted that an appeals court found the money was "never public money."14 Moody painted her race against Nixon as one between "safety and chaos,"15 and she declined to debate Nixon16.

Nixon also faced a steep climb. Democrats haven't won a Senate race in Florida since 2012, and Republicans outnumber Democrats by 1.5 million registered voters.13

In the governor's race, the fight was over cost of living. According to News4JAX, Donalds said Jolly's property-insurance plan, which entailed the state taking on the windstorm risk portion of a homeowner policy, would amount to a "hurricane tax"17. CBS12 reported that Donalds had a major financial advantage.18

People who study Miami politics explained its swing in different ways. Political strategist Michael Worley told The Miami Times it was partly economic concerns, particularly among non-Cuban Hispanic voters19. Democratic strategist Steve Schale told Axios Miami that a "hangover" from 202012, when Democrats were tied to socialist talking points and calls to defund the police, kept the county from moving even further.

The count is almost complete but not final. Duval was at 98% of its expected vote and Calhoun at 94% when these figures were drawn.
