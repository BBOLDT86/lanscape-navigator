Brent Taylor wins Tennessee's redrawn 9th District by 2,539 votes

Republicans split Memphis three ways in May to win the seat. They did, but only by 1.1 points, as Justin Pearson ran close and every county in the L-shaped district moved toward Democrats in the statewide vote.

Brent Taylor, a Republican state senator from Memphis, beat Democratic state Rep. Justin J. Pearson in Tennessee's 9th Congressional District by 2,539 votes, 111,968 to 109,429. With 99% of the expected vote counted, that is a margin of 1.1 points. Two independents, Dennis Clark and Michelle Davis Head, drew 4,217 votes between them, more than the gap between Taylor and Pearson. The Associated Press has called the race for Taylor, and Pearson conceded Wednesday1.

Republicans redrew the district this year, the Tennessee Lookout wrote, to give Republicans the upper hand2. Tennessee Republicans held a special session in May to redraw the 9th District2 after the Supreme Court's Louisiana v. Callais decision, and the new map split Memphis3 among the 5th, 8th and 9th districts. WSMV reported that Republicans said the map removed racial data from the mapmaking process entirely3. Democrats said it was meant to dismantle the state's only Black-majority district3.

The race

Taylor is a former funeral home director who previously served on the Memphis City Council and the Shelby County Commission4. He entered the race for the newly drawn district almost immediately and gained endorsements from President Donald Trump and U.S. Sen. Marsha Blackburn2. WKRN reported that he campaigned largely on supporting President Donald Trump5, on tougher immigration enforcement and on backing police. The Lookout noted that he repeatedly called Pearson a Democratic Socialist2 and put in over $1 million of his own cash6.

Pearson gained national attention in 2023 when he was expelled from the general assembly5 for protesting gun violence after the Covenant School shooting, and he was later reinstated. He launched his campaign for the 9th District in October 20252 to challenge Rep. Steve Cohen, but Cohen's retirement7 left the seat open. Pearson easily defeated three opponents8 in the August primary. By the third-quarter filings, the Lookout reported, he had nearly $1.2 million remaining9 to Taylor's about $822,0009. The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee6 added the seat to its list of targets.

Outside groups pushed Pearson's themes of big money and data centers. A Democratic-backing political action committee10, Three Star PAC, ran ads accusing Taylor of taking money from AI companies and passing data-center legislation. The ads aired in the Nashville TV market and streaming services10. JB Pritzker's Ready for the Fight PAC, which has already spent $1.6 million supporting Pearson11, began running an attack ad against Taylor in the last week of the race. The ad was based on a Nashville Scene report.

During October's early voting period, the Scene broke the news that Taylor admitted to sending unsolicited nude photos as recently as 2021.4 He called them "youthful indiscretions"4, and he told WREG that the timing was tied to the final stretch12 of the race. Taylor prevailed in the race despite13 the scandal. In his victory remarks, he said he would not have won without Williamson County14.

The district

The Lookout describes the seat as L-shaped2, running from the Memphis airport along the southern edge of rural West Tennessee and then up into Middle Tennessee to take in part of Williamson County. It holds all of Bedford, Giles, Hardeman, Hardin, Lawrence, Lincoln, Marshall, Maury, McNairy, Moore and Wayne counties and portions of Shelby, Williamson, Rutherford and Fayette3. The figures below are for those four counties as a whole, not just the parts inside the district.

The Watch compares the 2026 statewide vote with the 2024 presidential vote. On that measure, all 15 counties moved toward Democrats, with a weighted move of 13 points. Rutherford County, home to Murfreesboro, moved the most: 22 points, from R+21.8 to D+0.2. It is the only county in the district that flipped. WPLN reported that Rutherford County voters in that district favored15 Pearson. Bedford moved 19 points, to R+39.4, and Hardeman moved 15, to R+9.0. The smallest moves were in Hardin (8 points), Wayne (9) and Williamson (9), where R+32.4 became R+23.7. Pearson's statement said every county in the district swung between 10 and 30 points toward Democrats16. The Watch's measure uses the statewide races, not the House vote, and its moves run from 8 to 22 points.

Shelby County moved 12 points, from D+25.4 to D+37.2, on 248,486 votes. A move like that is not proof that voters were persuaded. County counts cannot separate people who changed their minds from changes in who turned out, and turnout in Shelby did change. The Daily Memphian reported that early voting there outpaces 2022 but trails the 2018 early voter turnout17. Voting advocates told MLK50 that anger over the redistricting effort that cracked Memphis' majority-Black congressional district18 was bringing infrequent voters to the polls.

What the result means

The old 9th had been represented by Democrats for more than 40 years2, and Cohen had held it since 200719. No incumbent lost, because the seat was open. For the first time in decades, Memphis will be represented in Washington by Republicans. Redistricting split Memphis into three districts, and Republicans won all three seats1. The Cook Political Report had rated the new seat "Solid Republican"2, though its lean was the weakest Republican advantage of the state's nine congressional districts2.

The race was part of a statewide shift. In a state Trump carried by 29.7 points, Blackburn won the governor's race by 2.5 points and Sen. Bill Hagerty was re-elected by 12.2. Democrats flipped District 4.3 Nationally, Democrats have been called the winners of 252 House seats, more than the 218 needed for a majority.

The count is unofficial until it is certified. Taylor also won the race for his current state Senate seat20, which leaves open what happens to that seat. Pearson won a state race for Tennessee House District 86 and will stay in that seat.</antml:cite>
