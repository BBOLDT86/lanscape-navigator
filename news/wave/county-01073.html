The wave · the biggest counties · Jefferson County, Alabama

Birmingham's county moves 21 points left, but Alabama stays red

Jefferson County gave Democrat Everett Wess a 31.6-point margin for Senate, triple Kamala Harris's edge in 2024, a 21-point move that ranked ninth among Alabama's 67 counties. Republican Barry Moore still won the seat statewide.

Jefferson County, home to Birmingham and its suburbs, cast more votes on Nov. 3 than any other county in Alabama. The Senate race there was not close. Democrat Everett Wess beat Republican U.S. Rep. Barry Moore 161,995 to 84,293, a margin of 31.6 points with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Wess is a Birmingham attorney and Midfield municipal judge1. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 10.4 points, so the county moved 21 points toward the Democrats.

What Jefferson County did

The Census Bureau puts the county's population at 665,7422 in its 2025 estimate, a little below the 2020 count. The county is about 42.2%2 Black. Its median household income is $66,3883, a bit above the state's figure of $63,9993.

Jefferson's move was close to the state's. Alabama as a whole moved 21.2 points toward the Democrats in the Senate race, but it started from a Trump margin of 30.6 points, so Moore still won by 9.4. All 67 counties moved left. The typical county moved 15.4 points, and Jefferson ranked ninth. Of the state's other big counties, neighboring Shelby moved 21 points but stayed Republican, going from R+40.8 to R+19.6. Tuscaloosa moved 24 points and flipped to the Democrats, and so did Madison and Mobile.

Other big counties that started near Jefferson's 2024 margin split widely. Bexar County, Texas (San Antonio) moved 26 points. DuPage County, Illinois moved 16 and Orange County, Florida 15. Oakland County, Michigan moved only 4 and Erie County, New York 1. The typical county nationally moved 14.1 points.

Why it moved

Turnout matters here. Jefferson cast 246,288 votes, 83 percent of its 2024 total and about 50,000 fewer ballots. Even so, the Democratic margin grew to 77,702 votes. A county's move mixes two things: which voters showed up and which voters changed sides. A county count cannot tell them apart. Across the state, the Alabama Reflector reported that Democrats nearly doubled their midterm turnout from four years ago4. It also found that Democratic votes in Jefferson rose sharply from 2022, a 25.3% increase5.

The governor's race was on the same ballot. Former Sen. Doug Jones, who grew up near the city6, ran against Sen. Tommy Tuberville. ABC 33/40 reported that Jones won Jefferson, Madison and Mobile7, but he lost the state by 4.0 points. Jimmie Stephens, the Republican president of the county commission, told reporters on election night that he "was concerned because of the Doug Jones effect, bringing everyone out."8

Tuberville made crime the center of his campaign. In one ad he called Mobile and Birmingham "dangerous"9 and promised to send in the National Guard on his first day, Alabama Daily News reported. WBHM, Birmingham's public radio station, reported that Birmingham Mayor Randall Woodfin rejected the idea. It added that violent crime and homicides have dropped in the last year in Birmingham and Montgomery10. Wess ran on affordable health care, economic opportunity, voting rights and equal justice1. He said rising grocery and gas prices were among the concerns he heard from Alabama families1.

The pre-election polls missed the size of the shift. A Cygnal poll for Alabama Daily News and Gray Television in October found Tuberville ahead of Birmingham voters6. That poll's Birmingham group was a region, not the county, and no county-level exit poll is available.

What it means locally

Democrats gained in Jefferson's suburbs as well as in Birmingham itself. In House District 15, which covers parts of Shelby and Jefferson counties, voters chose Democratic nominee Nell Brown over Republican incumbent Leigh Hulsey of Helena11. The Alabama Political Reporter wrote that around Madison and Jefferson, Democrats came within a handful of points of pulling off more upsets12.

At the courthouse, Sheriff Mark Pettway, a Democrat, won another term13. The commission race in District 3, in western Jefferson County, is still in dispute. Stephens narrowly beat Democrat Camara Blue, a Birmingham pastor, according to the unofficial count. Blue led by more than 4,000 votes for much of election night14 and is seeking a recount. The Birmingham Times reported that a delay of more than an hour in the Jefferson County vote returns being posted to the county's website yielded questions8 about the result. The county's official canvass will establish the certified totals.15

Republicans still hold every statewide office. State party chairman Scott Stadthagen said the political waters across our state remained bright red once the ballots were counted5. In the Reflector's reporting, one analyst said that even with high turnout, "there's not enough anger there to sweep Republicans out of office"4 in Alabama.
