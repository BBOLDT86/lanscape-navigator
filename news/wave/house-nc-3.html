The House · North Carolina 3

Redrawn to help the GOP, North Carolina's 3rd elects a Democrat

Raymond Smith, a former state legislator from Goldsboro, beat Rep. Greg Murphy by 258 votes. The eastern North Carolina district was reshaped last year to help Republicans win the seat next door. Murphy conceded and did not ask for a recount.

Democrat Raymond Smith has won North Carolina's 3rd Congressional District, defeating Republican Rep. Greg Murphy. It was one of the closest House races in the country. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, AP's tally has Smith at 118,434 votes and Murphy at 118,176, a gap of 258 out of 239,614 ballots. A third candidate, Daniel Cavender, drew 3,004 votes, more than 11 times the margin.

The lead changed hands after election night. Murphy led narrowly on election night and had already declared victory a day after the election, despite thousands of ballots that had not been counted.1 NC Newsline reported that once provisional and cured absentee ballots were counted2, Smith moved ahead. Murphy conceded on Nov. 11, saying in a statement, "I am sure he will serve the next two years with honor and integrity."3

The race

Murphy is a surgeon who has represented the district since 2019. WCTI reported that Murphy emphasized lowering health care costs and supporting farmers, while Smith focused on affordability, public education and rural health care.4 Smith is a former educator and member of the Wayne County Board of Education who represented Wayne and Sampson counties in the North Carolina House from 2019 to 2023.5 Talking to ABC11 after the race, he pointed to Duplin County, home to the country's largest pork producer, and also cited affordability and ending the war in Iran.6

Smith won with very little money. In late October, Roll Call reported that Murphy remains heavily favored7, noting that Smith had raised just $367,000 to Murphy's $2.9 million.7 WUNC, citing AdImpact, reported that Murphy and Republican-aligned groups spent $1.46 million on TV and digital ads, while Democratic-aligned groups spent about $285,000, including only $7,561 from Smith's campaign.8 The Daily Reflector in Greenville reported that Smith hasn't had television advertisements but is participating in community events and candidate forums9.

The statewide mood helped. East Carolina University political scientist Peter Francia told WCTI that "Cost of living and affordability, that issue really dominated in this election... it became an issue that Republicans really could not overcome."10

The district

This is largely a new district. In October 2025, legislators moved Beaufort, Carteret, Craven, Dare, Hyde, and Pamlico Counties, and a section of Onslow County, from District 3 to District 1, and moved Greene, Lenoir, Wilson, and Wayne Counties from District 1 to District 3.11 The new 3rd covers Duplin, Greene, Jones, Lenoir, Onslow, Pitt, Wayne, Wilson and part of Sampson9. WRAL described it as stretching from the eastern edge of Fayetteville to the coast, covering Wilson, Kinston, Greenville and Jacksonville.12 The redraw was meant to unseat Democrat Don Davis in the 1st District. WUNC reported at the time that it would raise the proportion of Black voters in the 3rd Congressional District to about 29%.13 Davis won anyway.

Murphy also had to introduce himself to new voters. WCTI reported that some reports say close to 40% of voters had never seen Murphy's name on their ballot.14 The Reflector's unofficial election-night count showed Murphy failed to win his home county, Pitt, while defeating Smith in Smith's home county, Wayne.15

The data behind The Watch's map does not yet break this seat down by county, so the county moves can't be read here. Statewide, Roy Cooper won the Senate race by 19.7 points on 4,222,288 votes, a 22.9-point move from Donald Trump's 3.2-point win in 2024. That move cannot tell voters who changed their minds from voters who stayed home or showed up, and we have no 2024 district turnout to compare against. Smith's own explanation is about turnout: he told WRAL that he believes local voters saw what GOP leaders were trying to do and became motivated to go to the polls and push back.12

What the result means

A Republican incumbent lost a seat his party had held comfortably. NPR noted it is a district where Democrats didn't even run a candidate in 2024.16 The margin was within the range for a recount. North Carolina allows a trailing candidate to ask for one when the margin is 1% or less of the total votes cast in the contest.17 The deadline was noon on Nov. 17. The News & Observer reported that Virginia Foxx in the 5th District is the only Republican congressional candidate to request a recount. Greg Murphy lost his race by an even smaller margin but conceded defeat18.

According to WRAL, AP called the race on Tuesday, after Murphy's concession, as Democrats tied their 2008 total19. AP's national count now stands at 257 seats for Democrats, 171 for Republicans and 7 not yet called. North Carolina's results are still unofficial. The state canvass, where results are certified, is scheduled for Nov. 24.20 The delegation went into the election at 10 Republicans and four Democrats16. If the 5th District result survives Foxx's recount, Democrats will hold 11 of the 14 seats.
