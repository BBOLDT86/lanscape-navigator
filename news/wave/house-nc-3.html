In Eastern North Carolina's Redrawn 3rd District, Murphy Leads by 233 Votes

The 2025 map that moved Wayne, Wilson, Greene and Lenoir counties into Rep. Greg Murphy's district was drawn to help Republicans take the seat next door. It left him 233 votes ahead of Raymond Smith, with AP yet to call it.

Four days after the polls closed, North Carolina's 3rd Congressional District is still unsettled. Republican Rep. Greg Murphy has 117,583 votes and Democrat Raymond Smith has 117,350. That is a 233-vote lead out of 237,904 cast, with 98.75 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press has not called the race. Public Radio East reported that Murphy described the contest as a "razor-thin victory"1. Smith says he's neither declaring victory nor conceding defeat2.

The race

Murphy is a physician who has practiced medicine in Eastern North Carolina for 35 years3. He has served in Congress since 2019 after winning a special election4. Smith is a former state lawmaker and Wayne County Board of Education member5 from Goldsboro. WCTI reported that Murphy emphasized lowering health care costs and supporting farmers, while Smith focused on affordability, public education and rural health care5. Smith also called for fully funding the Affordable Care Act6. Daniel Cavender, whom local outlets listed as the Libertarian7, did not respond8 to WCTI. He drew 2,971 votes, more than 12 times the gap between the two leaders.

Murphy had far more money. He raised more than $2.8 million, compared with about $366,000 for Smith9, according to federal filings reported by WCTI. WUNC reported that Murphy was one of the first incumbents to launch TV ads10. President Donald Trump rallied support for GOP candidates at Pitt-Greenville Airport11 on Oct. 20. Before the election, East Carolina University political scientist Peter Francia told WCTI that a Murphy loss would be "one of the big, huge, gigantic political upsets of 2026."9 Afterward, he told WITN that the shift comes down to two primary reasons: the high cost of living and voters' economic frustration12. WCTI also noted reports that close to 40% of voters had never seen Murphy's name on their ballot13.

The district

Roll Call called the 3rd a low-lying patchwork of small cities, piney woodlands and tobacco and cotton fields14. According to the Daily Reflector, it is built around Pitt County and also takes in Wilson, Wayne, Lenoir, Jones, Duplin and the majority of Onslow and Sampson counties15, along with Greene. WUNC reported that Republican legislators swapped counties to make the 1st District safer for their party, with significant Black populations in Greene, Lenoir, Wayne and Wilson counties moving to the 3rd District16. Roll Call cited a UNC analysis that put the 3rd District's Black share at 29 percent, up from 20 percent14.

On the wave map, all nine counties moved toward Democrats between the 2024 Harris–Trump vote and this year's Senate race. Democrat Roy Cooper won that race statewide over Republican Michael Whatley by 19.6 points. The county moves ran from 14 points in Jones to 24 in Sampson. Weighted by votes, the district's counties moved about 20 points, a little less than the state's 22.8. Pitt went from a 6-point Harris county to a nearly 25-point Cooper county, and Wilson went from essentially even to 19. Trump won Wayne, Lenoir and Greene in 2024, and all three voted for Cooper this year, Greene by less than half a point. Onslow, home to Jacksonville and Camp Lejeune, stayed Republican, but its margin fell from 36 points to 13. Sampson's fell from 30 to 6.

Across all nine counties, Cooper's margin works out to roughly 6 points. The figures cover whole counties, including the parts of Onslow and Sampson outside the district. Even allowing for those parts, Murphy ran well ahead of Whatley in this territory. Still, the Reflector reported that Murphy failed to win his home county, Pitt17.

A move is not persuasion. These figures compare a midterm Senate race with a presidential race, and a county count cannot separate voters who changed their minds from changes in who voted. Turnout was heavy. NC Newsline reported that NC early voting in 2026 set a record for a midterm18, and WITN reported that elections boards across eastern North Carolina reported turnout increases19 over 2022. In the Senate race, Pitt cast 62,410 votes and Onslow cast 50,348.

What the result means

Walter B. Jones Jr. had been the district's representative from 1995 until his death20 in 2019, and Murphy won the special election to replace him. In 2024, he did not face a Democratic challenger21. This year, WRAL reported, Democrats have unseated at least half of the Republicans who represent North Carolina in the U.S. House22. Democratic Rep. Don Davis also won despite being targeted22 by the new map. Before the election, Western Carolina University political scientist Chris Cooper told WUNC that a Murphy loss would make the redraw a "dummymander," which is a gerrymander that is drawn that actually backfires on the party10.

The count is not finished. County boards of elections will review each provisional ballot and decide whether to approve it, partially approve it or reject it. The results will be added during the 10-day canvass period23. WITN reported that Pitt County had the most provision ballots cast in the east24. The Reflector reported that some absentee and provisional ballots also must be counted during the election canvass on Nov. 1325. Under state law, a candidate can request a recount if they're trailing by less than 1% of total votes cast26, but not until after county election boards hold canvass meetings26. Smith's campaign is already reaching out to supporters to raise money for a "recount fund."27 Democrats have already been called the winners in 252 House seats, so this race will not decide control of the House.
