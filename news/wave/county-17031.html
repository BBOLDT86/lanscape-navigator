Cook County posts Illinois's biggest swing, 19 points toward Democrats

Juliana Stratton won Chicago and its suburbs by 61 points, 19 points more than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin, with about three-quarters as many votes cast. Local reporting credits who turned out as much as who changed sides.

What the county did

Cook County is Chicago plus the ring of older suburbs around it, and it was already Illinois's Democratic anchor. On Nov. 3 its margin grew much wider. Lt. Gov. Juliana Stratton beat Republican Don Tracy there 1,244,254 to 291,039, a margin of 61.2 points with 98.95 percent of the expected vote counted. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 42.0 points. That makes a 19-point move toward the Democrats, the largest of Illinois's 102 counties.

Statewide, Stratton won by 25.6 points, a 15-point move from Harris's 11.0. Gov. JB Pritzker's margin over Darren Bailey moved 14 points. Of Illinois's 102 counties, 96 moved left, and the typical county moved 6 points. The collar counties came close to Cook's swing: Kane and Will moved 18 points each, DuPage 16 and Lake 14. McHenry moved 16, turning a county Trump won by 5.3 points into one Stratton won by 11.0. Madison County, across the river from St. Louis, moved 13 points and nearly flipped.

Compared with the country's other big, heavily Democratic counties, Cook's move matched Travis County, Texas, at 19 points. It was larger than Brooklyn's 15 and far larger than Hennepin, Santa Clara and Middlesex, which each moved fewer than 5. Nationally, the typical county moved 14 points. About 1 percent of Cook's expected vote was still uncounted.

Who turned out, and why

Cook cast 1,558,622 votes, 76 percent of its 2024 total and roughly half a million fewer ballots. A county total cannot separate voters who changed their minds from voters who stayed home or newly showed up. In Cook, local reporting points strongly to turnout.

WBEZ and the Chicago Sun-Times examined the 90 precincts President Donald Trump won in 2024, which represent traditionally conservative areas of the city. Voter turnout in those precincts was down an average of 6.26 percentage points in the November election compared to 2022.1 Meanwhile, traditionally Democratic precincts that voted for Kamala Harris saw an increase in voter turnout by an average of 3.23 percentage points.2 Nearly all precincts in the 19th Ward, home to Beverly and Mount Greenwood, saw a double-digit drop in voter turnout3. Turnout rose in Little Village, Pilsen, Humboldt Park and South Shore. In the 7th Ward, where federal agents raided the South Shore building, voter turnout was up 5.3 percentage points.1 Those comparisons are to the 2022 midterm, not to 2024.

Those neighborhoods were at the center of Operation Midway Blitz. The Sun-Times reported that the federal deportation campaign, launched Sept. 8, 2025, brought hundreds of federal agents to the Chicago area and resulted in nearly 4,000 arrests.4 One Little Village voter told the paper she had voted only in presidential elections previously4 but turned out this time because of the raids. Early voting also ran ahead of the last midterm. The Cook County clerk reported suburban early voting a 31 percent increase compared with the same point in the 2022 election5, and ABC7 reported that early voting in Chicago is up more than 11% compared with the 2022 midterms according to the Chicago Board of Elections.6

The exit poll NBC News published covers the whole state, not the county. In it, the economy (38%)7 was the top issue, and 69 percent said Trump's policies had worsened economic conditions in the U.S.7 Sixty-four percent said the administration's immigration enforcement had gone too far.

The campaign gave Cook voters a clear contrast. Stratton grew up on the South Side and lives in Bronzeville.8 Capitol News Illinois reported that Tracy sought to paint Stratton as a tax-and-spend liberal and a socialist, contrasting himself as a pragmatic conservative.9 Both candidates put affordability first, but Tracy blamed Democratic taxes and spending. He was also notably absent from Trump's list of endorsements for Illinois Republicans9.

What it means for Cook's politics

What little Republican office-holding was left in Cook got smaller. The Daily Herald reported that Republicans also lost their only remaining seat on the Cook County Board.10 Bob Grogan, the state party chairman, told the paper the party's policies on taxes, the size of government and safer streets could still win in the suburbs, but that its message and messengers had not. "What we need to be competitive on a statewide level is someone who can actually speak suburban cocktail party language,"10 he said. The DuPage County GOP chairman put the losses down to lack of support for the war in Iran and other national issues10.

The next test comes soon. ABC7 noted that observers see this November's turnout patterns as a possible guide to voter engagement ahead of the municipal election less than four months away.6 The precinct analysis shows that conservative parts of Chicago voted less and neighborhoods hit by the raids voted more. What no one can yet say from the counts is how many 2024 Trump voters in Cook switched to Stratton rather than staying home.
