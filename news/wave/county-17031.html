Cook County gives Stratton a 60-point margin, 18 points past Harris

Chicago and its suburbs backed Juliana Stratton over Don Tracy by 60 points with 93 percent of the vote counted. That was the second-largest move left among Illinois's 102 counties, on turnout at 71 percent of 2024.

Juliana Stratton's Senate race was called early, but Cook County is where her margin came from. With 93 percent of the expected vote counted, Stratton, the Democratic lieutenant governor, had 1,153,039 votes in the county. Republican Don Tracy had 279,032. That is about 79 percent to 19 percent, a 60.1-point margin. Kamala Harris carried Cook by 42.0 points over Donald Trump in 2024, so the county moved 18 points toward the Democrats.

Cook is Illinois's largest county by far. The Census Bureau counts 5,194,625 residents in 2025, a 1.6 percent decline since 20201, and a population that is 27.8 percent Hispanic, 22.8 percent Black and 39.4 percent non-Hispanic white1. The county includes Chicago and a ring of older suburbs.

What Cook did

Stratton won the state by 24.3 points. Harris had won Illinois by 11.0, so the state moved 13.3 points. Cook's 18-point move ranked second of 102 counties, and 95 counties moved left. Cook cast about 36 percent of the Senate votes counted so far, but it gave Stratton about 874,000 votes of her roughly 989,000-vote lead. Outside Cook, she led by about 115,000.

The suburbs around Cook moved about as much. Will County went from D+1.6 to D+19.3, Kane moved nearly 18 points and DuPage 16. McHenry flipped from R+5.3 to D+11.0. Among big Democratic counties elsewhere, Cook looks most like Brooklyn (Kings County, N.Y.), which moved 15 points to D+59.0, and Travis County, Texas, which moved 19. Hennepin, Santa Clara and Middlesex counties moved 5 points or less. The typical county in the country moved 14.1 points. Among the largest counties, Harris County, Texas, moved 27 points and Dallas 23.

The electorate was much smaller than in 2024. Cook's 1,453,596 votes are 71 percent of the 2,056,800 cast in 2024. A county-level count cannot tell voters who switched sides apart from 2024 voters who stayed home, so the 18 points include both. The count is also unfinished: Chicago will keep counting mail-in ballots over the next two weeks as they continue to be returned if postmarked by Nov. 32, Block Club Chicago reported.

Why it moved

Election officials saw turnout rising before Election Day. Cook County Clerk Monica Gordon said early voting in suburban Cook was a 31 percent increase compared with the same point in the 2022 election3. Chicago's election board said city turnout was an increase over the 2022 midterm, but less than the 60 percent turnout back in 20184, ABC7 reported.

The Chicago Sun-Times found that voters at the polls cited inflation, the cost of living and Trump's deportation campaign5. That campaign, Operation Midway Blitz, brought hundreds of federal agents to the Chicago area and resulted in nearly 4,000 arrests5 starting in September 2025. Chicago voters strongly approved a ballot question asking the city to seek federal compensation for the operation, WBBM reported. The station also cited a University of Illinois Chicago estimate of $1.26 billion in lost retail and restaurant spending across Cook County6 in the first year of stepped-up enforcement.

The statewide exit poll published by NBC News, which does not break out Cook, found 33% approving7 of Trump's job performance. When respondents were asked how they voted in 2024, Harris voters made up 57% and Trump voters 37%7. That suggests the midterm electorate leaned more Democratic than the 2024 one.

Tracy, a former state Republican chairman, cast himself as a pragmatic conservative8, Capitol News Illinois reported. The Sun-Times noted that Stratton ran on a profane anti-Trump slogan. The paper also wrote that Trump remains unpopular with voters in Cook County and the collar counties9. In the governor's race, Darren Bailey campaigned on crime during a year when Chicago has seen a historic drop in homicides9.

What it means for Cook

County government is now almost entirely Democratic. Republican Liz Gorman appeared to be defeated by Democratic Lyons Township Clerk Elyse Hoffenberg according to unofficial results10 in District 17, the Republicans' only seat on the County Board. The Daily Line reported that Toni Preckwinkle won a fifth term as board president. She warned that federal Medicaid cuts are expected to have a substantial impact on the county health and hospital systems' finances next year11.

The gap with downstate Illinois widened too. All 11 counties voting on the non-binding measure approved it12, a referendum on exploring a new state without Cook. That brings the total to 44 of Illinois' 102 counties12. WBEZ summed up the night by saying the results left Illinois Republicans in their bleakest position in recent memory13.
