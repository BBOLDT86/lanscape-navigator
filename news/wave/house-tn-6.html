The House · Tennessee 6

Garrett holds Tennessee's redrawn 6th District by 3.3 points

Johnny Garrett, a state House Republican leader who helped redraw the map, beat Navy veteran Mike Croley by 7,773 votes. Every county in the district, from Nashville to Campbell County, swung toward Democrats.

Johnny Garrett, a Goodlettsville Republican, is going to Congress after eight years in the state legislature, including seven as whip for the Republican supermajority.1 He is going from a district he helped draw. The Associated Press called Tennessee's 6th District for him at 12:16 a.m. on election night. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, he leads Democrat Mike Croley 120,950 to 113,177, or 50.8 percent to 47.5 percent. That is a margin of 7,773 votes, or 3.3 points. Two independents, Christopher Monday and Angus Purdy, drew 4,097 votes between them.

The race

The seat was open because U.S. Rep John Rose made an unsuccessful bid in the Republican gubernatorial primary.2 Garrett beat former congressman Van Hilleary for the nomination in a primary that the Nashville Banner said was marked by AI-generated deepfake attack ads1. In the fall, WKRN reported, he ran on supporting President Donald Trump's "America First" agenda, including increased deportation of immigrants, supporting the 2nd Amendment, and keeping the 2017 tax cuts3 permanent. The Nashville Scene noted that during this year's legislative session he sponsored a bill requiring sheriffs' offices across the state to coordinate with ICE4. The Banner reported that Croley's repeated calls for a debate were ignored by Garrett, who also declined to respond to the Banner's candidate questionnaire.1

Croley is a Navy veteran and former state park ranger1 from Crossville who won a tight five-way Democratic primary.1 He told WATE that he worked for NOAA and the State and Agriculture departments until I was terminated by President Trump and Elon Musk5. He made affordability his number one priority5 and criticized the Iran war and the trade war6. The Banner said he also ran against data centers and roving masked immigration agents.1

The two campaigns had very different amounts of money. Croley's campaign reported $117,167.707 in receipts through Oct. 14. FEC figures compiled by Decision Desk HQ show that Garrett had raised $2,837,2728 by Sept. 30, with about $600,000 in outside support. Before the election, the Scene described all three districts that divide Nashville as considered safe for Republicans.9

The district

The legislature redrew the state's congressional map in May10. WPLN reported that Garrett and his fellow GOP lawmakers redrew Tennessee's congressional districts at the behest of President Donald Trump11. The new 6th includes a slice of left-leaning Nashville and stretches some 200 miles east to the Appalachian Mountains region in Campbell County.12 According to the Scene, it takes in portions of Sumner and Campbell counties, and all of Wilson, Smith, DeKalb, White, Putnam, Jackson, Clay, Overton, Cumberland, Morgan, Fentress, Pickett and Scott counties.13 The Tennessee Lookout reported that the lines reach into a small part of Sumner, a move that allows state Rep. Johnny Garrett to remain in the 6th14. A Banner analysis found that the redraw kept the seat safely Republican, but picking up more Davidson County Democratic voters to help other districts' margins grow.15

On The Watch's wave map, every one of these counties moved toward Democrats between the 2024 presidential race and this year's statewide vote. The weighted average for the district's counties is a 16-point move. No county changed sides. Davidson went from D+27.4 to D+44.8, a 17-point move. The suburban counties of Sumner and Wilson each moved 17 points, with Wilson going from R+40.1 to R+23.2. Putnam, which includes Cookeville, moved 16 points. So did Scott, which went from R+79.8 to R+63.5. The smallest move came in Croley's home county, Cumberland: 6 points, from R+58.9 to R+53.3. These are whole-county figures, so they include the parts of Davidson, Sumner and Campbell that lie outside the 6th.

The moves do not show, by themselves, that voters changed their minds. They compare a midterm electorate with a presidential one: 221,227 votes were cast in Davidson's statewide race this year, and 56,652 in Wilson's. A county count cannot tell people who switched parties from people who stayed home or turned out. The Chattanooga Times Free Press reported that statewide turnout was much higher than in 202216 and comparable to the 2018 elections16.

What the result means

Rose had represented the district since January 201917, so no incumbent lost. Garrett's 3.3-point margin is far closer than Bill Hagerty's 12.2-point Senate win statewide. It is slightly wider than Marsha Blackburn's 2.5-point win for governor. In that race, the Tennessee Lookout reported, Blackburn captured nearly all of Tennessee's rural areas while Green carried the state's urban counties, Hamilton, Knox, Davidson and Shelby.18 WKRN called Garrett's win a needed one for Republicans amid lagging approval for the president amid the ongoing war in Iran and inflation concerns.3

The race has been called, and 99 percent of the expected vote is counted. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still outstanding. Garrett will arrive as a freshman joining a now-GOP minority1.
