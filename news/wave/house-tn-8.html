The House · Tennessee 8

Kustoff holds West Tennessee's 8th as every county moves toward Democrats

Republican David Kustoff beat Democrat Heidi Kuhn by 5.3 points in a redrawn seat that reaches from Memphis to Perry County. All 12 counties in the district moved toward Democrats, Haywood and Crockett by about 17 points each.

David Kustoff, the Germantown incumbent1, won a sixth term in Tennessee's 8th Congressional District on Nov. 3. He beat Democrat Heidi Kuhn 114,045 votes to 102,406, or 51.6 percent to 46.4 percent. His 5.3-point margin was 11,639 votes out of 220,906 counted, with 99 percent of the expected vote in. The Associated Press called the race at 12:16 a.m. Eastern. Two independents, Pamela Moses and Horace Taylor, drew 1,035 and 861 votes.

This was his first race under new lines. Tennessee's Republican-led Legislature passed a new congressional map2 in May, and the new map split Memphis into three districts — 5, 8 and 9.3 The Daily Memphian describes the new 8th as covering much of Memphis and its northeastern suburbs1. From there, the Tennessee Lookout reported, it run[s] across rural West Tennessee counties such as Haywood and Madison, ending in Perry County in Middle Tennessee.4

The race

Kuhn is the former clerk of the Shelby County Criminal Court. She was elected in 2018 and reelected in 2022, and she ran for Shelby County mayor before this race, losing in the May primary.5 She ran on raising the minimum wage and tackling the high cost of living6 and opened her campaign at a gas station in Cordova. Local Memphis reported that her platform also included protecting SNAP benefits, ending the war in Iran, protecting Medicare and Medicaid, a moratorium on data centers, and community concerns about ICE.5

Kustoff is a former U.S. Attorney for the Western District of Tennessee and was an early supporter of Donald Trump in 2016.7 He backed the Memphis Safe Task Force, the federal law enforcement and National Guard deployment that Trump sent to Memphis at Gov. Bill Lee's request8. In March he flew with Trump on Air Force One to Memphis, where the president met with law enforcement about the task force.9 In October, Kuhn went on WKNO's "Behind the Headlines" and discussed concerns about the task force, rising costs, immigration, data center rules and foreign policy.10 The program said it had reached out to Kustoff but had not been able to book him.10

Kustoff had far more money. The Lookout found that this race's fundraising was far smaller than in the other two Shelby County seats.11 In the 90 days covered by the October reports, Kustoff raised just under $286,00011 and finished with nearly $3 million11 on hand. Kuhn raised nearly $55,500 from individuals11 and reported $20,500 on hand11. Late in the race, WMC Action News 5 reported, Kuhn suffered whiplash and fractures to her face in a crash on I-240 in Memphis on Oct. 23.12

The district

The Lookout's voter guide says the district covers large rural areas, but most of its vote comes from Memphis suburbs with some of the state's highest incomes.7 Besides part of Shelby County, the district reaches into Madison County (Jackson) and ten smaller counties: Fayette, Tipton, Gibson, Carroll, Henderson, Haywood, Chester, Crockett, Decatur and Perry.

The Watch's wave map compares each county's 2024 Harris–Trump margin with its 2026 statewide vote. All 12 counties moved toward Democrats, by 12 points on a weighted average. Haywood moved most, 17 points, from D+0.4 to D+17.2. Crockett also moved 17 points, from R+58.7 to R+42.0. Madison went from R+18.2 to R+4.6 on 27,195 votes, and Tipton moved 14 points. Fayette moved least, 9 points. No county changed sides. Decatur (R+59.3), Henderson (R+57.7) and Perry (R+57.2) are still heavily Republican.

Shelby County moved from D+25.4 to D+37.2 on 248,486 votes. Those are countywide figures that include the parts in the 5th and 9th districts, so they are not the district's own result. The district's counties also moved less than the state as a whole. The Senate margin moved 17.6 points as Bill Hagerty beat Marquita Bradshaw by 12.2. The governor's margin moved 27.2 points as Marsha Blackburn beat Jerri Green by 2.5.

A move toward Democrats does not mean voters changed their minds. The county count cannot tell people who switched sides apart from changes in who voted, and turnout did change. ABC24 reported that early voting in Shelby County represents roughly 31% of the county's registered voters and a 52% increase in early voter turnout over 2022.13

What the result means

Kustoff was elected in 2016 and has been reelected four times since.5 He kept his seat, but coverage after the election said the race was tighter than anticipated.14 In his victory speech, Kustoff said: "Nationally, this was a tough night for the Republican Party, there's no doubt about it"15.

Across Tennessee, unofficial results from the Tennessee Secretary of State's Office show Republicans won all three3 Memphis-area seats. In the 9th, Republican Brent Taylor defeated Democrat Justin J. Pearson.16 In the 4th, Democrat Victoria Broderick was projected to defeat Republican incumbent Scott DesJarlais3. The map is still being challenged in court. Memphis voters and civil rights groups sued over the map, but a federal court refused to block it, finding it was driven by politics rather than race.3

Nationally, 252 House seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. Kustoff told WREG he expects a divided government with a Republican president and a Democratic House and Senate15. In the 8th, 99 percent of the expected vote had been counted as of Saturday morning, and the results stay unofficial until the state certifies them.
