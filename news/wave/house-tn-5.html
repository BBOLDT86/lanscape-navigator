The House · Tennessee 5

Charlie Hatcher holds Tennessee's redrawn 5th, a Memphis-to-Williamson seat, by 4.9 points

The former agriculture commissioner, who ousted Rep. Andy Ogles in August, beat Columbia Mayor Chaz Molder 51.7% to 46.7% in a district Republicans drew in May to split Memphis. Every county in it moved toward Democrats in the statewide races.

Charlie Hatcher, a former Tennessee Agriculture Commissioner, dairy farmer and veterinarian1, will represent a district that did not exist a year ago. He beat Chaz Molder, the Democratic mayor of Columbia, 110,408 votes to 99,841, or 51.7% to 46.7%, a margin of 4.9 points, with 99% of the expected vote counted. Two independents, James Johnson and Micheál O'Leary, drew 3,456 votes between them. The Associated Press called the race at 12:17 a.m. Eastern.

The seat was remade in May. NPR reported that Tennessee Republicans passed a new congressional map that would crack Shelby County — home to majority-Black Memphis — into three different districts2 to eliminate the state's only Democratic-held House seat. GOP lawmakers defended the new map, saying their goal is partisan2.

The race

Hatcher got to November by beating the incumbent, Andy Ogles, a member of the Freedom Caucus3. President Donald Trump gave an early endorsement to Ogles4, while Hatcher had the backing of Gov. Bill Lee, under whom he had served4. The Tennessee Lookout reported that Hatcher was helped by $2 million in independent advertising spending by the Invest in Tomorrow Coalition5, a political action committee funded by renewable energy investors6, while the Freedom Caucus Fund pumped more than $810,000 into media buys and robocalls supporting Ogles or opposing Hatcher5. The president endorsed Hatcher after his primary victory3, and Hatcher appeared with him at a midterm Trump rally in Clarksville, Tenn., on Oct. 107.

Molder had more money. The Nashville Banner reported that he was able to raise more than $3.5 million throughout his campaign — nearly twice as much as Hatcher8. Hatcher's first TV ad, a biographical spot, started airing on Tuesday, nearly a month after his Democratic opponent9 went on the air. Molder's ads went after Hatcher for supporting federal legislation that provides billions of dollars in taxpayer funds to data center developers10, and later over tariffs, the war with Iran and cuts to rural hospitals. On the other side, the Nashville Scene reported that the super PAC MAGA Inc. spent more than $75,000 on ads against Molder11.

The campaign was fought over prices. At an October forum, the two clashed on President Donald Trump's handling of international tariffs, but agreed on the importance of constituent services and an urgent need to reduce the cost of living12. Molder said the tariffs and the war should both end right away, while Hatcher, according to the Banner, sought not to directly criticize the president he campaigned for8. Hatcher expressed his opposition to Trump's efforts to ramp up cattle imports and removed some pro-Trump language from his campaign materials9. Molder also campaigned against the new map itself. The Commercial Appeal reported that he embraced Memphis voters as now being part of the district13 and called the gerrymander an attack on Black voters. The new lines cut both the residences of Charlie Hatcher (Republican nominee) and Chaz Molder (Democratic nominee) out of District 51.

The district

The new 5th stretches from Memphis all the way north along the Mississippi River to Kentucky and then runs all the way to Williamson and Maury Counties1. In Memphis it includes areas such as Downtown, the Medical District, South Memphis, Frayser, and part of Midtown14. It also takes in western Williamson and part of Montgomery County15.

On The Watch's wave map, all 17 of the district's counties moved toward Democrats between the 2024 Harris–Trump vote and this year's statewide vote. Weighted by votes, the shift was 12.1 points. That is smaller than Tennessee's overall move in the Senate race (17.6) or the governor's race (27.2). Montgomery County (Clarksville) moved the most, 18 points, from R+18.4 to D+0.1. It is the only county in the district that flipped. Shelby went from D+25.4 to D+37.2. Williamson moved the least, 9 points, to R+23.7. Maury, home to Molder's city of Columbia, moved 14 points to R+30.7. The rural counties remained heavily Republican even after double-digit moves. Lake moved 16 points, but Stewart, Obion and Benton all still voted Republican by more than 52 points.

Two cautions apply. First, these are whole-county results from the statewide races, and several counties are split between districts. Shelby County alone cast 248,486 statewide votes, more than the 213,705 cast in this entire House race. Second, a county's move cannot tell voters changing their minds apart from changes in who turned out. ABC24 in Memphis reported that early voting in Shelby County represents roughly 31% of the county's registered voters and a 52% increase in early voter turnout over 202216.

What the result means

The old 5th was a Nashville seat. In 2022 Ogles became the first Republican to win Tennessee's 5th Congressional District seat since Reconstruction17. This year's map changed from incorporating a third of Nashville to including a third of Memphis17. No incumbent was on the November ballot, and this will be Hatcher's first elected office13.

The Commercial Appeal wrote that Tennessee Republicans' gerrymander gave Hatcher the upper hand13. Hatcher pointed to effort instead: "I do think I outworked him."18 In his concession, Molder called the 5th "a district drawn to make sure this race would never be this close"8 and argued that how close it was showed the redistricting had "backfired."

Across the state the result was mixed. Despite the upset in District 4, Republicans won eight of Tennessee's nine Congressional seats19. In that race, Democrat Victoria Broderick defeated Rep. Scott DesJarlais. Nationally, 252 House seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. In the 5th, 99% of the expected vote is counted, and the totals are according to unofficial race results13.
