The wave · the biggest counties · Volusia County, Florida

Volusia moves left by 6 points, trailing Florida's statewide shift

Ashley Moody carried Daytona Beach's home county by nearly 16 points, down from Donald Trump's 22, as about a quarter fewer voters turned out. Volusia moved less than Florida as a whole and stayed solidly Republican.

Volusia County runs from the beach towns around Daytona Beach inland to DeLand and Deltona. It is an older place. Its population was 606,573 in 2025, and the median age was 46.5 in 2024.1 The county's biggest city is not Daytona Beach but Deltona, a commuter suburb that counted 93,692 residents in the 2020 census to Daytona Beach's 71,4882. The Daytona Beach area is much younger: Census Reporter puts its median age at 32, about two-thirds of the county's figure3.

What Volusia did

Ashley Moody is the Republican senator Ron DeSantis appointed in 2025, after Marco Rubio left the seat to become Secretary of State4. She carried Volusia over Democrat Angie Nixon by 135,240 votes to 98,314, a margin of R+15.6, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Donald Trump won the county by 21.8 points in 2024. That is a move of 6 points toward the Democrats.

Statewide, Moody won by 2.9 points, a 10-point move from Trump's 13.1. In the governor's race, Byron Donalds beat David Jolly by 1.3 points. Every Florida county but one moved left. The typical county moved 7 points, and the average weighted by votes cast was 10. Volusia ranked 43rd of 67, so it moved a little less than the typical county and much less than the big metros. Miami-Dade moved 19 points, Broward 15 and Orange 15. Hillsborough moved 12 and flipped from R+3.1 to D+9.0. Pinellas moved about as much as Volusia, 6 points, and that was enough to tip it narrowly Democratic. Outside Florida, big counties that started from similar Republican margins moved further: Macomb, Michigan, moved 12 points, Suffolk, New York, 11, and Denton and Collin in Texas 22 and 23. The typical county nationally moved 14.

Who turned out, and why

The Senate race drew 236,704 votes, 76 percent of the 2024 total and about 74,000 fewer ballots. Moody's lead fell to about 37,000 votes from Trump's roughly 68,000. The county count can't show how much of that gap closed because Trump voters stayed home and how much because voters switched sides. Both probably played a part. Supervisor of Elections Lisa Lewis reported turnout well below 2024's general election but above the 2022 midterm5. Her office also said residents set a midterm early-voting record5.

Republicans have a large registration lead here. The supervisor's rolls list 192,756 Republicans, 127,188 Democrats and 126,196 voters with no party6, so independents decide how close a race gets. A statewide University of North Florida poll, reported by News4Jax, found Nixon led Moody by 15 points among independent voters7.

News4Jax described the race as a contrast between Moody's emphasis on economic policies, law enforcement and her record, and Nixon's focus on the cost of living, health care and families, with clashes over Nixon's ties to the Democratic Socialists of America and a Hope Florida grand jury report7. On election night, Moody said she had predicted "this election would come down to freedom versus socialism."7 In a Mason-Dixon poll reported by NBC Miami, 30% of Florida voters named inflation and the cost of living as the biggest influence on their vote8. WLRN reported that a majority of homeowners said property insurance, not property taxes, concerned them most9.

Nixon was not Volusia Democrats' first choice. In August, Alexander Vindman narrowly carried the county's Democratic Senate primary over her10, even though Nixon won statewide over a rival who out-raised her more than 17 to one11. The Watch found no exit poll or local survey that explains Volusia's move in particular. The local outlets mostly reported results rather than explanations. A finer breakdown is also harder this year: the West Volusia Beacon noted the county's new results system does not identify which individual precincts have submitted their results12. Lewis said precinct detail would be available next year12.

What it means locally

The narrower margin showed up in the House races too. Randy Fine, a Republican, held Florida's 6th District. Citing the Associated Press, News Daytona Beach reported that Fine out-polled Yonce in all six of the district's counties13, but Volusia was the closest13. The 7th District is made up of Seminole County and the southern portion of Volusia14. There, Republican Ryan Elijah's lead over Democrat Bale Dalton tightened after Election Night but stayed outside the threshold for a machine recount15. On Saturday, Central Florida Public Media reported that Elijah had claimed a narrow victory as canvassing boards submitted their unofficial results16.

In the nonpartisan County Council races, two incumbents lost. Wendy Anderson won the District 1 seat and former Deltona commissioner David Sosa won District 55, unseating Don Dempsey and David Santiago, according to News Daytona Beach's results17. Volusia remains a Republican county by a wide margin. This year's statewide swing narrowed that margin here less than it did in most of Florida.
