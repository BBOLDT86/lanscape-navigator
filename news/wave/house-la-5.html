Split GOP field puts Monroe Democrat Pat Moore in Louisiana runoff

Six Republicans split the vote in Louisiana's open 5th District, and state Rep. Patricia Moore finished first with 18 percent. She faces Republican Gabe Firment on Dec. 12. In the Senate race, every parish in the district swung toward Democrats.

Louisiana's 5th Congressional District has sent Republicans to Washington for more than two decades. With Tuesday's open primary nearly counted, a Democrat is in first place, but with far too few votes to win the seat.

State Rep. Patricia "Pat" Moore of Monroe led the field with 40,789 votes, about 18 percent, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Republican state Rep. Gabe Firment of Pollock was 1,658 votes behind with 39,131. Republicans Misti Cordell (33,366) and Michael Echols (32,902) came next. AP has not called the seat, but it has declared that Moore advances1, and she and Firment meet in a Dec. 12 runoff to replace Julia Letlow, who left her seat for a successful Senate bid2. There were three Democrats and six Republicans on the ballot3, and the Republicans together got 26.1 points more of the vote than the Democrats.

How a Democrat finished first

The race was set up late. Republican lawmakers redrew the state's House map after a late-April Supreme Court decision let them eliminate one of two majority-Black districts4. The state also dropped its new party-primary system5 and moved to an open primary on Nov. 3 with a Dec. 12 runoff6.

The Louisiana Illuminator's explanation is about vote-splitting. Several Monroe-area Republicans divided the vote there, which let a Monroe Democrat and a central Louisiana lawmaker take the top two places7. Cordell, of West Monroe8, was appointed Board of Regents chair by Gov. Jeff Landry9 and had the state Republican Party's endorsement9. Echols had a large financial advantage9. The Illuminator put his fundraising at $3.9 million, five times what Cordell raised7. The Center Square reported that Firment had raised $49,000 through mid-July9.

Firment chairs the state House Insurance Committee2 and called himself the most conservative candidate in the race at an October forum10. Energy and data centers came up often: Echols wanted more oversight of data centers' water and power use, and Firment said developers should not pass infrastructure costs on to ratepayers9. The district includes Richland Parish, where Meta is building a $50 billion data center11.

Moore also had to deal with a fight inside her own party. The Democratic State Central Committee announced it was endorsing her before realizing she had not won a majority of its votes. It then voted again and backed Antonio Wilson, a contractor from Tallulah.7 Her opposition to abortion also caused tension in the party7. She has said she has trouble supporting rape and incest exceptions because she was conceived through rape.12 In a statement after the switch, she said the race "isn't decided in a committee room."13 She served on the Ouachita Parish Police Jury and was elected to the Legislature in 2019, the same year as Firment7.

Every parish moved toward Democrats

Louisiana calls its counties parishes, and the redrawn 5th District covers 24 of them. They include the Delta parishes along the Mississippi River, such as East Carroll, Madison and Tensas; Ouachita (Monroe) and Lincoln (Ruston); Rapides (Alexandria) and rural Grant, LaSalle and Winn; St. Landry; and the Florida parishes from Washington west to the Felicianas.

In the Senate race on the same ballot, every one of these parishes moved toward Democrats compared with the 2024 presidential vote. The moves ranged from 10 points in East Carroll to 28 in Catahoula. Weighted by votes, the district's parishes moved 18.8 points. That is a little less than the 20.2-point move statewide, where Letlow beat Jamie Davis, a Tensas Parish farmer11, by 1.8 points.

Two parishes flipped. St. Landry went from Trump by 18.8 points to Davis by 3.1, and Davis's home parish of Tensas went from R+4.3 to D+22.3. Letlow won Morehouse by just 1.4. Ouachita moved 18 points to R+10.1, Rapides 21 points to R+16.3 and Richland 21 points. Davis made Letlow's nondisclosure agreement with Meta over the Richland project a centerpiece of his campaign.11 The most Republican parishes stayed that way: LaSalle was R+68.0 and Grant R+61.3.

These are changes in the margin, not a count of people who changed their minds. The Illuminator reported strong turnout among Black voters and Democrats in early voting.14 The count has only this year's totals, including 227,917 votes in the House race, so it cannot show how much of the shift came from voters switching sides and how much from who turned out. The Senate swing also did not carry fully to the House race. In 17 of the 24 parishes, including the six largest, Letlow won by less than the 26.1 points the Republican House candidates led by together.

What December decides

Republicans have held the seat since 2004, when Rodney Alexander, its last Democrat, switched parties.15 No incumbent was running this year. Little of the vote is left to count. Cordell is 5,765 votes behind Firment. Grant Parish, part of the area Firment represents in the Legislature10, has the least counted of any parish, at 93.6 percent of its Senate vote.

Firment said he had expected a runoff because the field had several strong Republicans, and he thanked central Louisiana.16 He said he would work with Echols to help northeast Louisiana.16 Moore asked voters who did not support her to give her a chance to talk with them more.17 AP has already called 252 House seats for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority, so the Dec. 12 runoff will not decide control of the House.
