The wave · state by state · South Carolina

South Carolina's Republican Upstate moved furthest as Andrews took the Graham seat

Every one of South Carolina's 46 counties moved toward the Democrats. Spartanburg and the Upstate's Republican strongholds moved the most. Hilton Head and the southern coast moved the least, and Republicans held the governor's office on the same ballot.

Annie Andrews, a Charleston pediatrician who had never held office, won South Carolina's U.S. Senate seat by 4.5 points: 1,043,719 votes to 951,904 for Sen. Darline Graham, with 99% of the expected vote counted. Donald Trump carried the state by 17.9 points in 2024, so the state as a whole moved 22.4 points toward the Democrats. All 46 counties moved in that direction. The typical county moved 20.9 points, against 14.1 for the typical county nationally. Andrews carried 25 counties, and 13 of them had voted for Trump, including Greenville, which cast the most votes in the state, and York.

According to NPR, Andrews becomes the first woman to win a Senate election in South Carolina, and the first Democratic Senator elected in the state since 1998.1 The counts are still unofficial. Results are being certified across the state2, WRDW reported.

The governor's race shows how unusual the Senate race was. On the same ballot, Republican Alan Wilson beat Democrat Jermaine Johnson by 4.5 points, a move of 13.4 points from 2024 and about 9 points less than the Senate race. Both races drew almost the same number of votes, 2,040,928 and 2,037,662. Even so, Wilson won 99,114 more votes than Graham, and Andrews won 83,443 more than Johnson.

An appointed senator and a divided party

Graham was new to politics. Her brother, four-term incumbent Lindsey Graham, won the Republican nomination in the June 9 primaries, but died on July 11, triggering an August 11 special primary.3 ABC News reported that Trump endorsed Darline Graham's candidacy to replace her brother in the November general election before she even entered the race4. She then beat Rep. Ralph Norman in the runoff. The SC Daily Gazette reported that Norman refused to campaign with her and made clear his entire family didn't support her5, and his son, Warren Norman, endorsed Andrews.5

Andrews made stopping planned Medicaid cuts6 her top promise. South Carolina Public Radio reported that in the candidates' only debate she cited the war in Iran as a main driving factor of rising prices7. Fortune reported that she rarely campaigned with national Democrats, and a week before the election she picked up the endorsements of two former governors8: Republican David Beasley and Democrat Jim Hodges. The polls had shown a close race but not a Democratic win. A New York Times/Siena poll in the final week showed the race tied.8

Who moved: the reddest counties, and where fewer people voted

These figures describe counties, not voters. Still, the pattern is clear. The 13 counties Trump won by 30 points or more moved 22.9 points, weighted by votes. The two counties Harris won by 30 or more moved 18.5. Bigger counties moved more than small ones. The 12 counties with more than 50,000 votes, which cast 71% of the state's vote, moved 21.9 points. The 13 counties under 10,000 moved 18.4.

The largest moves came in the Upstate and along the I-77 and I-20 corridors. Spartanburg went from Trump +33.6 to Graham +4.7, the biggest move in the state. Greenville went from Trump +22.2 to Andrews +2.2. The Post and Courier reported that Upstate voters sided with Norman during the contentious primary, and their support for Graham consistently lagged in the general election.9 Norman's district runs from Spartanburg County through the Charlotte suburbs to the Midlands9. Several counties along that route moved more than the state median: Kershaw by 27 points, Chester by 23 and York by 23. Lancaster, at 19.5, moved less. Lexington moved 26 points; the SC Daily Gazette identified it as Graham's home county5. Anderson County, where Trump held a rally in Pendleton on Oct. 21, went from Trump +47.4 to Graham +23.4.

Turnout fell everywhere. The 2026 count is 80% of the 2024 total, and the counties where it fell furthest tended to move most (a correlation of -0.61). The 27 counties at 65% to 80% of their 2024 vote moved 22.7 points. The 18 at 80% to 95% moved 20.6. Jasper, the only county close to its 2024 total at 96%, moved 11.7. That pattern fits Trump voters staying home. But a county count cannot separate who stayed home from who changed their mind.

The split ballot points to persuasion as well. The Gazette found that Wilson received a greater share of votes than Graham in every county.5 It noted that South Carolinians do not register by party and called this clear evidence of Republicans crossing over to vote for Andrews over Graham.5 The paper named seven counties that voted for both Wilson and Andrews: Barnwell, Calhoun, Clarendon, Colleton, Greenville, Jasper and York.5

Where the move was smallest

The smallest moves came along the southern coast and the Savannah River. McCormick moved 11 points, Jasper 12, Beaufort 12, Allendale 12 and Hampton 12. Beaufort, home to Hilton Head Island, went from Trump +14.5 to Graham +2.7. Its turnout held up better than most: the 2026 count was 83% of its 2024 total. The Post and Courier reported that voter turnout in Beaufort County reached a record 61 percent in the 2026 midterm election.10 Farther up the coast, Horry (Myrtle Beach) moved 17 points and Georgetown 16, both below the state median. Graham's 21.5-point margin in Horry was her largest by votes anywhere in the state.

Counties that were already Democratic moved fewer points, but their margins grew a lot. Richland went from Harris +34.6 to Andrews +53.2. Orangeburg went from +24.6 to +43.9, and Charleston, Andrews' home county, from +5.7 to +26.1. The record early vote helped: South Carolina's early ballots this fall more than doubled the number from the 2022 midterm election, but were below those seen in the 2024 general election.11 A University of South Carolina lecturer told Spectrum News that this was "the first race in legitimately a decade plus in South Carolina that is competitive."12
