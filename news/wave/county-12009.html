Brevard County moves 7 points left, less than Florida as a whole

On a Space Coast swamped by record flooding the day before the election, Ashley Moody carried Brevard comfortably. The county moved left about as much as a typical Florida county, but well short of the state's big metros.

What Brevard did

Brevard County is home to Kennedy Space Center, Melbourne, Palm Bay and Titusville, and it stayed Republican on Nov. 3, though by less than before. Ashley Moody, the appointed Republican senator, beat Angie Nixon, a Democratic state representative from Jacksonville, 152,473 to 113,922. With 99% of the expected vote counted, that is a margin of R+14.3. Donald Trump carried the county by 20.8 points over Kamala Harris in 2024.

The 7-point move matches Florida's typical county almost exactly. Brevard ranked 37th of 67, and all but one Florida county moved toward the Democrats. The state as a whole moved 10 points, though, because its big metro counties moved further. Miami-Dade moved 19 points and flipped. Orange moved 15 and Hillsborough 12. Pinellas, the other big Gulf-and-Atlantic suburban county, moved 6. Statewide, Moody won by 2.9 points, and Republican Byron Donalds beat Democrat David Jolly for governor by 1.3.

Brevard also moved less than the other big counties that Trump won by 10 to 21 points. Macomb, Michigan, and Suffolk, New York, each moved about 11 to 12 points. Denton and Collin in Texas moved more than 20. Nationally, the typical county moved 14 points, and 2,673 of 2,775 moved left.

Brevard cast 270,072 votes, 75% of its 2024 total of 361,449. Trump's 2024 margin there was about 75,000 votes, and Moody's was about 38,500. County totals cannot show how much of that drop came from Trump voters switching sides and how much from voters who stayed home in a midterm. The move mixes both.

Why: a flood, a campaign and a Republican base

The election came right after a disaster. According to Central Florida Public Media, some parts of southern Brevard County had more than 20 inches of rain1. WKMG News 6 reported that the flooding forced the county to move several Election Day polling locations2, and Palm Bay's mayor confirmed two deaths connected to the flooding3. The Weather Channel reported that the flooding kept some residents home on Election Day4. Even so, Central Florida Public Media reported shortly before polls closed that Brevard was among the counties with the next highest turnout5 in the region after Sumter. Supervisor of Elections Tim Bobanic said his top priority is to keep voters and election workers safe.6

The county's voter rolls lean Republican. As of Nov. 5, the elections office listed Republican 209,284, Democratic 113,726, Non-Partisan 108,2027. Florida Today described Brevard's legislative districts as mostly suburban, middle- to upper-middle-income with strong ties to aerospace and defense industries. Voters are older, with significant retiree populations.8 It said they share worries about flooding, homeowners insurance and property taxes.

Statewide, WJXT News4JAX described the Senate race as a contrast between Moody's emphasis on economic policies, law enforcement and her record in office, and Nixon's focus on the cost of living, health care and support for families.9 CBS News Miami reported that Moody was quick to highlight Nixon's membership with the Democratic Socialists of America.10 Nixon attacked Moody over the $10 million in Medicaid settlement funds connected to the Hope Florida Foundation11. In the exit poll published by NBC News, which covers the whole state and not Brevard alone, 37% of voters named the economy as the top issue and 33% the state of democracy. In the same poll, 47% said Nixon's views were too extreme.

Gov. Ron DeSantis credited Florida's resistance to the national swing to Republicans' 1.5 million-registered-voters advantage over Democrats and an infusion of billions of dollars into GOP campaigns12. Aubrey Jewett, a political scientist at the University of Central Florida, called the state a breakwater that stopped the Democrats' insurgent wave13.

What it means for the county

Republicans still won the top races in Brevard. Florida Politics reported that Rep. Mike Haridopolos has defeated Democrat Jennifer Jenkins in Florida's 8th Congressional District14, a race the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee never listed14 among its targets.

The closest race is in south Brevard and is not decided. Florida Politics reported that Democrat Dan McDow pulled ahead of Republican state Rep. Monique Miller15 in House District 33 after a late count of mail ballots erased the incumbent's Election Night lead.15 On Saturday, Bobanic said the canvassing board had found the margin small enough that a machine recount is required. The board has set aside Nov. 10-12 for recounts in the district that includes Palm Bay and parts of southern Brevard County.16

The Senate seat comes up again in 2028. CBS News Miami reported that Nixon left the door open to another run in 2028.17
