The House · Florida 26

Diaz-Balart leads by 1,704 votes in redrawn Hialeah-to-Broward seat

A new map meant to help Florida Republicans added Democratic southwest Broward to Mario Diaz-Balart's Hialeah-and-Doral district. With 99% counted, the incumbent since 2003 leads first-time candidate Nicole Locklin by 0.7 points, just outside recount range.

Mario Diaz-Balart, a Republican who has served in the House since 20031, leads Democrat Nicole Locklin by 1,704 votes in Florida's 26th District, 114,578 to 112,874. That's a margin of 0.7 points on 229,804 votes, with 99% of the expected vote counted. Deborah Meidinger Hosey, who ran without a party affiliation, has 2,352 votes. That's more than the gap between the two leaders. The Associated Press has not called the race.

Diaz-Balart declared victory the day after the election. In a statement reported by WLRN, he said the results were "available for anybody to see in black and white: this race is over."2 Locklin told Local 10 she was not conceding, and her campaign said it believes she won3, the Miami Herald reported.

The race

Locklin, 38, is a lawyer for Databricks who moved from California to Miami last year. This is her first run for Congress, and she framed her campaign around corruption as the root of South Florida's affordability crisis4, the Herald reported. Speaking to Local 10, she called for limits on luxury high-rises during a housing shortage, backed Bernie Sanders' call to tax billionaires and supported renewing Temporary Protected Status (TPS).5 She also called for abolishing Immigration and Customs Enforcement and hiring more immigration judges.4

Diaz-Balart pointed to the billions of federal dollars he says he has brought home for infrastructure, the Everglades, law enforcement, school security and housing.5 WSVN reported that worries about affordability and immigration in the district put him in a real fight to keep his seat.6 CBS News Miami reported that, according to census data, more than 70% of residents in both Doral and Hialeah are foreign-born7. Diaz-Balart told the station that securing the border was necessary but called some of the enforcement that followed an "overreaction,"7 and he told The Hill in September that he had always supported TPS for Venezuelans and Haitians4.

The voters CBS Miami talked to put the cost of living first.7 All three candidates blamed higher fuel costs on the war with Iran. Locklin wanted it ended immediately, while Hosey wanted Congress to debate it.7 WSVN reported that Diaz-Balart heavily outspent Locklin.8

The district

The seat was redrawn this spring, when Gov. Ron DeSantis pushed a mid-decade map through the Legislature that was intended to help Republicans capture as many as four Democratic-held seats. He signed it May 4.9 For the 26th, that meant losing an area of Collier County and gaining a section of western Broward5, Local 10 reported. The new district joins southwest Broward to Doral, Hialeah and Miami Lakes.6 NBC Miami described the Broward additions as heavily Democratic.9 A Miami Herald analysis found Locklin carried the Broward portion comfortably and Diaz-Balart carried the Miami-Dade portion by a similar margin.3

The Watch's wave map tracks whole counties, not just the parts inside the district. Both counties moved sharply toward Democrats in the statewide Senate and governor's vote. Miami-Dade went from R+11.4 for Donald Trump in 2024 to D+7.1, a 19-point move that flipped the county. Broward went from D+17.0 to D+32.3, a 15-point move. Weighted together, the two counties moved 17 points, more than Florida as a whole. The state was R+13.1 in 2024. This year it was R+2.9 in Ashley Moody's Senate win over Angie Nixon and R+1.3 in Byron Donalds' win over David Jolly for governor. The county counts (784,728 votes in Miami-Dade, 660,314 in Broward) cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from changes in who turned out.

What the result means

The Cook Political Report rated the seat solidly Republican1, and Diaz-Balart won easily in 2024 under the old boundaries.4 NBC6 political analyst Alex Penelas said the race was close because redistricting took reliably Republican areas out of the district to help Republicans in neighboring seats.9

There has been one public dispute over the count. Diaz-Balart said 3,000 votes briefly appeared in Locklin's column in news-media tallies before being corrected minutes later.10 The AP told CBS Miami that a data-entry error caused the wrong total to appear, and that it was quickly identified and corrected.11 Locklin told the station: "If they can't explain where 3,000 votes went, they owe me a recount."11

Broward's canvassing board accepted only about 100 of roughly 300 outstanding ballots it reviewed Friday. Officials in both counties said too few votes remain to change the outcome or trigger a recount11, CBS Miami reported. According to Florida Politics, Florida requires a machine recount when the margin is 0.5 points or less. County returns are due by noon Nov. 16, and the state certifies on Nov. 17.12 In The Watch's count, the margin is 0.7 points. Next door, Miami-Dade's elections supervisor said the 27th District race would go to a recount if its margin held.8

Nationally, Democrats have 252 called seats and Republicans 161. That is past the 218 needed for a majority, so this seat will not decide control of the House. It is one of 22 still uncalled.
