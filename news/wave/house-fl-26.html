Diaz-Balart survives redrawn Florida 26 by 1,704 votes

Florida's longest-serving House member beat first-time Democrat Nicole Locklin by less than a point in a district Republicans redrew to take in part of southwest Broward County. Locklin has not conceded.

Mario Diaz-Balart, the Republican who has represented Miami-area districts since 2003, won Florida's 26th Congressional District with 114,578 votes to 112,874 for Democrat Nicole Locklin. That is a margin of 1,704 votes, or 0.7 points, out of 229,804 cast, with 99% of the expected vote counted. Deborah Meidinger Hosey, an independent, drew 2,352 votes, which is more than the gap between the two leaders.

The race

Locklin was a newcomer. The Miami Herald described her as a 38-year-old lawyer for Databricks who moved from California to Miami last year, a first-time congressional candidate who framed her campaign around fighting corruption as the root cause of the affordability crisis affecting South Florida.1 CBS News Miami reported that she also campaigned on lowering tariffs and shifting more of the tax burden to billionaires and large corporations.2

Diaz-Balart ran on his seniority. He emphasized his experience in Washington and his efforts to reduce federal spending2, and he pointed to $12 billion in spending cuts through a congressional subcommittee he chairs2.

Immigration hung over the race. Per CBS News Miami, the district includes Doral and Hialeah, where more than 70% of residents are foreign-born2. Diaz-Balart defended efforts to secure the U.S. border but acknowledged that some subsequent immigration enforcement actions amounted to an overreaction.2 In a Spanish-language interview with Martí Noticias, he went further and called arrests of people going through legal immigration proceedings "unacceptable"1.

He also had a large money advantage. The Miami Herald's editorial board noted that Locklin raised $600,000 to Diaz-Balart's $2.76 million.3 The Herald reported the seat had been rated likely Republican1 by pollsters.

The district

The district was drawn this year. Gov. Ron DeSantis called lawmakers into a special session and presented his proposed map on April 27. The Legislature approved the map two days later, and DeSantis signed it into law May 4.4 The 26th kept its northwest Miami-Dade base around Hialeah, Miami Lakes and Doral2. In place of Collier County, it now includes parts of Pembroke Pines and Miramar, and Southwest Ranches in Broward County.5

That change is why the race was close. According to the Herald's editorial board, she won Broward while Diaz-Balart carried the Miami-Dade portion of the district3.

The Watch's results for this seat come as a district total and are not broken down by county. Because the lines changed, there is also no matching 2024 result for this district. The 229,804 ballots cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from differences in who turned out, or from the effect of the new boundaries.

The statewide picture gives some context. Republican Ashley Moody won the Senate race by 2.9 points, 10.2 points closer than Donald Trump's 13.1-point Florida win in 2024. Republican Byron Donalds won the governor's race by 1.3 points, a move of 11.8 points.

What the result means

Diaz-Balart, first elected to Congress in 2002, is Florida's longest-serving current member of Congress.2 His win keeps the seat Republican, but the count after election night was contested.

On election night, Locklin briefly appeared to be ahead. The AP said a data entry error caused an incorrect vote total to briefly appear for Nicole Locklin in Florida's 26th Congressional District race. We quickly identified and corrected the total.6 Diaz-Balart said those 3,000 votes "never appeared in the official election results of Miami-Dade County, Broward County, or the State of Florida"7.

Locklin disputes that account. WLRN reported that she claims she held a lead of 2,545 votes at the end of Election Night Tuesday8, and she said "we have received conflicting accounts from the AP and the Miami-Dade SOE"8.

A few ballots were still being reviewed. Broward's elections supervisor, Joe Scott, reported that out of approximately 300 outstanding ballots under review in Broward County, only about 100 were accepted.9 Officials in both counties said there are not enough outstanding votes remaining to alter the outcome or trigger a recount9.

Under Florida law, an automatic machine recount is triggered at the 0.5-point threshold6. The 0.7-point margin here is above that line. Florida Politics reported that the state officially ordered a recount in Florida's 27th10 district next door, but not in this one.

Reporting on when the race was called is inconsistent. The AP results file The Watch uses marks the call at 4:33 p.m. ET and labels it election night. But the day after the election, the AP told CBS it has not declared a winner in this race6, and WLRN reported the AP call on Monday, Nov. 9. Locklin said that day "The race for Florida's 26th Congressional District is not over"8, and she is seeking records from the Miami-Dade elections office.

Nationally, Democrats had 252 House seats called and Republicans 165, with 18 races still uncalled.
