Dubuque pastor Lindsay James flips Iowa's open 2nd District seat

Democrat Lindsay James beat Trump-endorsed Republican Joe Mitchell by 4 points for the seat Ashley Hinson left to run for Senate. All 22 counties in the district moved toward Democrats in the statewide races.

Lindsay James, a Democratic state representative and Presbyterian minister from Dubuque who made her faith a centerpiece of her campaign1, won northeast Iowa's 2nd District on Nov. 3. She beat Republican Joe Mitchell 171,514 votes to 157,724. With 99% of the expected vote counted, her margin is 4.0 points, or 13,790 votes. The Associated Press called the race at 12:20 a.m. Eastern. Independent Dave Bushaw and Libertarian Rick Stewart also ran2. Stewart got 9,500 votes and Bushaw 7,470, which together is more than the gap between the top two.

The seat was open because Congresswoman Ashley Hinson instead chose to run for the U.S. Senate2. She lost that race to Democrat Josh Turek. KCRG reported that Democrats won the governor's race, the U.S. Senate race and three of four U.S. House seats.3

The race

Mitchell is 29. He became the youngest member ever of the Iowa House when he was sworn in at 214 and previously served as regional director for the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development during the current administration.5 The Gazette reported that Trump's endorsement effectively clears the Republican primary field6. Questions about where he lived followed him through the race. He grew up in southeast Iowa and represented the Mount Pleasant area7 in the Legislature, then moved to Des Moines. He switched his voter registration to Clear Lake shortly before he launched the campaign.

According to Iowa Public Radio, James held public town halls in each of the district's 22 counties.8 The Hill reported that she ran on the cost of living, arguing that Trump's tariffs and the GOP's sweeping tax and spending law were squeezing families9. Mitchell pitched lower taxes and his experience as a housing developer as solutions to rising costs.9 In their Iowa PBS debate, James called for rolling back the tariffs. Mitchell credited the administration's tariff policies for the $15 billion steel mill proposed for Lee County10. James was born in Portland, Oregon11, and Mitchell characterized James as a transplant who does not understand Iowa. He also criticized James for missing over half of the votes taken during the 2026 legislative session.8 She called him a former lobbyist. He says he founded the Iowa Real Estate Developers Association but never lobbied on behalf of the group.12

Punchbowl News, citing AdImpact, reported a total of $11.3 million has been spent and reserved in ads in the 2nd District since the start of September4. Democratic groups were outspending Republicans even after a MAGA Inc.-aligned PAC committed $2.3 million. An NPR analysis found that the two parties together spent $6.6 million on final-week ads8, with Democrats ahead by nearly $2 million. Iowa Public Radio concluded that Mitchell's defense of Trump administration policies appears to have hurt him in the end8.

The district

The district covers 22 counties in northeast Iowa, including Cedar Rapids, Waterloo, Dubuque, Decorah, Grinnell and Mason City13. The Watch's wave report compares each county's 2026 vote for Senate and governor with the 2024 Harris–Trump vote. These are top-of-the-ticket numbers, not James's own county results. By that measure, every county moved toward Democrats. The smallest move was 13 points in Grundy and the largest was more than 17 in Black Hawk. Weighted by votes, the district moved 15 points.

The size of the moves was similar from county to county, but the counties started in very different places. Linn County cast 106,737 votes and went from D+9.9 to D+23.9. Black Hawk went from D+1.2 to D+18.6. Three counties that voted for Trump favored Democrats this time. Dubuque, James's home, went from R+8.6 to D+5.3. Winneshiek, which includes Decorah, went from R+9.3 to D+3.6. Cerro Gordo, home to Mitchell's town of Clear Lake7, went from R+11.7 to D+3.5. Poweshiek, home to Grinnell, finished at R+1.1. The farm counties stayed Republican by smaller margins: Butler went from R+45.4 to R+29.8.

These shifts do not show that voters changed their minds. Secretary of State Paul Pate called it "the largest midterm in three decades here in Iowa."14 The county count cannot tell voters who switched parties from changes in who turned out, and The Watch has no 2024 county turnout to compare.

What it means

Hinson won the district easily two years ago15, and Trump also carried it in 2024. That year Iowa Democrats did not flip one of Iowa's four congressional districts.16 James's 4.0-point margin was almost the same as Turek's 4.1-point statewide win and well below Rob Sand's 11.0 points for governor. The Iowa Capital Dispatch compared the year to 2018: both were midterms during President Donald Trump's first and second terms in office, and resulting in major Democratic victories across the state.17

No incumbent lost this race. Mitchell conceded the race at a watch party at the Surf Ballroom in Clear Lake.8 About 1% of the expected vote is still uncounted, and results remain unofficial until the state canvass on Nov. 24.18 Nationally, 252 House seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.
