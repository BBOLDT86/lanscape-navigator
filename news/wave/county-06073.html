San Diego County moves left, but at half California's pace

Xavier Becerra leads Steve Hilton by 22 points in San Diego County with about two-thirds of the expected vote counted. That is 5 points better for Democrats than 2024, while California as a whole moved twice as far.

San Diego County is the second-largest county in California, with 8.3% of the state's population1. It is a border county where, by Census estimates, 35.7% of residents are Hispanic or Latino, 41.3% are non-Hispanic white and 14.1% are Asian2. It also has more than 180,000 veterans. In this year's race for governor, the county moved toward the Democrats more slowly than the rest of the state.

The race was for an open seat. Becerra is a former California attorney general and U.S. health secretary.3 Hilton is a former Fox News host and political commentator making his first run for office.3 Becerra won statewide and became the state's first Latino elected governor.4

What San Diego did

With 68 percent of the expected vote counted, Xavier Becerra has 536,035 votes in the county to Steve Hilton's 341,840. That puts the Democrat 22 points ahead. Kamala Harris carried the county by 16.8 points in 2024, so the move is 5 points toward the Democrats. Statewide, Becerra leads by 30.4 points, against Harris's 20.1, a move of 10 points.

San Diego ranks 31st of California's 58 counties by size of move. Fifty-four of the 58 moved left. The typical county moved 6 points, close to San Diego's figure. But the vote-weighted state average was 9 points, because the biggest counties moved further: Los Angeles 12 points, Riverside 17 and San Bernardino 19. Orange County matched San Diego at 5 points, and Santa Clara moved only 1.

Counties outside California that voted like San Diego in 2024 moved much more. Broward in Florida moved 15 points, Pima in Arizona 16, Hamilton in Ohio 10, and Cobb and Gwinnett in Georgia 19 and 26. Among the country's eight biggest counties, San Diego and Orange tied for the smallest move, far below the national typical move of 14 points.

Why: what the local press found

The 877,875 votes counted so far equal 59 percent of the county's 2024 total, and about a third of the expected vote is still out. That makes this a smaller and different electorate from 2024, so a 5-point move mixes changes in who voted with voters who switched parties, and the county count cannot separate the two. Turnout looked strong. The Registrar of Voters said there was a high turnout for the midterm elections in San Diego County5, and KPBS reported it could rank among the highest midterm turnout rates in recent county history, along with 1982, 2010 and 2018.5 Before Election Day, Registrar Shawn Brom said early mail returns were about one-third higher to date compared to the last gubernatorial election in 2022.6

Polling suggested the county would run behind the state. A late-October ABC 10News/Union-Tribune poll had 49 percent saying they would vote for Xavier Becerra, while 37 percent said they'd vote for Steve Hilton7. The same pollsters found Becerra ahead 54 to 32 statewide. A statewide poll reported by NBC Bay Area found that 34% of San Francisco/San Jose voters called illegal immigration a "very serious" problem, compared with 50% in Northern California and San Diego.8

Local reporters described an election decided by national issues. On KPBS's Midday Edition, a reporter said voters were responding to what the Trump administration has been doing, the federal immigration raids, the war in Iran.9 KPBS had earlier reported that the South Bay and other San Diego-area communities have been roiled by increased immigration enforcement10. Political analyst Wendy Patrick said frustration with costs was driving people to the polls in a midterm election where, traditionally, they may not have taken the time to cast a ballot.11

The NBC News exit poll does not break out San Diego. Statewide, it found Latino voters backing Becerra 68%12 to 32%. Locally, Voice of San Diego found the leftward shift largely stopped at the South Bay. It reported that interviews there showed voters distinguishing between national races – in which they loudly expressed their disgust with President Donald Trump and his MAGA allies – and local issues.13 Chula Vista re-elected its Republican mayor, John McCann.

What it means for the county's politics

The biggest change came in Congress. Darrell Issa was the only Republican Congressmember representing the San Diego County region14, and he retired after the Proposition 50 redistricting. CalMatters reported that Democrat Marni von Wilpert, a Democratic San Diego City councilmember prevailed over Republican San Diego County Supervisor Jim Desmond15 in the redrawn 48th District. KPBS noted that under the new map, there are more Democrats than Republicans16 registered in the district.

County government changed less. The Board of Supervisors currently has a 3-2 Democratic majority17. Democrats hoped to win a 4-1 supermajority by taking Desmond's North County seat. Instead, NBC 7 reported that San Marcos Mayor, Republican Rebecca Jones will win the contest over Democrat Kyle Krahel.18

The count is not finished. The Registrar counts all mail ballots postmarked by Election Day and received within seven days19, and certification is expected by December 3, 2026.20 San Diego's final margin and its move from 2024 could still change.
