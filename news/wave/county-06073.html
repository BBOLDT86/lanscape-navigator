San Diego County moves left but trails California's swing toward Becerra

Xavier Becerra carried San Diego County by about 24 points, up from Kamala Harris's 17. The county's last Republican House seat also fell. Even so, the county moved less than California did and less than most of the country's biggest counties.

Xavier Becerra won San Diego County easily on Nov. 3. Still, the border county moved less than the state did. With about 83 percent of the expected vote counted, Becerra, the Democrat, led Republican Steve Hilton 663,978 to 403,802, a margin of 24.4 points. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 16.8, so the county moved about 8 points toward the Democrats.

What the county did

California moved further. Becerra's statewide margin is 31.5 points, against Harris's 20.1, a move of 11.4. San Diego ranks 26th of the state's 58 counties by move. That puts it ahead of the typical California county (6 points) but behind the vote-weighted statewide figure of about 10. Its neighbors went different ways. Los Angeles County moved 14 points. San Bernardino and Riverside, which Trump narrowly carried in 2024, moved 21 and 17 and flipped to Becerra. Orange County moved 6, close to San Diego.

The national comparison shows a bigger gap. Among the country's biggest counties, only Orange moved less than San Diego. Counties that voted about the way San Diego did in 2024 moved roughly twice as far or more: Broward in Florida by 15 points, Pima in Arizona by 16, and Cobb and Gwinnett in Georgia by 19 and 26. The typical county nationwide moved 14.

About 1.07 million votes have been counted so far, 72 percent of the 2024 total, and that share will rise as the remaining ballots are counted. Because this electorate is smaller and made up differently, the move mixes changes in who turned out with voters who changed sides. County totals cannot separate the two.

Why it moved

The county's election office pointed to strong turnout. KPBS reported that Registrar of Voters Shawn Brom's figures would put this election among the highest midterm turnout rates in recent county history, along with 1982, 2010 and 2018.1 Before Election Day, Brom said mail ballot returns were running about one-third higher to date compared to the last gubernatorial election in 2022.2 On Election Day, wait times at multiple voting centers across San Diego County exceeded one or even two hours in the late afternoon and evening1.

Voice of San Diego reported that turnout among young and Latino voters hit record highs for a midterm election.3 On KPBS's Midday Edition, a panelist said voters were responding to what the Trump administration has been doing, the federal immigration raids, the war in Iran4. KPBS also reported that in immigrant communities there was fear of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents showing up at polling places.5

South County is a test case. After 2024, Voice of San Diego found the county's rightward shift was more pronounced in South County than in any other part of San Diego – and even more pronounced in precincts close to the border with a high proportion of Latino voters.6 By May, its reporter wrote that the shift appeared to be reversing. After this election, though, the outlet reported that local South Bay races stood in sharp contrast to a countywide leftward shift7. Interviews on Election Day showed South County voters distinguishing between national races7 and local ones.

Across California, the Associated Press reported, Becerra repeatedly tied his opponent, Republican Steve Hilton, to the president8. Hilton argued that one-party rule was to blame for the state's high cost of living and homelessness crisis.8 AP's voter poll found that most voters who said the economy was the top issue facing the country supported Becerra8. Locally, the race tightened from June to November. Fox 5 reported that Hilton has received the majority of votes in San Diego County so far9 in the June primary count. A late ABC 10News/Union-Tribune poll of the county had 49 percent of those asked said they would vote for Xavier Becerra, while 37 percent said they'd vote for Steve Hilton10.

What it means for the county

The clearest result is in Congress. KQED reported that Rep. Darrell Issa did not run again after his 48th District seat added friendly Democratic terrain in Palm Springs and northern San Diego County11, under the map voters approved in Proposition 50. Democrat Marni von Wilpert, a San Diego City Council member, defeated Republican Jim Desmond, a San Diego County supervisor.11 The Times of San Diego noted that Republicans held three of the county's five seats at the dawn of Barack Obama's second term.12 Rep. Sara Jacobs said that growing up, she never imagined a San Diego County without a single congressional Republican12.

Voice of San Diego reported in February that local Republicans had lost the voter-registration race, with recent polling showing approval in the mid-20s across the broader San Diego–Orange County region13 for Trump. Republicans still hold some local offices. NBC 7 reported that early results showed Desmond's open supervisor seat would likely remain in Republicans' hands.14 Counting continues, and the registrar says by law, election results must be certified by Dec. 3.15
