San Diego shifts toward Becerra, but trails California's bigger leftward swing

San Diego County gave Xavier Becerra a 26-point win for governor, 9 points better for Democrats than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin. That was a smaller move than California's, and some local races still went to Republicans.

What the county did

Democrat Xavier Becerra won San Diego County with 781,021 votes to 460,143 for Republican Steve Hilton in the Nov. 3 race for governor. That is a margin of 25.9 points, with 98.3 percent of the expected vote counted. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county by 16.8 points over Donald Trump, so the county moved 9 points toward Democrats.

This was a reversal from the June primary. A week after that vote, Fox 5 San Diego reported that according to San Diego County registrar data, Hilton has received the majority of votes in San Diego County so far1. In that crowded primary, he led the field in the county.

The county still moved less than the state. California went from D+20.1 for Harris to D+31.2 for Becerra, an 11-point move. San Diego ranked 20th of 58 counties. That beat the typical California county's move of about 6 points but fell short of the state's vote-weighted average of 11. Among the state's other big counties, San Bernardino moved 21 points, Riverside 19 and Los Angeles 14. Orange and Alameda moved 8 each, and Santa Clara moved 4. Elsewhere in the country, Broward County in Florida moved 15 points, Pima County in Arizona 16 and Gwinnett County in Georgia 26. All three had 2024 margins close to San Diego's. Ventura County, up the coast, moved about 9, the same as San Diego. Nationally the typical county moved 14 points. Of the country's eight biggest counties, only Orange moved less than San Diego.

San Diego is a large border county. Census estimates put its population at 3,282,2482, with Hispanic or Latino, percent(b); 35.7%3, compared with 41.2 percent statewide, and 14.1 percent Asian.

Why it moved

Fewer people voted, and that matters for reading the result. The governor's race drew 1,241,164 votes, 84 percent of the 2024 total. Becerra's lead was about 321,000 votes. Harris's lead was about 250,000, out of roughly 237,000 more ballots. The county-level count cannot separate voters who changed sides from voters who stayed home or showed up. The 9-point move reflects both. Registrar Shawn Brom said in October that early mail returns were about one-third higher to date compared to the last gubernatorial election in 20224. After Election Day, KPBS reported that the Registrar of Voters said there was a high turnout for the midterm elections in San Diego County5. Some vote centers had waits of an hour or more, many of those longer waits tended to be on college campuses5.

The campaign was about the cost of living and about Trump. Hilton brought his $3-a-gallon gas pledge to a Miramar gas station in late October, where cars lined up around the Stars and Stripes gas station6, Fox 5 reported. The Associated Press reported that Becerra repeatedly tied his opponent, Republican Steve Hilton, to the president7. In the final Berkeley IGS poll, its director, DiCamillo, said Becerra's backers were "over the top on fighting Trump administration policies"8. The NBC News exit poll covers the whole state, not this county. It found Latino voters were 23 percent of the electorate and Latino (23%) 68% 32%9 in Becerra's favor.

South County, the area along the border, deserves a closer look. Voice of San Diego reported that the county's 2024 rightward shift was more pronounced in South County than in any other part of San Diego10. By May the outlet reported that Latinos are reverting to many of their previously held views – though that does not necessarily mean unwavering allegiance to Democrats11. That caveat held up locally. After the election, Voice of San Diego wrote that South County election results stood in sharp contrast to a countywide leftward shift12, and Chula Vista's Republican mayor, John McCann, won another term.

What it means

At the federal level, the county is now entirely Democratic. Democrat Marni von Wilpert won the redrawn 48th District, which gave Democrats all five seats in the region13, Times of San Diego reported. Voters had approved Proposition 50, which redrew the district to stretch north from more conservative areas, in North San Diego County and Riverside County, through Temecula and Hemet into Palm Springs14. A KPBS Midday Edition panel said the new map absolutely boosted Democrats chances in these races. But based on the numbers that we're looking at, it seems like some of these races could have been won even without prop 50.15 San Diego State political scientist Stephen Goggin told Times of San Diego that "San Diego County has always been kind of trending in the Democratic direction, particularly in the recent decade or two"14.

Local offices changed less. NBC 7 San Diego reported that the Board of Supervisors' make-up likely to stay the same after 2026 midterm election16. Counting is not finished. The county has 30 days to certify the election17, so the final margin may still change slightly.
