The wave · the biggest counties · Johnson County, Kansas

In Hamilton's home county, Kansas suburbs supplied the Senate-winning margin

Johnson County moved about 20 points toward the Democrats from 2024 and gave Adam Hamilton a 28-point win at home. In votes, that margin was bigger than his whole statewide lead, even though fewer people voted than in 2024.

What Johnson County did

Johnson County is the state's most populous and wealthiest county1. It takes in Overland Park, Olathe, Shawnee, Lenexa and Leawood2. It gave Adam Hamilton, the Democrat, 187,261 votes and Sen. Roger Marshall, the Republican incumbent, 104,124, with 98.78 percent of the expected vote counted. That is a Democratic margin of 28.0 points. In 2024 Kamala Harris beat Donald Trump here by 8.5 points, so the county moved about 20 points.

Hamilton's lead in the county was about 83,100 votes. His lead across Kansas was about 55,600. That means Marshall finished ahead in the rest of the state combined, and Johnson County alone turned that into a statewide win. This is a county that chose Republican presidential candidates in 2012 and 2016 but voted Democratic in 2020 and 20243.

All 105 Kansas counties moved left. The state went from R+16.1 to D+5.1, a 21.2-point move. Johnson County ranked 32nd: a little above the typical county's 18 points and close to the vote-weighted average of 20. Wyandotte, next door, moved 25 points. Sedgwick, which includes Wichita, moved 22 and flipped from R+13.8 to D+7.7. Leavenworth moved 22 and ended at R+0.9. Johnson's move was far larger than those of similar big counties in New York and Michigan: Erie moved 1 point, Oakland 4 and Kent 8. It was smaller than the Texas counties' moves, with Bexar at 26 and Harris at 27. The typical county nationally moved 14.

Our count can't tell how much of the move came from people switching sides and how much from who turned out. Johnson County cast 297,051 votes, 86 percent of its 2024 total of 343,780 and about 46,700 fewer. Still, Election Commissioner Connie Schmidt told the Johnson County Post it was the third-highest number of raw votes counted in Johnson County ever4, behind only 2020 and 2024. Some people had worried that the Johnson County Election Office's decision to shutter 8 early voting sites could suppress turnout4. The Post reported that about two-thirds of those votes were cast in the early voting period.4

Why, according to the reporting

Hamilton ran at home. He is the founding pastor of the 25,000-member Church of the Resurrection, the nation's largest United Methodist congregation5, the AP reported. The AP said he won by appealing to moderate Republicans and independent voters who were frustrated with the rising cost of living6 and who disapproved of how Trump has handled the economy and the war with Iran.

KSHB reported that in Johnson County, his promise to meet voters in the middle is what drove much of his support7. One Olathe-area voter it interviewed said: "I'm a Republican, and I voted for Adam Hamilton because I felt like he's going to make a difference"7. Alexandra Middlewood, a political scientist at Wichita State University, described it as a case of "pocketbook voting."8 She said some people who voted for Marshall in 2020 switched because of rising costs.

Marshall called his opponent Adam Hamilton too woke and too weak for Kansas9, the Johnson County Post reported. NPR counted that in their final debate before Election Day, Marshall sought to tie Hamilton to Schumer 15 times.10 FOX4 reported that Hamilton also faced personal attacks, including being called a "pedophile protector" and labeled a "groomer."11 NPR noted that the race was the most expensive in Kansas history, with Hamilton breaking multiple fundraising records.10

What it means for the county's politics

The change went beyond the Senate race. Reporting for KCUR, the Johnson County Post said Democrats are poised to flip up to 11 House seats in Johnson County from red to blue.12 Statewide, Democrats gained at least 20 seats12, which ends the Republican two-thirds majority in the Kansas House. The Kansas City Star reported that Democrats are now expected to hold 25 of the county's 27 seats in the Kansas House.13 Among them, Democrat Megan Phillips looks poised to unseat Republican House Majority Leader Chris Croft12.

The governor's race is still undecided. Cindy Holscher, the Democrat, is a state senator from Overland Park4. In our data she leads Ty Masterson, the Republican, by 0.7 points statewide, and AP has not called the race. The governor's contest is one of several undergoing "close race" audits required by law4. Masterson has not yet conceded4. The county canvassing board will certify the final official results on Friday, Nov. 13.14

The Star described a yearslong trend of turning the previously purple county solidly blue15. Brandon Woodard, the Kansas House minority leader, who is from Lenexa, gave part of the credit to Democrats running candidates who listen to constituents13. Hamilton was also a well-known local pastor running in his own county. The count alone can't show how much of the 20-point move belongs to him and how much to his party.
