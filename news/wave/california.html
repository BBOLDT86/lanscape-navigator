Inland California swings to Becerra; ten Trump counties flip

Xavier Becerra leads Steve Hilton by 31.5 points with 83 percent of the expected vote counted, a margin 11 points wider than Kamala Harris's. The biggest moves came in Imperial County, the Central Valley and the Inland Empire.

Becerra is a former state attorney general and federal health secretary.1 He will be California's next governor. Associated Press figures show 83.3 percent of the expected vote counted, and in that count he leads Steve Hilton, the Republican, 7,451,961 votes to 3,879,315. That is a margin of 31.5 points. Kamala Harris carried the state by 20.1 points in 2024, so the Democratic margin has grown by 11.4 points. He will be the state's first Latino governor in more than 150 years.2

The shift showed up almost everywhere. Of 58 counties, 54 moved toward the Democrats and three toward the Republicans. Amador County did not move at all. Ten counties Donald Trump won in 2024 now show Becerra ahead, and no county flipped the other way. The typical California county still moved less than the typical county nationally, about 6 points against 14. California started from a much bluer baseline.

Imperial, the valley and the Inland Empire

Imperial County, on the Mexican border, moved the most. Trump won it by 0.9 points; Becerra leads it by 30.9, a 32-point move. Kern County went from Trump by 21 to Becerra by 2. San Bernardino County, with 97 percent of its vote counted, went from Trump by 2 to Becerra by 19. Merced, Stanislaus, San Joaquin and Riverside counties each moved 17 to 20 points, and Fresno County moved 12. Tulare, Madera and Kings counties stayed Republican but by much less: Tulare went from Trump by 21 to Hilton by 2.

The same region produced the state's most-watched House upset. ABC30 in Fresno reported that Randy Villegas, who unseated Rep. David Valadao, campaigned heavily on affordability issues affecting working-class families throughout the Central Valley3. The station also reported that immigration emerged as a major issue during the campaign, particularly as concerns mounted over increased ICE activity across the Central Valley. Valadao suggested immigration enforcement efforts may have damaged Republican prospects3. Post-election survey figures reported by GV Wire, a Fresno news site, found roughly three-quarters of Latino voters in the state backing Democrats in the House, a shift of 29 percentage points from 20244. The Republican consultant Mike Madrid told Axios that Becerra's win is largely because of anti-Republican, anti-Trump feelings in California2.

Who moved

Counties are not voters, but the charts show where the movement was strongest. The 10 counties that cast more than 250,000 votes each make up 74 percent of the count, and together they moved 10.7 points toward the Democrats. The 13 smallest counties moved 5.9. The biggest shift came in the 11 counties Trump won narrowly, by less than 10 points. They cast 16 percent of the vote and moved 16.6 points, and the typical one moved 13.6. In counties Trump won by 10 to 30 points, the vote-weighted move was 14.1, but the typical county moved only 5.8. Big counties like Kern, Tulare and Stanislaus swung sharply, while small foothill and mountain counties barely moved. The six reddest counties moved 8.4.

There is a similar gap in the bluest places. The 12 counties Harris won by 30 points or more cast nearly half the vote and moved 9.4 points when weighted by votes, but the typical one moved just 4.3. Los Angeles County, at 13.5 points, pulls that average up. San Francisco and Santa Clara counties moved 5 points or less.

So far, 11.3 million votes have been counted, 71 percent of the 2024 total. That figure combines lower midterm turnout with ballots still being counted. Riverside and Sacramento counties are each at about 60 percent of their expected vote. Counties below 65 percent of their 2024 vote moved 10.5 points. Those between 65 and 80 percent moved 6.7. Those between 80 and 95 percent moved 12.6 weighted, a figure driven by Los Angeles, but the typical one moved only 4.2. The correlation between a county's turnout and its move is weak (r = −0.23). County totals cannot show whether voters changed their minds or a different group of voters turned out. Reporting points to some of each. Before the vote, the head of UnidosUS told ABC7 that "This will undoubtedly be the largest turnout of Latino voters ever in a Midterm election"5. The survey shift among Latino voters suggests that some voters also switched sides.

The campaign, the exceptions and the count

According to Axios, Becerra debated Hilton once and did a few media interviews and very little campaigning.2 Trump endorsed Hilton, a Fox News host and political commentator, during the primary.2 CalMatters reported that Hilton called for tax cuts, abolishing the Department of Motor Vehicles, canceling the high-speed rail project and ending the state's climate goals6. He raised more than $33 million to Becerra's $38 million6. He also pledged to bring gas prices down to $3 a gallon7.

The three counties that moved right are all close to Sacramento or San Francisco. Marin County moved 4.5 points toward Hilton, from Harris by 63.9 to Becerra by 59.5. Only 68 percent of its vote is counted, so that number may change. San Mateo County was essentially flat. El Dorado County, with 99 percent counted, moved a point toward Hilton. The same ballot included Proposition 40, the billionaire tax, which voters rejected. NPR reported that the measure divided Democrats in a state where they heavily outnumber Republicans8. No reporting so far connects it to the governor's vote in those counties.

None of these numbers is final. Lake County, which flipped, has only 35 percent of its expected vote counted. Fresno, Madera and Tulare counties are around 60 percent. Hilton conceded on election night, and Becerra's win is not in doubt. The county margins may still change before the state certifies.
