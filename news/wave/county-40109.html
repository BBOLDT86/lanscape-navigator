Oklahoma County flips to Democrat in a Senate race Hern won easily

Donald Trump narrowly carried the county around Oklahoma City in 2024. Two years later it backed N'Kiyla Jasmine Thomas for the Senate by nearly 14 points, elected Democrats to county offices and voted down a jail tax.

What Oklahoma County did

Donald Trump carried Oklahoma County by 1.7 points in 2024. On Nov. 3 the county voted for Democrat N'Kiyla Jasmine Thomas over Republican Kevin Hern for the U.S. Senate by 13.6 points, 125,838 votes to 94,849, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. That is a 15-point move toward Democrats, and it flipped the state's largest county.

It did not decide the race. Hern won statewide by 17.9 points, 639,510 to 439,109. Hern is a four-term congressman1, and the seat is the one Markwayne Mullin left to become Homeland Security secretary2. Oklahoma County cast about a fifth of the state's Senate votes and gave Thomas nearly 29 percent of her statewide total.

The county's move was large but not unusual in Oklahoma, where all 77 counties moved left. It ranked 19th in the state. The statewide margin moved 16 points, and the typical county moved 13. Cleveland County also flipped, from R+14.9 to D+1.1. Tulsa County narrowed to R+1.3. Canadian County moved 21 points but still went Republican by 20. Big counties elsewhere that Trump carried by similarly thin margins moved by similar amounts: 12 points in Duval County, Florida, 17 in Riverside County, California, and 20 in Maricopa County, Arizona. Nationally, the typical county moved 14.

The county has about 822,100 people, making it the most populous county in Oklahoma and home to 19.9% of the state's residents3. Most of them live in Oklahoma City, with Edmond3 and Midwest City next in size. Hispanic residents make up 21% of the Oklahoma County population3.

Who voted, and why

Turnout is part of the story. The county cast 228,408 Senate votes, 79 percent of its 2024 presidential total and about 60,500 fewer ballots. County totals cannot separate voters who changed sides from shifts in who showed up. Both are mixed into the 15 points.

Across the state, early voting was strong but did not carry through. State Election Board Secretary Paul Ziriax said the early numbers "did not equate to a high voter turnout overall,"4 and the board reported that turnout was down compared to the last two gubernatorial elections in 2022 and 20184. News 9 reported that Oklahoma County had the most early voters in the state5.

The candidates were far apart. NonDoc described Thomas, 31, as a nurse and Chickasaw Nation citizen from Ardmore6 and the state's first major-party nominee to call herself a democratic socialist. NBC News reported that Thomas had just $456 in her campaign account as of Aug. 5, while Hern's campaign had $2.6 million on hand7. Trump campaigned on Hern's behalf during a recent visit to the state8 and said Thomas wanted to end capitalism. After winning, Hern called the race, as KTUL reported, "a choice between common sense and communism"9.

NBC News' exit poll covered the whole state, not this county. In it, the economy (41%)10 was the top issue, followed by the state of democracy (30%)10, and a majority said Trump's tariffs had hurt (56%)10 their local economy.

Thomas was not the only Democrat to carry the county. Republican Mike Mazzei won the governor's race, but Oklahoma and Tulsa counties, which are the state's two most populous, both supported11 Cyndi Munson, the House minority leader from Oklahoma City. In the 5th Congressional District, NonDoc reported that Nelson outpaced Bice in Oklahoma County12, but Republican Stephanie Bice held the seat with strong results in the district's other counties. KGOU found that Jena Nelson likely could have won with the previous district boundaries13. In 2021, lawmakers took the southwestern portion of Oklahoma County13 out of the district.

What it means at the courthouse

The change shows up most clearly in county government. NonDoc reported that voters rejected a proposed first-of-its-kind sales tax14 to build and run a new jail, while Democrats swept all three races for county office14. News 9 reported that the Board of County Commissioners is under Democrat control for the first time since 197815.

Democrat Jennifer Mayo won the District 3 commission seat by a very narrow margin. Free Press OKC reported that the jail project proved to be the primary driving campaign issue for Mayo16, who opposed the tax. Commissioner Brian Maughan said there was no backup plan if the tax failed17. NonDoc called the results a big shift toward Democrats in county politics, but noted that the County Budget Board will be split evenly with four Republicans and four Democrats14.

Above the county, the state stayed Republican. Republicans won each of the eight statewide offices on Tuesday's ballot18. The results are still unofficial. County boards were set to certify Friday after 5 p.m.4, and state and federal election results will be certified on Tuesday, Nov. 10.4
