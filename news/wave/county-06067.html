Sacramento County moves 10 points toward Democrats, tracking California almost exactly

The capital's home county gave Xavier Becerra a wider margin than it gave Kamala Harris. That shift is close to California's own. With about four in ten expected ballots still uncounted, the electorate so far is much smaller than in 2024.

What the county did

Sacramento County, home of the state capital, voted for Democrat Xavier Becerra over Republican Steve Hilton by 230,528 votes to 125,443 in the race for governor. That is a margin of 29.5 points with about 59 percent of the expected vote counted. Two years ago, Kamala Harris carried the county by 19.7 points over Donald Trump. The move toward the Democrats is 10 points.

The county looks a lot like the state. California as a whole went from D+20.1 in 2024 to D+30.4 for governor, a move of 10.3 points with about 71 percent of the expected vote counted. Sacramento has been within a point of the statewide margin both times. It ranks 20th of California's 58 counties by size of move. Fifty-four of the 58 moved left. The typical county moved about 6 points, and the vote-weighted average was about 9. The largest shifts among the big counties came inland. Riverside went from R+1.3 to D+15.5 and San Bernardino from R+2.1 to D+16.8, so both changed sides. Coastal Orange and San Diego moved 5 points each, and Los Angeles moved 12.

Big counties outside California with similar 2024 margins moved much further. Allegheny County in Pennsylvania, Dallas County in Texas and Montgomery County in Pennsylvania each moved about 23 points, and Broward County in Florida moved 15. Nationally, the typical county moved 14 points, while California's typical county moved about 6.

Why it moved

Becerra was born in Sacramento to a mother from Mexico and a father who was born in California1. He gave his victory speech in the area. KQED placed the party in Sacramento, and CalMatters placed it across the river in West Sacramento. The Associated Press reported that Becerra argued he was best positioned for the job after serving more than 35 years in state and federal office2. Hilton, a former Fox News host and adviser to former U.K. Prime Minister David Cameron, has never held elected office2. He blamed one-party rule for the state's high cost of living and homelessness crisis2. CapRadio noted that Trump endorsed Hilton in the primary3, and Becerra has been eager to tie Hilton to the president at every turn.3

The AP Voter Poll measured the whole state, not the county. It found that most voters who said the economy was the top issue facing the country supported Becerra4. He also won a majority of voters who said the cost of living was "crucial"4.

Within the county, the pattern was urban against rural. Abridged, PBS KVIE's local news site, mapped the precincts and found that Becerra led in nearly all urban portions of Sacramento County and Yolo County5. Meanwhile, Hilton led in several rural areas, along with Placer and El Dorado counties.5

Turnout matters as much as the margin. The 355,971 votes counted so far equal 54 percent of the county's 2024 total, and that share will rise because about 41 percent of the expected vote has not been counted yet. County elections officials post updates for Tuesdays and Fridays, with a few exceptions, until the election is certified6, which will happen no earlier than Tuesday, Dec. 1, 2026.6 CapRadio reported that many first-time voters waited hours at Sacramento State to cast their ballots on Election Day.7 Because the electorate is so much smaller than in 2024, the county's 10-point move mixes two things that county totals cannot separate: who turned out, and who changed sides.

What it means here

The Republican most clearly hurt by the new map was a local one. Democrat Richard Pan beat Kevin Kiley in the 6th Congressional District. CBS News reported that Kiley left the Republican Party in March and ran in a new district after Proposition 50 realigned his old one.8

The county's most closely watched race was between two Democrats. Rep. Doris Matsui faced Sacramento City Councilmember Mai Vang in the 7th District. FOX40 reported that the district stretches from downtown and midtown Sacramento, through Elk Grove, all the way to Placerville and rural San Joaquin County.9 Before the election, The Sacramento Bee found that several Republicans in the district will reluctantly support Matsui to prevent Vang from winning the seat.10 After Friday's update, the Bee reported that Vang had narrowed the gap but that the incumbent is still on track to retain the seat.11 Vang had not conceded. She said turnout among young people, voters of color and Democrats was the highest rate this district has seen in a generation11. That is her campaign's claim, and the county's turnout figures do not yet show it.

The Board of Supervisors race also stayed unsettled. The Bee reported Friday that Flojaune Cofer increased her lead over Sacramento City Councilmember Eric Guerra12 while officials worked through hundreds of thousands of ballots. Republicans have been shut out of the county's major races, and its closest contests are now between Democrats. Every number here will change before certification.
