Ada County, home to Boise, picks independent Achilles over Sen. Jim Risch

Idaho's largest county gave Donald Trump a 10-point win in 2024 and backed independent Todd Achilles by about 3 points this year. Jim Risch still won statewide by 23, and Ada's move was close to Idaho's own.

What Ada County did

Ada County gave Todd Achilles 105,874 votes and Jim Risch 99,414, a 2.9-point margin for the independent with 99% of the expected vote counted. In 2024, Donald Trump carried the county by 10.3 points over Kamala Harris. That is a 13.2-point move toward the Democratic side. The Watch counts Achilles on that side because no Democrat was on the ballot. The Idaho Statesman noted that Ada County has gone for the Republican candidate in every U.S. Senate race dating to at least 1994, the oldest data available on the Secretary of State's website.1 The loss came on Risch's home ground: he lives in Boise, and at 27, he was elected as the Ada County prosecutor.2

The county is the seat of state government and the center of a fast-growing metro area. It has grown from 392,385 in 2010 to 589,500 in 2026, a 50% increase3, according to the regional planning agency COMPASS, as reported by Idaho News 6. Ada cast 226,077 of the state's 710,749 Senate votes, nearly a third.

Ada did not swing harder than the rest of Idaho. Statewide, Risch won by 23.0 points, compared with Trump's 36.5. That is a 13.5-point move, slightly more than Ada's. Ada ranked 20th of Idaho's 44 counties by move, and 43 of the 44 moved left. Neighboring Canyon County moved 14.8 points but still went to Risch by 31.9. Bonneville moved 18.2 points, and Kootenai only 5.8. Ada flipped because it started closest to even. Its path almost exactly matches that of El Paso County, Colorado, another big county that Trump carried narrowly. Ada's move was also close to the national typical move of 14.1 points.

Achilles is a former Democratic state legislator from Boise who left the party and Statehouse seat to run for the U.S. Senate as an independent in 2025.4 The Idaho Capital Sun reported that he outraised any recent U.S. Senate challenger in Idaho, collecting more than $1.8 million4 and publicly promised to not caucus with either party if elected4. About 20,800 Ada votes in the race went to other candidates, including independent Natalie Fleming and Libertarian Matt Loesby.

Why it moved

Ada's 226,077 Senate votes were 85% of its 2024 total, about 41,000 fewer. That is still a large turnout for a midterm. County Clerk Trent Tripple told Idaho News 6 that it's a record turnout for a midterm5. Before the election, he pointed to Idaho's Proposition 1 as a likely driver for heightened voter engagement6. Proposition 1 was the abortion-rights initiative. It lost overall, but won in Ada County7, the Statesman reported.

Republicans read the turnout differently. Ada County Republican chairman Thad Butterworth told the Statesman that early voter data showed "Republican disinterest" in the election8. He said some voters who weren't seeing economic gains decided to sit this one out8. Statewide, GOP chair Dorothy Moon said opposition to the initiative was a "main driver" of Republican voter turnout9.

Local Republicans pointed to the economy. Daniel Reynolds, a GOP precinct committeeman in West Boise, told the Statesman that "it all came down to affordability," even in races that have little control over Idahoans' cost of living.1 Boise State political scientist Charles Hunt called it "a really significant shift that I think shouldn't really be understated"1. No exit poll specific to Ada County turned up in the reporting.

The county count cannot say how much of the 13-point move came from voters switching sides and how much came from who voted. The clerk's Prop 1 explanation and the Republican chairman's "sat out" explanation both describe a smaller midterm electorate that was made up differently from 2024's, not necessarily one that changed its mind.

What it means for Ada's politics

The shift showed up further down the ballot. Democrat Kelceymarie Warner beat incumbent Ada County Commissioner Ryan Davidson by just 400 votes10. She will be the first Democrat on the commission since former Commissioner Kendra Kenyon decided not to run for a second term in 2022.7 Democrat Dotti Owens won the coroner's race. In West Boise's District 15, voters elected Democrat Nancy Gregory to the Idaho Senate and Democrat Jae Etchart to the House, flipping two seats.11 Rep. Mike Simpson and Attorney General Raúl Labrador, two Republican candidates for higher office, also lost Ada to Democratic opponents.10

Republicans still won other countywide offices, including treasurer and assessor. Davidson has pointed to a precedent. He noted that the year resembled 2018, when Democrats grabbed two commission seats7, also a midterm under Trump. Warner was careful about what it meant: "Maybe it's just a step towards purple."7 Risch's own verdict on election night was shorter: "Idaho is still Idaho."1
