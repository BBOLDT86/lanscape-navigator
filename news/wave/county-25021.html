The wave · the biggest counties · Norfolk County, Massachusetts

Norfolk County moved toward Markey, but less than almost anywhere in Massachusetts

Edward Markey carried the suburbs south and west of Boston by 31 points, up from Kamala Harris's 29. Only one Massachusetts county moved less, and nearly a quarter fewer voters turned out, so his raw-vote margin was smaller than hers.

Norfolk County runs from Brookline, on Boston's western edge, through Quincy and the South Shore to Wellesley, Needham and Foxborough. The Enterprise of Brockton counts it as the county's 27 towns and one city1. On Nov. 3 it gave Sen. Edward Markey 188,134 votes to Republican John Deaton's 95,445, a Democratic margin of 31.4 points with all of the expected vote counted. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried it by 28.8. That is a move of 3 points toward the Democrats. It is real, but small by this year's standards.

What Norfolk did

Statewide, Mr. Markey won by 31.2 points with about 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Ms. Harris had won Massachusetts by 25.3, so the state moved 6 points. All 14 Massachusetts counties moved left, and Norfolk ranked 13th. Its neighbors moved further: Plymouth by 4 points, Middlesex by 5, Suffolk by 7, Essex by 9 and Bristol by 11. In 2024 Norfolk was 3.5 points more Democratic than the state. This year it was 0.2 points more.

Big counties elsewhere with similar 2024 margins moved much further. Wayne County, Mich., and Franklin County, Ohio, each moved 14 points, Cuyahoga County, Ohio, 12 and Wake County, N.C., 22. The national typical move was 14. Norfolk's closest company is Westchester County, N.Y., another wealthy suburban county, which moved 5.

Turnout matters here. Norfolk cast 295,500 votes, 77 percent of its 2024 total of 383,258. Mr. Markey's lead came to about 92,700 votes. Ms. Harris's lead was about 110,000. County totals cannot separate voters who changed sides from voters who stayed home or showed up for the first time. A move like this one mixes both. Nearly 12,000 Norfolk ballots went to neither Mr. Markey nor Mr. Deaton. The statewide ballot also included Shiva Ayyadurai of Belmont, Independent2.

The campaign and the electorate

The race was not close in polls. WGBH reported that the national political climate of dissatisfaction with President Trump worked against Deaton3. WBUR described Mr. Deaton as having ran as a non-MAGA Republican4. The Boston Globe reported that Mr. Markey had been downshifting his public campaigning for much of the last two months5. A Suffolk University/Boston Globe poll found that nearly 45 percent of voters surveyed had never heard of Deaton5. Mr. Deaton spent election night in the county. He spoke to reporters outside his post-election party in Weymouth3. Mr. Markey had already beaten Rep. Seth Moulton in a September primary. In Brookline, Brookline.News reported, it was Markey who won over the town6.

The best guide to who moved is a set of New York Times estimates reported by Boston.com. They found that nearly every demographic group swung more blue this cycle, when looking at the U.S. Senate race, with the exception of the 65-and-older group.7 Voters under 30 were the most Democratic group, but these voters made up a lower percentage of the electorate compared to 2024 and 20207. Those estimates cover the whole state. They do not break out Norfolk, and they cannot explain why the county lagged on their own.

Election officials described heavy turnout for a midterm. Secretary of State William Galvin said that high voter turnout and the unusually long, two-page ballot created longer wait times for voters8. Voters faced a record-tying nine statewide ballot questions8.

What it means for the county

The busiest local race was for district attorney. Michael Morrissey did not seek reelection after 16 years in office9. Boston.com reported the open seat drew unusual competition after a recent public spotlight on prosecutions in Norfolk County, most notably the Karen Read trials in 2024 and 20259. The Enterprise reported that Democrat Djuna Perkins of Dedham beat independent Joseph Pagliarulo in a landslide. There was no Republican candidate.1 Ms. Perkins told the paper she would follow through on her campaign pledges to shake up the DA's office1.

Question 7, which would have allowed single-family homes on smaller lots, split the county's Democratic towns. The Globe reported that Brookline was among the places where more than 60 percent voted yes. It said that some wealthier suburbs like Lexington and Wellesley, opposed it10. The Swellesley Report found Wellesley voters across all precincts rejected this idea11. In early returns, Banker & Tradesman reported, supporters scored notable wins in communities like Milton and Needham12. Statewide, the measure failed narrowly.

Norfolk is still solidly Democratic. Mr. Markey won it by more than 30 points, and a Democrat will take over the prosecutor's office. But the county that was clearly bluer than Massachusetts in 2024 voted almost exactly like the state this year. The housing vote suggests that its toughest arguments now often fall within the Democratic majority rather than between the two parties.
