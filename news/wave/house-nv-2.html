Benitez-Thompson takes Nevada's 2nd District, a seat Democrats had never won

With Reno's Washoe County moving eight points toward Democrats and the retiring Republican congressman refusing to back his party's nominee, Teresa Benitez-Thompson beat David Flippo by six points in a district no Democrat had ever won.

Teresa Benitez-Thompson, a social worker and former Assembly majority leader from Reno1, has won Nevada's 2nd Congressional District. AP's count as of Saturday morning had 97.37 percent of the expected vote in. She had 160,867 votes and Republican David Flippo had 141,736, or 50.3 percent to 44.3, a margin of 6.0 points. Lynn Chapman, the Independent American Party candidate,2 drew 17,449.

The Nevada Current noted that no Democrat has ever won the district since it was created after the 1980 Census.3 The Associated Press declared Benitez-Thompson the winner at 9:29 p.m.4 Eastern. AP's own story and the Las Vegas Review-Journal place that call on Wednesday evening5, the day after the vote.

A nominee his predecessor would not back

The seat opened when Republican Rep. Mark Amodei, who had held it since 2011, announced a surprise retirement6 in February. The Las Vegas Sun described Flippo as a retired Air Force lieutenant colonel who has never held elected office. He moved to Northern Nevada after Amodei announced his retirement.6 Earlier in the year he had been running in Nevada's fourth congressional district, hoping to unseat Democratic Rep. Steven Horsford.3 Amodei and Gov. Joe Lombardo had endorsed James Settelmeyer in the Republican primary. But Trump endorsed Flippo, and Flippo won.7

After that, Flippo's campaign was mostly on defense. The Washington Post reported in September that he had falsely claimed multiple times in recent years that he received a Bronze Star "for valor."6 The medal he received in 2004 did not carry the "V" device that denotes valor.6 His campaign blamed "honest mistakes" made by aides8. Nevada Republican Party Chairman Michael McDonald6 defended him. The Nevada Current noted that he had not released required financial disclosure forms9 even though he had self-funded much of his campaign. Amodei called him a fraud, and Amodei's office confirmed10 to KTVN's 2News that he voted for Chapman. The Review-Journal reported that his refusal to endorse Flippo prompted the Washoe County Republican Party to kick Amodei out of the organization.5

AP reported that Benitez-Thompson was critical of the president's handling of the economy and has called for banning stock trading by members of Congress4. She told KOLO that many in her district will choose to go without because healthcare is just too expensive for them.11 One of her ads said Flippo "won't fight to stop dirty data centers"12. His spokesman called her "a socialist who only wants tax breaks for data centers."9

Most of the money went to Flippo. The Nevada Independent reported that the race was the costliest in the district's recorded history in terms of outside spending — almost all supporting Flippo.13 That included a $260,000 media buy14 by the Freedom Caucus Fund and $710,000 in TV ads that Club for Growth Action has reserved.14 On the other side, a clean energy group called Invest in Tomorrow Coalition invested $2 million in flipping the seat15, and the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC) put her on its Red to Blue list. In September, The Cook Political Report and Sabato's Crystal Ball9 still rated the seat "solid Republican."

Reno and the rural north

The district spans the northern half of the state16. It covers Washoe, Churchill, Douglas, Elko, Eureka, Humboldt, Lander, Lincoln, Lyon, Pershing, Storey and White Pine counties, along with Carson City1, the capital, which counts as its own county. Washoe dominates: it cast 205,200 of the roughly 324,600 votes counted for governor across the 13.

The Watch's wave map compares this year's governor's race with the 2024 presidential vote. On that measure the district moved 6.3 points toward Democrats, weighted by votes. Nevada as a whole moved 10.8 points as Aaron Ford beat Lombardo statewide by 7.7. Washoe went from D+1.0 for Kamala Harris to D+8.6 for Ford. Further down the ballot, the Nevada Independent reported that Democrats will control the Washoe County Commission for what appears to be the first time ever17.

Elko County, the northeast's mining and ranching center, moved the most: 11 points. It is still R+46.1, and only 91 percent of its vote is counted. Humboldt moved 8 points, Lander and Lyon 7 each, and Carson City 5. Douglas County, which takes in Minden, Gardnerville and Stateline, went the other way, 3 points toward Republicans. Eureka moved slightly right, and Lincoln barely moved.

These moves do not show who changed their mind. KUNR reported unusually high turnout for a midterm election without any U.S. Senate races18 in Washoe. County totals cannot separate voters who switched sides from changes in who voted.

What the county numbers do show is how far Benitez-Thompson ran ahead of the top of her ticket. By The Watch's arithmetic, Lombardo still carried the 13 counties by about 7 points, while Benitez-Thompson won much the same territory by 6. Chapman's 17,449 votes were fewer than Benitez-Thompson's 19,131-vote lead.

An open seat, and a first

No incumbent lost, because the seat was open. Benitez-Thompson becomes the first woman, the first Latina and the first Democrat to hold the seat2, KTNV noted. The Washington Post reported that her win will mean the state's delegation is entirely Democratic.19 Flippo acknowledged defeat Wednesday night, noting that he had called Benitez-Thompson to congratulate her.5

Ballots are still being counted. Elko and Humboldt are at 91 and 90 percent, and Washoe's registrar said the last day to cure your ballot is the 9th of November20. Nationally, Democrats have won 252 called seats and Republicans 161, with 22 not yet called.
