Boebert loses Colorado's most Republican House seat to a retired admiral

Eileen Laubacher, a former Republican and retired Navy rear admiral, beat Rep. Lauren Boebert by 3,525 votes in the 4th District. The seat is anchored by Douglas County and spans the Eastern Plains, and Democrats last held it in 2011.

Democrat Eileen Laubacher, a first-time candidate, has unseated Rep. Lauren Boebert in Colorado's 4th Congressional District. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Laubacher had 187,745 votes and Boebert had 184,220. That is a lead of 3,525 votes, or 0.9 percentage points, out of 384,707 cast. Douglas Mangeris, the Libertarian nominee, who appears as an independent in AP's count, received 12,742 votes.

The race

Laubacher is a retired Navy rear admiral and former National Security Council official1. Colorado Newsline reported that she was a longtime Republican and switched her party affiliation, first to unaffiliated, and then to Democratic in 2024.1 In the campaign's only debate, she said the Republican Party she used to support was "nearly unrecognizable."1 Axios reported that she leaned on her national security credentials, fiscal conservatism and moderate messaging to woo voters2. She also said she opposed Medicare for All, because of its negative impact on rural hospitals.3

Colorado Public Radio reported that health care and costs were at the center of her campaign. She criticized Boebert for her continued support of Republicans' One Big Beautiful Bill, which includes Medicaid cuts that Laubacher says are expected to hurt rural hospitals. Laubacher also criticized Trump's broad tariff policies and his decision to launch a war against Iran4. At the debate, Boebert argued that rural hospitals are suffering from state cuts to Medicaid, not federal cuts. She also touted her support for a $50 billion fund for rural hospitals5. Her own message emphasized tighter border security, tougher immigration enforcement, more domestic energy production and less federal spending.2

Laubacher had far more money. She raised nearly $14 million — more than 10 times the $1.2 million raised by Boebert.6 The Denver Gazette reported that outside groups spent $1.5 million against Boebert, mostly funding TV and digital ads drawing attention to events surrounding the "Beetlejuice" affair — and benefited from about $600,000 a Republican-aligned group spent promoting her.6 Westword reported that Boebert did little, if any, street-level campaigning during the cycle.7 After the election, Boebert blamed the financial backing Laubacher received for her loss1. She told reporters, "I refuse to spend money like that again"1.

The district

Colorado Politics describes the seat this way: anchored by Douglas County, the 4th CD covers Colorado's Eastern Plains and the Loveland area and is the state's most solidly Republican congressional seat.8 Laubacher said she traveled 40,000 miles across all 21 counties.9 Douglas County holds most of the voters. CPR called it a Republican stronghold in the state, but one that has slowly been trending toward purple.10 In September, the district is 27% active Republican voters to 20% Democrats. The majority are unaffiliated.11

Secretary of State totals showed that Laubacher carried the district's more densely populated portions in Douglas, Larimer and Arapahoe counties, while Boebert led in the district's smaller, rural counties.12 Axios Denver wrote that Douglas County played the pivotal role in Laubacher's win.2

The Watch's data for this seat does not include county-by-county results yet. That means the wave map cannot show how each of the 21 counties moved from 2024, and we have not filled the gap with other outlets' figures. The statewide races show how big the shift was. Sen. John Hickenlooper won by 25.5 points and Phil Weiser won the governor's race by 27.9, in a state Kamala Harris carried by 11.0 in 2024. That is a move of 14.5 and 16.9 points. A move is not the same as persuasion, though. County counts cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from voters who stayed home, and the data has no 2024 turnout baseline for this district to test either explanation. Writing about the national results, the Deseret News reported that in many cases, Republicans and independent voters either stayed home or backed Democrats this year.13

What the result means

AP described Boebert as a three-term Republican and staple of conservative media14. After barely surviving her first reelection campaign, Boebert moved across Colorado to a more Republican district.14 The last Democrat to represent Colorado's 4th District was Betsy Markey, who served one term from 2009 to 2011.15 In the week before the election, national forecasters moved the district into the "leans Republican" category12. Colorado Politics reported that her loss leaves just one Republican in the state's 10-member federal delegation.12

It took more than a week to settle the race. Boebert's campaign said at one point it was reviewing "unusual vote patterns" as ballots continue to be counted.16 AP called the race for Laubacher at 2:53 p.m. Eastern Friday1, Nov. 13, ten days after Election Day. That evening, Boebert took to social media to announce she conceded the race to Laubacher. In a video, Boebert said she waited to concede until every vote was counted.17 Boebert said she would not run in 20281 if the loss held. The count is not final. CBS Colorado noted that the results are not yet certified, Boebert could still request a recount, potentially paying for it out of pocket.17

The seat adds to a large Democratic majority. As of early Saturday, Democrats had won 256 House seats and Republicans 169, with 10 races not yet called.
