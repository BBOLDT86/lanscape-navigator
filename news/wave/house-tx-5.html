Lance Gooden holds Texas' 5th by 2.9 points as Kaufman County swings

Republican Lance Gooden beat Democrat Chelsey Hockett, who has never held office, by 7,423 votes in a district redrawn in 2025. All five of the district's counties moved toward Democrats in the statewide races.

Lance Gooden is the Republican who has served the district since 20191. He won reelection in Tuesday's election by less than 3 points. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Gooden had 130,278 votes to 122,855 for Democrat Chelsey Hockett, or 51.5 percent to 48.5 percent. That is a lead of 7,423 votes out of 253,133 cast.

A long-shot challenger and a statewide fight

NBC DFW reported that Hockett, who has not held public office, defeated Ruth Torres1 in the Democratic primary runoff. Torres had run against Gooden in 20242. Both of this year's nominees live in Terrell, a Kaufman County town. Gooden lives there with his wife and two children3, and Hockett's candidate profile says she belongs to a union family in Terrell4.

The money was lopsided. Federal Election Commission filings compiled by the voter guide VoteSee show that, through mid-October, Gooden had raised about $1.5 million and Hockett about $57,0005. The reporting reviewed for this piece found no sign of major outside spending in the House race itself.

What reached the district instead was the statewide contest. The Dallas Morning News described the general election as a fight over affordability, corruption and competing visions of Texas6. James Talarico, the Democratic Senate nominee, argued that Ken Paxton had enriched himself while ordinary Texans struggled with the rising cost of groceries, housing and health care6. In Kaufman County the argument had a local face. CNN reported that the county is home to two huge data center projects that have drawn local backlash, and that Forney, its largest city, is already moving to ban new data centers7. Democratic mailers warned residents that a data center was near them, adding: "Ken Paxton made it easier to build."8

After the vote, the Houston Chronicle grouped Gooden with incumbents who had looked safe but saw closer results than expected, and he was still awaiting a call Wednesday morning9.

Five counties, unevenly moved

The district is new. The Texas Tribune reported that Republicans redrew the state's congressional map after pressure from President Donald Trump's political operatives10. On December 4, 2025, the U.S. Supreme Court allowed the map to be used in the 2026 elections.11 The Wood County Monitor reported that Gooden's district moved south12 to Kaufman, Van Zandt, Henderson and Anderson counties and an eastern section of Dallas County12. That takes in Mesquite, Terrell, Palestine, Athens, Canton, Kaufman, the southern halves of Garland and Rowlett, and the Lakewood and Lake Highlands parts of Dallas13. The Cook Political Report said the district's baseline partisanship remains reliably Republican14.

The Watch's wave map compares each county's 2026 vote in the Senate and governor's races with the 2024 Harris–Trump result. On that map, all five counties moved toward Democrats, and Kaufman moved the most. Trump carried it by 27.9 points. This year it went Democratic by 1.3 points, a 29-point move. That is bigger than the state's 26-point Senate shift, and Kaufman is the only county in the district that flipped. CNN reported that even after Talarico's broad win, one area stood out to Democratic operatives: Kaufman County8.

Dallas County went from D+22.2 to D+45.6, a 23-point move. But the 5th holds only the county's eastern edge, and most of its 783,308 votes were cast in other districts. So the district's weighted move of 23 points depends heavily on numbers that mostly describe other neighborhoods.

The East Texas counties moved less than the state and stayed heavily Republican:
• Henderson went from R+63.5 to R+46.1, a 17-point move.
• Anderson went from R+61.8 to R+45.5, a 16-point move.
• Van Zandt, still the district's most Republican county, went from R+74.8 to R+60.9, a 14-point move.

None of this shows that voters changed their minds. The county totals (62,251 votes in Kaufman, 31,073 in Henderson, 22,153 in Van Zandt and 15,257 in Anderson) say how many people voted. They do not say whether those were the same people who voted in 2024. Statewide, the Dallas Morning News reported a record midterm turnout15. The Senate race drew about 9.9 million votes.

What the result means

The seat stays Republican, and no incumbent lost here. The Associated Press called the race for Gooden16 at 5:12 p.m. Eastern on Wednesday, Nov. 4. The roughly 1 percent of the expected vote still out is smaller than his lead.

Gooden's margin is far from the statewide results. Talarico won Texas by 12.1 points, and Gina Hinojosa beat Gov. Greg Abbott by 7.1. The county figures here cover whole counties, and the House vote is not broken out by county, so the map cannot show exactly where Gooden ran ahead of his party's statewide candidates.

Gooden had company. NBC DFW reported that Republicans held on to most North Texas congressional seats1. The Houston Chronicle reported that Democrats won four of the five seats Republicans had targeted in redistricting9. Nationally, 252 House seats had been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called.
