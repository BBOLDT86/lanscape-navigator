The wave · the biggest counties · Santa Clara County, California

In Silicon Valley, Becerra gains ground, but less than California did

Santa Clara County gave Xavier Becerra a wider margin than it gave Kamala Harris in 2024. But its move was one of California's smaller ones, and about 18 percent fewer people voted.

What the county did

Santa Clara County includes San Jose and the cities of Silicon Valley. In the Nov. 3 race for governor, it gave Democrat Xavier Becerra 439,799 votes and Republican Steve Hilton 173,230. That is a 43.5-point margin, with 98.46 percent of the expected vote counted. In 2024, Kamala Harris won the county over Donald Trump by 40.0 points, so the county moved about 4 points toward Democrats.

The state moved much more. The AP called the race just after the polls closed in the Golden State at 8 p.m.1 Statewide, Becerra leads by 31.2 points with 96.1 percent of the vote counted. Harris won California by 20.1 points, so the state moved 11.1 points. Santa Clara ranks 41st of California's 58 counties by size of move. Fifty-five counties moved left, and the typical one moved about 6 points. Los Angeles moved about 14 points, Riverside 18 and San Bernardino 21. Santa Clara's Bay Area neighbors moved less: Alameda about 8 points and Contra Costa about 5. Outside California, Middlesex County, Massachusetts, started near Santa Clara's 2024 margin and also moved about 5 points. Cook County, Illinois, and Travis County, Texas, each moved about 19. The typical county nationally moved about 14 points.

Fewer people voted this time: 613,029 ballots, 82 percent of the 750,433 cast in 2024. Becerra won a bigger share than Harris did, but his lead in votes was smaller, about 267,000 against her roughly 300,000. Because turnout changed, the county count cannot tell voters who switched sides apart from voters who stayed home or newly showed up. The 4-point move mixes both. The count is not final: results will be certified by December 11, 2026.2

Why it moved less

The county has about 1.9 million people. Census figures put Asian residents at 44.0 percent and Hispanic or Latino residents at 24.9 percent, against 17.5 and 41.2 percent statewide3. Its median household income of $164,281 compares with $99,122 for California3.

The statewide campaign was mostly about Trump. The Associated Press reported that Becerra repeatedly tied his opponent to the president and promised to defend California against Trump's policies4. San Jose Inside wrote that Hilton couldn't escape the fatal weight of an endorsement by President Donald Trump5.

Hilton also had ties to the area. Since he moved to the United States, Hilton has taught at Stanford University6 and co-founded Crowdpac, a political fundraising website. The San Francisco Standard reported that multiple tech billionaires, including Larsen, Brin, and Palantir cofounder Joe Lonsdale, have also donated to Republican Steve Hilton7. San Jose Mayor Matt Mahan ran for governor with heavy tech backing, but San Jose Inside reported in May that his campaign was fading. After the primary, Mission Local found that tech donors split. Robinhood co-founder Baiju Bhatt gave $39,200 to Steve Hilton on June 1. Tenev gave $250,000 to an outside committee supporting Becerra two days later.8

The ballot's big tax fight did not divide the two candidates. Becerra also opposes Proposition 40. So does his Republican opponent, Steve Hilton.9 Voters statewide rejected the billionaire tax. The morning after the election, the San Francisco Standard found it winning in San Francisco, Alameda and Sonoma counties in the Bay Area10; Santa Clara was not on that list.

The biggest statewide change came from Latino voters. CALO News, citing New York Times exit polling, reported that Latinos shifted 28 points to the left11. NBC News' exit poll showed Asian voters statewide backing Becerra 67%12 to 33%. Santa Clara has a smaller Latino share than the state, which fits with its smaller move. The county count alone cannot show that, though. The reporting reviewed for this piece included no breakdown of which Santa Clara neighborhoods moved. Before the election, the county's registrar of voters was projecting the final turnout for this election will likely be in the range13 of comparable past general elections. The registrar also opened a new public dashboard that provides breakdowns by political party registration, daily ballot return trends and other election statistics14.

What it means for the county's politics

Santa Clara is still one of the most Democratic big counties in the country. The bigger divide there is now inside the Democratic Party. On one side are tech-funded moderates like Mahan. On the other are progressives and organized labor. After Proposition 40 failed, CNN quoted Rep. Ro Khanna, who represents Silicon Valley in the House15, blaming Gov. Gavin Newsom: "The reason we didn't win is because of Newsom."15 Hilton, conceding statewide, said after losing to Becerra that "the change we hoped for may be delayed, but the change is coming."16 In Santa Clara, the numbers show Democrats gaining ground on a smaller electorate, and the county's Democrats arguing among themselves.
