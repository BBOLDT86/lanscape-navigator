Silicon Valley's Santa Clara County moves only slightly left as California swings to Becerra

The home county of San Jose stayed one of California's most Democratic. But its 2.5-point move toward Xavier Becerra was among the smallest in the state, in an election where most of California moved more than four times as far.

What the county did

Santa Clara County is home to San Jose and the Silicon Valley cities at the south end of San Francisco Bay. In the Nov. 3 governor's race it voted for Democrat Xavier Becerra over Republican Steve Hilton by 42.5 points, 367,908 votes to 148,569 in the count so far. In 2024, Kamala Harris beat Donald Trump here by 40 points, so the Democratic margin grew by 2.5 points.

The rest of California moved much further. Becerra leads statewide by 31.5 points, 11.4 points better than Harris, and 54 of the 58 counties moved toward the Democrats. Santa Clara ranks 47th by the size of its move, against a typical county move of about 6 points. Los Angeles County moved 13.5 points. San Bernardino and Riverside counties, which narrowly backed Trump, moved 20.8 and 17 points and now favor Becerra. The Bay Area moved less: Alameda 8.1 points, Contra Costa 4.6. Outside California, among counties that also gave Harris about 40 points, Middlesex County, Massachusetts, moved 4.7 points. Travis County (Austin) moved 19.2, Cook County (Chicago) 18.1 and Milwaukee County 13.5, though those were Senate races with different candidates. The result: Santa Clara is now 11 points more Democratic than the state, down from about 20 points in 2024.

The count is not finished. AP estimates 76.1% of the expected vote is in. The county registrar's latest estimate showed tens of thousands of mail ballots not yet processed, and vote‑by‑mail ballots that are postmarked on or before Election Day and received by the Registrar of Voters' Office no later than seven days after Election Day must be counted.1 So far, the ballots counted equal 69% of the 2024 total. Because the electorate is smaller and different, the 2.5-point move combines two things the county totals can't separate: who turned out and who changed sides.

Why it moved, and why so little

Hilton is in some ways a local candidate. CalMatters reported that he moved in 2012 to Silicon Valley, where his wife was a Google executive, and dabbled in startups before launching a weekly Fox News show in 20172. Trump endorsed Hilton in the primary; Hilton has since tried in the general election to court Democratic voters by saying he wouldn't align with the president on everything.3 Metro Silicon Valley reported that Becerra worked to cast Republican Steve Hilton as a proxy for unpopular midterm president Donald Trump4. Hilton campaigned on $3 gas and to remove the $800 annual state tax on business entities4.

Tech money ran through the race. The San Francisco Standard reported that tech figures spent millions on San Jose Mayor Matt Mahan's primary run for governor, and that multiple tech billionaires, including Larsen, Brin, and Palantir cofounder Joe Lonsdale, have also donated to Republican Steve Hilton5. Mahan officially bowed out after the June primary.6

The polling here is statewide or national. Emerson College's final California poll found the economy topping the list at 45%. Housing affordability came in at 17%, followed by healthcare and immigration, both at 10%.7 Nationally, according to an NBC News exit poll, AI and data centers were "a crucial or a major factor" for 56 percent of voters in their choice for Congress.8 People disagree about what that means. Daniel Cochrane of the conservative Institute for Family Studies told AFP that "concerns about AI's social and economic impacts shifted votes"8. Adam Kovacevich of the tech-funded Chamber of Progress said: "Let's not confuse an anti-Trump wave with an anti-tech mandate"8.

None of the reporting reviewed for this piece explains why Santa Clara moved less than the state, and no published survey breaks out the county's voters. Its population makes it unusual. San José Spotlight, citing 2023 Census data, reported that roughly 41.4% of residents identify as Asian9 and roughly 24.7% of county residents identify as Hispanic or Latino9. KQED reported that there is roughly 140,000 Vietnamese Americans in Santa Clara County. San Jose, by some estimates, has the largest Vietnamese population outside of Vietnam.10 Until precinct results are in, it is unknown which of these communities, if any, account for the small move.

What it means for the county

Becerra won the county by a wide margin. Its politics remain overwhelmingly Democratic, and most of the contests that matter locally are between Democrats or nonpartisan. Voters also decided mayor's races, school parcel taxes and a regional measure that issues a sales tax for 14 years to fund public transportation in Alameda, Contra Costa, San Francisco, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties.4

Before Election Day, the registrar projected that final turnout would be in line with comparable past general elections11. Whether the county's small move holds up, and what it says about the county's politics, depends on the ballots still being counted and on precinct-level results not yet released.
