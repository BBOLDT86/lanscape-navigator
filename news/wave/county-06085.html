The wave · the biggest counties · Santa Clara County, California

In Silicon Valley, the governor's race barely moved off Harris's 2024 margin

Xavier Becerra carried Santa Clara County by 41 points with about 58% of the expected vote counted. That is almost the same margin Kamala Harris won in 2024, while California as a whole moved about 10 points toward the Democrats.

Santa Clara County, which takes in San Jose and most of Silicon Valley, gave Democrat Xavier Becerra a lopsided win over Republican Steve Hilton in the Nov. 3 governor's race. With 58% of the expected vote counted, Becerra had 277,503 votes to Hilton's 115,879, a margin of 41.1 points. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 40.0 points. The county moved about 1 point toward the Democrats.

That is a small move in a state that moved a lot. Statewide, with 71% of the expected vote counted, Becerra led by 30.4 points, compared with Harris's 20.1. Fifty-four of California's 58 counties moved left, and Santa Clara ranked 50th. Los Angeles County moved 12 points. Riverside and San Bernardino moved 17 and 19 and flipped from Trump to Becerra. Alameda County, next door, moved 8. Orange and San Diego each moved 5.

Santa Clara also moved the least among the large counties around the country that Harris won by about 40 points. Contra Costa County in California and Middlesex County in Massachusetts each moved about 5 points. Milwaukee moved 14, Cook County 18 and Travis County, Texas, 19. Nationally, 2,673 of the 2,775 counties counted so far moved left, and the typical move was 14 points.

It is an unusual place. The Census Bureau counts about 1.9 million residents; 44.0% are Asian, 24.9% Hispanic and 25.6% non-Hispanic white, and the median household income is $164,2811.

A partial count, and a different electorate

The count is far from finished. So far the county has counted votes equal to 52% of its 2024 total. Ballots were mailed to every registered voter2, and the county registrar notes that ballots postmarked by Election Day can be accepted for up to seven days after polls close3. A registrar spokesman told NBC Bay Area that counting would pick up speed Tuesday and continue for days4. The margin may change as those ballots come in.

Because the votes counted so far add up to only about half of 2024's electorate, county totals cannot separate voters who switched parties from changes in who voted. A 1-point move could mean a few people changed sides, a different mix of people returned their ballots early, or both.

What the campaign looked like from the valley

The Associated Press said Trump, the state's high cost of living and political experience5 were the main issues. Becerra repeatedly tied Hilton to the president6, while Hilton blamed one-party rule for the state's high cost of living and homelessness crisis6. Republican consultant Mike Madrid told Axios the race was "a referendum on Donald Trump"7.

In Santa Clara, Hilton was a local figure of sorts. CalMatters reported that he moved to Silicon Valley permanently in 2012 and ran a political tech startup8. His donors included Google founder Sergey Brin and Ripple executive Chris Larsen9. On artificial intelligence, he wants to reduce regulations to keep the AI industry growing in California10. No reporting found for this piece shows whether that pitch won him votes in the county.

The polls offer only statewide clues. The late-October UC Berkeley poll co-sponsored by the Los Angeles Times found that Becerra received a warmer reception from Black and Asian American voters11. It gave no breakdown for Santa Clara.

The same ballot also showed the county's own leanings. Fortune reported that Silicon Valley voters rejected the billionaire wealth tax12, Proposition 40, while Los Angeles and San Francisco voters supported it. Citing the Palo Alto Weekly, San José Spotlight found that at least a dozen billionaires from Silicon Valley spent millions of their own money13 against the tax. According to CalMatters, the measure revealed divisions in the Democratic Party14.

What it means

Democrats still dominate the county. Becerra's 41-point margin is larger than his statewide lead. But Santa Clara did not join the wider swing that helped him elsewhere. His gains came mostly in Los Angeles and the Inland Empire, and Santa Clara's margin stayed close to where Harris left it.

The county's political class has also been testing a different kind of Democratic campaign. Before the primary, San Jose Mayor Matt Mahan ran for governor with money from wealthy Silicon Valley executives and investors15. Becerra and Hilton finished as the top two in June. The county's final numbers will not be known until counting ends, and the state says results will be certified by December 11, 202616.
