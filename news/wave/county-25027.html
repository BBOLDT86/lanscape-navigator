Worcester County shifts six points left, about the same as Massachusetts

Ed Markey won Massachusetts' second-biggest county by 16 points. Kamala Harris had won it by 10. About a quarter fewer people voted than in 2024, and a Republican from a Worcester County town couldn't get out from under President Trump.

Worcester County covers the middle of Massachusetts. It runs from the city of Worcester through mill cities like Fitchburg, Leominster and Gardner to small towns near the New Hampshire line. It is still one of the least Democratic of the state's big counties. On Nov. 3, Sen. Edward Markey carried it over Republican John Deaton by 174,846 votes to 124,048, a margin of 16.2 points. Kamala Harris won it by 10.0 points in 2024. All of the expected vote has been counted.

What the county did

The county moved 6 points toward Democrats, almost exactly as much as the state. Markey won Massachusetts by 31.2 points, while Harris won it by 25.3. All 14 counties moved left, and Worcester ranked seventh, right in the middle. Bristol County moved 11 points and Essex 9. Norfolk moved 3 and Middlesex 5. Worcester County still voted 15 points to the right of the state as a whole.

Compared with big counties elsewhere that voted about the same way in 2024, Worcester's move was small. Bexar County, Texas, moved 26 points and DuPage County, Illinois, moved 16. Oakland County, Michigan, moved 4 and Erie County, New York, moved 1. Across the country, the typical county moved 14 points left.

Fewer people voted. The county cast 312,766 votes, 75 percent of its 2024 total and about 106,000 fewer ballots. County totals can't show how much of the move came from people switching parties and how much came from who turned out, such as Trump voters who skipped a midterm with no presidential race. The 6-point move mixes both.

Why it moved

The campaign centered on Trump. At the race's only debate, Markey repeatedly tried to tie Deaton to President Donald Trump's agenda, arguing that a Democratic Senate majority would be necessary to provide a check on the president.1 Deaton is a Bolton Marine veteran and attorney2, which makes him a Worcester County resident himself. He told WBUR that he's not part of MAGA, has never voted for Trump, and were he to win, he would not do Trump's bidding.3 GBH reported that the national political climate of dissatisfaction with President Trump worked against Deaton4, and that a relatively recent transplant to Massachusetts4 may have had trouble getting known.

Polls before the election pointed the same way. A UMass Lowell survey found only 25% of Massachusetts likely voters approve of the job Trump is doing as president5. In the Suffolk University/Boston Globe poll, sixty percent of voters indicated they had negative views of Republican candidates connected to or supportive of the president.6 Prices were also on voters' minds. The UMass Amherst/WCVB poll found that 4 in 10 (42%) identifying the cost of living as the most important factor in their vote for governor7. Those voters were more likely to blame Trump than Healey.8

In the city of Worcester, the Telegram & Gazette reported that there weren't many surprises in Worcester this midterm season, but the city did see a slight uptick in voter participation.9 Nine statewide ballot questions may also have brought voters in. Secretary of State William Galvin said this year's record nine ballot questions show a desire for change10, and he pointed to a "dramatic increase" in early in-person voting.11

The governor's race moved much less. Statewide, Gov. Maura Healey beat Michael Minogue by 27.2 points, only 1.9 points more than Harris's margin. Boston.com reported that Minogue flipped 11 municipalities that Healey won in 202212, including Westminster and Athol in Worcester County, though he didn't take home more than 52 percent of the vote in any of those flipped municipalities.12

What it means for Central Massachusetts

Results further down the ballot were split. The Telegram & Gazette reported that Democrats flipped the 1st Worcester House District for the first time in 20 years, while Republican incumbents retained the Worcester/Hampshire Senate seat13. That House district covers Holden, Rutland, Paxton, Princeton and Westminster.14 Krista Ferrante of Princeton won the seat after Republican Rep. Kimberly Ferguson retired. State Sen. Peter Durant held his seat across the district even though he lost in Worcester to Democratic challenger Shannon M. Teabo9. A local roundup of Greater Gardner found the Senate race in Gardner could hardly have been closer.15 Rep. Paul Frost, a Republican who has represented the district since 199713, held the 7th Worcester.

So the county's Republican vote held up in many small towns, but it shrank in a year when both statewide Republicans ran as distant from Trump as they could. Neither Senate candidate is likely to be on the ballot again. Markey has said this would be his final term in the Senate16. After losing, Deaton indicated he most likely would not run for Senate again and suggested his campaign could mark the end of his political career.17
