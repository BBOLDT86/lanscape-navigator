Bergen, New Jersey's biggest county, swings 21 points toward Cory Booker

Two years after Kamala Harris carried Bergen County by 3.4 points, Cory Booker won it by 24.6 with fewer voters turning out. The county's move almost exactly matched New Jersey's swing toward Democrats.

Bergen County is New Jersey's most populous county, a wealthy suburb across the Hudson River from Manhattan. It has about 978,000 people spread across 70 municipalities1, 2. 31.8 percent of residents were born abroad, compared with 23.9 percent statewide, and the median household income is $124,884, against $103,556 for the state.3 In 2024, Kamala Harris carried it by just 3.4 points out of 459,077 votes. On Nov. 3, Sen. Cory Booker, the Democrat, beat Republican Justin Murphy here by 24.6 points, 190,856 to 114,027, with about 97 percent of the expected vote counted.

What Bergen did

That is a 21-point move toward the Democrats. Statewide, Mr. Booker won by 27.3 points, 1,819,769 to 1,023,549, which is a 21-point move from Ms. Harris's 5.9-point margin. Bergen moved with the state, not ahead of it, and stayed a few points more Republican than New Jersey as a whole, as it was in 2024. All 21 New Jersey counties moved left. Bergen ranked seventh, above the typical county's 18 points. Middlesex moved 28 points. Ocean went from R+36.0 to R+8.6. Morris and Monmouth flipped from Republican to Democratic, and Essex moved 18.

Outside New Jersey, the contrast is clear. Orange County, Calif., voted about the way Bergen did in 2024 but moved only 5 points this time. Clark County, Nev., moved 13. Fort Bend and Harris counties in Texas moved 27 each. Across the country the typical county moved 14 points.

The electorate was also much smaller. Bergen cast 312,990 votes, 68 percent of its 2024 total. That means the county count cannot tell voters who changed sides apart from 2024 voters who simply stayed home. Some ballots are still being counted. The Pascack Press reported that mailed ballots must be postmarked by 8 p.m. Nov. 3 and reach the county Board of Elections by 8 p.m. Monday, Nov. 91, and that the county expects to certify results Nov. 18.1

Why voters moved, by the local account

The 2024 result was a sharp break. Daily Voice counted 20 Bergen County municipalities4 that went from Biden to Trump, from Garfield and Lodi in the south to Mahwah and Ramsey in the north. This summer, New Jersey Globe described Bergen as a county Republicans could still contest5. It also noted that Booker grew up in Harrington Park5 and played football at Northern Valley Regional High School.

Mr. Murphy was not a strong challenger on paper. The Associated Press described him as a little-known attorney who emerged from a crowded field in his party's June primary.6 Mr. Booker campaigned on the cost of living by pitching the Keep Your Pay Act7, Fox 29 reported. NJ Spotlight News called affordability the race's defining issue. It cited a Fairleigh Dickinson poll in which 90% of voters surveyed said affordability was important to their vote8.

The statewide exit poll on NBC News's results page points the same way. About half of voters called the cost of living crucial, and Mr. Booker won 71%9 of that group. Nationally, the AP Voter Poll found that voters nationwide pinned the blame on Trump for his tariffs, his war in Iran and his unfulfilled campaign promise10 to lower prices. These polls are statewide or national. The local reporting reviewed for this piece did not break down Bergen's movement by group. Rep. Nellie Pou, whose district covers Bergen's southwestern towns, attributed Trump's success in the district in 2024 to frustration with household costs and a desire for change.11

What it means in Bergen

The Democratic gains reached down the ballot. County Executive James Tedesco won a fourth term, which makes him the longest-serving county executive in Bergen County history.12 Democrats Joan M. Voss and Rafael Marte12 kept their commissioner seats. Daily Voice reported that unofficial results show Democrats winning council seats previously held by Republicans in Hillsdale, Westwood, Elmwood Park, Hasbrouck Heights, Midland Park, Norwood, Oradell, Ramsey, Ridgefield, Waldwick, and Wallington.13 Five of those towns (Elmwood Park, Hasbrouck Heights, Midland Park, Ramsey and Ridgefield) were on the list of places that voted for Donald Trump two years ago.

Rep. Josh Gottheimer, a Democrat, won a sixth term in the Bergen-centered Fifth District. In his victory speech, he said he will focus on "common sense" and affordability.14

The result shows that Bergen's 2024 slide could be reversed in a midterm. It does not show whether the voters who drove that slide changed their minds or stayed home. With roughly two-thirds of the 2024 electorate voting, the count can't answer that.
