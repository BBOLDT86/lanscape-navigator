Akron's Summit County more than triples the Democratic margin for Brown

Sherrod Brown carried the Akron area by nearly 23 points, compared with Kamala Harris's 7, in a midterm electorate about four-fifths the size of 2024's. His campaign leaned on FirstEnergy, data centers and the cost of living.

What Summit County did

Summit County is the Akron area: about 538,000 residents in the Census Bureau's 2025 estimate1, a population that has dipped 0.4 percent since 20202. It is anchored by a city that was once a center of the tire industry, where Goodyear Tire and Rubber is still based3. The county has leaned Democratic for a long time, but by less in recent years. Kamala Harris carried it by 7.0 points in 2024.

On Nov. 3, Sherrod Brown, the Democrat trying to win back the Senate seat he lost two years earlier, beat Sen. Jon Husted here 132,890 votes to 82,026. That is a 22.6-point margin and a 16-point move toward the Democrats. About 98 percent of the expected vote is counted, so the totals are not final.

Statewide, Brown won by 4.9 points in a state Donald Trump carried by 11.2, a 16.1-point move. Summit's move was about the same size and ranks 56th of Ohio's 88 counties. All 88 moved left. Summit moved more than the state's urban giants: Franklin moved 14 points, Cuyahoga 12, Lucas 13 and Hamilton 10. Of Ohio's big counties, only suburban Butler moved slightly more. Outside Ohio, counties that started close to Summit's 2024 margin went different ways. Kent County, Michigan, moved 8 points and Erie County, New York, 1. Harris and Bexar counties in Texas each moved more than 25.

Who voted, and why

The county count can't separate voters who changed their minds from changes in who turned out. Both happened here. In the Senate race, 225,274 votes were cast, 82 percent of the 2024 presidential total and roughly 48,000 fewer. Signal Akron reported that this was still the highest turnout of any midterm election in Summit County in the last two decades4. Harris's edge in 2024 was about 19,000 votes. Brown's is about 50,900, built from a smaller electorate.

The local story behind the race was close to home. Brown attacked Husted over a scandal in which an Akron-based FirstEnergy paid $61 million in bribes to get a $1.3 billion ratepayer bailout5, the Ohio Capital Journal reported. In March, Husted testified via Zoom6 in the trial of two former FirstEnergy executives, held in a Summit County courtroom in Akron. Husted has never been accused of any crime associated with the $60 million bribery scandal7, the AP noted, but the footage was used to blanket the airwaves. Brown also called Husted "the face of data centers" in Ohio8, NPR reported. Statewide, the Columbus Dispatch wrote that Brown capitalized on President Donald Trump's poor approval rating and voter discontent with the cost of living9. Husted, for his part, argued that Brown was out of date and too liberal for Ohio voters10.

AP VoteCast found that Brown substantially improved on his 2024 performance among young voters10, with smaller gains among moderates, lower-income voters and women. Those figures are statewide, not for Summit alone. Analysts disagree about rural Ohio. The AP said tariffs and fuel prices gave Brown a fresh opening with rural and working-class voters11. On election night, Wright State political scientist Lee Hannah told the Ohio Capital Journal that Brown did not appear to be doing better there.

Inside Akron, Signal Akron's precinct analysis found that Brown won all 120 of Akron's precincts over Husted12. That includes the precincts Trump carried in 2024, which are clustered in Kenmore and Summit Lake12, in Ellet and Goodyear Heights, and in the Merriman Valley. The same analysis found that 104 of Akron's 120 precincts lost registered voters in the last two years12. At an East Akron polling site, a volunteer said that many of them were first-time voters13. Another voter there told the outlet he rarely votes but is fed up with the Trump administration and wants to see change13.

What it means in Summit

Democrats did well up and down the ballot. U.S. Rep. Emilia Sykes won reelection in a 13th District that, after redistricting in 2025, now covers all of Summit County14 and parts of Portage and Stark counties. Democrats won three Summit County Council seats15, the three at-large seats on the ballot. In Akron, voters approved housing Issues 11 and 12, police oversight Issue 134. The Akron Beacon Journal's editorial board, which had endorsed the winners, called the result a broad rejection of Donald Trump's harmful, self-serving agenda16. That is the board's opinion, not a measurement.

Because this was a special election for the rest of JD Vance's term, Brown will have to run for reelection in 2028 to keep the seat8. Summit County's margin will be part of the baseline for that race. The county's official count is still to come.
