Mark Lamb holds Arizona's 5th District as the East Valley swings left

The former Pinal County sheriff, dogged by sexting allegations and questions about a Tennessee ranch, beat nurse Elizabeth Lee by 3.4 points in a Republican stronghold where both counties swung about 19 to 20 points toward Democrats for governor.

Mark Lamb, the former Pinal County sheriff who bills himself as "America's Sheriff,"1 kept Arizona's 5th Congressional District Republican, but by much less than the seat usually gives the party. With 98.6% of the expected vote counted, The Associated Press has Lamb at 166,930 votes and Democrat Elizabeth Lee at 155,814. That is a 3.4-point margin, about 11,100 votes. KTAR reported that Lee took a surprising lead after the first results came out on election night2. The race then stayed too close to call for nearly three days3, until the AP called it Friday2.

The race

Nobody was defending the seat. Rep. Andy Biggs gave it up to run for governor.4 Lamb, endorsed by President Donald Trump, beat businessman Daniel Keenan in the Republican primary.4 KJZZ reported that the bad press didn't sink him there, thanks to name recognition from his years as sheriff and, more importantly, Trump's endorsement5.

Much of that press came from The Arizona Republic. It reported women's allegations that Lamb, running as a traditional-values conservative, used threats and intimidation to hide a history of sexting, sharing nude photos and inviting intimate encounters outside his marriage6. Lamb belongs to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and he has dismissed the reporting as politically motivated7. In June, Pinal County Attorney Brad Miller announced that a preliminary inquiry had found no abuse of power or criminal violations8. Then came Tennessee. His family opened a ranch on 140 acres it bought for $3 million in rural McNairy County, roughly 1,500 miles from the district.9 Lamb says he still lives in Arizona and only plans to visit the ranch.9

Lee is a nurse who specializes in reproductive health care, and this was her first run for Congress3. She left the Republican Party in 2018 and ran as a progressive2. The Queen Creek Independent reported that she focused on what health care costs, reproductive rights, public schools, housing costs and water10, while Lamb campaigned on border security, immigration enforcement and support for police10. According to KTAR, Lamb skipped the only general-election debate and avoided interviews with reporters, though he went on partisan podcasts2. He had far more money: about $1.8 million raised, nearly five times Lee's $376,0002. Outside groups spent under $11,000 on the race.2 The Arizona Mirror noted that Lamb gave his Trump connection less space on his campaign website as the general election approached11.

In October, NPR reported that the allegations were turning many voters away from Lamb12. It talked to Latter-day Saint women in the district, among them an independent voter who did not plan to vote for him12.

The district

The 5th includes parts of Gilbert, Queen Creek, Chandler, Mesa, San Tan Valley and Apache Junction13, the southeastern suburbs of metro Phoenix. The Deseret News pointed out that Maricopa County has the largest concentration of Latter-day Saints outside Utah14. A PAC run by Latter-day Saints that worked for Gov. Katie Hobbs and her running mate, former Mesa mayor John Giles, also endorsed Lee.15

Both of the district's counties moved sharply on The Watch's wave map. Maricopa voted for Trump by 3.5 points in 2024 and for Hobbs by 16.2 points this year, a 20-point move. Pinal went from R+22.1 for Trump to R+2.9 for Biggs, a 19-point move. Weighted for the district, the move comes to +19.7. That is a little more than the state's +18.7, where Hobbs won by 13.1 points. These numbers come from the governor's race and cover whole counties; the district contains only part of each.

The Deseret News reported that a majority of the East Valley voted for Democrats this year for the first time in political memory16, and that Biggs lost even his hometown of Gilbert16. County totals can't tell us how much of that came from people switching sides and how much from who voted and who didn't. The 1,526,333 governor votes in Maricopa and 158,896 in Pinal come from a midterm electorate, and they are being compared with a presidential-year one.

What the result means

Biggs held the seat for five terms.17 KJZZ called it a deep-red East Valley district that has long been safe for Republicans3. No incumbent lost here, and the seat stays with the same party. Elsewhere in Arizona, Democrats flipped two other U.S. House seats and took both chambers of the Legislature for the first time in 60 years16. Across the country, the AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still open.

As of Saturday morning, about 1.4% of the district's expected vote had not been counted. On Friday, the Arizona Mirror judged that a change in the lead was unlikely18.
