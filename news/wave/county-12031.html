The wave · the biggest counties · Duval County, Florida

In Angie Nixon's Jacksonville, Duval swings 12 points toward Democrats

Duval County, which Donald Trump carried narrowly in 2024, gave its hometown Senate candidate an 11-point win. Its 12-point move outpaced Florida's, on an electorate a quarter smaller than two years ago.

Angie Nixon lost her race for the U.S. Senate on Nov. 3, but she won at home. In Duval County, Nixon, a Democrat, beat Republican Sen. Ashley Moody 195,811 votes to 157,167. That is a margin of 10.8 points, with 98.33 percent of the expected vote counted. Two years earlier, Donald Trump carried the county over Kamala Harris by 1.5 points, so the county moved 12 points toward the Democrats. Nixon has represented Jacksonville in the Florida House since 2020, and the Senate race was her first attempt at statewide office.1

Duval and Jacksonville are one consolidated city-county2. Census estimates put its population at 1,062,9633 in 2025. About 30 percent of residents are Black, 14.5 percent are Hispanic, and roughly 80,000 are veterans. Before the election, Florida Politics called Duval another swing area. Trump narrowly carried it in 2024 and 2016, while Biden carried it narrowly in 2020.4

What Duval did

Moody won statewide by 2.9 points in a state Trump carried by 13.1, a move of about 10 points. Duval moved a little more than that and ranked 10th of Florida's 67 counties. Sixty-six of the 67 moved left. The typical county moved 7 points, or 10 when weighted by votes. Among Florida's biggest counties, Duval's move matched Hillsborough's, which went from R+3.1 to D+9.0. It was larger than the moves in Palm Beach (about 10 points) and Pinellas (6). It was smaller than the moves in Miami-Dade (19), Broward and Orange (15 each). Nationally, the typical county moved 14 points, and comparable swing counties in the West moved further: Riverside 17, San Bernardino 19 and Maricopa 20. By Florida standards Duval's move was large. By national standards it was ordinary.

In the governor's race, Alachua, Duval, Leon and Gadsden Counties also voted for the Democratic candidate5, David Jolly. Byron Donalds, the Republican, still won statewide by 1.3 points.

Why it moved

Turnout tells part of the story. Duval cast 356,347 votes in the Senate race, 76 percent of its 2024 presidential total. Even so, the county's elections supervisor, Jerry Holland, called the turnout a midterm election record for Duval County6 during early voting. By the day before the election, Action News Jax reported, Democrats had cast nearly 27,000 more votes than Republicans in Duval County.7 The county Democratic chair, Millie Leeds, noted it's the largest lead local Democrats have ever seen heading into Election Day. She said she believes having Jacksonville's Angie Nixon at the top of the Democratic ticket helped boost local enthusiasm.7 That edge came in a county where registration is close to even. In April, the elections office showed 240,404 registered Democrats to 235,337 Republicans.8

That is why the 12-point move should not be read as 12 points of persuasion. A county's move mixes who turned out with who changed sides. The electorate was a quarter smaller than in 2024, and Democrats outvoted Republicans in early ballots. Some of the shift could come from which voters stayed home rather than from voters changing their minds. The county count cannot separate the two, and no Duval-specific voter poll has been published.

As for issues, a Democratic City Council member, Reggie Gaffney Jr., told Jacksonville Today that local voters "are sick and tired of the gas prices, the rent costs, food."1 News4Jax described the race as a contrast between Moody's emphasis on economic policies, law enforcement and her record in office, and Nixon's focus on the cost of living, health care and support for families9. The campaigns also fought over Nixon's ties to the Democratic Socialists of America and a grand jury report involving the state's Hope Florida program.9 Statewide, a Stetson University poll in October found that cost of living and inflation remain the leading concern, with 40% of likely voters naming them10. Nationally, according to the Associated Press, about 9 in 10 Black voters supported the Democratic candidate in their local U.S. House race this year, up from about 8 in 10 who supported Harris in 2024.11 Those figures are national. They do not measure Duval's voters.

What it means for Jacksonville

Gaffney called the result "the second time in the last three to four months that Duval has went blue."1 In August, Democrat Moné Holder flipped a Jacksonville City Council seat held by the Duval County GOP for 11 years.12 A local analyst who spoke to Jacksonville Today then said he sees "Duval County becoming purple, and in some cases, maybe leaning a little blue."12

The picture further down the ballot was more mixed. The county's three school board races were each decided by slim margins13, and Jacksonville Today reported that the board's political makeup is unlikely to change.13 Before Election Day, Jacksonville Today also reported that Duval County Republicans have closed their registration gap with Democrats by more than 15,000 voters8 since 2023.

The next test is the city election. The mayoral election will take place March 9, 2027, along with races for City Council, sheriff, property appraiser and tax collector.14 Democratic Mayor Donna Deegan is seeking a second term. Wyman Duggan, a Republican state legislator, enters the race as the presumptive GOP front-runner14. Nixon, meanwhile, hinted at another run in 202815. It is not yet known whether the turnout she drew will show up in a city election without her name on the ballot.
