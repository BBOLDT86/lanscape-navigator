Austin Rogers keeps Florida's 2nd Republican as every county moves toward Democrats

Republican Austin Rogers won the open North Florida seat by 4.2 points over Democrat Amanda Marie Green. Tallahassee's Leon County and its neighbors swung hard toward Democrats in the statewide races, but not far enough to cost Republicans the seat.

Republican Austin Rogers will succeed Neal Dunn in Florida's 2nd Congressional District. He beat Democrat Amanda Marie Green 161,148 votes to 148,215, a margin of 4.2 points on 309,363 votes, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race at 8:53 p.m. Eastern on election night. Rogers held the seat in a year when Democrats have been called the winners of 252 House seats nationwide and when every county in the district moved toward Democrats in the statewide races.

The race

The seat opened when Dunn, a urologist and former Army surgeon1, announced that he would not seek a sixth term1. The Tallahassee Democrat described Rogers as a 35-year-old Bay County attorney and former counsel to2 Sen. Rick Scott. He emerged from a crowded Republican field in the August primary, capturing the nomination over the likes of businessman Keith Gross and Florida GOP Chair Evan Power3. Florida Politics reported that he overcame a spending disadvantage to win the Republican nomination4 and that a super PAC called Panhandle Patriots has supported Rogers with more than $1.7 million in spending across the course of the campaign5.

Green had worked in foreign aid6, and she beat out three other candidates to earn the party's nod7. The Tallahassee Democrat reported that her campaign focused on affordability, healthcare access, agriculture and conservation8. It also tried to tie Tallahassee's Democratic precincts to the Panhandle's small towns. Blue Dog PAC, focused on electing moderate Democrats, helped Green5, but Rogers had more money: through Oct. 14, Rogers raised $1.36 million for the race while Green raised more than $658,0005.

The two candidates shared a stage only once, at the only forum the two will have ahead of the election, and healthcare was one of the main topics9. WCTV reported that Green said she opposes cuts to Medicare9 and that rural residents travel too far for good care. Rogers called for expanding health savings accounts and association health plans9. On immigration, Rogers said Congress must "seal the border" before considering broader immigration reforms2, and Green called that approach extremist2. Florida Politics wrote that the makeup of the district gave Rogers an edge, with about 238,000 Republicans, 169,000 Democrats and 99,000 other voters5.

The district

The 2nd is the largest congressional district in Florida by land area and consists of all of Bay, Calhoun, Franklin, Gadsden, Gulf, Holmes, Jackson, Jefferson, Leon, Liberty, Madison, Taylor, Wakulla, and Washington counties, as well as portions of Walton and Lafayette.10 In Walton, only the east side of the county11 is in the district. The redraw this spring left Districts 1-7 unchanged12.

On The Watch's wave map, the district's counties moved a weighted 7.6 points toward Democrats between the 2024 presidential race and this year's Senate and governor's races. None of them changed sides. The biggest moves were around the capital. Leon County (Tallahassee) went from D+21.8 to D+31.4, a shift of nearly 10 points. Next-door Wakulla moved 10 points, from R+44.3 to R+34.4, and Gadsden, already Democratic, moved 7 points to D+37.6. Bay County, the Republican base around Panama City, moved nearly 8 points but stayed at R+39.8 on 70,329 votes. Elsewhere the shift was smaller: about 5 points in Holmes and Walton, under 4 in Lafayette and in Jefferson, Leon's eastern neighbor, and less than one point in Gulf. The Walton and Lafayette figures cover the whole counties, not just the parts inside the district.

Those are changes in margins, not proof that voters changed their minds. Leon alone cast 125,659 votes in the statewide races, about two-fifths of the 309,363 cast in the House race, so who turned out there matters a great deal to the district total. The county counts cannot separate new or returning voters from voters who switched sides. On election night Green's campaign pointed to energized turnout in Democratic-leaning Leon County8. The Tallahassee Democrat wrote that the race's outcome could hinge on whether Democratic turnout in Leon County is large enough to offset Republican strength in Bay County and the district's rural counties8.

What it means

The Tallahassee Democrat noted that the GOP has held the seat for 14 of the last 16 years13. The exception was Democrat Gwen Graham, who defeated incumbent Republican Steve Southerland in 2014 and served one 2-year term13. Dunn then comfortably won reelection14 four times. No incumbent was on the ballot this year, and Rogers' 12,933-vote margin is narrow by the standard of Dunn's decade. The race came in a state where Republicans Ashley Moody and Byron Donalds won the Senate and governor's races by 2.9 and 1.3 points, after Donald Trump carried Florida by 13.1 points in 2024.

The count is nearly finished. Calhoun County, at about 94 percent of its expected vote, is the furthest behind, and the results are unofficial15. Green acknowledged the loss on her own social media, saying she was proud of a campaign that refused money from corporations and billionaires5. At his party in Panama City, Rogers asked to sit down with her. He also says he will spend his first 100 days in office focusing on ways to limit spending to help lower the cost of living16.
