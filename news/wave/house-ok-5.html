Bice holds Oklahoma's 5th by 3,364 votes as Oklahoma County swings

Republican Stephanie Bice beat Democrat Jena Nelson by 1.3 points in a central Oklahoma district redrawn in 2021 to add rural territory. Oklahoma County swung hard toward Democrats, and the added rural counties gave Bice the margin.

Rep. Stephanie Bice, the Republican who has held Oklahoma's 5th Congressional District since 2021, won another term by 3,364 votes. She beat Democrat Jena Nelson 130,972 to 127,608, a margin of 1.3 points on 264,647 votes, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press has called the race for Bice. Two independents, Robert Henri and Austin Nieves, drew 4,024 and 2,043 votes. Together that is nearly twice the gap between Bice and Nelson.

Nationally, 252 House seats have been called for Democrats, past the 218 needed for a majority, and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called. NonDoc noted that while Democrats gained control of Congress amid national backlash over the war with Iran, affordability issues and data centers, they failed to flip any federal seats in Oklahoma1.

The race

Bice is a former state senator who won CD 5 in 2020 by defeating one-term Democratic Rep. Kendra Horn2. Nelson previously served as acting executive director and director of programs for the Oklahoma Faith Network and has a background in education3.

Nelson told KGOU that her biggest priority was lowering the cost of living.4 A spokesperson for Bice's campaign said she was focused on "cleaning up Congress" and on policies like paid family leave.4 NonDoc set the race against central Oklahoma gasoline near $4 a gallon as prominent petroleum companies move their headquarters to Texas5. The Iran war also came up in the race. Leaked audio captured Bice expressing concern about the president's Iran war strategy and relaying a business leader's remark about "Trump fatigue."6

Bice declined proposals for a debate5. Her stated reason was that Nelson had missed a federal financial disclosure deadline.5 The ads followed national lines. One Bice ad called Nelson "extremely liberal" and said she supported higher taxes on Social Security benefits, tips and overtime.5 A PAC supporting Nelson attacked Bice over the Epstein files, cuts to Oklahoma hospitals, tax breaks for data centers and the war in Iran.5 Bice disputed the Epstein charge, saying she had voted yes.5

Federal Election Commission filings cited by KGOU showed Bice had spent $1.8 million and Nelson nearly $700,000.4 National Democrats tried to help Nelson late with an influx of cash and staff1, NonDoc reported.

The district

The district covers all of Lincoln, Pottawatomie and Seminole counties and parts of Oklahoma, Canadian and Logan counties.4 In 2021, lawmakers removed the southwestern part of Oklahoma County and added rural parts of Canadian, Logan and Lincoln counties. Republican officials denied the map was partisan.7

On The Watch's wave map, every county in the district moved toward Democrats in this year's statewide vote compared with the 2024 Harris–Trump margin. The weighted shift was nearly 16 points. Oklahoma County went from Trump +1.7 to Democrats +13.6, a 15-point move, on 228,408 votes. Canadian County moved the most, 21 points, from R+40.4 to R+19.9. Pottawatomie moved 14 points, Seminole 13, and Logan and Lincoln 12 each. Those four counties still voted Republican by 34 to 52 points. The figures cover whole counties, including parts outside the district.

Further down the ballot, Democrats swept all three Oklahoma County office races, and voters rejected a jail sales tax8. News 9 reported that the Board of Commissioners is under Democratic control for the first time since 19789.

These county moves don't show that voters changed their minds. Oklahoma County's 228,408 votes were more than twice the other five counties combined, and county totals can't separate people who switched parties from changes in who showed up. The Oklahoman reported that statewide turnout fell to its lowest level among comparable elections since 2014, even as early voting reached some of its highest levels.10

In the end, the map decided the race. Nelson outpaced Bice in Oklahoma County, but she fell flat in Canadian, Logan, Lincoln, Seminole and Pottawatomie counties1, NonDoc reported. A KGOU analysis found that Bice received a surplus of votes from those additional rural counties7, and that Nelson likely could have won with the previous district boundaries.7

What the result means

The district had been held by Republicans since the mid-1970s11 until Horn won it in 2018. Bice now keeps it for a third straight election under the current lines, and no incumbent lost here. Statewide, Republican Kevin Hern beat Democrat N'Kiyla Thomas for the Senate by 17.9 points. Republican Mike Mazzei beat Democrat Cyndi Munson for governor by 6.0 points. Trump carried the state by 34.3 points in 2024, so those races moved 16 and 28 points.

After the loss, Nelson said: "We took a race that they thought they could take for granted and we made them fight for it."12 The results are unofficial; official results will not be certified by the Oklahoma State Election Board until13 later this month.
