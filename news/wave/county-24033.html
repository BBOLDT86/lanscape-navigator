The wave · the biggest counties · Prince George's County, Maryland

Prince George's gives Moore an even wider edge on a smaller turnout

Maryland's majority-Black suburb beside Washington went 82 points for Gov. Wes Moore, seven points past Kamala Harris's 2024 margin, while only about two-thirds as many ballots have been counted so far and federal job cuts hung over the county.

Prince George's County runs along Washington's eastern edge, and federal jobs shape it more than almost anywhere else. Capital B News reported that until around 2022, Prince George's was the wealthiest majority-Black county in the U.S., its affluence propelled by federal employment.1 The median household income is around $100,0001, and there are some 65,000 federal workers in Prince George's, accounting for 17.4% of the county's total workforce1.

What the county did

In the count so far, Democratic Gov. Wes Moore leads Republican Dan Cox 228,680 to 18,007 in the county. That is a margin of 82.2 points. Kamala Harris carried Prince George's by 75.2 points in 2024 over Donald Trump, so the county moved about 7 points toward Democrats. That was the fifth-largest move among Maryland's 24 counties. Statewide, Moore's margin was 31.5 points, against Harris's 28.8, a move of 2.7.

The Washington suburbs moved the most. Montgomery County next door moved nearly 10 points toward Democrats, Frederick 8 and Howard 5. Baltimore County went about a point the other way, and Anne Arundel County and the city of Baltimore barely moved.

By national standards, Prince George's moved modestly. The typical county moved 14 points left. Philadelphia moved 20, and DeKalb County, Georgia, another majority-Black suburb, moved 13. Alameda County, California, moved 8, Multnomah County, Oregon, 5, and Manhattan didn't move. A county Harris had already won by 75 points had little room left to grow.

The count is not finished. The 256,249 votes tallied so far are about 78.5 percent of what AP expects, and they equal 64 percent of the county's 401,881 votes in 2024. The margin could still change.

Why

The race was a rematch. NBC Washington reported that Moore and Cox sparred on the economy, affordability, Trump and more in their only debate2. The Washington Post noted that Moore won a second term that he has not committed to finishing.3

Maryland Matters reported that Election Day turnout followed record early voting for a gubernatorial election, with more than 1 million ballots cast before Tuesday.4 The Baltimore Banner reported that, according to officials, Prince George's County records the highest early voter turnout in Maryland5. Election Day was harder in Laurel. The Banner reported that long waits, ballot shortages, and other issues forced some Prince George's County voters to leave without casting ballots.6 A county judge turned down a request from the NAACP and the League of Women Voters of Maryland that she extend voting in a Laurel precinct until midnight4. State Elections Administrator Jared DeMarinis said the state will conduct a review after the election to investigate what caused the long lines in Laurel.6 The Diamondback reported that Prince George's County is the only jurisdiction in Maryland with designated election monitors7 from the Justice Department. Maryland Matters said they were checking compliance with federal law requiring language assistance be provided to voters who need it.4

Federal layoffs were the main local issue. The Banner reported that the county had been bracing for the financial impact of massive federal job cuts under President Donald Trump's administration8. The county's fiscal watchdog estimates it loses about $3.2 million in income tax revenue for every 1,000 federal jobs cut8. After the election, WTOP reported that local Democrats expect to flex some muscle and push back on federal job cuts and relocations that hit the D.C. region, especially Prince George's County, very hard.9

The Watch found no exit poll or voter survey that breaks out Prince George's. The county count can't tell people who switched sides from people who simply didn't vote. With ballots so far at 64 percent of the 2024 total, the 7-point move mixes who showed up with any real change of mind, and the numbers can't separate the two.

What it means

Within the county, the real contests are among Democrats. The Diamondback reported that the Democratic primary winner for county executive will advance to the general election on Nov. 3 unopposed by a Republican.10 Aisha Braveboy won that primary and now has her first full term. The Banner wrote that she will face a chronically underperforming school system, a depleted federal workforce, a council under fire for questionable grant awards11.

Voters also changed the shape of their government. The Banner reported that voters approved eliminating the council's two at-large seats, reducing the body from 11 to nine members12, a change which will take effect in December 2030.13 At-large Councilmember Jolene Ivey had questioned why the county would eliminate a form of representation also used in neighboring jurisdictions.14 The day after the vote, Braveboy said "I think Prince George's County won last night big time,"9 and pointed the next fight at Washington.
