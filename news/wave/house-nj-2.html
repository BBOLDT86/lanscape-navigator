Cape May's mayor unseats Jeff Van Drew in South Jersey's 2nd District

Democrat Zack Mullock ousted four-term Republican Jeff Van Drew in New Jersey's 2nd District, leading by 7,260 votes with 97 percent counted, as all six counties moved toward Democrats in the Senate race.

Zack Mullock, the Democratic mayor of Cape May, has unseated Rep. Jeff Van Drew, the four-term Republican who has held the South Jersey 2nd Congressional District seat since 20191. With 97.26 percent of the expected vote counted, Mullock has 137,791 votes (50.8 percent) to Van Drew's 130,531 (48.1 percent). That is a lead of 7,260 votes, or 2.7 points. Ramon Mora, an independent, has 2,831.

Van Drew conceded defeat at his campaign watch party in Sea Isle City2 on Tuesday night, before the Associated Press had called the race. The AP declared Mullock the winner late Thursday afternoon3, WHYY reported.

The race

Mullock is a 41-year-old business owner, former pro golfer, and Cape May mayor4, and in the June Democratic primary he defeated three other challengers for the nomination4. Gothamist reported that he ran on affordability, often touting how he hasn't raised taxes in Cape May in his six years as mayor4. He also argued his ability to win three straight local elections in typically conservative Cape May proved he could beat Van Drew4.

His main line of attack was Van Drew's closeness to President Donald Trump. Van Drew is 73, a retired dentist from Dennis Township5. He switched his party to Republican in 2019, a year after being elected as a Democrat6, at a publicized Oval Office meeting where he pledged "undying support" to the president7. Mullock went after his vote for Trump's One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which made large cuts to Medicaid6, as The Philadelphia Inquirer described it. He also faulted Van Drew for his refusal to require the president to seek congressional approval for his war with Iran1. The two met once, on Oct. 22 at Stockton University. The debate was held without an audience, at the request of the Van Drew campaign8, and there Van Drew denied accusations from Mullock that he voted to cut Medicaid8.

The campaign also had a local fight. Gothamist reported that a $17 billion data center in Vineland, backed by Microsoft, was a key topic, and Mullock argued for a federal moratorium on data centers4. Van Drew has said he has no authority over the project6.

Van Drew had more money. The New Jersey Globe reported that he raised $2.4 million to $850,948 plus a $110,000 personal loan through Oct. 141. Mullock's late help came from outside. According to the Globe, Gov. Mikie Sherrill, through Mission to Deliver, and George E. Norcross III were behind a late super PAC expenditure of more than $1.5 million, along with the Laborers' union, through Project 2181. Gothamist gave a different account, saying the money came from a super PAC tied to Democratic opposition research firm American Bridge 21st Century4. One week before the vote, the Cook Political Report rated the race "solid Republican"; by the weekend it had moved to "likely Republican"7.

The district

The 2nd represents all of Atlantic, Cape May, Cumberland and Salem counties and parts of Gloucester and Ocean counties9. The Watch's wave compares each county's U.S. Senate vote with the 2024 presidential vote. In the Senate race, Cory Booker beat Justin Murphy statewide by 27.3 points. All six of the district's counties moved toward Democrats.

Ocean moved the most: Trump won it by 36.0 points, and Murphy carried it by 8.6, a 27-point swing. Gloucester (R+2.8 to D+18.5), Cumberland (R+3.8 to D+15.3) and Atlantic (R+3.0 to D+14.0) all flipped. Salem moved 16 points and Cape May 11, but both stayed Republican. Cape May is home to both candidates, two Cape May County men10. Taken together, the six counties moved 22 points, close to the state's 21.

Two cautions. First, these are whole-county numbers, and much of Ocean and Gloucester lies outside the district. Those two counties cast nearly 329,000 Senate votes, more than the 271,153 cast in the entire House race. Second, county counts can't tell voters who changed sides from changes in who turned out. Mullock noted many Republicans appear to have voted for him and singled out the turnout in Cumberland County10, but these numbers can't confirm or rule out either explanation.

The swing also reached local races. Democrats flipped control in Cumberland County, where the GOP sheriff and three county commissioners lost re-election11. In Atlantic County, Democrats captured the sheriff's and clerk's offices and all three commissioner seats on the ballot, reducing the Republican majority from 8-1 to 5-412.

What it means

Republicans won the seat in 1994 and held on to it until Van Drew won it as a Democrat in 201813, KYW Newsradio noted. Van Drew attributed his loss to a national political wave rather than dissatisfaction with his performance5. "That was not a blue wave, that was a blue tsunami," Van Drew said.9 In the 7th District, Democrat Rebecca Bennett defeated Republican Tom Kean Jr.14 The New Jersey Globe says that leaves an 11-1 Democratic delegation11. Nationally, 252 seats have been called for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority, and 161 for Republicans. Twenty-two are still uncalled.

Counting isn't finished. About 2.7 percent of the expected vote is still out. By the AP's estimate that is roughly 7,600 ballots, only slightly more than Mullock's lead, so Van Drew would need nearly all of them. He has already conceded. Mullock, according to the Press of Atlantic City, said he hopes this shakes the trees to find Republicans willing to work with Dems15.
