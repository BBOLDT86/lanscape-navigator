The wave · the biggest counties · DuPage County, Illinois

In DuPage, Illinois' old Republican suburb, a 16-point swing left

Juliana Stratton carried Chicago's western suburbs by nearly 29 points, more than double Kamala Harris's 2024 margin, on a quarter fewer ballots. Democrats took every county office on the ballot, ousting Republicans who had held seats for decades.

DuPage County is the wide band of suburbs west of Chicago that includes Naperville, Wheaton, Elmhurst and Hinsdale. It has 934,298 residents, according to the Census Bureau's 2025 estimate1. Of adults 25 and older, 51.8% hold at least a bachelor's degree, against 35.7% nationally, and the median household income is $112,096, against $80,734.2 For decades it was the Republicans' home base in Illinois. The Chicago Sun-Times wrote that the suburbs "provided the lifeblood of the Illinois GOP for generations."3

What DuPage did

In the race for U.S. Senate, Lt. Gov. Juliana Stratton, a Democrat, beat Don Tracy, a former chair of the state Republican Party, by 215,234 votes to 117,326 in DuPage, with 98.4% of the expected vote counted. That is a margin of D+28.9. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county against Donald Trump by D+13.3, so the county moved 15.6 points toward the Democrats.

Statewide, Ms. Stratton leads by D+24.3 with 95.8% of the expected vote counted, a 13.3-point move from the 2024 presidential margin of D+11.0. Gov. JB Pritzker's race against Darren Bailey moved 12.5 points. DuPage ranks seventh of Illinois' 102 counties by size of move. Ninety-five of the 102 moved left, and the typical county moved 6 points. The other collar counties moved about as much or more: Will 18 points, from a near-tie to D+19.3; Kane 18; McHenry 16, flipping from R+5.3 to D+11.0; and Lake 14. Cook moved 18. Elsewhere, DuPage landed near the national typical move of 14 points. Among big counties that voted like it in 2024, it moved slightly more than Orange County, Fla. (15) and less than Cobb County, Ga. (19).

Fewer people voted. The county cast 338,941 ballots, 75% of its 2024 total. Even so, the Democratic margin grew from about 60,000 votes to nearly 98,000. A county count cannot say how much of that came from voters switching sides and how much from which voters came out in a midterm and which stayed home. The 16-point move is a mix of both.

Why, according to those watching

The two candidates offered a clear choice. The Sun-Times described Ms. Stratton as a progressive voice with a campaign slogan of "F- - - Trump"4. Mr. Tracy, a Springfield lawyer, ran on cost of living and keeping his distance from the White House5.

Local reporting points mostly to national politics. The Daily Herald reported that the tide against President Donald Trump's policies ousted myriad moderate and MAGA Republican state lawmakers from Cook and the collar counties6, and that experts in both parties agreed. Kevin Coyne, the DuPage Republican chairman, told the paper that lack of support for the war in Iran and other national issues made it hard for local Republicans up and down the ballot7. He added: "It's sad that our county treasurer and sheriff's races are being determined by international and foreign issues, but clearly they are."7

Melissa Mouritsen, a political scientist at the College of DuPage, called the result a resounding rebuke of MAGA politics6. She also said "a lot of people are just tired of the culture war stuff."6 Shaw Local reported that the results surprised her8. Money mattered too. Before the election, the Daily Herald reported that Mr. Pritzker's campaign gave the Democratic Party of DuPage County $200,0009. The Watch did not find a published voter survey that looks at DuPage alone, so who switched and who stayed home remains unmeasured.

What it means for DuPage politics

The Daily Herald reported that Democrats solidified their rule Tuesday in DuPage County, sweeping every county race.10 The losses reached long-serving Republicans. Voters ousted longtime Treasurer Gwen Henry11, who has served as the DuPage County treasurer since 200612, even though the Daily Herald's editorial board had endorsed her. Jim Zay, the county board's longest-serving member and a Republican from Carol Stream10, lost to the newcomer Jesse Gutierrez. Peter Coolidge won the sheriff's race. Before the vote, the Daily Herald noted that electing DuPage's first Democratic sheriff since the Civil War would be historic9.

The county board shows how fast the change has come. Democrats took control of the DuPage County Board in 2020 when they secured 11 of the board's 18 seats.8 Shaw Local reported they will dominate the board by 17-1 margin once victorious candidates are sworn later this year.8 Deb Conroy, who was re-elected chair, became the first Democrat in decades13 to hold the job when she won it in 2022.

Republicans say the shift can be reversed. Mr. Coyne said the county "went from red to blue on a dime here and it can go the other way too."7 Bob Grogan, a former DuPage County auditor who now chairs the state party, thinks Republican policies about lower taxes, the size of government and safer streets can be winning issues for the GOP in the suburbs7. He acknowledged that the party's message and messengers have not been. He also said the party needs a statewide candidate who "can actually speak suburban cocktail party language."7 Results are unofficial until the canvass is finished.
