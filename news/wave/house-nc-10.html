The House · North Carolina 10

Pat Harrigan holds North Carolina's 10th by 2,509 votes

The first-term Republican held a Hickory-to-Winston-Salem seat that Republicans have kept since 1969. Statewide, Democrats won big. A recount produced no change, and Democrat Ashley Bell conceded on Nov. 20.

Rep. Pat Harrigan, a Republican, will return for a second term from North Carolina's 10th Congressional District. He won the closest House race in the state by one of the smallest margins in the country. AP's count gives Harrigan 146,590 votes to 144,081 for Democrat Ashley Bell, a gap of 2,509 votes, or 0.8 points. Steven Feldman, who is listed as an independent in AP's file, drew 4,711 votes. That is more than the margin between the top two. About 99 percent of the expected vote has been counted.

The race

Harrigan is a former Army Green Beret. He served in the U.S. Army and was deployed to Afghanistan. He became a Green Beret after being selected for Special Forces training.1 He won the seat in 2024 after Rep. Patrick McHenry retired. Bell is a physician associate, and she built her campaign around health care. Asked by the Elkin Tribune which household cost she would tackle first, she answered health care and said she had spent 16 years watching what it does to families' budgets.2 In a WSOC-TV candidate guide, Bell said Harrigan "has supported each and every one of Trump's decisions"3. She also went after his weapons-manufacturing business and the late filing of his financial disclosures. Feldman appeared on the ballot as a Libertarian3.

Harrigan had far more money. WUNC reported in late October that Bell had raised about $147,000, while Harrigan had $1.3 million.4 The station found that by then, only two of North Carolina's congressional incumbents, Pat Harrigan and Mark Harris, hadn't run TV ads4. It described those districts as among the state's reddest.

The race was run in a difficult year for North Carolina Republicans. In the Senate race, Democrat Roy Cooper beat Michael Whatley by 19.7 points. That is a 22.9-point move toward Democrats from the 2024 presidential result, which Donald Trump won by 3.2 points. CBS 17 reported that Harrigan is one of only three Republicans projected to have won their House races in North Carolina this year after the GOP entered the election with a 10-4 congressional majority.5

The district

The 10th stretches from Hickory in the west to Winston-Salem in the east. It takes in all of Catawba, Iredell, Lincoln, and Yadkin counties, plus most of Forsyth. A large part of Winston-Salem sits inside it.6 Hickory and Winston-Salem first landed in the same congressional district when the General Assembly passed new maps in 2023, the Hickory Daily Record reported. The result is a mix of Democratic Winston-Salem and the Republican manufacturing towns and fast-growing Charlotte exurbs along Interstate 40 and Interstate 77.

The Watch's data for this seat does not include county-by-county counts. This piece therefore cannot say how each of the five counties moved from 2024. The district-wide total of 295,382 votes also cannot show, on its own, whether the race narrowed because voters changed their minds or because different voters turned out. Reporting does show where the late votes came from. The Winston-Salem Journal reported that provisional ballots put the race into recount territory with Democrat Ashley Bell gaining nearly 900 provisional ballots, including 690 in Forsyth County.7

What the result means

Republicans have held this seat for decades. Republicans have won the district continuously since the 1960s. The last Democrat to represent the district was Basil Whitener, from 1963 to 1969.8

The margin was close enough for a recount. Under state rules, the trailing candidate in a non-statewide race may ask for one when the gap "is 1% or less of the total votes cast in the contest,"9 according to the State Board of Elections, and Bell asked for one. WHKY in Newton reported that after Catawba County's recount on Nov. 18, there were no changes in the order for the 10th District Congressional race10. Harrigan had already declared victory. Time reported that he respected Bell's right to a recount but remained "confident in the results."11

Bell conceded on Nov. 20. WXII reported that she said the race represented a significant shift from the previous election and argued that the campaign demonstrated growing support for Democrats in the district.12 She also put the incumbent on notice: "While I am conceding the race, this in no way means that Representative Harrigan should become comfortable in Washington,"12 she said.

Sources disagree on when the race was called. AP's results file, which The Watch uses, records the race as called for Harrigan on election night. But Axios Raleigh reported on Nov. 17 that AP has not yet called the race.13 Either way, the result is now settled in practice, though the count is still unofficial until the state certifies it.

Nationally, AP has called 257 House seats for Democrats and 173 for Republicans, with five still uncalled. Harrigan's seat adds to the Republicans' count.
