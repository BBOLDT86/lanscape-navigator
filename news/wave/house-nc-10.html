Harrigan leads by 3,029 votes in North Carolina's 10th, still uncalled

In a district Republicans have held since 1969, Rep. Pat Harrigan leads Democrat Ashley Bell by one point with 99% counted. In the Senate race, every county from Winston-Salem to Lincolnton moved 20 to 23 points toward Democrats.

With 99% of the expected vote counted, Republican Rep. Pat Harrigan leads Democrat Ashley Bell 146,166 to 143,137 in North Carolina's 10th Congressional District. That is a gap of 3,029 votes, or 1.0 point, out of 293,976 cast. Steven Feldman, on the ballot as a Libertarian1, has 4,673. The Associated Press has not called the race. Some state outlets have gone further. The North State Journal reported that Harrigan had won a second term2, and WRAL wrote that he appeared to defeat Bell3.

The race

Harrigan is a first-term congressman. He served in the Army in Afghanistan and became a Green Beret. He also holds a nuclear engineering degree from West Point.2 In 2022, WFAE reported that he owned ZRODelta, a Burke County maker of handguns and assault-style rifles4. That year he ran in the 14th District against Democrat Jeff Jackson4. After Rep. Patrick McHenry retired, Harrigan won the 10th in 2024.2

Bell told PBS North Carolina that she has been a physician associate for 15 years, graduated from Wake Forest School of Medicine and teaches as a professor5. In March, WFMY reported, she won a crowded six-candidate Democratic primary.6 Health care was the center of her campaign. In WSOC-TV's candidate guide she said she supports moving toward Medicare for All7. In the same guide she accused Harrigan of "voting with Trump policies 100% of the time"7 and criticized his weapons business and the extensions he sought on his financial disclosures7.

Harrigan had far more money. Federal Election Commission figures through mid-October showed him raising about $1.32 million to Bell's roughly $148,000.8 After the primary, WFMY said Bell faced a hard general election in a Republican-leaning district, against an incumbent with significant structural advantages.6 The only public poll listed for the race, taken in March by the Republican firm Ragnar Research Partners, had Harrigan well ahead.8

The bigger change came from the state as a whole. Roy Cooper built his Senate campaign around affordability9, while Michael Whatley's focused on crime and immigration9. The AP's voter survey found that most North Carolina voters thought Whatley was too supportive of Trump.10 WRAL described a nationwide Democratic surge amid President Trump's sagging popularity3. In the 10th, the North State Journal reported that Harrigan trailed early in the evening before pulling ahead2.

The district

WRAL described the district as one that includes Winston-Salem and follows Interstate 77 down to Mooresville3. It takes in all of Catawba, Iredell, Lincoln and Yadkin counties and most of Forsyth.8 It has historically been the state's most Republican district and usually its most exurban.11 Adding most of Forsyth, including much of Winston-Salem, cut its Cook Partisan Voting Index from R+22 to R+9.11

The Watch's wave map uses the Senate vote in each whole county. By that measure, every county in the district moved sharply toward Democrats: 22 points on a weighted basis, close to the state's 22.8. Catawba County, home to Hickory, moved the most, 23 points. It went from R+38.0 for Trump in 2024 to R+15.1 in the Senate race. Iredell (Statesville and Mooresville) went from R+32.6 to R+10.6, a 22-point move. Yadkin went from R+62.4 to R+40.6. Lincoln moved the least, 20 points, and is still R+26.1. Forsyth, already Democratic, went from D+13.2 to D+35.5. Cooper won the state by 19.6 points, but none of the four Republican counties flipped.

These moves don't necessarily mean voters changed their minds. County counts can't separate Trump voters who backed Cooper from Republicans who stayed home. What they do show is the math of the seat. Forsyth cast 152,572 Senate votes, fewer than the 187,748 cast in the other four counties combined, and part of Winston-Salem is in the 6th District11. The Winston-Salem Chronicle reported that Bell won most of the district's Forsyth votes, but Harrigan held on across all five counties.12

What the result means

Republicans have won the 10th in every election since 1969.11 If Harrigan's lead holds, the streak continues and no incumbent loses here. That would set the seat apart in a state where, WRAL reported, Republicans went into Election Day holding 10 of the 14 seats and Democrats could hold as many as 10 by January3. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called.

The count is not final. Each county will count its provisional ballots by Nov. 10.13 County boards hold their canvass meetings on Nov. 13.13 Under state law, the trailing candidate in a race that isn't statewide can ask for a recount if the final margin is 1% or less of all votes cast.14 On the current count, Harrigan's lead is 1.03% of the vote, about 90 votes over that line. In districts that cross county lines, recount requests are due by noon on Nov. 17.15
