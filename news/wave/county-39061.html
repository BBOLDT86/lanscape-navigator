Hamilton County gave Brown a bigger win, but moved less than Ohio

Cincinnati's county voted for Sherrod Brown by 25 points, up from Kamala Harris's 15-point edge in 2024. Every Ohio county moved left; Hamilton moved less than all but two, on a midterm turnout about 84% of 2024's.

Hamilton County, which covers Cincinnati1, cast 342,187 votes in Ohio's special U.S. Senate election on Nov. 3, with 97.4 percent of the expected vote counted. Democrat Sherrod Brown won 207,080 of them and Republican Sen. Jon Husted won 120,368, a margin of 25.3 points. In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 14.9 points. That same year Brown lost his Senate seat. This race returns him to the office he lost1. Husted was appointed by Ohio Gov. Mike DeWine to fill Vice President JD Vance's seat at the start of 20252.

What Hamilton County did

A 10-point move to the left is large. In Ohio this year it was small. Brown carried the state by 4.9 points, and Trump had carried it by 11.2 points, so the state moved 16 points. In the governor's race, Democrat Amy Acton beat Republican Vivek Ramaswamy by 7.9 points, a 19-point move. All 88 counties moved left. The typical county moved 17 points, and Hamilton ranked 86th. Ohio's other big urban counties also moved less than the state as a whole: Cuyahoga moved 12 points, Lucas 13 and Franklin 14. Summit moved 16 and Montgomery about 15. Next door, Republican Butler County went from R+26.3 to R+10.3, a 16-point move.

Hamilton also trailed similar counties in other states. Cobb County, Georgia, started from the same 2024 margin, D+14.9, and moved 19 points. Pima County, Arizona, and DuPage County, Illinois, each moved 16. Orange County, Florida, moved 15. Nationally, the typical county moved 14.

The raw numbers show why a move is not the same as persuasion. Hamilton cast about 67,600 fewer ballots than in 2024, or 84 percent of the presidential turnout. Even so, Brown's margin of roughly 86,700 votes was larger than Harris's margin of about 61,000. A county count cannot separate voters who switched sides from voters who stayed home or came back. This move is a mix of both.

Why: turnout, costs and a home-county candidate

WVXU, Cincinnati's public radio station, reported that a lot more voters turned out across Greater Cincinnati Tuesday than the last midterm election, likely helping Democrats3. Before Election Day, county elections director Sherry Poland told the station that early voting had set a record for a mid-term election4. She added that "the majority of Hamilton County voters still cast their ballots on Election Day."4 Nationally, The Washington Post reported that midterm turnout reached its second-highest peak in more than a century5.

The Senate campaign was fought over affordability issues and data center expansion6. Brown painted Husted as "the face of data centers"7 and pointed to Husted's links to the FirstEnergy bribery scandal. He also campaigned on what he called the "High Cost Husted Tour."8 Husted's campaign pointed to legislation Husted backed that would require data centers to pay for their own power9, and it said Brown had flip-flopped on the issue.

The governor's race had a local candidate. The Cincinnati Enquirer noted that Ramaswamy was raised in the Hamilton County suburb of Evendale10. WOSU reported that he lost Hamilton County11 anyway. Acton's running mate, David Pepper, served on Cincinnati City Council and the Hamilton County Commission12.

The Watch found no published exit poll or voter survey for Hamilton County itself. NPR's national voter poll found that young voters, Latinos and Asian Americans, men and independents all moved heavily to the left13. That national finding cannot be assumed to describe Hamilton County.

What it means here

The Democratic gains reached far down the ballot. The Enquirer reported that Democrats swept the nine contested judge races this year, ousting four incumbent Republican judges14. That will leave three Republicans on the county's common pleas court. WCPO reported that Democrat Meeka Owens wins race for Hamilton County Commissioner15.

In Congress, Democratic Rep. Greg Landsman won a third term in a district that covers all of Warren and Clinton Counties, as well as southern and western Hamilton County16. Northern Hamilton County sits in a different district that has voted reliably Republican in the past3. Republican Warren Davidson won that district again. WVXU reported that his Democratic opponent, Enoch, ran well ahead in the Hamilton County portion, in part thanks to higher turnout there3.

These results come from a midterm electorate that was smaller than the 2024 one. The seat Brown won is up for re-election in 20286. That race will tell more than this count can about whether Hamilton's voters have changed. The numbers here are not final: about 2.6 percent of the county's expected vote had not yet been counted.
