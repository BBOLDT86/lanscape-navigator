Central Jersey's Middlesex County moves 28 points left for Cory Booker

Two years after Kamala Harris carried it by 8 points, the county of Edison, New Brunswick and Perth Amboy gave Cory Booker a 36-point margin. That was one of New Jersey's largest moves, on two-thirds of 2024's vote.

In 2024, Kamala Harris carried Middlesex County by 8.0 points out of 365,187 votes. On Nov. 3, Sen. Cory Booker carried it by 35.7 points, 160,579 votes to 73,910 for the Republican, Justin Murphy. That is a 28-point move toward the Democrats. About 96 percent of the expected vote has been counted, and results are considered unofficial until certified by the county Board of Elections, a process that usually takes a week to complete.1

What Middlesex did

Statewide, Booker won by 27.3 points, compared with Harris's 5.9, a move of 21.4 points. All 21 New Jersey counties moved left. The typical county moved 18.5 points, and only two moved further than Middlesex. Among the state's other big counties, Bergen and Union each moved 21 points and Essex 18. Ocean moved 27 but still voted Republican. Monmouth and Morris both flipped to the Democrats.

Outside the state, the comparison is mixed. Harris County (Houston), which Harris carried by 5.5 points, moved 27 points, almost the same as Middlesex. Erie County (Buffalo), which started at D+9.7, moved about 1 point. Oakland County, Mich., moved 4 points and Kent County, Mich., 8. Nationally, the typical county moved 14.

Middlesex is a large and diverse suburban county. The Census Bureau estimates 883,3352 residents. Its population is 28.2%2 Asian, against 11.6%2 statewide, and 25.2%3 Hispanic. In Edison, the county's largest town, 52.8%4 of residents are Asian. The New Jersey Globe describes the county's Democratic strength as reinforced by a powerful county organization and heavily Democratic municipalities such as New Brunswick, home to Rutgers University.5

Why it moved

Much of this move was a return after 2024. After the presidential race, the New Jersey Globe found that Democrats lost the most ground in towns with large Hispanic and Asian American communities.6 It listed Middlesex among the North Jersey counties where many towns had moved 20 or 30 points toward Republicans in four years. The Globe reported that Perth Amboy, a mostly Latino city on Raritan Bay, experienced one of New Jersey's most dramatic rightward shifts at the presidential level5. In 2025, the Globe reported, many of those voters appeared to have shifted back toward the Democratic Party5 in Mikie Sherrill's race for governor.

The Senate race itself was lopsided. The Associated Press described Justin Murphy as a little-known attorney who emerged from a crowded field in his party's June primary.7 He is a former deputy mayor of Tabernacle, in Burlington County. WABC reported that Murphy campaigned online and sought to introduce himself to voters by focusing on affordability8. Booker blamed rising costs on President Donald Trump, and he cited health care cuts and higher gas prices caused by the war with Iran.

NJ Spotlight News reported that the cost of living was the dominant concern this year, made worse by inflation and Trump's tariffs. In a Fairleigh Dickinson University poll, 90% of voters surveyed for the FDU poll said affordability was important to their vote9. CBS New York reported that the poll found Republican gains among Hispanic and younger voters have largely been erased10. NPR's national voter poll found that young voters, Latinos and Asian Americans, men and independents all moved heavily to the left.11 Neither survey measured Middlesex on its own, so their findings cannot be applied directly to the county.

Turnout also changed. Middlesex cast 66 percent as many votes as it did in 2024, so about a third of the 2024 electorate did not vote. That means the 28-point move combines two effects: some 2024 Trump voters may have switched to Booker, and some may have stayed home. County-level totals cannot separate the two. Before the election, Ashley Koning of Rutgers' Eagleton Center said the result may not be so much Cory Booker, but more so about people expressing with their feet by going to the booth12.

What it means locally

According to the New Jersey Globe, Middlesex Democrats extended a winning streak that began 35 years ago13 in countywide races, keeping a 7-0 majority on the Board of County Commissioners. The clearest local change was in Sayreville. Trump carried the borough in 2024. This year, Democrats won both council seats and ousting two Republican incumbents by overwhelming margins14, which the Globe called a sharp reversal from 202314. The county Democratic chairman, Kevin P. McCabe, said: "Every contested race, in every town, went to a Democrat"15.

The county will also have a new member of Congress. Adam Hamawy won the 12th District, which includes parts of Middlesex. WHYY reported that because New Jersey no longer uses the county-line ballot design, he won the primary without needing the endorsement of party bosses or county chairs16. Before the election, the Globe had also noted Rep. Frank Pallone's relatively underwhelming Democratic primary performance5. That suggests the county's Democratic voters do not always follow the party organization, even in a year it swept.
