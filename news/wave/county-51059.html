Fairfax County, home to 80,000 federal workers, swings 17 points toward Warner

Virginia's biggest county gave Mark Warner a 52-point win, 17 points better than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin there. It voted on 80 percent of the 2024 turnout, after a year of federal layoffs.

Fairfax County is Virginia's largest county and its biggest source of Democratic votes. On Nov. 3 it gave Sen. Mark Warner 356,533 votes to 111,800 for Republican Bert Mizusawa, a margin of 52.0 points. With 95.97 percent of the expected vote counted, 470,209 ballots were in. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county by 35.0 points over Donald Trump. That is a 17-point move toward the Democrats.

The county moved about as much as Virginia did. Statewide, Warner won by 22.7 points, compared with Harris's 5.8, a move of 16.9. All 133 of Virginia's counties and independent cities moved left. Fairfax ranked 37th. Two big neighbors moved further: Prince William, by 25 points, and Loudoun, by 22. Arlington, which started from D+58.6, moved 11. Elsewhere, Milwaukee County also ended at D+52.0, but it moved only 13.5 points. Mecklenburg County, N.C., moved 21. Nationally, the typical county moved 14.

Who turned out

The vote was a midterm vote. Fairfax cast 80 percent as many ballots as it did in 2024. FFXnow reported that after a surge in early voting, overall turnout in Fairfax County fell closely in line with the previous Congressional midterms in 20221. It also didn't reach the heights of the last presidential election in 20241. Even so, Warner's lead in votes was roughly 40,000 larger than Harris's, on about 114,000 fewer ballots. County totals can't tell us how much of that came from people changing sides and how much from who voted and who stayed home. The move is a mix of both. The count is also not final: the Fairfax County Times reported that the Fairfax County Electoral Board is scheduled to certify the results Nov. 13.2

Federal cuts and the cost of living

Fairfax is a wealthy, well-educated suburb of Washington. The Census Bureau puts its population at 1,167,8733 and its median household income at $153,6373. About 31 percent of residents were born abroad. Its economy depends on the federal government. FFXnow, citing the county's economic development authority, reported that approximately 80,000 residents — about 13% of the workforce — are employed by the federal government4. Joblessness rose after the Trump administration's cuts. FFXnow reported that the county's non-seasonally-adjusted unemployment rate rose from 2.7% to 3.8%5 between January 2025 and January 2026. In a spring survey by Gallup and the Greater Washington Community Foundation, 49% of respondents said federal job cuts or furloughs negatively impacted their household6. That figure covers Fairfax County plus the cities of Fairfax and Falls Church.

The Associated Press made the same connection statewide. It noted that Virginia's federal workers bore the brunt of President Donald Trump's efforts to slash the government workforce early in his second term7. WSET reported that Warner campaigned on the cost of living, including high diesel prices, health care8 and ending the war in Iran. Mizusawa put election integrity8, border security and proof of citizenship to vote at the top of his list. The Washington Post reported that Warner far outraised Mizusawa, who did not receive help from national Republican groups.9 Statewide, NBC News published a national exit poll that showed women backing Warner 65%10 to 35 percent and men backing him 56 to 44. No poll released for Fairfax alone breaks down who moved.

Local Democrats tied the result to the national mood. Jeremy Cullimore, executive director of the county Democratic committee, told FFXnow it was "a fantastic night for Democrats, including in Fairfax County"1. Mizusawa conceded in a statement: "While these are not the results we hoped for, I congratulate Senator Mark Warner and his team on their victory."11

What it means here

Two years ago the mood among Fairfax Democrats was gloomier. After the 2024 election, FFXnow reported that the county Democratic committee's director thought Republicans had seemed "more mobilized locally"12 than in 2020. The 2026 result reverses that trend, at least in a midterm with a smaller electorate. It follows the 2025 governor's race. Afterward, Rep. James Walkinshaw of Fairfax called the statewide shift to the left "a backlash" to Gov. Glenn Youngkin, who had expressed support for the Trump administration's job cuts and tariffs policies.13

Warner has said this term will be his last. When he ran again, Fairfax was already the state's biggest Democratic stronghold. It is bigger now. But a 17-point move with one-fifth fewer voters says more about who showed up in a midterm year than about how the next presidential electorate will vote.
