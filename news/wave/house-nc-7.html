Rouzer keeps southeastern North Carolina seat by 2,311 votes amid Democratic surge

Six-term Republican David Rouzer finished 0.7 points ahead of Democrat Kimberly Hardy, a Fayetteville State professor, in a Wilmington-to-Fayetteville district that ratings treated as safe, while Roy Cooper carried North Carolina's Senate race by nearly 20 points.

The race

Republican David Rouzer has held North Carolina's 7th District since 2015, and he won a seventh term with 160,702 votes. Democrat Kimberly Hardy got 158,391. That is a gap of 2,311 votes, or 0.7 points, out of 322,266 cast, with 99% of the expected vote counted. Maad Abu-Ghazalah, the Libertarian on the ballot1 (AP's file lists him as an independent), took 3,173 votes, more than the difference between the two leading candidates.

The margin was within recount range. Hardy decided not to ask for one. She announced in a video posted to her socials on Sunday that she would not file for a recount2, Port City Daily reported, telling supporters, "We really just don't think we will be able to find that number of votes in a recount"2. Border Belt Independent reported that she will run against Rouzer again when he is up for reelection again in 2028.3

Hardy was a social work professor at Fayetteville State University and a leader of the state Democratic Party4. Her campaign was built mostly around health care. Hardy's campaign focused on health care. In an interview with Port City Daily last year, Hardy railed against the federal cuts.5 She also went after Rouzer over the Cape Fear Memorial Bridge in Wilmington. She noted that Rouzer voted against [The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act] that could have helped that project6, and many residents oppose a toll on the bridge. In October, Rouzer said he would try to find a way to extend the grant deadline for the bridge replacement by a year or secure additional funding7. Port City Daily noted that Hardy and Abu-Ghazalah came to an October candidates forum that Rouzer had been invited to as well.

WUNC reported that Rouzer hasn't spent money on TV ads since 20184 and bought airtime this fall for a spot that said "Politicians talk, David Rouzer delivers"4. Hardy's campaign has tied Rouzer to President Donald Trump4. She said she would not take the corporate PAC contributions that make up a big chunk of Rouzer's fundraising total4, and WUNC put her fundraising at about $400,000 in late October. The seat is rated as likely Republican by the nonpartisan Cook Political Report.8

The district

District 7 encompasses all of Bladen, Brunswick, Columbus, New Hanover and Pender counties, as well as portions of Robeson, Sampson and Cumberland, including part of Fayetteville.9 The Wilmington StarNews identified coping with the district's population boom10 as one of the big issues facing whoever won. Border Belt Independent described the political divide: six of the eight counties that make up the 7th District—Sampson, Robeson, Pender, Bladen, Columbus, and Brunswick—are rural. Those same six went for Rouzer, while Hardy prevailed in the urban Cumberland and New Hanover counties.5

Democrats also gained in local races down the ballot. In New Hanover, WECT reported, Democrats are on track to flip control of the county commission, school board and state house.11 In Robeson, voters ousted two Republican county commissioners who changed their party affiliation from Democrat to Republican last year.12

The data behind this report has no county-by-county counts for this seat, so the wave map's county moves can't be broken out for the district here. Even with them, a county count can't tell voters who changed their minds apart from changes in who voted. Nadine Gibson, a political scientist at UNC Wilmington, told WECT that high turnout suggests that people who don't normally vote in midterms did show up, and it could also be the case that Republicans stayed home.11 That is her interpretation. The vote totals can't confirm it.

What the result means

Across the state, Democrat Roy Cooper beat Republican Michael Whatley in the U.S. Senate race by 19.7 points, 2,480,636 votes to 1,648,858. Donald Trump carried North Carolina by 3.2 points in 2024, so that is a 22.9-point swing toward the Democrats. Rouzer held on through that swing, though by less than a point. Nationally, AP has called 256 House seats for Democrats and 171 for Republicans, with 8 still uncalled. North Carolina's 7th was not one of the Democrats' pickups.

For Rouzer, it was a close call in a district where WUNC described a Democratic win as a long shot for Democrats4. Border Belt Independent reported that Hardy came the closest to beating Rouzer since the congressman was first elected in 2014.3 Rouzer had seen it coming. He told Port City Daily on election night "it was going to be a rough night for Republicans," anticipating a blue turn.2

The count is effectively over. Hardy conceded following Friday's election canvass2, and Rouzer has said publicly that the county tallies are official. AP's results file lists the race as called for Rouzer. For days after the election, though, WPDE and other outlets reported that it had not been called by the AP.13
