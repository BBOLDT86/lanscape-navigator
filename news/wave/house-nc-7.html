Rouzer Leads Hardy by 2,425 Votes in Uncalled North Carolina 7th

David Rouzer, a six-term Republican who skipped candidate forums, leads Democrat Kimberly Hardy by less than a point with 99% counted. In the Senate race, every county from Wilmington to Fayetteville moved 16 to 27 points toward Democrats.

With 99% of the expected vote counted, Rep. David Rouzer leads Democrat Kimberly Hardy 159,985 to 157,560. That is a margin of 2,425 votes, or 0.8 point. Rouzer is a Wilmington Republican who has represented the district since 20151. Maad Abu-Ghazalah (L)2 has 3,135. The Associated Press has not called the race. Rouzer declared victory, but Hardy hasn't conceded.3

The seat is one of several North Carolina races that remain too close to call and within the state's threshold for a recount4. Statewide, Roy Cooper beat Michael Whatley by 19.6 points for the open U.S. Senate seat, in a state Donald Trump carried by 3.2 points in 2024. According to the Border Belt Independent, five Democrats flipped congressional races; Hardy could be the sixth3.

The race

Hardy worked as a school social worker for several years and has been a professor at Fayetteville State University since 20161. She has been second vice-chair of the North Carolina Democratic Party since 20231. Her campaign focused on health care3, especially Rouzer's vote for the 2025 tax law, which cut federal funding for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, called SNAP, and Medicaid3. More than 30% of District 7 residents were enrolled in Medicaid in 2024, according to a KFF analysis3 cited by the Border Belt Independent. Rouzer defended his vote, saying the program needed long-term changes for fiscal responsibility and stricter eligibility rules5.

The other issue was Rouzer himself. WWAY reported that he has not held a town hall for his constituents since 20176. He also chose not to take part in an Oct. 12 forum at UNC Wilmington that Hardy and Abu-Ghazalah attended2. The Border Belt Independent reported that after years of easy wins, he stopped showing up to candidate forums, answering questionnaires, or responding to most interview requests7. He did make a handful of late visits, and on Election Day he traveled across Pender and Brunswick counties.7

Rouzer had far more money. WUNC reported that he hasn't spent money on TV ads since 20188 but went on the air this fall with a spot built on the line "Politicians talk, David Rouzer delivers."8 She has raised around $400,000, while Rouzer's campaign has $2.5 million (with roughly $1.5 million coming from PAC contributions).8 Across the state, analysts told WRAL the results reflect the electorate's displeasure with Trump's tariffs and the war with Iran9.

The district

The district runs from Wilmington and the Brunswick County beaches on the coast to Hope Mills, Fayetteville, and the rural southern and eastern sides of Cumberland County10. It takes in all or part of eight counties. Some of those counties are split with other districts: the district covers more than three-quarters of the land in Cumberland County11, and Mark Harris and David Rouzer each represent a portion of Robeson County12. That means the county figures below, which come from the Senate race, cover more ground than the seat.

All eight counties moved at least 16 points toward Democrats from the 2024 presidential vote. The weighted average was 21 points, a little less than the state's 23. New Hanover, Wilmington's county, went from D+0.6 to D+22.4. Cumberland, home to Fayetteville, went from D+13.4 to D+35.1. The largest move came in Robeson, where generations of voters, including members of the Lumbee Tribe of North Carolina, reliably picked Democratic candidates13 before a political shift to the GOP over the past decade13. It went from R+27.7 to R+0.6, a 27-point move. Sampson moved 24 points, Pender 23, Columbus 20 and Brunswick 19. Bladen moved least, 16 points, to R+4.3. No county changed sides. In the House race, the Border Belt Independent reported, Rouzer carried six counties while Hardy prevailed in the urban Cumberland and New Hanover counties3.

New Hanover and Cumberland cast 197,878 of the 387,047 Senate votes counted in the eight counties. County totals cannot show whether a move came from voters switching sides or from a different mix of people turning out. WECT reported that more people voted early in North Carolina this election than in any other previous midterm election14, and that New Hanover was among the busiest counties.

What the result means

Rouzer has been through a recount before. He lost to Democrat Mike McIntyre in 2012, requested a recount, and conceded afterward in what was the closest House race in the country.15 McIntyre, a moderate Democrat, retired rather than run again in 2014, and Rouzer has won easily ever since.3 The seat is rated as likely Republican by the nonpartisan Cook Political Report.16

The count is not final. In each county, provisional ballots will be counted by Tuesday, November 10.17 County canvass is scheduled for 11 a.m. Friday, November 13. State canvass will be held at 11 a.m. Tuesday, November 24.18 The 1% margin for a recount is measured after that canvass, and the Nov. 17 deadline follows for the candidate trailing to request a recount.19 The Wilmington StarNews reported that Rouzer's campaign website has a button and a pop-up window inviting people to contribute to the "Recount Fund."20 Hardy has said it's important to "follow the law and make sure every vote is counted — and, if necessary, recounted."20

This seat will not decide control of the House. Democrats have already been called for 252 seats, 34 more than a majority.
