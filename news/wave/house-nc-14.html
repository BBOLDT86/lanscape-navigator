North Carolina's Tim Moore loses the seat he helped draw to a first-time Democrat

Lakesha Womack spent about $56,000 and beat the former state House speaker by 2.2 points. Every county in the district moved 20 to 25 points toward Democrats in this year's Senate race.

Lakesha Womack, a Democrat running for office for the first time, has unseated Rep. Tim Moore in North Carolina's 14th Congressional District. The Republican-led legislature redrew the seat in 2023 while Moore was speaker of the state House. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Womack had 147,388 votes to Moore's 140,973. That is a lead of 6,415 votes, or 2.2 percentage points. The Associated Press has called the race for her.

The race

Moore is a lawyer from Kings Mountain who was first elected to the General Assembly in 20021. He then spent a record 10 years as N.C. House Speaker2. Womack, a community organizer and ordained minister3, moved to North Carolina in 2016 to campaign for Hillary Clinton and never left4. WFAE reported that she grew up in Alabama, and said her campaign focused on the more rural parts of the district.5

The money was lopsided. The Election Hub and The Charlotte Ledger reported that Moore spent $1.9M on the race, with donations from the National Rifle Association, Blue Cross Blue Shield of North Carolina and the banking and utility industries.6 Womack, in contrast, spent just $56,000.6

Womack ran on everyday costs. Queen City News reported that her campaign focused heavily on affordability issues and lowering health care costs7. After the vote she blamed the administration's economic policies, gas prices and the war in Iran, and said voters saw Moore as a rubber stamp for President Trump. 8 WFAE reported that President Trump's record-low approval ratings gave Womack — and other long-shot congressional Democrats — hope.5

The final weeks turned on Womack's past. The Charlotte Observer reported that she was served with a criminal summons at a Polk County campaign rally in mid-October in connection with a felony charge dating back nearly seven years. The charge stems from a rental car that Womack allegedly failed to return in 20199. Womack has not been convicted in the case.10 Moore's campaign ran ads about the charge, and she called ads from "Friends of Tim Moore" about the charge "deceptive."9 WBTV also reported earlier this month that she purchased a home in north Charlotte, outside the 14th District, while her registered voting address in south Charlotte was listed for rent.10 Congressional candidates are only required to live in state and do not have to live in the district they represent.10

Moore sounded confident. On the first day of early voting, WUNC reported, he said he was knocking on doors "even though I probably wouldn't have to if I didn't want to."11

The district

The 14th includes all of Burke, Cleveland, Gaston and Rutherford counties, as well as portions of Mecklenburg and Polk counties12. It stretches from heavily Democratic areas of southwest Mecklenburg County into reliably Republican counties west of Charlotte.7

The Watch's wave map compares the 2024 presidential vote with this year's Senate race, in which Democrat Roy Cooper beat Republican Michael Whatley. On that map, all six counties moved between 20 and 25 points toward the Democrat, or 21 points weighted by votes. The state as a whole moved 23. These figures cover whole counties, not just the parts inside the district. Gaston County, which Trump carried by 25.0 points, came out effectively tied. Burke went from R+41.2 to R+18.0. Cleveland, Moore's home county, went from R+35.0 to R+13.4. Rutherford stayed the most Republican at R+27.5, and Polk narrowed to R+5.3. Mecklenburg went from D+32.9 to D+53.6. But it cast 439,955 Senate votes, more than the House race's 288,361 in total, so most of the county lies outside the 14th.

In the House race itself, Business North Carolina reported that Moore won all five more rural counties2 and lost Mecklenburg. WFAE reported that Womack rolled up huge margins in suburban parts of west and north Mecklenburg, and then cut into Moore's leads in the other counties.5

County totals cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from voters who stayed home. An Election Hub analysis found that Moore lost ground in every county13, with the largest shift in Gaston. It also found that Moore's vote fell much further below his 2024 total than the Democratic vote did. 6 That suggests turnout mattered, but it cannot show how many voters switched sides.

What it means

Democrat Jeff Jackson held the 14th until the 2023 redraw, which eliminate[d] a large part of Charlotte and heavily favor[ed] Republicans1. Jackson ran for attorney general instead. Moore won the new seat easily in 2024, and this year the race was rated "Solid Republican" by Cook Political report.12 He now leaves after one term. NPR counted the 14th among four North Carolina seats Democrats flipped, all of them in districts drawn to favor Republican candidates14. Statewide, Cooper won by 19.6 points in a state Trump carried by 3.2. Nationally, Democrats have been called the winners of 252 House seats, more than the 218 needed for a majority, and 22 races are still uncalled.

Moore told Business North Carolina that he was disappointed and congratulated Womack. "I sincerely wish her success, because when our communities succeed, we all succeed," Moore said.2

The count is not finished. On election night, the Election Hub described Womack's lead as seemingly insurmountable15, with provisional and overseas ballots still to be counted. All 100 county boards of elections are scheduled to complete their canvasses on Nov. 13. The North Carolina State Board of Elections is scheduled to certify statewide results on Nov. 24.16 Womack's court case is also still open: The Hill reported that she is still facing felony charges for allegedly not returning a rental car in 2019.17
