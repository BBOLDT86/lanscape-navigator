Van Epps holds Tennessee's redrawn 7th District by 5,233 votes

Republican Matt Van Epps won his first full term by 2.2 points over Democrat Darden Copeland, while all eight counties in the district moved between 14 and 19 points toward Democrats on the statewide vote.

Matt Van Epps has kept Tennessee's 7th District, but under new boundaries and by a much closer margin than his December 2025 special-election win1. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, AP has the Republican at 121,100 votes, or 50.3 percent. Democrat Darden Copeland has 115,867, or 48.1 percent. That is a gap of 5,233 votes, or 2.2 points. Two independents, Andrew Koontz and Lowell Reynolds, drew 3,919 votes between them. AP has called the race for Van Epps. The Nashville Banner reported that Copeland conceded at 10 p.m., after an introduction from Nashville Mayor Freddie O'Connell2.

The race

Van Epps is 43, grew up in Ohio and lives in Nashville. He served in the Army National Guard and was a commissioner of the Tennessee Department of General Services3. He won the seat in a 2025 special election after Mark Green retired, beating Democratic state Rep. Aftyn Behn of Nashville4. In August's primary he ran unopposed5.

Copeland, a corporate lobbyist and Nashville business owner6, had lost the 2025 Democratic primary to Behn7. This time he billed himself, in the Nashville Scene's account, as a "different kind of Democrat" in an attempt to appeal to rural, middle- and working-class voters8. He told NewsChannel 5 the new seat "really is a toss-up district, and I'm simply a moderate."9

Van Epps ran on his record in Congress and on Republican policies on taxes, costs and security10. He said a Trump rally in Clarksville energized Republican voters10. He argued that Copeland was not the moderate he claimed to be, since he had backed Behn once she became the nominee11. He also accused Copeland of supporting abortion without restrictions late in pregnancy, which Copeland strongly denied12.

Copeland went after Van Epps' votes against congressional efforts to restrict U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran12. In October he asked the Office of Congressional Conduct to investigate whether Van Epps' senior adviser Alex Joyner, a former campaign manager, had done campaign work while on the House payroll13. Van Epps' office called the complaint misleading and a political attack14. Neither candidate agreed to a debate9. Before the election, the Nashville Scene said voters were unusually engaged because of an unpopular president, anger over consumer prices and the war in Iran15.

The district

Tennessee's Republican supermajority redrew the congressional map in May, hoping to win all nine seats. The new lines made the 7th less Republican.16 Davidson County is still split three ways, now among the 4th, 6th and 7th districts17. The new 7th takes in most of Davidson County; all of Cheatham, Robertson, Dickson, Macon and Trousdale counties; and most of Sumner and Montgomery counties8.

On the wave map, which measures each whole county's statewide vote, every one of the eight counties moved toward Democrats. Montgomery County, home to Clarksville, went from Trump +18 in 2024 to D+0.1. It was the only county in the district to flip. Cheatham moved the most, 19 points, from R+46 to R+27. Macon moved the least, 14 points, though it is still R+60. Davidson went from D+27 to D+45, and Sumner from R+42 to R+25. Weighted by votes, the eight counties moved 17 points, close to the 17.6-point statewide move in the Senate race. In the House race itself, the Tennessee Lookout, citing AP, reported that Copeland ran best in the Davidson portion and Van Epps did better in the other seven counties18.

A move is not the same as persuasion. Davidson cast 221,227 of the roughly 402,000 statewide-race votes in these eight counties. The data has no 2024 turnout to compare against, and a midterm electorate is not a presidential one. Local outlets reported that early voting ran well ahead of 202219. The county counts cannot tell how much of the shift came from voters changing their minds and how much from who turned out.

What it means

The 7th has a history of sending members to higher office. Marsha Blackburn held it from 2003 to 201820, and this week she won the governor's race by 2.5 points over Democrat Jerri Green. Sen. Bill Hagerty won re-election by 12.2 points over Marquita Bradshaw. Republicans held all but one of the state's nine House seats16. The exception was the 4th, where Victoria Broderick defeated Scott DesJarlais21.

No incumbent lost here. In a statement, Van Epps said "I believe in showing up, getting to work, and delivering results."16 He returns to a House where Democrats have 252 called seats to Republicans' 161, with 22 still uncalled. He also returns facing the ethics complaint Copeland filed8. About 1 percent of the expected vote remains, and results are unofficial until certified22.
