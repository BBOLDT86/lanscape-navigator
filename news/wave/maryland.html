Moore widens Maryland's Democratic margin, but the Eastern Shore holds firm

Gov. Wes Moore ran six points ahead of Kamala Harris's 2024 margin. The biggest swings came in the Washington suburbs and the western mountains. The Eastern Shore, where the redistricting amendment was unpopular, stayed put or moved toward Dan Cox.

In a rematch of the 2022 race that made him Maryland's first Black governor and the country's third Black man elected to lead a state, Moore has won a second term1, again beating Republican Dan Cox. With about 98 percent of the expected vote counted, Moore led 1,460,213 to 682,141 in the Associated Press count. That is a 34.8-point margin, 6.1 points wider than Kamala Harris's 28.8-point win over Donald Trump in Maryland in 2024.

That is a modest swing for this year. Across the country the typical county moved about 14 points toward Democrats. In Maryland the typical jurisdiction moved 3.5. Twenty-one of the state's 24 jurisdictions (23 counties plus the independent city of Baltimore) moved left and three moved right. None switched parties from 2024. Capital News Service noted that Moore's win came amid a national Democratic wave that flipped governorships in Republican-led states such as Texas and Ohio. In deep-blue Maryland, the results looked much like 2022.2

The race was lopsided from the start. Maryland Matters reported that Cox reported raising $371,822 in his campaign finance report last month with the Maryland State Board of Elections, to just under $17 million reported by Moore.3 Moore largely focused his campaign on his efforts to combat cost-of-living woes and to fight Trump's agenda.3 Cox went after the state's high property taxes and electricity bills4 and called for a statewide moratorium on new data centers.5

The suburbs and the mountains

Montgomery County made the largest move in the state. It went from D+53.3 to D+64.5, an 11-point shift, and it cast more votes than any other county. Only 90 percent of its expected vote is in. Bethesda Magazine reported that the county's elections board still had ballots to count6 late last week, so this figure could still change. Prince George's County moved 8 points, to D+82.8, and Charles County moved 9. These counties depend heavily on federal paychecks. The state comptroller's office reported that the state had lost 24,900 federal jobs between January and November 2025, due to job reductions implemented by the Trump Administration.7 In a regional survey, 57% of county households said federal job cuts or furloughs hurt them over the past year.8

Frederick County is where Cox once served as a delegate, and it moved 10 points, from D+8.8 to D+19.2. Maryland Matters called the Adamstown area Maryland's ground zero for data center development9, and a UMBC poll found nearly 8 in 10 Maryland voters opposed the construction of data centers9. Cox ran on that issue, yet Frederick moved toward Moore. The county data cannot show what role the issue played.

Farther west, Washington County moved from R+23.0 to R+13.2, and Allegany County moved from R+40.3 to R+30.7. The Baltimore Banner reported that Moore spent time in the closing week on the campaign trail in rural, largely Republican areas of the state in hopes of running up the score10.

The Eastern Shore held

The Eastern Shore went against the trend. Dorchester County moved 2.5 points toward Cox, to R+17.1, the biggest move right in the state. Talbot County was tied in 2024 and went to Cox by 1.1 points. Somerset moved slightly right, and Kent was unchanged at R+2.8. Worcester, Caroline and Queen Anne's moved less than half a point. Capital News Service noted that two small Eastern Shore counties, Kent and Talbot, which he narrowly won in 2022 were picked up by Cox this year.2

The redistricting amendment, Question 3, appears to have mattered here. It passed statewide, but Question 3 was rejected in all nine Eastern Shore counties.11 Capital News Service found that opposition to Question 3 ran ahead of Cox's support in most counties there.2 Dan Schwartz was the Democrat who lost to Rep. Andy Harris on the Shore. He told columnist Dan Rodricks that the measure "drove Republicans and even independents to the polls"12. One Shore county broke from the pattern: Wicomico moved from R+5.5 to R+0.3, nearly even. Moore had campaigned on the Eastern Shore on Friday, including at First Baptist Church in Salisbury13.

Who moved: turnout more than persuasion

Counties are not voters, so these figures describe places, not people. Bigger places moved more. The three counties with more than 250,000 votes cast 44 percent of the state's vote and moved 7.7 points left as a group. The eight counties with 50,000 to 250,000 votes moved 4.9 points. The 11 counties with 10,000 to 50,000 votes moved 3.2. Kent and Somerset, the only two under 10,000 votes, moved 0.3 points right.

The movement was strongest in the most lopsided places. The five places Harris won by 30 points or more moved 7.8 points left. The four counties Trump won by 30 or more moved 5.9. Counties in between moved much less. Of the nine Trump won by 10 to 30 points, the typical one moved only 0.3 points.

The vote count gives a clue about why. Maryland cast 2,234,145 votes for governor, 74 percent of its 2024 presidential vote. Counties where turnout fell further tended to move further left; the correlation is -0.51. Talbot and Kent were the only counties that kept more than 80 percent of their 2024 vote. Together they moved 0.7 points toward Cox. Among the counties with the steepest drop were Allegany, Washington and Cecil, each down to around 68 or 69 percent of its 2024 vote. All three moved 6 to 10 points left. One reading is that many Trump voters in the west stayed home, while Question 3 brought Shore Republicans out. County totals cannot tell that apart from voters changing their minds, and Montgomery's turnout share will rise as its count is finished. Turnout was low across the board. The Daily Record noted that while national turnout surged, in Maryland turnout held nearly flat from the 2022 midterm.14
