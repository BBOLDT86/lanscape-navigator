Castor holds redrawn Tampa seat as Hillsborough swings toward Democrats

Florida Republicans redrew Kathy Castor's Tampa district to favor their party. She won an 11th term anyway, beating Mike Beltran by 5.4 points as Hillsborough County moved 12 points toward Democrats.

Kathy Castor won an 11th term on Tuesday in a Tampa district that was changed in ways designed to give Republicans an advantage1. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, AP's tally gives the Democrat 153,785 votes, or 51.2 percent. Republican Mike Beltran has 137,702, or 45.9 percent. That is a margin of 16,083 votes, or 5.4 points. Brian Lambert drew 8,746 votes. AP called the race at 8:13 p.m. Eastern on election night.

The race

DeSantis signed the new map into law on May 4.2 Florida Politics reported that it removed St. Petersburg and northwest Hillsborough County from her former constituency3. Beltran served in the Florida House of Representatives from 2018 to 20244. He was one of eight Republicans5 the new lines drew into the primary, and he won it. The Florida Phoenix noted that he ran a campaign ad aligning himself with President Donald Trump6 and calling himself "MAGA Mike Beltran." He also had Trump's endorsement7. The National Republican Congressional Committee identified District 14 as a target, while the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee placed Castor in its Frontline program2. WUSF reported that Lambert ran as the Libertarian5 nominee, though AP lists him as an independent. In late October, The Cook Political Report, Inside Elections, and Sabato's Crystal Ball rated the general election as a Toss-up4.

Much of the campaign was about health care, insurance and the president. Florida Politics reported that Castor warned that Trump's federal tax and spending legislation could threaten Medicaid coverage, while Beltran defended its work requirements3. A DCCC ad accused Beltran of siding with insurance companies and utilities during his time in Tallahassee7. According to the Tampa Bay Times, Castor was referring partly to a $1 billion state fund for property insurers8 that Beltran backed after Hurricane Ian. Beltran rejected the accusations and questioned Castor's own investments and contributions from political action committees.7 He also called the county's Rays stadium deal a "boondoggle"9. Coverage after the election said her campaign focused relentlessly on the affordability crisis and the president's unpopular decisions, like the Iran war.10

The Times reported that both parties spent millions flooding Hillsborough's airwaves with negative ads. Trump's midterm political action committee put almost $5 million into this contest11. Federal Election Commission reports showed Castor raising more than four times7 Beltran's total from July 30 to Sept. 30. In the final week, Florida Politics reported, citing AdImpact, that a Trump-aligned super PAC moved at least $750,000 in the Tampa television market12 out of this race. The money went to defending Rep. Anna Paulina Luna next door. On election night, Castor said special interests had spent $25 million to defeat me13.

The district

The new 14th lies entirely within Hillsborough County. It includes parts of Tampa and all of the Brandon-Riverview area through Plant City14, and MacDill Air Force Base15 stayed in. Castor's campaign said she kept just 35% of her previous constituency7. Registered Republicans now outnumber Democrats16 in the seat. Local outlets agree that Trump carried the redrawn district in 2024, though their estimates of his margin differ slightly 16, 17.

On The Watch's wave map, Hillsborough went from R+3.1 for Trump in 2024 to D+9.0 in this year's Senate and governor's races. That is a 12-point move toward Democrats, a little more than the statewide moves of 10.2 points in the Senate race and 11.8 in the governor's race. The county figure covers all of Hillsborough, including the parts in the 12th and 15th districts, so it is not this district's own swing. The 300,233 House ballots counted here equal about 58 percent of the 517,016 votes counted countywide.

A county count also cannot tell voters who changed their minds from changes in who turned out. Before Election Day, Florida Politics reported that voter turnout in Hillsborough County is strongly favoring Democrats17 even though Republicans had a registration edge. The site asked whether the seat would turn out to be a "dummymander," a map drawn for one party that ends up helping the other.

What it means

Castor was first elected to Congress in 200614, and she keeps the seat; no incumbent lost here. Bay News 9 reported that this was her narrowest victory since first being elected to Congress in 20061. Eckerd College political scientist Anthony Brunello told the station the result shows how redistricting can sometimes backfire1, and pointed to broader voter frustration with the political climate1. Castor's victory was one of several setbacks for Republicans in Florida's redrawn districts. Jared Moskowitz also won reelection, and Democrat Pia Dandiya won an open seat in a district reshaped to favor Republicans.18

Republicans still won both statewide races. Ashley Moody beat Angie Nixon for the Senate by 2.9 points, and Byron Donalds beat David Jolly for governor by 1.3. The Times reported that in Tampa Bay's congressional races, Republican incumbents held on to their seats by much narrower margins than in 2024.16 Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority, and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called.

WFLA reported that Mike Beltran conceded19, and WFTS reported that he says he plans to run again in 2028.20
