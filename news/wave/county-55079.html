Milwaukee County Makes Wisconsin's Biggest Move Toward Its Own County Executive

David Crowley carried his home county by 52 points on his way to the governor's office. That was the largest shift toward Democrats in any of Wisconsin's 72 counties, even though nearly a quarter fewer people voted than in 2024.

On Nov. 3, Milwaukee County backed the man who runs it. David Crowley, the county executive, beat Republican U.S. Rep. Tom Tiffany in the county 271,129 votes to 85,633, with 99% of the expected vote counted. That is a margin of 52.0 points. Two years earlier, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 38.5 points. The move of about 14 points toward the Democrats was the largest in any of Wisconsin's 72 counties.

What Milwaukee County did

Statewide, Crowley won by 8.0 points. That is an 8.9-point swing from Trump's 0.9-point win in Wisconsin in 2024. Seventy-one of the 72 counties moved left, and the typical county moved about 6 points. Dane County, home to Madison, went from D+51.7 to D+60.9, a move of about 9 points. Suburban Waukesha County stayed Republican but moved about 5. Brown, Racine and Winnebago counties, all carried by Trump, moved 8 to 10 points and went to Crowley.

Among big counties elsewhere that voted about as Democratic as Milwaukee in 2024, Milwaukee's move was larger than those in Contra Costa County, Calif., and Middlesex County, Mass. (about 5 points each) or Santa Clara County, Calif. (1 point). It was smaller than Cook County, Ill. (18) and Travis County, Texas (19). Nationally, the typical county moved about 14 points.

Milwaukee is the state's most populous county. The Census Bureau estimates 924,2161 residents in 2025, about 27% of them Black and 18% Hispanic, with non-Hispanic white residents a 46.6%1 minority. The largest city or town in Milwaukee County, WI is Milwaukee with about 563,500 residents2.

Why it moved

Crowley was running at home. Wisconsin Public Radio described his rise as an extraordinary ascent from a difficult childhood in one of Milwaukee's most troubled neighborhoods3. The Wisconsin Examiner reported that he focused on pitching his record of governing Milwaukee County4. Late in the race, NBC News reported, he brought in a series of high-profile Black surrogates with an eye on boosting turnout5, Harris among them.

Tiffany made that same record his main target. NBC reported that he and his allies went after Crowley for raising property and sales taxes as Milwaukee County executive and over statements he had made in support of undocumented immigrants.6 FOX6 reported that Crowley raised the county sales tax from 0.5% to 0.9%, the highest county sales tax in the state7, and that he blamed state underfunding and inflation. The Wisconsin Examiner noted that the property tax rate actually paid by individual homeowners has increased less than the rate of inflation8.

The statewide mood mattered too. A September Marquette Law School poll found that 45% of Wisconsin voters cited inflation and the cost of living as their most important issue9, and that 60% of voters disapproved of Trump's job performance.9 CBS News reported that Crowley successfully tied Tiffany to President Trump10.

The shift reached the county's suburbs. Milwaukee Magazine reported that Franklin, Hales Corners and Oak Creek, which it called the county's last three Republican suburbs, went for Crowley, joining Greendale and Greenfield among the communities that flipped blue during the Trump era.11

The county's count can't tell us how much of this was people changing sides and how much was a change in who voted. Most likely it was both. The governor's race drew 356,762 votes, 77% of the 2024 total and about 106,000 fewer ballots. Even so, Crowley's net lead of about 185,000 votes was larger than Harris's lead of roughly 178,000. Comparing against the last governor's race, Isthmus reported that county turnout rose over 2022 and that Tiffany received 11,838 fewer votes than Michels did in 202212. That fits Democrats turning out at higher rates and some Republican-leaning voters staying home, as well as some switching sides. The returns can't separate those effects.

What it means

The result sends a Milwaukee politician to the governor's office for the first time in more than a century. WisPolitics reported that Crowley is the first Milwaukee Dem to take the office since 1890.13 NBC reported that Crowley's base in Milwaukee County, along with support from Dane County6 gave him a critical boost statewide.

It also leaves the county's top job open. CBS 58 reported that Crowley stays county executive until he resigns or is inaugurated Jan. 4, 2027, whichever comes first.14 County Board Chairwoman Marcelia Nicholson-Bovell will then serve as acting executive, and an interim appointee will serve until a special election, which does not have a date yet.14 The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reported that Nicholson-Bovell plans to run in a special election to succeed Gov.-elect David Crowley15. The winner will serve the rest of his term, which expires in April 2028.16
