The wave · state by state · Virginia

All 133 Virginia localities moved toward Warner, from Manassas to the coalfields

Mark Warner won a fourth Senate term by about 23 points, 17 more than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin. The biggest swings came in Prince William County and its two cities, and in Southwest Virginia, where turnout fell most.

Virginia has 95 counties and 38 independent cities, and the state counts each city separately. All 133 moved toward the Democrats between the 2024 presidential race and Tuesday's Senate race. Sen. Mark Warner, a Democrat, led Republican Bert Mizusawa 2,188,657 votes to 1,378,325. That is a 22.7-point margin, with about 98 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris carried the state by 5.8 points, so the state moved 16.9 points. The typical county nationally moved 14.1.

The race got little attention. Cardinal News reported in September that it had taken a back seat to political dramas in Texas, Michigan, Maine, South Carolina and a handful of other states.1 The Washington Post described Mizusawa as a retired Army major general who had never sought elective office2 and noted that he did not receive help from national Republican groups.2 WTOP reported that there have been no debates between the two candidates3. It also reported Virginia Public Access Project figures showing Mizusawa had raised less than $400,000 through July 5, while Warner had more than $25 million.3 Warner has said this term will be his last.

The Virginia Mercury reported that Warner argued ending the war with Iran and reversing Trump's tariffs would offer the quickest relief from high prices4. Mizusawa argued Virginia would be better served with a senator Trump will actually listen to.4 Further down the ballot, Democrats flipped two U.S. House seats. Shannon Taylor lost to Democrat Shannon Taylor5 is how CBS put Rep. Rob Wittman's defeat in the 1st District, and Former Democratic Rep. Elaine Luria defeated GOP Rep. Jen Kiggans in Virginia's 2nd District5. Voters also approved amendments relating to reproductive rights, marriage equality and voting rights.6

Prince William and the suburbs

The largest moves among big localities came in the outer suburbs of Washington. Prince William County went from Harris by 18.0 points to Warner by 42.9, a 25-point move on 183,232 votes. Manassas Park, an independent city inside the county, moved 32 points, more than any other locality in the state. The city of Manassas next to it moved 26. VPM has described the House of Delegates district covering both cities as one of the state's most diverse, where Latinos make up nearly half7 of the population. Loudoun County moved 22 points to D+37.8. Fairfax County moved 17 points to D+52.0 and cast 470,209 votes, the most in the state, with 96 percent counted.

The AP noted that Virginia has some of the highest numbers of federal workers, who bore the brunt of President Donald Trump's efforts to slash the government workforce8. Warner told the Mercury he wished he had done more to prevent federal workforce cuts9 made by the Department of Government Efficiency.

The same pattern held elsewhere. Stafford County went from D+0.8 to D+20.3. Virginia Beach (D+17.9) and Chesapeake (D+18.9), both independent cities, moved 15 points each. Both are in or near the Hampton Roads district Luria won back. Outside Richmond, Chesterfield and Henrico counties moved 16 points each.

The coalfields, and eleven flips

The largest moves outside Northern Virginia came in the far southwest, though every locality there stayed Republican. Dickenson County went from R+62.2 to R+31.3, a 31-point move. Buchanan County moved 26 points, Russell County 23, and the cities of Norton and Bristol 25 and 24. Turnout in this area fell sharply. Buchanan cast 58 percent as many votes as in 2024, Russell 67 percent and Dickenson 71 percent, against 80 percent statewide. Before Election Day, Cardinal News reported that early voting in Southwest Virginia isn't coming anywhere close to meeting even its relatively low early voting numbers of two years ago10. Dickenson was the exception because of a commonwealth's attorney race driving the turnout.10

Eleven localities flipped from Trump to Warner: Spotsylvania, York, Fluvanna, Caroline, Nelson, Prince Edward, Lancaster, Essex and Surry counties, and the cities of Lynchburg and Waynesboro. Lancaster County, on the Northern Neck, barely made it. It moved 8 points, one of the smallest moves in the state, from R+7.0 to D+0.6. In Lynchburg, Democrats also won all three open city council seats, ending what was a 6-1 Republican majority11. The city's registrar told WSET that City Council and the amendments on the ballot were driving the turnout12.

The smallest moves came in the rural Piedmont and Southside. Charlotte County moved 8 points, Appomattox and Amelia counties 9 each, and Bedford County 9, where Mizusawa still won by 41.3 points. Rockingham County moved 10 points. Harrisonburg, the city it surrounds, moved 25, with only 89 percent of its vote counted.

Who moved: turnout or persuasion

These are counties and cities, not voters, so the counts cannot show who changed their minds and who stayed home. With that limit, the pattern is broad. Localities with under 10,000 votes moved 15.4 points, weighted by votes. Those with 10,000 to 50,000 moved 14.7, those with 50,000 to 250,000 moved 16.7, and Fairfax alone moved 17.1.

By 2024 result, the biggest move came in the 16 localities Harris won by 10 to 30 points, which cast a fifth of the vote. They moved 20.1 points; Prince William, Loudoun and Manassas are among them. The 51 places Trump won by 30 or more moved 14.5. The 15 most Democratic places moved 14.7 weighted, and the typical one moved only 11.3. Arlington, Richmond and Falls Church each moved between 8 and 11 points, from margins that were already above 58 points.

Every locality cast fewer votes than in 2024, which is normal for a midterm after a presidential year. Even so, the drop was uneven, and it lines up with the move. The correlation between a locality's turnout change and its move is -0.58: the more turnout fell, the further a place moved toward the Democrats. Three localities, including Buchanan and Norton, cast under 65 percent of their 2024 vote. They moved 23.8 points. The 44 localities that cast 80 to 95 percent moved 15.6, and the typical one 12.5. In the coalfields, that points toward Republican voters staying home more than toward voters switching sides.

Turnout does not explain the suburbs as well. Prince William, Fairfax and Loudoun each cast about 80 percent of their 2024 vote, close to the state average, and still moved 17 to 25 points. That leaves room for some voters there to have switched parties, but the counts alone cannot show it.
