In Florida's Pasco County, Moody wins easily but on a far smaller margin

Ashley Moody carried the fast-growing county north of Tampa by 14 points, compared with Donald Trump's 25 in 2024. The number of ballots fell to three-quarters of the 2024 count, and the Republican edge shrank by about 46,000 votes.

What Pasco did

Pasco County stretches north from the Hillsborough line through new subdivisions around Wesley Chapel to older towns along the Gulf. On Nov. 3 it gave Republican Sen. Ashley Moody a comfortable win. With 99% of the expected vote counted, Moody led Democrat Angie Nixon 135,516 to 101,306, a margin of 14.2 points. In 2024, Donald Trump carried the county over Kamala Harris by 25.2 points. That is a shift of 11 points toward the Democrats.

Pasco moved a little more than Florida did. Statewide, Moody won by 2.9 points, compared with Trump's 13.1, a 10-point shift. Pasco ranked 15th of the state's 67 counties by size of move. All but one of those counties moved left, and the typical one moved about 7 points. Pasco's neighbors went different ways: Hillsborough moved 12 points and went from Republican to Democratic, while Pinellas moved 6 and ended close to even. Other big Republican-leaning suburban counties moved by similar amounts. Macomb County, Michigan, moved 12 points and Suffolk County, New York, 11. Nationally, the typical county moved 14.

The kind of place matters here. Pasco ranked second within the region and fourth statewide, adding 106,000 residents—a 16.3% increase1 between 2020 and 2025, according to state estimates compiled by Plan Hillsborough. Bay News 9 has pointed to new housing, such as apartments and homes2 and new traffic around Wesley Chapel and Zephyrhills.

Fewer people voted this time. The county cast 240,319 Senate votes, 75% of its 2024 total. Moody's margin was about 46,000 votes smaller than Trump's. A county count cannot separate Trump voters who stayed home from voters who switched sides. These totals fit either explanation, and probably some of both.

Why it moved

Statewide reporting describes a contrast in campaigns. News4Jax said the race set Moody's emphasis on economic policies, law enforcement and her record in office3 against Nixon's focus on the cost of living, health care and help for families. Moody appeared to embrace the president until the end, campaigning alongside him4. Nixon is a state representative from Jacksonville who describes herself as a democratic socialist. She pulled off an upset victory against a more moderate opponent in the primary5, CBS News reported. The Florida Phoenix reported that Nixon kept bringing up Moody's role in the transfer of $10 million in Medicaid funding6 to the Hope Florida Foundation.

Money worries ran through the voter polling. In CNN's Florida voter poll, 85% of respondents said the cost of living was a crucial or major factor in their vote. Nixon won that group 53% to 46%7, while Moody won the rest by a wide margin. A University of North Florida poll published before the election found that most homeowners worried more about property insurance than property taxes, and that a majority of every partisan group still ranked insurance as the greater worry8. In the state Senate race covering southwest Pasco, Fox 13 reported, Democrat Jordan Hensley campaigned on rising rent, doubling insurance rates and stagnant wages9.

Turnout also played a part. Days before the vote, Gov. Ron DeSantis said, as Axios reported, that "I don't think the turnout is as strong as we would like to see,"10 and Axios noted that polls showed independents favoring Democrats by double digits. After the election, WTSP reported a University of Florida analysis finding that registered Democrats voted at a higher rate than registered Republicans statewide. The station added that turnout numbers alone cannot establish why particular candidates won or lost11. The Watch found no Pasco-specific exit polling, so who moved inside the county cannot be measured directly.

What it means for Pasco

The narrower margin did not cost Republicans in the county's local races. WTSP reported that Moody carried Pasco and Polk counties by wide margins on her way to victory11, and that Republican Byron Donalds won Pasco and Polk12 in the governor's race. The Tampa Bay Times found that Tampa Bay Republicans held strong on Tuesday despite a powerful showing from Florida Democrats, who broke the Republican House supermajority.13 Chris Nocco, Pasco's longtime sheriff, won the state Senate seat covering parts of Pasco and Pinellas. Republican Commissioner Seth Weightman also kept his seat, beating Democrat Stephanie Vazquez in the county's highest-spending race14, the Hernando Sun reported.

Analysts have not agreed on what Florida's results mean. A Florida Phoenix column argued that the national wave missed the state. It also cited analysts who saw a wave in the turnout: data consultant Matt Isbell called it the highest turnout for a midterm election for Democrats in living memory15. Pasco ended up somewhere in between. Republicans still lead comfortably there, by a margin less than three-fifths the size of the one they had two years ago.
