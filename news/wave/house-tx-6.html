Ellzey holds Texas' 6th by 4 points while Democrats sweep statewide

Republican Jake Ellzey beat Danny Minton, a first-time Democratic candidate who raised a fraction of his money. Every county in his redrawn district south of Dallas–Fort Worth moved more than 20 points toward Democrats.

Jake Ellzey, the Republican who has represented Texas' 6th Congressional District since 2021, won another term on Nov. 3. He beat Democrat Danny Minton 140,014 votes to 128,394, a margin of 4.3 points, or 11,620 votes, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race at 2:20 a.m. Eastern time on election night. Higher on the same ballot, Democrat James Talarico won Texas' Senate race over Ken Paxton by 12.1 points, and Gina Hinojosa beat Gov. Greg Abbott by 7.1.

The race

Ellzey lives in Waxahachie. He is a veteran who has held his seat since 20211 and was deployed nine times in his 20 years in the Navy before becoming a commercial airline pilot2. The Fort Worth Report described Minton as a surgical technology specialist and vice president of sales at Clarix Imaging who has never held public office1. He lives in Arlington, a medical salesman3 by KERA's description, and ran unopposed in the Democratic primary4. His campaign biography says he identified as a Republican out of loyalty to his roots5 for years.

The two campaigns ran on familiar platforms. Ellzey's website listed defending the Second Amendment right to possess guns, maintaining anti-abortion laws, securing the U.S.-Mexico border, protecting private property rights and cutting taxes1. Minton's priorities were making healthcare affordable, ending unwanted wars, maintaining a strong border and compassionate immigration policies, and protecting public education1. The money was lopsided. Filings through Oct. 14 showed Ellzey raised $5.5 million, spent nearly $2.6 million and had almost $2.6 million in cash on hand. During the same period, Minton reported raising $147,1891.

The statewide races mattered more than anything the two campaigns did. The Texas Tribune reported that Paxton's favorability remained deeply underwater, below that of Trump and other statewide Republicans6. NBC News reported that Texans were showing up in record-breaking numbers in many counties7 during early voting, that Latino voters swung back toward Democrats, and that Talarico focused on appealing to independents and Republicans unhappy with Paxton's history7. The AP described the national result as a drubbing that reached deep into reliable GOP territory8.

The district

The 6th was redrawn in 2025, when Abbott signed a bill enacting a new congressional map that shifted five Democratic districts toward Republicans9. As the Fort Worth Report noted, the district used to cover parts of Tarrant, Dallas and Johnson counties as well as Ellis, Navarro, Freestone, Anderson and Cherokee counties. Now the boundaries include only Tarrant, Dallas, Johnson, Ellis and Navarro counties4. It takes in all of Ellis and Navarro and parts10 of the other three. That puts suburban stretches of the Fort Worth and Dallas areas in the same district as Waxahachie and Corsicana.

On The Watch's map, those five counties moved between 23 and 27 points toward Democrats from the 2024 presidential vote to this year's Senate and governor vote, 24 points when weighted by votes. Ellis County moved furthest, 27 points, going from R+31.1 to R+4.5. The Dallas Morning News found that Ellis and Rockwall counties had an above-average shift toward Democrats despite Paxton ultimately winning. In Ellis County, that shift was in double digits.11 Tarrant County moved 24 points and flipped, from R+5.1 to D+19.2. Johnson (24 points) and Navarro (23) stayed firmly Republican at R+27.2 and R+29.1. Dallas County, already Democratic, moved 23 points to D+45.6.

These are whole-county figures, and the 6th holds only slices of Dallas, Tarrant and Johnson, so they describe the area around the district rather than its exact lines. They also cannot show why the counties moved. A county count cannot separate voters who switched parties from voters who turned out or stayed home. Tarrant cast 728,259 votes in this year's statewide vote and Ellis 88,524. KERA reported that Texas, including North Texas, saw record breaking numbers in voter turnout12 during early voting. The Houston Chronicle found that Tarrant County bucked the trend13, with turnout above its 2018 level.

What the result means

Ellzey first won the seat in a 2021 special-election runoff against Republican Susan Wright (the widow of Ron Wright and the endorsee of then-former President Donald Trump)14. At the time, the Texas Tribune wrote that Ellzey benefited from a more bipartisan appeal15. The next year he was re-elected unopposed in the general election2.

This time he won by a little over 4 points while his party lost statewide. The Watch's count does not include how Talarico or Hinojosa did inside the district's lines, so it cannot say whether Minton ran ahead of or behind them there. Ellzey stays in office, and the seat stays Republican, as Democrats take the House. As of Saturday morning, AP had called 252 seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled; 218 is a majority.

About 1 percent of the expected vote is still outstanding. That is far fewer ballots than Ellzey's 11,620-vote lead. The totals remain unofficial1 until counties finish their canvass.
