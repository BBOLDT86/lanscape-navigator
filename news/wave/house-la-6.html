Banks and Miguez head to runoff in Louisiana's redrawn 6th District

Democrat Chauna Banks finished first by 665 votes in a Baton Rouge-area district Republicans redrew in May. Blake Miguez edged fellow Republican Rick Edmonds for the other runoff spot. Every parish in the district moved toward Democrats in the Senate vote.

A three-way finish

Chauna Banks, a Democrat and former East Baton Rouge Metro Councilwoman1, finished first in Tuesday's open primary for Louisiana's 6th Congressional District. She won 50,878 votes, 19.4 percent of the 261,951 cast. Behind her came two Republican state senators: Blake Miguez, 665 votes back with 50,213, and Rick Edmonds, another 972 back with 49,241. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, the Associated Press had not called the race as of Saturday morning. But unofficial results on the secretary of state's website put Banks and Miguez in a Dec. 12 runoff, WAFB reported2.

On April 29 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in Louisiana v. Callais that the state's 2024 map, which created a second majority-minority district, was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander3. Gov. Jeff Landry suspended the spring primary so lawmakers could redraw the map4. The Legislature's Republican supermajority approved a new 6th District, and Landry signed it into law May 295. Rep. Cleo Fields, the Democrat who held the old district, chose not to run again and sought a state Senate seat instead6. The race also reverted to Louisiana's "jungle" primary, with every candidate on one ballot7.

Miguez, of New Iberia, first challenged Sen. Bill Cassidy, then switched to the 5th District, then qualified for the 6th on the last day8. President Donald Trump had endorsed him in February for the 5th District race9. WAFB reported in August that the White House had not extended that endorsement to the new race10, but WBRZ this week described Miguez as endorsed by Trump and the state Republican Party11. Edmonds, of Baton Rouge, attacked Miguez for living outside the district12. Miguez's side said Edmonds lives in Mississippi, where he is a senior pastor; Edmonds says he lives in Baton Rouge and commutes, the Louisiana Illuminator reported13.

Miguez had far more money. The Center Square reported he raised more than $6.6 million, nearly 13 times Edmonds's total14. WBRZ, citing federal filings, put the figure close to $8 million11. His ads cast him as an outsider running against "career politicians"14, and the Club for Growth kept its endorsement10. Both men ran as Trump supporters focused on immigration enforcement, government spending and economic development14. Miguez skipped the Oct. 9 televised debate, citing a scheduling conflict15. That left Edmonds as the only Republican there, defending Trump policies on immigration, the war in Iran and using National Guard troops against crime16.

Three Democrats split their party's vote. Lindsay "Rubia" Garcia, who had the state party's endorsement17, drew 33,544 votes, and Pat Forbes also ran. Banks climbed from third to first only late in the count, the Illuminator reported18. WAFB said Miguez secured his runoff spot on late East Baton Rouge Parish returns1.

The district

The new 6th is centered on Baton Rouge and reaches into surrounding communities and parts of the River Parishes2. It curls around the majority-Black 2nd District along the Mississippi River5. The Center Square described the redraw as turning a majority-Black district into a majority-white one19, and the Cook Political Report rated it solid Republican6.

The Watch's wave compares each parish's 2024 presidential margin with this year's Senate race between Republican Julia Letlow and Democrat Jamie Davis. All eleven of the district's parishes moved toward Democrats, by a weighted 20.2 points, the same as the statewide move. The biggest moves came in Republican suburbs and small towns. Ascension moved 25 points, from R+33.9 to R+8.9. Assumption moved 24 and Livingston 23, though Livingston stayed at R+45.1. West Baton Rouge (R+11.7 to D+10.5) and Iberville (R+0.7 to D+17.9) flipped. The smallest moves came where Democrats were already strongest: East Baton Rouge moved 18 points to D+28.7, and St. John the Baptist moved 18 to D+47.0.

Two cautions. First, the wave measures whole parishes, but the district takes in only portions of several18. The eleven parishes cast 354,710 Senate votes, 152,163 of them in East Baton Rouge, while the House race counted 261,951. Second, a move is not persuasion. Parish counts can't tell voters who changed their minds from voters who simply turned out. Red River Radio, citing the secretary of state, reported that turnout beat the 2022 and 2018 midterms20, and that more Democrats than Republicans voted early20.

What it means

When Fields won in 2024, it was only the second time in nearly 50 years that a Democrat had won the 6th, the AP reported21. No incumbent was on Tuesday's ballot. Statewide, Letlow won by 1.8 points, and the AP did not call that race until nearly all votes were in, the Illuminator reported22.

Added up by party, Republican candidates in the 6th led Democratic ones by 16.0 points. The Illuminator wrote that Miguez, as a Republican, will be heavily favored in the Dec. 12 runoff23, but Miguez himself warned conservative voters not to assume a win1. Until the runoff, the seat is one of 22 House races the AP has not called.
