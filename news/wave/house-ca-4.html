Mike Thompson trails fellow Democrat Eric Jones in California's redrawn 4th

With 54 percent of the expected vote counted, newcomer Eric Jones leads 14-term Rep. Mike Thompson by 3,801 votes in a Democrat-on-Democrat race shaped by Prop. 50's new Sacramento Valley territory. AP has not called it.

Four days after the polls closed, the congressman who has represented much of the current district for 27 years1 is behind. Eric Jones, a 35-year-old former venture capitalist with no political experience2, leads Rep. Mike Thompson 71,227 votes to 67,426 in California's 4th District. That is 51.4 percent to 48.6 percent, with 54 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press has not called the race.

Both finalists are Democrats, so the seat shows as D+100 on The Watch's House map and will stay Democratic whatever the final count. What's being decided is the 3,801-vote gap between the two men.

The race

Thompson, 75, is a Vietnam War veteran and senior member of the House's powerful tax-writing Ways and Means Committee1, CalMatters reported. Jones has never served in public office, and has lived in the 4th District for just a couple of years3, according to the Press Democrat. Jones placed second to Thompson in the primary, and ahead of Republican Raymond Riehle4.

Jones started out on the left, with the endorsement of Our Revolution, the progressive group founded by Vermont Sen. Bernie Sanders5. KQED reported that after the primary he ramped up his outreach to Republicans, hiring GOP strategist Rob Stutzman and winning the endorsement of Ray Riehle5. Riehle's website put the pitch this way: "In this district, a vote for Eric Jones is a vote against the Democratic establishment."6 Thompson, meanwhile, piled up endorsements from prominent Democrats such as Gov. Gavin Newsom and elected officials throughout the 4th District7.

Money set the terms of the race. Jones put $14.8 million of his own money into it, a California record for self-funding by a congressional candidate according to Sonoma State political scientist David McCuan; Thompson raised about $5.5 million5, KQED reported. Including outside groups, the Napa Valley Register counted $37.2 million8 and called it the most expensive House race in California. Thompson said Jones was trying to buy the seat, and Jones criticized Thompson for taking PAC contributions, singling out PG&E.7 The Press Democrat found that Jones' own ads pushed Thompson from the left, while those from New Leadership Now, a PAC backing the challenger, often hemmed him in from the right2.

The district

Proposition 50 made nearly half of the district new territory5. The new lines brought in conservative parts of the North State once represented by the late Republican Rep. Doug LaMalfa and cut out bluer areas of Sonoma and Lake counties5, KQED reported. The district now stretches from Wine Country to the Sierra Nevada foothills3.

The House vote has split along that line. The Press Democrat reported that Thompson leads in Sonoma, Napa and Yolo counties, which he represented before Proposition 503. The Napa Valley Register found Jones ahead partly because of his strength in recently added Republican-leaning areas6. McCuan told the Register that Republican voters might find Thompson closer to their views and still see him as a Democrat and an incumbent6.

The Watch's wave map tracks a different race: the governor's race, where Xavier Becerra (D) leads Steve Hilton (R) statewide by 30.4 points, a 10.3-point move from Kamala Harris' 2024 margin. Every county in the district moved toward the Democrat, by 8.1 points weighted by votes. The reddest counties moved most. Colusa County went from R+28.2 to R+11.0, Sutter from R+31.4 to R+14.6 and Yuba from R+25.8 to R+15.4. Lake County, which Donald Trump carried at R+1.4, went D+12.2 for Becerra, the only county in the district to switch sides. Sacramento and Yolo moved 10 points each. Napa and Sonoma, Thompson's base, moved 6 each, and Placer moved 3.

Two cautions apply. These are whole-county figures, and parts of Sonoma, Lake, Sacramento and Placer9 are outside the district. Sacramento County alone has counted 355,971 votes for governor, more than twice the House race's total so far. And a move is not persuasion. The counts are uneven: 74 percent of Colusa's expected vote is in, but only 36 percent of Yuba's and 35 percent of Lake's, where the 14-point move rests on 7,707 ballots. The numbers cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from differences in who turned out or whose ballots were counted first.

What the result means

Thompson has held elective office since 19904 and is seeking a 15th House term. KQED noted he has not sailed through the election with his typical ease5. If Jones' lead holds, Thompson would be unseated by a member of his own party. Patch noted that the loss could cost the district a senior voice on tax policy10. Control of the House doesn't turn on this seat: Democrats hold 252 called seats to Republicans' 161, with 22 still uncalled, and this one goes Democratic either way.

About 46 percent of the expected vote is still out. The Press Democrat reported that the outstanding ballots are in both pro-Thompson and pro-Jones counties, which makes the final count hard to predict3. Jones said on election night he was confident the "outstanding ballots will move in our direction"4. Counties must certify by Dec. 3, and the Secretary of State certifies on Dec. 11.11
