In Fulton County, Ossoff widens Atlanta's Democratic lead on fewer ballots

Georgia's biggest county moved 14 points toward the Democrats between 2024 and 2026. That was close to the typical county in the state, while the suburbs around it moved further. North Fulton's Republican seats fell further down the ballot.

Fulton County, which includes most of the city of Atlanta, cast 457,118 votes for U.S. senator on Nov. 3, more than any other county in Georgia. Sen. Jon Ossoff, the Democrat, got 363,995 of them. His Republican challenger, Rep. Mike Collins, got 93,123. That works out to a 59.3-point Democratic margin, up from Kamala Harris's 44.9-point win over Donald Trump in 2024. The county moved 14 points toward the Democrats, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted.

What Fulton did

Statewide, Mr. Ossoff won by 18.0 points in a state Mr. Trump carried by 2.2, a move of 20.2 points. All 159 Georgia counties moved left. Fulton ranked 85th, almost exactly in the middle; the typical county moved 14.5 points. The big suburban counties around Atlanta moved more: Gwinnett by 26 points, Henry by 23 and Cobb by 19. Cherokee is still Republican, but it moved 18 points. DeKalb started even further left than Fulton and moved 13. The counties that were already lopsided had the least room to move.

Fulton looks a lot like other deep-blue big counties around the country. Kings County, New York (Brooklyn), moved 15 points and Cook County, Illinois, moved 18. Hennepin, Santa Clara and Middlesex moved between 1 and 5 points.

Fulton is dense and fairly well off. Census figures put its population at 1,098,791 in 2025, with a median household income of $95,292 against $80,734 nationally1.

Fewer people voted. Fulton's 2026 turnout was 85 percent of its 2024 turnout. Even so, Mr. Ossoff's lead was about 271,000 votes, roughly 30,000 more than Ms. Harris's lead of about 240,000, on some 78,000 fewer ballots. A county count can't tell how much of that came from people changing sides and how much from who showed up or stayed home. The move mixes both.

Why

The Georgia Recorder reported that Collins was peppered by a series of scandals throughout his campaign2. It also said he was probably hurt by his ties to Mr. Trump, whose approval rating is at a low point in Georgia as voters struggle with high gas prices and inflation2. WABE, Atlanta's public radio station, said Mr. Collins had to answer for a House ethics investigation and his son-in-law's antisemitic social media posts3. WABE also reported that he and Collins clashed on tariffs, cost of living, healthcare and immigration3, and that Mr. Ossoff outraised Mr. Collins by more than 10-to-13.

The statewide surveys point to independents and white voters as the groups that shifted. In the ABC News Voter Poll, two-thirds of independents voted for Ossoff4. Four in 10 white voters backed Ossoff in this year's race, while 29% supported Warnock back in 2022.4 These are statewide numbers; no Fulton-only breakdown was published.

On turnout, an Atlanta Journal-Constitution analysis during early voting found that DeKalb and Fulton counties are seeing modest gains compared with 20225, the last midterm. FOX 5 Atlanta reported that Fulton voters turned out in force Tuesday to anchor major Democratic victories in Georgia6.

The vote came after a tense year for the county's election office. In January, the FBI seized original 2020 voting records while serving a search warrant at the county's Elections Hub and Operations Center7. The Associated Press reported that Mr. Trump said he wanted to "take over" elections from Democratic-run areas8. On Election Day, county spokesperson Regina Waller told WSB-TV that some voters reported getting text messages saying polling places had changed9. She said the county had not sent them and that she's also not received any reports of problems with voting equipment9. The AJC reported that the last memory card reached the county's election headquarters at 1:40 a.m.10

What it means

The biggest local changes came in north Fulton, the suburbs above the Chattahoochee River. Appen Media reported that Democrat Maggie Goldman beat Republican Commissioner Bridget Thorne, becoming the first Democrat to represent Fulton County's District 1 in more than 30 years11. The district includes the city of Johns Creek, roughly half of Alpharetta and a portion of East Roswell and Sandy Springs.11 Rough Draft Atlanta reported that Democrat Beth Fuller flipped the District 53 seat in the State House12, defeating Republican state Rep. Deborah Silcox.

The AJC reported that, based on preliminary results, the Fulton County Commission will have new faces in five of its seven seats next year13. Mo Ivory won the commission chair, becoming the first black woman and Latina to be elected12 to the post.

Keisha Lance Bottoms, Atlanta's former mayor, won the governor's race. Back in July, the AJC noted that in the primary her victory was 8 points smaller on her home turf, Fulton County, than her statewide margin14. The Watch's map follows the Senate race, so it doesn't show how she did in Fulton in November.
