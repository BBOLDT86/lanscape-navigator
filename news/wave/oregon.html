Every Oregon county moved toward Merkley, while Kotek ran 10 points behind him

Jeff Merkley beat Kamala Harris's 2024 margin by five points and carried Marion, Tillamook and Wasco, three counties Donald Trump won. On the same ballots, Gov. Tina Kotek won by less than Harris did.

All 36 of Oregon's counties moved toward the Democrats in this year's U.S. Senate race. None moved the other way. Jeff Merkley, the Democrat seeking a fourth term, led Republican David Brock Smith by 19.4 points, 1,081,831 votes to 719,976, with 88.7 percent of the expected vote counted. That is 5 points better than Kamala Harris's 14.4-point margin over Donald Trump in 2024.

That is a small move for this year. Nationally, the typical county moved about 14 points to the left. In Oregon the typical county moved 4.7. The governor's race went the other way. Gov. Tina Kotek beat Christine Drazan by 9.6 points, 4.8 points less than Harris's margin. On nearly the same number of ballots, Drazan got almost 100,000 more votes than Brock Smith did. Whatever moved Oregon this fall depended on who was on the ballot.

A safe seat and a hard one

Before the election, different outlets called the Oregon Senate race one of the safest in the country for Democrats.1 OPB reported that Merkley vastly outraised Brock Smith and was supported by a nationwide push among Democrats seeking to push back against the Republican congressional majority.2 Brock Smith is a state senator from Port Orford. According to KOIN, he ran on a platform of ending homelessness in Oregon, making the state more affordable and preventing data centers from driving up costs.3 Merkley would not debate him. KTVZ said Merkley called Brock Smith a candidate who is not prepared for the role.4 On election night Merkley told supporters in Portland, "Tonight is the night that we toppled Trumpism."2

The governor's race was a rematch of 2022, and the polls were close. A poll from OPB showed Drazan leading 47% to 46%, while the Oregonian put Kotek ahead 46% to 44% — both within the margin of error.5 Newsweek noted that in the OPB survey Trump was viewed favorably by 37 percent of likely voters, 1 point behind Kotek at 38 percent.6 Part of the burden was the gas tax. Kotek signed a transportation package that raised it, and in May more than 83% of votes tallied as of Wednesday morning were against Measure 1207, OPB reported. Kotek has made tying Drazan to Trump the center of her campaign.6 Drazan conceded the Oregon Governor's race to Tina Kotek8 on Friday.

Where the map moved

The biggest change on the map is in Marion County, home of Salem. Trump won it by 2 points in 2024. Merkley leads there by 5.4, a 7.4-point move. It is also the least settled result in the state. Only 65 percent of Marion's expected vote is counted: 95,126 ballots, against 155,732 cast in 2024. The Salem Reporter reported that the machine key to processing ballots in Marion County broke for around six hours, stalling the counting process9 on election night.

Two other Trump counties also flipped. In Tillamook County, on the coast, an R+1.4 margin became D+2.4. In Wasco County, around The Dalles, R+5.7 became D+0.7, a lead of less than a point with about a tenth of the expected vote still out. Jackson County, home of Medford, nearly flipped as well. It went from R+6.4 to R+0.2 on 101,226 votes. Deschutes County, around Bend, widened its Democratic margin from 10.1 points to 15.5.

The largest moves came in small, deeply Republican counties east of the Cascades. Gilliam moved 10 points on 978 votes, and Morrow moved 9. Both still voted Republican by more than 35 points. The smallest moves came at opposite ends of the state's politics. Benton County, home of Corvallis and Oregon State University, moved 1 point. Remote Wallowa County also moved 1. On the south coast, where Brock Smith lives, Coos County moved 3 points and Curry County 4, both below the state's typical move. Multnomah County, which includes Portland and cast the most votes, went from D+62.1 to D+66.7.

Who moved, and what the counts can't tell

Counties are not voters, and the charts beside this section compare places, not people. By size, the 10 counties with fewer than 10,000 votes moved 5.5 points, weighted by votes. Together they cast 2 percent of the ballots. The eight counties with 50,000 to 250,000 votes cast 56 percent of the ballots and moved 4.2. Multnomah moved 4.6.

By partisanship, the closest Trump counties moved the most. The six he won by less than 10 points moved 5.8 points and cast 17 percent of the vote. The four counties Harris won by 30 points or more cast 35 percent of the vote and moved 3.9. The 16 counties Trump won by 30 or more moved 4.2 weighted, though the typical one moved 5.1, because the smallest of them moved the most.

A move is not the same as persuasion. So far, 1,862,634 Senate votes have been counted, 84 percent of the 2024 total, and the count is not finished. Counties where turnout fell further moved slightly further left, but the correlation is weak (r = -0.27). Part of it comes from Marion, whose low share reflects uncounted ballots, not voters who stayed home. Before Election Day, DHM Research's John Horvick told Lookout Eugene-Springfield that turnout is slightly outpacing previous years, particularly on the Democratic side10. That fits a turnout story. The split between the Senate and governor's races fits a persuasion story: a large number of voters marked Merkley for Senate and did not mark Kotek for governor.

Three eastern counties moved more than the state as a whole: Morrow by 9 points, Umatilla by 6 and Malheur by 6. OPB has reported that all three are at least one-quarter Latino11. This report's data has no Census measures for Oregon, so it cannot test whether those residents explain the shift.
