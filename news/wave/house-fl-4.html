Bean edges Holloway in Florida's 4th as Duval swings toward Democrats

Republican Aaron Bean held Florida's 4th District by 2.1 points over Democrat LaShonda Holloway as Jacksonville's Duval County swung 12 points toward Democrats in the statewide races and Clay and Nassau counties moved less.

Aaron Bean is going back to Washington for a third term, but northeast Florida gave him a much harder race than before. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted as of Saturday morning, the Republican had 151,163 votes to 144,796 for Democrat LaShonda Holloway. That is a lead of 6,367 votes, or 2.1 points, out of 300,985 cast. The Associated Press called the race at 8:51 p.m. on election night.

Few people expected it to be close. The Washington Post noted the race was on few people's radars and was rated "Likely Republican" by the Cook Political Report1, and that Holloway had raised only $58,0001. WGCU, the public broadcaster in Fort Myers, wrote that Bean avoided a shock upset2.

The race

This was their third showdown for the congressional seat that Bean won in 20223. Bean is a Fernandina Beach native. He was mayor of his hometown in 1996 and was elected to the Florida Senate in 2012, where he served as Senate President Pro Tempore4. Holloway graduated from the University of Florida School of Law and Edward Waters University and worked on Capitol Hill for the late Congresswoman Carrie Meek4. Her platform centered on affordable healthcare, fair living wages, and veteran services5. In a Clay Today questionnaire, she pointed to rising costs for housing, insurance, groceries, prescriptions, electricity and child care, and said she would go after the reinsurance costs and flood-program problems driving up Florida premiums6. She also said her campaign refused PAC money6.

Bean campaigned alongside the top of his party's ticket. Clay Today reported that he campaigned with Republican candidate Byron Donalds in Green Cove Springs7 in October. After the race was called, the Florida Times-Union reported that he promised to keep working on "the rising cost of living, access to affordable and quality healthcare, and the safety of our communities."3 Two other candidates were on the ballot: Todd Schaefer, a no party affiliation candidate, and a candidate with the Florida Forward party8, Mike Sell. Together they won 5,026 votes, fewer than Bean's margin.

The district

The 4th covers Duval County west and north of the St. Johns River and all of Nassau County and Clay County8. That is how News4JAX's voter guide described it under the new state map, which redrew 20 of Florida's 28 U.S. House districts2. The Times-Union's David Bauerlein explained why the race was close. The district contains the part of Duval County that is the power base of the Democratic Party, while Nassau and Clay are overwhelmingly Republican3. The results followed that split: Holloway racked up a huge margin in the western half of Duval County but Bean was able to make up that ground by winning the Nassau and Clay county portions3.

The Watch's wave map compares each county's 2026 Senate and governor vote with the 2024 presidential vote. Weighted by votes, the district's counties moved 11 points toward Democrats. That is close to the statewide moves of 10 points in the Senate race and 12 in the governor's race. Duval accounts for most of the shift. Donald Trump carried it by 1.5 points in 2024, and this year Democrats carried it by 10.8, a 12-point move. Clay moved 9 points, from R+39.3 to R+30.1. Nassau moved 5, from R+46.9 to R+42.0. The Duval figures are for the whole county, including the eastern half that is in Rep. John Rutherford's 5th District seat that covers much of the eastern half of Duval County3.

A move is not the same as persuasion. Duval cast 356,347 votes in the statewide races, Clay 93,314 and Nassau 50,549. County totals cannot tell voters who switched parties apart from changes in who turned out. Reporting suggests turnout played a part. First Coast News reported that Duval elections supervisor Jerry Holland called the turnout a midterm election record for Duval County9 during early voting, and that registered Democrats in Duval County are out-voting registered Republicans9 in early ballots. The Jacksonville Free Press, citing preliminary county data, reported that turnout was better than in 2022 but lower than in 201810. Democrats' Senate nominee, Angie Nixon, is the Jacksonville resident and Florida House member11 who ended her rally in Jacksonville, which is her hometown12, after a day of campaigning. County totals cannot show whether her campaign helped Holloway.

What the result means

The seat came out of the last redistricting fight. Bean won his seat after the Legislature redrew the previous Congressional District 5, a majority Black district that included Jacksonville13. That district runs from northwest Jacksonville to west of Tallahassee14. Bean beat Holloway in 2022 and 2024, and the Times-Union wrote that the outcome in their third contest was much closer15. Clay Today reported that even in Clay, results were much closer than from the past7.

No incumbent lost here, and Republicans also won both statewide races: Ashley Moody took the Senate race by 2.9 points and Donalds the governor's race by 1.3. Nationally, 252 House seats had been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. That is past the 218 needed for a majority, so Bean will return to a House run by Democrats. About 1 percent of the district's expected vote is still to be counted, and Duval's statewide count stood at 98 percent. All figures are unofficial until certified.
