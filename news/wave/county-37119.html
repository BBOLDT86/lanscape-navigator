Charlotte's Mecklenburg County moves 21 points toward Cooper, about North Carolina's pace

Roy Cooper carried the Charlotte-based county by 54 points, 21 more than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin. Fewer people voted than in 2024, but Cooper's raw-vote lead grew, and local reporting points to Republican drop-off alongside a Democratic turnout push.

What Mecklenburg did

Mecklenburg County includes Charlotte, North Carolina's largest city, and six smaller towns. In the U.S. Senate race it gave Democrat Roy Cooper 333,549 votes and Republican Michael Whatley 97,841, with 99% of the expected vote counted. That is a 53.6-point margin. Kamala Harris carried the county by 32.9 points over Donald Trump in 2024, so the county moved 21 points to the left. All 100 North Carolina counties moved left, and Mecklenburg ranked 46th by the size of its move. Statewide the swing was 23 points: Trump won North Carolina by 3.2 points in 2024, and Cooper won it by 19.6.

Mecklenburg moved about as much as the state's other big counties. Wake and Forsyth moved 22 points, Guilford 20 and Durham, already the most Democratic of them, 14. It moved further than big Democratic counties elsewhere that started from similar 2024 margins: 12 points in Los Angeles and Cuyahoga (Cleveland), 14 in Wayne (Detroit) and Franklin (Columbus), and 17 in Fairfax, Va. The typical county nationally moved 14.

Turnout is the main caveat. Mecklenburg cast 439,955 votes, 77% of its 2024 total of 573,679, or about 134,000 fewer. Wake County cast about 83,000 more votes than Mecklenburg did. Even so, Cooper's lead of about 236,000 votes was larger than Harris's lead of roughly 189,000. The county count alone can't show whether that happened because Republican-leaning voters stayed home, because voters changed sides, or both.

Who turned out, and why

The Charlotte Ledger studied the results precinct by precinct and concluded that lower Republican turnout and suburban shifts fueled Democrats' landslide1. The early signs pointed that way. Michael Dickerson, the county's elections director, said 17,700 people cast ballots on the first day of early voting, up 61% from the first day of the 2022 midterm2. The Charlotte Observer, citing NC Election Live, reported that registered Democrats account for 48.2% of ballots cast3 during early voting, well above their 38.7% share of registered voters.

WFAE reported that more than 16,000 Hispanic voters cast ballots in Mecklenburg County during the early voting period, surpassing the total number of Hispanic voters who participated in the 2022 midterm elections4. That came nearly a year after federal immigration agents conducted Operation Charlotte's Web, arresting Latino immigrants across the city5. The Assembly found that urban counties with larger Black populations generally saw greater increases in turnout than rural counties, and Mecklenburg led the way6. It credited organizing by Black churches, historically Black colleges and the Black Political Caucus of Charlotte-Mecklenburg6.

The campaign itself kept coming back to Charlotte. Cooper focused his campaign on the slogan "Make Stuff Cost Less."7 Whatley made crime his central issue and pointed to the killing of Iryna Zarutska on the city's light rail. WSOC reported that one of his digital ads showed Iryna Zarutska's final moments on the light rail when she was stabbed to death8, although her family had asked that the footage not be shared. PolitiFact rated false Whatley's claim that Cooper bore 'direct responsibility' for Charlotte stabbing9. In early October, GOP groups, staring down double-digit polling deficits, yanked their funding and Whatley paused his television ads7, The Assembly reported.

The voter surveys cover the whole state, not Mecklenburg alone. CBS News exit polls found that Cooper performed better than former Vice President Kamala Harris did in 2024 among key groups, including independents and White women10, and that most North Carolina voters thought Cooper's opponent, Republican Michael Whatley was too supportive of President Trump10. ABC News' early voter polls suggested Cooper improved among college-educated voters, winning this group by over 30 percentage points11. A county with a large college-educated population would be expected to feel that shift, but the polls do not measure Mecklenburg directly.

What it means here

Democrats already ran Mecklenburg, and this election strengthened their hold. The Ledger reported that Democrats now control 18 of 19 state legislative seats, 10 of 11 City Council seats plus all nine county commissioner seats12. In south Mecklenburg, Ken McCool flipped this district by crushing incumbent Tricia Cotham13, who had switched to the Republican Party in 2023. That area was once a Republican stronghold. WRAL has described how voting maps showed a wedge shape in southern Mecklenburg County around several country clubs and posh neighborhoods14.

The county also mattered in two U.S. House races. Democrat LaKesha Womack unseated Republican Rep. Tim Moore in the 14th District. WUNC reported that Moore won the district's western counties but he lost by effectively a two-to-one margin in the district's 28 Mecklenburg County precincts15. As of Wednesday morning, Axios reported that the AP did not declare a winner13 in the 8th District, where Rep. Mark Harris was narrowly ahead.

For years Mecklenburg Democrats have struggled with low turnout. In the 2022 Senate race, WFAE found Mecklenburg County's turnout in the November election was 45% — below the state rate of 51%16. This year's early-voting surge was a break from that pattern, and the Ledger noted that researchers are still sorting out how much of the change came from who voted and how much from voters switching sides.
