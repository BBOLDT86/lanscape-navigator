Every Massachusetts county moved toward Markey, Bristol most of all

Edward Markey won a third full term by a wider margin than Kamala Harris had in 2024. The biggest moves came in the closest counties, especially on the South Coast, and in places where fewer 2024 voters returned.

Edward Markey won a third full term in the U.S. Senate on Tuesday by a wider margin than Kamala Harris carried Massachusetts by two years ago, and the margin grew in every county. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Markey led John Deaton 1,650,772 to 837,152. That is a 31.2-point margin, compared with Harris's 25.3-point win over Donald Trump. All 14 counties moved toward the Democrats. None changed hands, because Harris had carried all of them.

By this year's standards the shift was modest. The typical Massachusetts county moved 6 points, while the typical county nationwide moved 14. The biggest moves came on the South Coast and in the state's third-largest city, not in Boston or its suburbs. Bristol County, home to Fall River and New Bedford, went from a 1.3-point Harris edge to a 12.6-point Markey win. Hampden County, which includes Springfield, went from D+8.9 to D+18.2.

A safe seat, and a campaign about Trump

The Associated Press called the race for Markey just after the polls closed Tuesday night.1 Markey, 80, had already faced the harder test in September, when Democratic challenger Rep. Seth Moulton targeted Markey's age in a failed effort to oust him.2 Markey said then that this would be his last time running for office.2

Deaton is a Marine veteran and lawyer from Bolton who lost to Elizabeth Warren in 2024. WBUR reported that he launched his campaign by calling Markey "MIA" in Washington3, criticizing his age and blaming high housing, energy and child care costs. Markey's reply was mostly about the president. Boston.com counted that Markey invoked the president's name more than 40 times throughout the hour-long debate4, and he told viewers that Deaton would be "a blank check for Donald Trump."5

Deaton has said he never voted for the president6. The Boston Globe reported that he toured the state by bus, going to places as blue as Cambridge and Brookline6. Even so, a large number of voters, in the month prior to the election, said they didn't know who the second-time Senate candidate was.6 GBH News wrote that Deaton struggled with the broader challenge of being tied to a deeply unpopular president.7

Who moved: the close counties, and the ones that stayed home

The charts beside this section compare counties, not voters, so they show where the margin moved but not why. Three counties gave Harris less than a 10-point win: Bristol, Hampden and Plymouth. Together they cast 22 percent of this year's vote and moved 7.9 points toward the Democrats, weighted by votes. The seven counties Harris won by 30 points or more moved 5.3. Size mattered less. The seven midsize counties, with 50,000 to 250,000 votes, moved 6.8. The four largest, with 58 percent of the vote, moved 5.4.

Turnout lines up more clearly. About 2.6 million people voted for senator, 75 percent of the 2024 total. Eleven counties cast between 65 and 80 percent of their 2024 vote, and they moved 6.2 points toward the Democrats. The three counties that kept more than 80 percent, Barnstable, Franklin and Hampshire, moved 2.6. Across the 14 counties, the correlation between how far turnout fell and how far the margin moved is -0.67. Suffolk County cast 70 percent of its 2024 vote and moved 7 points; Bristol, at 73 percent, moved 11. Barnstable kept 81 percent and moved just 1 point.

Early-voting reports pointed the same way. NBC Boston reported that among large communities, Springfield had the lowest turnout during early voting8, with Lawrence, New Bedford, Lowell, Chelsea and Fall River8 just ahead of it. Cape towns such as Eastham and Orleans8 were near the top. Several of those cities had moved toward Trump: in 2024, the Globe reported, Trump increased his vote share significantly in majority-Latino communities in Massachusetts9, and CommonWealth Beacon noted that Fall River voters also narrowly sided with Republican President Donald Trump in the 2024 election10.

This evidence fits a story in which many of the less regular voters who leaned toward Trump in 2024 stayed home. But county totals cannot show whether a given Trump voter skipped the race or switched sides. Turnout is the stronger explanation in this data, but it rests on a correlation across only 14 counties.

Two Democrats, two different maps

The governor's race shows that the turnout story is not the whole picture. Gov. Maura Healey won on the same ballots by 27.2 points, only 1.9 points better than Harris. Markey won about 25,000 more votes than Healey, while Michael Minogue won about 74,000 more than Deaton. Boston.com reported that Minogue raised three times as much as Healey, including more than $12 million out of his own pockets.11 WBUR described a governor's race dominated by talk of affordability and President Trump.12

The difference was clearest on the South Coast, the region that moved most toward Markey. Boston.com listed Westport, Seekonk, Fairhaven, Dartmouth11 among the towns Minogue took from Healey's 2022 column. In Fall River, the Fall River Reporter found that Healey trailed the rest of the Democratic ticket13. The New Bedford Light called the region's results very much a mixed bag, and not at all part of the big Blue Wave14, and the Republican Jesse St. Gelais flips seat for the GOP15 in the 8th Bristol state House district. Some voters there chose Markey and the Republican for governor on the same ballot, which turnout alone cannot explain.

Statewide, Democrats swept the major races, and the state Republican Party's executive director, Haley Jones, described what State House News Service reported as a "blue undertow"16. Counting is nearly done. Berkshire and Hampshire counties are each at 98 percent of the expected vote, and every other county is complete.
