J.D. Ford ousts Victoria Spartz, flipping Indiana's 5th District to Democrats

A Democratic state senator beat the three-term Republican by 3.3 points in a seat redrawn in 2021 to favor Republicans. He campaigned on grocery, utility and gas bills and on her family's stock trades.

Indiana's 5th Congressional District will send a Democrat to Washington. J.D. Ford, a Democratic state senator, beat Republican Rep. Victoria Spartz 134,476 votes to 125,851, or 51.7% to 48.3%. That is a margin of 8,625 votes, or 3.3 points, with 99% of the expected vote counted. AP called the race at 1:03 a.m. Eastern on election night.

PBS NewsHour called it an upset that many analysts did not see coming1. Ford will be the first openly LGBTQ+ Hoosier to represent Indiana in Congress.2

The race

Ford, Indiana's first openly gay state lawmaker, has represented Senate District 29, northwest of Indianapolis, since 2018.3 Spartz was born in Ukraine. She is a certified public accountant and business owner who previously served in the Indiana Senate4. She had sought a fourth term5.

Ford ran on household costs. He told WFYI that voters across the district talk to me — gas, utilities, groceries3. He also pressed for protecting Medicaid funding, preserving rural hospitals, reproductive rights5. He called for requiring data centers to pay their own water and utility expenses, and challenging tariffs4. He also went after Spartz's votes on the war with Iran, and WFYI noted that Spartz voted no each time6 on House resolutions to pull U.S. forces out. Spartz campaigned on reducing federal spending, balancing the budget and increasing transparency and competition in health care5. She also took up data centers: Indiana Public Radio reported that she sent a letter to federal officials in September requesting an independent federal review of the LEAP District7. The two also split on last year's failed push to redraw the state's congressional map. Spartz supported the proposed new boundaries, while Ford voted against them in the state Senate.5

The campaign got personal. Ford questioned Spartz's financial disclosures and transactions involving her family. Spartz disputed the allegations and accused her opponent of misleading voters.8 The Indianapolis Star reported that Ford framed Spartz as corrupt9. Spartz, in turn, used his legislative record to pin the Democrat to unpopular positions on gender and police9.

Accounts of the money differ. WFYI, citing NPR's analysis of AdImpact data, reported Republican groups are spending $711,788 and Democratic groups adding $469,710 in the district10. The same report noted that Cook found the super PAC Invest in Tomorrow Coalition PAC, funded by Chris Larsen, is spending money against Spartz10. In the third quarter, the Star reported, Ford collected $667,369.72 from individuals and PACs while Spartz got $291,436.3111. Spartz also loaned her campaign $450,00012. The Cook Political Report moved the race from "Solid R" to "Likely R" in October, then on Oct. 30 reclassified the district from "Likely Republican" to "Leans Republican"10.

The district

The district stretches from Hamilton County's northern suburbs into communities including Anderson, Muncie, Marion and Kokomo.5 That covers Carmel, Fishers and Noblesville, the Madison County seat of Anderson, Muncie in Delaware County, Marion in Grant County and the Kokomo area. In the 2021 redraw, WFYI reported, the 5th District lost portions of Marion County and gained portions of rural counties to the north10.

Before the vote, IU Indianapolis political scientist Aaron Dusso told the Star: "If Ford doesn't win Hamilton County, he cannot win this district"11. On election night, Ford immediately took a decisive lead with an early vote drop in Hamilton County. Spartz never once took the lead as the votes were counted.9 Further down the ballot in the same county, Fox59 reported, house speaker Todd Huston was defeated13, and Charles Hollowell became the first democratic sheriff to win election in the county in 90 years13.

In her concession statement, Spartz blamed changing demographics in Hamilton County. She said there were not enough rural Indiana voters to offset those numbers14. The Watch's data for this seat does not yet break the vote out by county. For now, the map can't test her explanation or show how Anderson, Muncie or Kokomo moved. Even when county counts arrive, a move won't tell voters who changed their minds apart from a different group of people turning out. The 260,327 votes counted here are a midterm electorate. Fox59 reported that voting advocacy groups credit the victory to months of work13.

What the result means

Spartz is an incumbent who lost in a seat that was redrawn by the state GOP in 2021 to make it less competitive and more solidly Republican3. WRTV reported that the last Democrat to hold the 5th was Jim Jontz, who served in the U.S. House of Representatives from 1987 to 199314. The district's lines were different then.

AP's count still shows about 1% of the expected vote outstanding, and all results are unofficial until counties certify them. Statewide, Beau Bayh became the first Democrat to win a statewide office since 201215, and the GOP's 14-year supermajority in Indiana's House of Representatives was snapped15. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 not yet called. That puts Democrats past the 218 needed for a majority.
