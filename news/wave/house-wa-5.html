Baumgartner holds narrow lead in Eastern Washington rematch AP hasn't called

Rep. Michael Baumgartner led Democrat Carmela Conroy by 9,359 votes with about four-fifths of the expected vote counted in a Spokane-based seat Republicans have held since 1995. AP had not called the race Saturday morning.

At 8:29 a.m. Eastern on Saturday, Rep. Michael Baumgartner, the Republican who represents Eastern Washington, led Democrat Carmela Conroy by 9,359 votes, 140,011 to 130,652, with 941 write-ins. That is a margin of 3.5 points on 271,604 ballots, with 81 percent of the vote AP expects now counted. AP has not called the race.

The Spokesman-Review, Spokane's daily paper, has gone further. Its Friday evening story reported that Spokane Congressman Michael Baumgartner will serve a second term1. It noted that he led by less than a percentage point after Tuesday's count, but all later counts have leaned Republican1. Baumgartner told the paper that winning despite what he called a "Democratic hurricane"1 showed how hard his campaign had worked for the district.

A rematch in a hard year

The candidates were the same two who ran for the seat in 2024.2 Baumgartner was raised on the Palouse and graduated from Washington State University2. Conroy is a retired diplomat and former chair of the Spokane County Democratic Party1.

The first sign of trouble for Baumgartner came in the August primary. His result there was the worst result a Republican had seen in the district since 1992, the last year the district was won by a Democrat3. The primary also featured a relatively popular independent, Spokane firefighter Nate Powell3. Powell endorsed Conroy after his defeat in August3, though the paper said it was unclear whether she could win over enough independents.

Conroy ran against Baumgartner's votes backing President Donald Trump on tariffs, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the actions of Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents and the war in Iran1. The Spokesman-Review's issue guide found that Baumgartner has voiced concerns about the economic effects of tariffs but has voted several times against repealing them4. Conroy argued that Trump's use of tariffs hurt producers in Eastern Washington4. She also supports undoing the cuts to Medicaid4, a program he called "a broken system that costs too much and does not serve the most needy well"4. Baumgartner told voters to look at Olympia instead. He blamed Washington's climate policies for soaring gas prices and pointed to tax breaks championed by Republicans in D.C. and a recent bipartisan housing affordability package5. His ads called Conroy "too far left for Eastern Washington"5.

Trump endorsed Baumgartner in a September endorsement video filmed from the Oval Office6. The Spokesman-Review wrote that Baumgartner had made some attempts to distance himself3, including an ad pointing to his disagreements with the White House. Even so, the paper said, his campaign appears to have been weighed down by an unpopular president3. He had far more money: over $2.1 million raised as of the end of September, compared to $720,000 given to the Conroy campaign3. The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee targeted the race. Baumgartner raised money off that targeting, while Conroy raised money off Trump's Oval Office endorsement.5

Spokane against the countryside

The district covers eastern Washington, including Spokane and rural communities7, running from Oregon to Canada. It includes Walla Walla, Columbia, Ferry, Stevens, Pend Oreille, Lincoln, Spokane, Whitman, Garfield and Asotin counties, as well as parts of Adams and Franklin counties8.

On election night, Conroy was leading in Spokane, Walla Walla and Whitman counties, while Baumgartner had large leads in the remaining rural counties3. The Spokesman-Review called Spokane County the most important part of the district for Democratic hopefuls9. Conroy was still ahead there, but her lead in the county had shrunk since Tuesday.

The Watch's data for this seat do not yet include county-by-county counts, so the wave map's county moves are not shown here. Even with them, a count cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from shifts in who turned out. All the district total shows is 271,604 ballots counted so far, about 81 percent of what AP expects.

What it means

Republicans have held the seat since 1995, when George Nethercutt beat incumbent Democrat Tom Foley10, who was then speaker of the House. The Spokesman-Review called this the closest in at least 46 years9. The paper also reported that Conroy's showing is better than any Democrat who has run for the seat in more than three decades1.

No incumbent has lost here, and the count is not finished. On AP's estimate, about 63,000 ballots remain. To pass Baumgartner, Conroy would need to win them by about 15 points, and the count has been moving his way since Tuesday. Each county will report precinct results until the election is certified on November 24, 2026.11 Earlier in the week, Conroy said the race was too close to call, as votes were still being counted12. She did not return a call seeking comment Friday evening1.

Control of the House does not depend on this seat. AP has called 252 seats for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority, and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. Northwest Public Broadcasting noted that if Baumgartner wins his second term, he will find himself in the minority party12.
