Baumgartner holds Eastern Washington seat in closest race there in decades

Republican Michael Baumgartner won a second term in Washington's 5th District, beating Democrat Carmela Conroy by under four points in a rematch of a race he won easily two years ago.

Michael Baumgartner, the first-term Republican who represents Spokane and the rest of Eastern Washington, has won a second term, but by a much smaller margin than last time. With 89.88 percent of the expected vote counted, he has 154,299 votes to Democrat Carmela Conroy's 142,959. That is a lead of 11,340 votes, or 3.8 points, out of 298,335 ballots. Write-ins took 1,077. The Associated Press has called the race for Baumgartner.

The Spokesman-Review reported that the race was closer than at any time since at least 1980, when Tom Foley defeated John Sonneland1. Local outlets treated it as too close to call when counting ended on election night. After that, the paper wrote, all later counts have leaned Republican2.

The race

This was a rematch. Baumgartner was first elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 20243, and before the night was over, Conroy already declared she would seek a rematch4. Conroy is a retired diplomat and former chair of the Spokane County Democratic Party5. Baumgartner grew up on the Palouse and studied at Washington State University and Harvard. He first won elected office in the Republican wave of 20106, when he won a seat in the state Senate.

Cost of living dominated the campaign. KXLY reported that gas prices in Spokane remain over $5 per gallon on average7. Conroy blamed the Iran war and the tariffs for rising prices, and she criticized Baumgartner's votes backing policies of President Donald Trump on tariffs, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the actions of Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents5 and the war. Baumgartner backed the Iran war. He argued that Washington's state taxes make gas more expensive, and he has voiced concerns about the economic effects of tariffs but has voted several times against repealing them8. National parties were involved too: Baumgartner has taken to fundraising off the back of his race being targeted by the DCCC – as Conroy has fundraised off the back of Trump's Oval Office endorsement4.

Baumgartner had far more money. The Walla Walla Union-Bulletin reported he had roughly $1.7 million raised9, compared with almost $400,0009 for Conroy. The Spokesman-Review wrote that despite talk from state and national Democratic groups that the seat was in play, their money is noticeably absent from where their mouths are10. In the final weeks, several recent mailers in support of Baumgartner were paid for by the state party11. The paper found some ads on both sides misleading. Baumgartner said Conroy wanted to raise Social Security taxes, which is true, but only for high earners11. Conroy accused him of supporting tax breaks for data centers.

The primary complicated the race. Spokane firefighter Nate Powell ran as an independent, and a super PAC called Fire House PAC spent over $600,000 – almost all of it in July – to buoy Powell and sink Baumgartner12. Though Powell endorsed Conroy after his defeat in August, it remained unclear headed into election night whether Conroy could capture enough of the independent vote13.

The district

The 5th District is Washington's largest district in Congress14. It takes in all or part of 12 counties: Adams County, Asotin County, Columbia County, Ferry County, Franklin County, Garfield County, Lincoln County, Pend Oreille County, Spokane County, Stevens County, Walla Walla County15 and Whitman County. The Spokesman-Review named Spokane, Walla Walla and Whitman counties as the district's most Democratic areas and the remaining rural counties16 as Baumgartner's strongholds.

The Watch's data for this seat has the district-wide count but no county-by-county returns, so the wave map can't yet show how each county moved. Even with that breakdown, the count alone can't tell persuasion from turnout. The 298,335 ballots counted so far are a midterm electorate. Conroy's gain could come from voters switching sides, from Republicans staying home, or from both. Baumgartner offered his own explanation: some traditional Republican voters, he said, were frustrated that the president has been focusing on things, like renaming the Kennedy Center17. The returns cannot confirm or rule that out.

What the result means

The incumbent survived, and the seat stays Republican. It has been easily carried by Republicans since 199416, and 1992 was the last year a Democrat, former House Speaker Tom Foley, won the district4. Even in defeat, Conroy's showing is better than any Democrat who has run for the seat in more than three decades18, the Spokesman-Review reported.

Baumgartner will return to a House where Democrats have won 252 seats to Republicans' 165, with 18 still uncalled. He pointed out that he has experience working in the minority6. He called his win a result of hard work despite a "Democratic hurricane."

Counting is not finished. About a tenth of the expected vote is still out, which works out to roughly 33,600 ballots. To overtake Baumgartner, Conroy would need about two-thirds of them, a much larger share than the later counts have given her so far. The Hill reported that Conroy said in a video posted to Facebook that the race was still too close to call and that the campaign is working to cure ballots19. Results stay unofficial until certification, which Spokane County has set for 11/24/202620.
