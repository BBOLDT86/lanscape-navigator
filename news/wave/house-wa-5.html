Baumgartner holds Eastern Washington seat in a much closer Conroy rematch

Republican Michael Baumgartner beat Democrat Carmela Conroy by 4.3 points in Washington's 5th District, a seat Democrats lost in 1994. The early returns showed a near tie, and the ballots counted later went his way.

Rep. Michael Baumgartner, a Republican, has won a second term in Eastern Washington's 5th Congressional District. In the Associated Press count he has 164,742 votes to 151,062 for Democrat Carmela Conroy, a margin of 4.3 points, with about 95 percent of the expected vote counted. Conroy, a retired diplomat, conceded the race Tuesday after a week of ballot counts.1 The Spokesman-Review reported that she came closer to winning the seat than any Democrat since the seat was lost by Democratic Speaker of the House Tom Foley in 1994.2

The race took days to settle. On election night the candidates were nearly even. After that, the Spokesman-Review reported, all later counts have leaned Republican.3 Our results file logs the AP call at 8:37 p.m. Eastern on election night. The Walla Walla Union-Bulletin, though, reported that the Associated Press called the race on Monday, Nov. 94. That date matches how the count unfolded.

The race

This was a rematch. Conroy lost to Baumgartner in 2024 in the open race to replace former Rep. Cathy McMorris Rodgers5. Before Congress, Baumgartner served as Spokane County treasurer and, before that, a state senator representing the 6th Legislative District.6 Conroy has worked as a foreign service officer for the U.S. Department of State and as a Spokane County deputy prosecutor.4 In Washington's top-two primary in August, Baumgartner was the only Republican in a 12-candidate field and fell short of a majority. Conroy argued afterward that this showed the race was winnable1. The Spokesman-Review noted that the independent candidate, Spokane firefighter Nate Powell, endorsed Conroy after his defeat in August7.

Most of the campaign was about trade and health care. Baumgartner has voiced concerns about the economic effects of tariffs but has voted several times against repealing them.8 Conroy said tariffs had cut wine sales across the region and that rural hospitals rely on Medicaid reimbursements much more than urban hospitals.9 She strongly opposes the work requirements10 for Medicaid that Baumgartner supports. President Donald Trump endorsed Baumgartner in an Oval Office video, calling him a pivotal vote for the 2025 tax and spending package11. In the final weeks, a Spokesman-Review review of the ads found Conroy charging Baumgartner with backing tax breaks for data centers. Baumgartner's ads said Conroy wanted to raise Social Security taxes, which is true, but only for high earners.12

Baumgartner had far more money. His campaign raised over $2 million this cycle13, while her campaign raised only about a third of the money of Baumgartner's.2 In late October, RANGE Media counted more than $120,000 spent by the Congressional Leadership Fund in support of Baumgartner, and $25,000 spent by MAGA Inc.14 Conroy said she had to confine our spending to the Spokane media market, which covers about 95% of the district15.

The district

The district includes Walla Walla, Columbia, Ferry, Stevens, Pend Oreille, Lincoln, Spokane, Whitman, Garfield and Asotin counties, as well as parts of Adams and Franklin counties.10 It combines the Spokane area, the university town of Pullman and Walla Walla with wheat, timber and ranch country. The Spokesman-Review reported on election night that Conroy was leading in all of the district's most Democratic districts, including Spokane, Walla Walla and Whitman counties, while Baumgartner had large leads in the remaining rural counties.16 The Inlander reported that in later tallies she kept losing ground in Spokane County, where the majority of 5th District voters live.17

The data block for this seat has no county-level counts and no 2024 baseline. That means this piece cannot say how each county moved on the wave map, or how many voters turned out compared with two years ago. Even with those numbers, a county's shift would not show whether voters changed their minds or a different set of people voted. Statewide figures from the secretary of state's office showed the usual age gap in ballot returns, with the older the voter, the more likely they are to have voted.18

What the result means

The incumbent held the seat, and Republicans have now won it every time since 1994. Still, the Spokesman-Review called it the closest a challenger has come to unseating an incumbent Eastern Washington representative – without succeeding – since 1980.19 Baumgartner will return to a House where AP has called 256 seats for Democrats and 169 for Republicans, with 10 still uncalled. He noted Friday night that he has experience working in the minority.3

Counting is not finished. About 5 percent of the expected vote remains, and Spokane County lists its certification date as 11/24/202620. The concession also led to a dispute: Baumgartner said neither he nor his team had any record of Conroy calling1, and Conroy provided a screenshot of a congratulatory text. As for running a third time, she's not ready to commit either way.15
