The wave · the biggest counties · Wayne County, Michigan

In Wayne County, Detroit and Dearborn give El-Sayed Michigan's biggest swing

Abdul El-Sayed carried Michigan's largest county by 43 points. That is 14 points better than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin and the biggest leftward move of any Michigan county, with turnout about 14 percent below the presidential year.

What Wayne County did

In the Nov. 3 race for U.S. Senate, Wayne County gave Democrat Abdul El-Sayed 520,570 votes and Republican Mike Rogers 203,724. That is a 42.9-point margin with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Two years ago Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 29.0 points. The 14-point move to the left was the largest in Michigan. All 83 of the state's counties moved toward the Democrats, and the typical one moved about 6 points.

Wayne County is home to Detroit and about 1.77 million people1. Census Bureau estimates put its Black population at 36.8 percent, against 13.9 percent statewide1. The county has shrunk 1.4 percent since 20201.

The county carried much of the statewide result. El-Sayed won Michigan by 7.1 points, about 340,000 votes. Wayne alone gave him a net lead of nearly 317,000, though it cast only about 15 percent of the state's 4.8 million votes. Among its neighbors, Oakland County moved 4 points left. Macomb moved 12 points, cutting Rogers's lead there to under 2 points.

Outside Michigan, Wayne's move nearly matched that of Franklin County, Ohio (Columbus), at 14 points. It was ahead of Cuyahoga County (Cleveland) at 12 but well behind Wake and Mecklenburg counties in North Carolina, at 22 and 21. The typical county in the country moved 14 points, so Wayne's swing was about average nationally and the largest in Michigan.

A few precincts are still being entered. The county clerk's results page lists a Northville City early-vote precinct2 as outstanding. It also says Belleville, Sumpter Township and Van Buren Township results are being entered by hand after a ballot reprint2.

Who moved, and why

The county cast 738,765 votes, 86 percent of the 856,511 cast in 2024, or about 118,000 fewer. A county count cannot separate voters who switched sides from 2024 voters who stayed home. Wayne's move reflects both.

Turnout varied from place to place, according to local officials. Daniel Baxter, who runs operations for Detroit's elections department, estimated Detroit's turnout at 40 to 45 percent. Michigan Public reported that would be only a few points below the city's 47 percent in 20243. City Clerk Janice Winfrey said turnout was up about 21 percent from the 2022 midterms4. On election night, Dearborn Mayor Abdullah Hammoud said his city was nearing its all-time turnout record, set in 20205.

The Washington Post reported that Dearborn's Arab American voters, frustrated by U.S. support for Israel, had helped hand Michigan to Trump in 20246. This year Axios Detroit reported that El-Sayed ran far ahead of Harris's 2024 showing in Dearborn7. Speaking on NPR, Hammoud gave credit to El-Sayed's policies as well as his identity. He said voters were drawn by candidates who focused on the issues of working-class families8. The National reported that Trump's repeated mocking of El-Sayed's name had offended Muslim and Arab American voters9.

Detroit's Black voters were the other open question. CNN reported that Black voters in and around Detroit had mostly backed Haley Stevens10 in the August Democratic primary. Rogers opened a storefront campaign office on Livernois Avenue10 to court them. Two days before the election, Stevens endorsed Rogers after attending a service with him at a mostly Black Detroit church4. The Conversation wrote that the move may have pushed some Black voters to turn out for El-Sayed4. NPR reported that Rogers lost Detroit by a wider margin than he did in 202411. Preliminary data cited by The Conversation found about 86 percent of Black Michiganders voted for El-Sayed12, which it said was below some other Democrats' support.

El-Sayed also had local ties. He once ran the Wayne County and Detroit health departments13. Bridge Michigan reported that a county medical-debt program he championed has cleared over $95 million in bills for more than 100,000 residents14.

What it means for the county

Rogers did not lose everywhere in the county. Fox 2 Detroit reported he carried Grosse Pointe Farms, Sumpter, Huron, Flat Rock, Rockwood, Gibraltar, Trenton and Grosse Ile15, mostly white Downriver and lakeshore communities.

In much of the rest of the county, Republican gains from 2024 did not carry over to this race. Oakland University political scientist David Dulio told the Detroit News that Trump had gained among African Americans in 2024, mainly men and young men16. The same report found Rogers did better than polls suggested among Black voters, but not enough to matter16.

The result was not only about El-Sayed. NPR noted he ran about two points behind Jocelyn Benson, the Democratic nominee for governor, statewide11. The Detroit News reported Rogers had not called to concede as of Wednesday morning17. In Wayne County, El-Sayed won by holding together two groups who had split from each other in this year's politics: Black voters in Detroit who backed his primary rival, and Arab American voters who backed Trump two years ago. Whether those groups stay together without him on the ballot is something these returns cannot show.
