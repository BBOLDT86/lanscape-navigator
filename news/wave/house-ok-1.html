Tedford holds Tulsa's open House seat for Republicans by 4.6 points

Republican state Rep. Mark Tedford beat Tulsa school board member John Croisant for Kevin Hern's old seat as Tulsa County and its neighbors moved 14 to 15 points toward Democrats from 2024.

Republican Mark Tedford will represent Tulsa in Congress. He is a two-term member of the Oklahoma Legislature1 and runs Tedford Insurance, which is headquartered in Jenks2. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, he had 121,260 votes to 110,521 for Democrat John Croisant. That is a margin of 4.6 points, or 10,739 votes out of 231,781. The Associated Press called the race at 11:56 p.m. Eastern on election night.

The seat opened when Kevin Hern ran for the Senate. The Hill reported that it was Oklahoma's open Senate seat, which was previously occupied by Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin3. Hern won that race by 17.9 points statewide.

The race

The Tulsa World's Randy Krehbiel wrote that what began in April as a 13-candidate scramble for an unexpectedly open seat4 turned into a relatively quiet fall contest. President Donald Trump did not endorse Tedford at first, instead choosing to back Jackson Lahmeyer, a Tulsa pastor3. Tedford and Lahmeyer emerged as the top two finishers in the Republican primary5. NonDoc reported that Lahmeyer then withdrew from the race amid revelations of an inappropriate relationship with a former Miss Oklahoma1. Tedford later received an endorsement from President Donald Trump6, News On 6 reported. The Frontier noted that this was Tedford's first contested election. He ran unopposed in both 2022 and 20247.

Croisant is a former teacher and coach, as well as a businessman5, and sits on the Tulsa Public Schools board. He had been campaigning for two years, first running against Congressman Kevin Hern6. KRMG reported that Tedford campaigned on economic growth, government accountability, and border security, while Croisant focused on education, family issues, and affordability8. Croisant pushed for a debate. The Black Wall Street Times reported that Tedford's campaign did not answer9 its questions and that no news organization or civic group had offered to host a debate as of Oct. 19.

Tedford had far more money. The Tulsa Flyer reported that he raised nearly $2.5 million between January and Oct. 14, including more than $1.5 million in personal loans10. Croisant raised about $217,00010. After the loss, Croisant told News On 6 that his campaign was "outspent 10-to-1"11.

The district

The district contains all of Tulsa County as well as portions of Creek, Rogers and Wagoner counties12. That means the wave map's figures for the three outer counties include voters who live outside it. Tulsa County cast 191,607 votes in this year's statewide voting and makes up most of the district's electorate.

All four counties moved toward Democrats by nearly the same amount, and none flipped:
• Tulsa County went from R+15.2 in the 2024 presidential race to R+1.3, a 14-point move.
• Rogers County moved 14 points, from R+55.0 to R+40.8.
• Wagoner County moved 15 points, from R+49.8 to R+34.8.
• Creek County moved 14 points, from R+55.8 to R+42.1.

The weighted move across the four was 14.1 points. That is smaller than the state's move in either statewide race: 16.3 points in the Senate race and 28.2 in the governor's race, which Republican Mike Mazzei won by 6.0. The Frontier reported that Democrats also expanded their gubernatorial margins in Oklahoma, Tulsa and Cleveland Counties13.

A move on the map does not mean voters changed their minds. The county count cannot tell people who switched sides apart from changes in who voted. These were midterm electorates: 191,607 votes in Tulsa County, 32,251 in Rogers, 26,782 in Wagoner and 20,717 in Creek. Reporting suggests the mix of voters may have shifted. The Tulsa County Election Board reported record early voting that topped the ballots cast during early voting ahead of the 2024 presidential election14. The Tulsa World found that registered Republicans made up a smaller share of early in-person ballots than four years ago15. Statewide, News On 6 reported that turnout seems to have hit a new low16.

What it means

The Hill noted that the House seat has been held by Republicans since 1987 and was carried by President Trump in each of his presidential bids3. No incumbent was on the ballot. The Tulsa Flyer reported that Croisant got closer than any Democrat in 40 years17 to winning the seat. Ballots are still being counted, but the roughly 1 percent of the expected vote still out is much smaller than Tedford's lead.

All five of Oklahoma's House seats stayed Republican. NonDoc reported that Stephanie Bice (R-OK5) narrowly won reelection1. Nationally, the Tulsa seat is one of 161 called for Republicans. Democrats have 252, and 22 seats have not been called.

Hudson Harder, chair of the Tulsa County Democratic Party, told The Frontier that Croisant narrowly lost to Tedford even with much less money13. He added that "a little more investment in Tulsa will go a long way."13
