The House · New York 21

Trump-backed Constantino holds Stefanik's North Country seat by 1.9 points

Sticker Mule chief Anthony Constantino beat dairy farmer Blake Gendebien by 5,241 votes in Elise Stefanik's open seat. In the governor's race, the district's northern counties moved toward Republicans even as the state moved the other way.

Anthony Constantino, the Sticker Mule chief executive, will succeed Elise Stefanik in New York's 21st Congressional District. Donald Trump endorsed him after he put a 100-foot "Vote for Trump" sign on the roof of his building in Amsterdam.1 With 96.83 percent of the expected vote counted, Constantino had 140,428 votes. Blake Gendebien, the Democratic dairy farmer from Lisbon2 in St. Lawrence County3, had 135,187. That is a margin of 5,241 votes, or 1.9 points.

The race

The seat opened in December 2025, when Stefanik ended a short-lived campaign for governor and said she would not run for another House term.4, 5 Constantino then beat Assemblyman Robert Smullen in the June primary. Smullen was a retired Marine colonel backed by state Republican leaders and 12 of the district's 15 Republican county committees6. The Times Union reported that Trump's endorsement of Constantino proved crucial6.

Gendebien, a former vice chairman of the Agri-Mark dairy cooperative board7, presented himself as a bipartisan moderate8. He campaigned against Medicaid cuts he said would squeeze rural hospitals9. He also argued that tariffs raise costs for farmers and businesses on both sides of the Canadian border9. The Times Union noted that the region feels Trump's tariffs on Canada especially, because Canadian tourists matter to its economy8. An October Siena poll for the Times Union, WPTZ and WAMC had Gendebien narrowly ahead, within the margin of error10. It also found voters preferred him on every issue tested except undocumented immigration11.

Constantino ran as an open Trump ally. North Country Public Radio called that rare in a year when many Republican candidates kept their distance from the president.12 He accused Gendebien of posting bail for an undocumented worker from his farm and of dumping trash on his land8. A Gendebien commercial showed antisemitic and crude stickers printed by Sticker Mule. Constantino said liberal Democrats had designed them.13

Constantino paid for his own campaign and spent more than $14 million12, NCPR reported. North Country Now reported that Gendebien raised about $7.7 million14. Days before the election, the pro-Democratic House Majority PAC announced $1.7 million in ads for Gendebien.6, 15 On the other side, a super PAC tied to Trump's MAGA Inc. had spent $555,000 on ads for Constantino16, the Times Union reported, citing AdImpact. Vice President JD Vance held a rally for Constantino in Rome, the district's largest city,17 the day before the election.

The district

The 21st covers New York's far north, from Fort Drum in the west to just north of Albany18. It takes in all of 12 counties and parts of Jefferson, Oneida and Saratoga.19 The governor's-race figures for those three counties therefore include voters who live outside the district.

The Watch's wave map compares this year's governor's race with the 2024 presidential vote. Weighted by votes, the district's 15 counties moved 1.8 points toward Republicans. Statewide, Kathy Hochul beat Bruce Blakeman by 19.6 points, 7.0 points better than Kamala Harris did. Inside the district, the north and south moved in opposite directions. The northern counties swung toward Blakeman: Jefferson by 9 points, Lewis by 8, and Clinton, St. Lawrence and Franklin by 6 or 7 each. Essex, which Harris narrowly carried, went to Blakeman by 3.5. Closer to Albany, Fulton, Saratoga, Montgomery, Schoharie and Warren each moved roughly 3 points toward Hochul. Only Saratoga, at D+4.8, actually voted for her. The Siena poll had found that 60 percent of the district's likely voters viewed Hochul unfavorably20.

The House race did not follow that map. NCPR reported that Blakeman ran ahead of Constantino across the district, including in counties both men carried21. North Country Now reported that Gendebien led in Clinton, Essex, Warren and his home county of St. Lawrence, while Constantino was strongest in Lewis, Herkimer, Fulton and Oneida14. Blakeman carried Clinton by 9.5 points, yet it favored Gendebien for Congress. That gap means some voters split their tickets or skipped one of the races. The county counts cannot say how many. Nor can they say whether the moves since 2024 reflect people changing their minds or a different set of voters turning out. They show that Saratoga cast 100,543 votes for governor and Lewis 10,024, but not who stayed home.

What the result means

Republicans keep a seat that Stefanik first won in 201422. Spectrum News noted that Democrats last won it in a 2009 special election marked by infighting on the right23. The Cook Political Report moved its rating twice this year, from solid Republican to lean Republican.6 NCPR reported that Gendebien did better than any recent Democratic candidate3. Nationally, 252 House seats have been called for Democrats, 161 for Republicans, and 22 are not yet called. The 21st is one of the Republican seats that held. In his victory speech, Constantino told Gendebien's voters they had made "a big mistake"24.

The Associated Press called the race at 11:23 a.m.25 Friday. Gendebien conceded the same day, saying the ballots still outstanding were unlikely to change the result.26 About 3 percent of the expected vote had yet to be counted in AP's tally. Official results will be certified later this month.21
