Democrat Pia Dandiya wins Florida's 22nd, a seat redrawn to favor Republicans

Democrat Pia Dandiya, a former school principal, beat Naples businessman Casey Askar by 3.8 points in a seat Republicans redrew to flip, as all four of its counties moved toward Democrats.

Pia Dandiya, a Democrat running for office for the first time, won Florida's 22nd Congressional District1 on Tuesday. She beat Republican Casey Askar 153,177 votes to 141,939, or 51.9 percent to 48.1 percent. Her margin of 3.8 points comes to 11,238 votes, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race at 9:11 p.m. on election night.

Republicans redrew this seat hoping to win it. WLRN reported that the 22nd was among the four seats Florida Republicans are supposed to flip from the Democrats this year following their mid-cycle redistricting2. Under the new lines, it shifted from a district that voted for Kamala Harris in 20242 to one that backed Donald Trump.

The race

Dandiya is a former teacher and high school principal who most recently worked at Apple3 on public-sector projects. She dropped her challenge to Republican Brian Mast in Congressional District 21 to run in the new CD 22.2 Askar is a Naples businessman and Marine Corps veteran who built a career in restaurant franchising4. The Naples Press noted that he was born in Iraq and moved with his parents to the U.S. when he was 7.5 In a crowded Republican primary, Carbonara, Keiser and Askar have each invested millions of their own money6. After Askar won it, President Donald Trump on Wednesday endorsed7 him.

WPTV reported that both candidates built their campaigns around affordability 8, but they split on health care. Askar's website pledged to "end Obamacare once and for all" and replace it.9 Dandiya said her first priorities would include ending tariffs that she says raise prices for consumers and restoring Affordable Care Act subsidies.10 One Askar ad claimed she "supports higher taxes on Social Security."8 Her own ads accused members of Congress of getting rich off their office 9. The Naples Press wrote that Askar's party — and the president — are being blamed for higher gas prices, inflation and the war in Iran.5 In its statewide analysis, WFSU said Democrats in targeted seats were able to seize on voter disenchantment with Trump amid high gas and grocery prices.11

Askar paid for much of his own campaign. Florida Politics reported that he reported $7.42 million raised for his campaign, with $6.69 million of that coming courtesy of a candidate loan. Meanwhile, Dandiya raised $4.62 million12. Counts of the outside money differ by source and date. Florida Politics found that a super PAC aligned with House Speaker Mike Johnson spent $2.37 million on ads attacking Dandiya12. India New England News, citing federal records, reported that No Going Back PAC, a super PAC funded by MAGA Inc., reported spending approximately $6.5 million against Dandiya through October 24. Meanwhile, House Majority PAC, a major Democratic political organization, spent approximately $7.9 million opposing Askar.13

The district

The new district includes the cities of Wellington and Parkland, then sprawls further west into the rural Hendry and southern Collier County to include Marco Island.14 The Naples Press counted about 473,000 registered voters; about 182,000 are registered Republicans, 143,700 Democrats and 148,000 have no affiliation or belong to another political party.5 The Democratic data analyst Matt Isbell pointed out that it contains a large block of moderate voters2. He also noted that on the day it voted for Trump in 2024, the area backed the abortion-rights amendment 2.

The county numbers on The Watch's map come from the Senate and governor races, counted across each whole county. They are not the House vote, and most of Broward and Palm Beach lies in other districts. All four counties moved toward Democrats compared with the 2024 presidential vote, but none switched parties. Broward went from D+17.0 to D+32.3, a 15-point move. Palm Beach went from D+0.8 to D+10.3, about 10 points. Collier, Askar's home county, moved the least: from R+33.1 to R+28.8, about 4 points. Weighted by votes, the four counties moved about 12 points. Statewide, the Senate race moved 10.2 points and the governor's race 11.8.

Hendry made the biggest move, and the district takes in all of rural Hendry County2. People there told WINK News that agriculture, job opportunities and affordability are their biggest focuses15. The county went from R+38.3 to R+11.6, a 27-point swing, on 10,088 votes. WINK says the county has 19,680 active registered voters16. With so few voters, a change in who turns out can shift the margin as much as people changing their minds, and county totals can't tell the two apart. Gov. Ron DeSantis attributed the election results to a surge in Democratic turnout17. The returns alone cannot confirm that.

What the result means

The Palm Beach County district has been held for 14 years by Democrat Lois Frankel.2 This year Frankel dropped out of the race and ran instead in Congressional District 232, so no incumbent was on this ballot. The non-partisan Cook Political Report says the district leans Republican14.

The redrawn map targeted four seats held by Democrats, and the 22nd was one of them. In two of the others, Democratic incumbent Kathy Castor won reelection17 and Jared Moskowitz won reelection in House District 2517. Reports differ on the fourth. WFSU reported that Democrats were able to hold on to four heavily reworked U.S. House districts11. Al Jazeera, however, described the race between incumbent Darren Soto and Republican challenger Dan Green in Florida's 9th district1 as the closest of the four, with votes still being counted on Wednesday.

In the 22nd, 99 percent of the expected vote has been counted, and the results stay unofficial until they are certified. Nationally, as of Saturday morning, AP had called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.
