The Bronx swings 21 points back to Democrats, most in New York

Two years after the Bronx moved sharply toward Donald Trump, Kathy Hochul carried it by 67 points with fewer voters turning out, the largest move toward Democrats of any of New York's 62 counties.

What the Bronx did

Gov. Kathy Hochul, a Democrat, carried Bronx County, which is the same territory as the borough of the Bronx, by 201,864 votes to 40,316 over Bruce Blakeman, the Republican Nassau County executive. That is a margin of 66.6 points, with about 92 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris won the borough by 45.4 points in 2024. The 21-point move toward the Democrat was the largest of any county in New York.

Statewide, Ms. Hochul won by 19.6 points. Ms. Harris won the state by 12.7, so the state moved 7 points. In the rest of the city, Queens moved 20 points toward the Democrat and Brooklyn (Kings County) 15, while Manhattan (New York County) stayed about where it was. Only 31 of the state's 62 counties moved left at all. NYS Focus reported that the governor lost ground in 30 upstate counties despite making gains downstate and in the Hudson Valley.1 Among big counties elsewhere that Ms. Harris won by similar margins, the Bronx moved more than Cook County, Ill. (18 points), Fulton County, Ga. (14), Hennepin County, Minn. (3) and Santa Clara County, Calif. (1). It also beat the typical national move of about 14 points.

The Bronx is home to 1,406,3322 people, and Census figures show it is more than half Hispanic or Latino. Its median household income of $48,6762 is far below the national $80,734, and 28.7%2 of residents live in poverty.

Far fewer people voted this time: 242,617 ballots, or 67 percent of the 359,844 cast in 2024. A county's move blends two things, voters who changed sides and voters who sat this one out, and the totals cannot pull them apart. With a third of the 2024 electorate missing, the count alone cannot show how many Trump voters switched and how many simply stayed home.

Why it moved

The 2024 result is the starting point. Gothamist reported then that Mr. Trump narrowed his margin of defeat by more than 20 percentage points in the Bronx and Queens3 compared with 2020. The Bronx Times wrote that the shift upended traditional assumptions about how communities of color vote in America.4

This year that shift reversed. NYS Focus, which also found a 21-point gain over Ms. Harris in the Bronx, cited an analyst who said Black, Latino and Asian voters swung heavily toward Democrats this year after drifting toward the GOP in recent years.1 THE CITY reported that nearly all the Latino neighborhoods that made a strong showing for Trump in 2024 voted for Hochul5. It also named Bronx Park among the neighborhoods with a notable swing to the Democrats compared with 2022.

CBS News reported that both candidates' campaigns centered around affordability.6 Ms. Hochul campaigned in Concourse Village, where, ABC7 reported, she highlighted policies including a ban on cell phones in schools and expanded access to community college and child care.7 Mr. Blakeman toured the borough on Oct. 29. In the South Bronx he joined Pentecostal minister and former state Sen. Ruben Diaz Sr. at an event with conservative Latino voters.8 News 12 reported that he argued state policies are fueling crime that spills into the suburbs9. ABC7 noted that he embraced President Trump despite polling that indicates the president remains unpopular in New York state and particularly in New York City.8 He also called Mayor Zohran Mamdani, who campaigned for Ms. Hochul, his "secret weapon"10.

At a polling site in Mott Haven, voter Iris Aponte told News 12: "Groceries, health care, child care — all that needs to be fixed."11 National polls of Latino voters pointed the same way. UnidosUS found their top concerns were inflation, jobs, affordable housing, the cost of gas and healthcare12, with immigration sixth.

Turnout reporting cut both ways. Before the election, Bronx Democrats said a major challenge for Democrats in the borough is voter turnout.7 amNY reported that the Bronx was the only borough to record more early voters than in the 2025 mayoral election13. It also reported that registered Democrats made up a larger share of the borough's early voters than anywhere else in the city, and that those voters skewed old. Even so, News10 reported that the Bronx's 15th Congressional District had the worst turnout in both raw numbers and share14 of any district in the state, leaving aside Westchester's incomplete count.

What it means

Democrats held every congressional seat in the borough. The Bronx Times reported that the NY-13, NY-14 and NY-15 races were called for Darializa Avila Chevalier, Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Ritchie Torres.15 State Republicans are trading blame. amNY reported that Elise Stefanik called Mr. Blakeman's campaign "low energy"16. Party chairman Ed Cox blamed the national political environment rather than shortcomings in the campaign.16

For the Bronx, the result answers one question: in this midterm, with this smaller electorate, the 2024 move toward Republicans did not hold. It does not show whether the voters who went to Mr. Trump two years ago came back or stayed home. About 8 percent of the expected vote was still uncounted, so the final figures may still shift.
