Joe Wilson's 25-year South Carolina seat comes down to 2,926 votes

Republican Joe Wilson leads 25-year-old Democrat Zyon Khalifa by less than 1 percent in a seat he has held since 2001. The counties around Columbia and Aiken moved about 21 points toward Democrats. AP has not called the race.

Republican Joe Wilson has held South Carolina's 2nd District since 2001. As WIS-TV pointed out, that is the same year Khalifa was born.1 Four days after the Nov. 3 election, Wilson led Democrat Zyon Khalifa 147,791 votes to 144,865, or 49.8 percent to 48.8 percent, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The gap is 2,926 votes, just under 1 percent of the 296,664 cast. The Associated Press has not called the race. An independent, Dayna Smith, drew 3,674 votes, which is more than the gap between the two leaders.

The race

Khalifa, 25, is an Aiken native, Air Force veteran and attorney. He framed his campaign as a call for change, arguing that families are struggling with the cost of food, gas, housing and health care2, WIS reported. The Aiken Standard noted that he was the youngest nominee for a congressional seat in South Carolina history.3 He won a June runoff against David Robinson II, a disabled U.S. Army combat veteran4, who finished first in the June 9 primary5.

Wilson was seeking his 14th term6. He is a retired colonel in the S.C. Army National Guard and serves on the House Committee on Armed Services and the House Committee on Foreign Affairs3, and he focused on his record on national security, infrastructure and bipartisanship.1 In October the two met at a forum hosted by River Bluff High School's student government7 in Lexington. There Wilson attributed inflation to federal overspending and energy policy7 and emphasized his experience and support for President Donald Trump's policies.8 Khalifa argued that money spent on military operations could instead support Americans struggling with health care expenses, high gasoline prices8 and other bills. According to WFXG, he said: "When somebody says, 'Whatever the costs,' you are the cost."8

One campaign had far more money than the other. Khalifa told WIS that Wilson "spent about a million dollars compared to my $30,000."1 He told WJBF in Augusta that his campaign's ground game helped close the gap9 and that his roots in Aiken helped him connect with voters.10 Jon Parker, a conservative strategist, called it an unusually tight contest11 and told WACH that someone who has built as many positive relationships as Joe Wilson has "should've seen a better result."11

The district

The district includes all of Aiken, Barnwell, and Lexington counties and parts of Richland and Orangeburg counties3. It reaches from most of the Columbia suburbs12 to the area around Aiken. It also contains the Savannah River Site, the Department of Energy-owned facility south of Aiken12, which Wilson has long promoted.

The Watch's map compares each county's 2026 statewide vote with the 2024 Harris–Trump result. On that measure, all five counties moved toward Democrats, by 21 points weighted together. That is close to the state's 22-point move in the Senate race. Lexington County, the district's Republican anchor, made the biggest move: 26 points, from a 33.5-point Trump margin to R+7.7. Aiken County moved 21 points, from R+25.9 to R+4.5. Barnwell County flipped, going from R+15.5 to D+3.8. Richland and Orangeburg were already Democratic, and each moved about 19 points further. The figures for those two counties cover the whole county, not only the parts inside the district.

The five counties cast 384,001 votes in the statewide race. County totals cannot tell voters who switched sides apart from voters who simply turned out, and turnout was high this year. The Post and Courier reported the highest turnout in a non-presidential election since 199413. According to WLTX, Richland County's elections director said early voting numbers were about double what the county typically sees during a midterm election.14

What the result means

The seat had been in GOP hands without interruption since a 1965 special election.15 Statewide, the results were split. Democrat Annie Andrews won the Senate race by 4.5 points and, NPR reported, becomes the first Democrat elected to the Senate here since 199816. She beat Darline Graham, who was appointed to the seat after the death of her brother, longtime Sen. Lindsey Graham.17 Republican Alan Wilson, the congressman's son, won the governor's race by the same 4.5 points.

On the AP count, the House margin falls just inside South Carolina's recount rule. Under that rule, a recount is mandatory when the difference between any winning candidate and any other non-winning candidate is 1% or less of the total votes cast for all candidates for that office18. WACH reported Friday that the race is headed to recounts19, along with two state House races. It cited Khalifa, who said certified election results show Republican U.S. Rep. Joe Wilson ahead by 0.99 percentage points19. State officials plan to certify results, including those for U.S. House Districts on Tuesday of next week. Any necessary recounts will be ordered at that time18.

Khalifa told WJBF that his campaign had received reports of voters being turned away from the polls. He also says he will accept the final results.9 Wilson's campaign had not responded to a request for comment on the close race.1

Democrats have 252 called seats nationally, more than the 218 needed for a majority, so this seat will not decide control of the House.
