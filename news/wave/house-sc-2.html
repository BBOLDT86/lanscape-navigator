Joe Wilson keeps South Carolina's 2nd District by 2,840 votes after recount

Joe Wilson, a Republican who has held the Midlands seat since 2001, beat Zyon Khalifa, a 25-year-old Democrat, by one point. Democrats won South Carolina's Senate race the same night, and Khalifa spent a tiny fraction of what Wilson spent.

Joe Wilson, the Republican who has represented South Carolina's 2nd Congressional District for a quarter-century, won another term by 2,840 votes. He received 147,785 votes to 144,945 for the Democrat, Zyon Khalifa. That is 49.8 percent to 48.8 percent of the 296,737 votes cast, a margin of 1.0 point. Dayna Smith, a third-party candidate, drew 3,673 votes, more than the gap between the top two. There were 334 write-ins.

The race

Khalifa was an unusual challenger. At only 25 years old, Khalifa is the youngest nominee for a congressional seat in South Carolina history. Wilson has held the seat since the year Khalifa was born.1 An Aiken lawyer who ran track at the University of South Carolina, he won a June 23 runoff against David Robinson II, a disabled U.S. Army combat veteran, with 53.8 percent of the nearly 5,700 votes cast2, the Post and Courier reported.

The money was lopsided. The Post and Courier reported that Khalifa had raised only $29,039 during the campaign, while Wilson took in over $1 million.3 With so little to spend, Khalifa put ads on digital billboards and Roku, a streaming service, in the campaign's final weeks.4 His campaign, largely fueled by volunteer support, focused on courting Republicans and emphasized affordability and local infrastructure needs.3 Democrats running long-shot state House races handed out his literature along with their own as they knocked on doors.

WIS-TV in Columbia described the contrast this way: Khalifa centered his campaign on affordability and inflation. Wilson focused on his record on national security, infrastructure and bipartisanship.5 At an October debate held at River Bluff High School, Wilson emphasized his experience and support for President Donald Trump's policies.6 After the election, Khalifa credited GOP crossover votes and support from down-ballot candidates, as well as the country's broad leftward shift amid cost-of-living concerns and an unpopular president who endorsed Wilson.7

The district

The district runs from Columbia to the Georgia line. It includes all of Aiken, Barnwell, and Lexington counties and parts of Richland and Orangeburg counties.8 The Aiken Standard reported that Wilson won in Aiken, Barnwell and Lexington Counties. Khalifa won in Richland and Orangeburg Counties.8 The Watch's data for this seat has no county-by-county breakdown, so this piece does not report how each county moved. The statewide picture comes from our data. Donald Trump carried South Carolina by 17.9 points in 2024. This year Democrat Annie Andrews won the Senate race over Darline Graham by 4.5 points, a 22.4-point move toward Democrats. Republican Alan Wilson won the governor's race over Democrat Jermaine Johnson by 4.5 points, a 13.4-point move.

Lexington County, the Republican base of the district, saw some of the same shift. SC Daily Gazette reported that House Majority Leader Micah Caskey's loss in a Lexington County district considered deep red was arguably the most surprising9 of the 10 Republican state House defeats. He lost by 106 votes.

Margins alone can't show whether voters changed their minds or a different set of voters turned out. Turnout seems to explain part of this race. The Post and Courier reported that Wilson's vote total didn't shift from 2022, the last midterm election. "My view is that they got the vote out," Wilson said7. Khalifa pointed to people moving into the growing district and what he called frustration with Wilson's long tenure.7

What the result means

The seat has stayed Republican. It had been in GOP hands without interruption since a 1965 special election10, and the Cook Political Report had rated it solidly Republican11. ColaDaily reported that the result marked Wilson's closest congressional race since he first took office in 200112.

South Carolina law triggers a recount when the margin is within one point. The margin narrowed as provisional ballots were counted and the results were certified. State election officials on Tuesday ordered a retallying4, and the commission certified the recount results Wednesday morning, making Wilson's victory official.13 Our AP data puts the call for Wilson at 11:11 a.m. Eastern and labels it an election-night call. WLTX and WIS, however, reported that the AP had held off calling the race13 and made its call Wednesday, after the recount. Outlets reported slightly different final margins. This piece uses the AP count. All of the expected vote is in, and nothing in the race remains uncounted.

Khalifa isn't leaving the race behind. Democratic challenger Zyon Khalifa says he plans to run again in 2028.14
