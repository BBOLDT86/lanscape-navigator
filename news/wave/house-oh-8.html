Davidson holds Ohio's 8th by four points in fifth race against Enoch

Republican Warren Davidson beat Democrat Vanessa Enoch for a fifth time, by 4.3 points, as every county in his district moved 10 to 16 points toward Democrats and Ohio elected Democrats for senator and governor.

Warren Davidson, a Republican from Troy1, won another term in southwest Ohio's 8th District on Nov. 3. It was by far the narrowest of his five races against Democrat Vanessa Enoch. With 97.67% of the expected vote counted, Davidson had 168,689 votes to Enoch's 154,828. That is a lead of 13,861 votes, or 4.3 points. The Associated Press called the race at 11:03 p.m. on election night. The Cincinnati Enquirer noted that Enoch had run against Davidson four other times, losing each race by at 25 to 38 percentage points.2

The race

Enoch runs a consulting business, Cultural Impact, LLC, and lives in West Chester.1 Davidson has held the seat since a special election in June 2016, which was held due to the retirement of former House Speaker John Boehner.3

The Dayton Daily News reported that Davidson ran on his record and did not respond to questions about this race submitted by this news outlet.3 In a statement, he pointed to "a proven, conservative record that has delivered real results for our district"3. He also argued that Republicans had to keep the majority to build on "a secure border, low taxes, and efforts to detect, prosecute, and eliminate fraud."3 On Oct. 21 he spoke before Vice President JD Vance's rally at the Sharonville Convention Center2.

He does not always vote with his party. The Journal-News reported that Davidson has broken from his party around 15% of the time, according to votesee.org, including on Iran war powers, the GENIUS Act (crypto policy), and FISA reauthorization.4 He was one of four House Republicans who voted to curb President Donald Trump's power to wage war against Iran.4

One local fight ran through the rural north of the district. Davidson introduced the CHARGE Act in response to residents' concerns about a proposed transmission project that could include 765-kilovolt lines, with routes under consideration in Darke and Miami counties5. The Springfield News-Sun reported that the bill does not establish a deadline, identify a funding source or prescribe a particular research method.5 Enoch backed updated research but said the bill does not address broader questions about who will pay for new infrastructure, effects on water supplies and farmland5. She argued that economic development should pay its own way3, and her second priority protects healthcare, Social Security and Medicare.3

Higher on the ballot, Amy Acton beat Vivek Ramaswamy for governor by 7.9 points. Sherrod Brown beat Jon Husted by 4.9 points in the costliest race in Ohio history6, WVXU reported. Donald Trump had carried the state by 11.2 points in 2024. NBC4 in Columbus said Ohio's results came following a campaign dominated by frustration over living costs, the Iran war and President Donald Trump's leadership.7

The district

The 8th District was redrawn for this election. On October 31, 2025, the Ohio Redistricting Commission voted unanimously to approve a new congressional map.8 The Enquirer reported that the map moves some Hamilton County suburbs including Blue Ash, Evendale, Sharonville, Wyoming and Mount Healthy to the 8th District9, while western Hamilton suburbs went to the 1st District. WVXU described the new district as stretching from northern Hamilton County through Butler, Preble, Darke and a portion of Miami County10. The Enquirer called it a very Republican district, including the conservative suburbs of Butler County and rural areas of Darke and Preble counties.2

On The Watch's wave map, every county in the district moved toward Democrats in the Senate/governor vote. The weighted move was 12 points, smaller than the state's 16-point move in the Senate race:

• Preble County: 16 points, from R+59.1 to R+42.7
• Butler County: 16 points, from R+26.3 to R+10.3
• Miami County: 14 points
• Darke County: 13 points
• Hamilton County: 10 points, from D+14.9 to D+25.3, the smallest move

No county changed sides. These are figures for whole counties, not just the parts inside the district. Hamilton's 342,187 votes include Cincinnati, which is in the 1st District, and that one county cast more votes than the whole House race's 323,517.

Turnout was also up for local counties compared to the 2022 gubernatorial race11. The Journal-News reported that Butler County's early vote was the most the county has ever seen ahead of Election Day.12 WVXU suggested higher turnout might have boosted10 Enoch. Still, a move is not proof that voters were persuaded. The 2026 totals, such as 146,581 votes in Butler and 15,352 in Preble, show how many people voted. They cannot show whether the margins moved because voters changed their minds or because a different mix of voters turned out.

What the result means

The 8th Congressional District has been represented by Republicans for decades, and races have not been close.13 This race was close, but the incumbent held on. Davidson returns to a House where AP has called 252 seats for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority. Republicans have 161, and 22 seats are not yet called.

The count is not final. Election officials throughout the region will review provisional ballots, and outstanding military and overseas ballots remain11 before the official canvass. The Enquirer said that even though Democrats lost the seat, it shows the power of the blue wave that swept Democrats into office throughout Ohio14.
