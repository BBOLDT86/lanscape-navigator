Smokejumper Sam Forstag flips Montana's western House seat for Democrats

Democrat Sam Forstag beat Trump-endorsed radio host Aaron Flint by 3.5 points for the open seat Ryan Zinke left. He is the first Montana Democrat to win a House seat since 1994, and all but one of the district's counties moved left.

The race

Sam Forstag, a 32-year-old smokejumper and union leader from Missoula,1 beat Republican Aaron Flint 132,003 votes to 122,694. That is a margin of 3.5 points, or 9,309 votes, with 97.7 percent of the expected vote counted. Nick Sheedy drew 7,522 votes. AP's results list him as an independent, and Montana Free Press described him as the Libertarian2.

The seat opened late. On March 2, two days before the filing deadline, Republican Rep. Ryan Zinke announced that he would not run again. He cited3 health issues2. Flint, a talk-radio host and Army veteran from Kalispell, entered the race the same day and quickly lined up endorsements from Zinke, Gov. Greg Gianforte, Sen. Tim Sheehy, Rep. Troy Downing and President Donald Trump4. Forstag won the Democratic primary with more progressive messaging, with endorsements from Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Sen. Bernie Sanders5. NPR reported that he got into the race when the administration's DOGE team began cutting public lands agencies6.

Montana Public Radio reported that both men put affordability first. Flint's pitch was "keeping the crazy out of Montana," aimed at cities like Seattle and Portland7, while Forstag wanted to let anyone buy into Medicare7. The Flathead Beacon reported that Flint's ads called Forstag a "liberal lobbyist" from Portland and attacked his lobbying against bills barring transgender women from women's sports8.

The Daily Montanan reported that Forstag raised $4.3 million to Flint's $2.8 million9 through mid-October. Outside groups leaned the other way, with more than $1.2 million spent for Flint and about $250,000 for Forstag8. Montana Free Press found that the Trump-aligned No Going Back PAC spent $743,275 on mailers against Forstag10. Trump campaigned for Flint in Kalispell3, and Vice President JD Vance and House Speaker Mike Johnson also visited9.

The Daily Inter Lake reported that forecasters rated the seat likely Republican in March and that the forecasts moved toward Forstag as worries about the cost of living and the Iran war grew11. University of Montana analyst Lee Banville credited a well-run campaign and a favorable environment12. Montana State political scientist Kal Munis pointed to voters' dissatisfaction with Trump13 and a campaign that understood "the power of the ground game."13

The district

The district covers western Montana, including Kalispell, Missoula, Butte and Bozeman14. Most of its vote comes from a few places. Missoula and Gallatin counties cast about 116,000 of the roughly 264,000 votes counted in its 16 counties, or 44 percent. Flathead County cast another 52,000.

The Watch's map measures each county's move in the Senate race. There, the Democratic side is the independent Seth Bodnar alone, because he and the Democratic nominee, Alani Bankhead, split moderate and left-leaning voters15. Measured that way, the district's counties moved a weighted 8.2 points toward Democrats from 2024. The state as a whole moved 9.3 points.

The biggest moves came in small, deeply Republican counties: Granite (15 points), Mineral (13), and Madison, Powell and Ravalli (11 each). Missoula went from D+21.4 to D+32.1, Gallatin from D+3.2 to D+12.0, and Silver Bow County (Butte) from D+7.0 to D+16.6. Flathead moved 6 points. Flint had recently moved there, and Montana Free Press noted it is the county with the most Republican voters in the district3. Lincoln barely moved, by less than a point. No county changed sides.

Glacier County was the only county that moved toward Republicans, going from D+19.6 to D+12.1. It is home of the Blackfeet Nation, about two-thirds of the county's population16. Bankhead's votes are left out of the map's measure, so that number may say more about the three-way Senate race than about the county. Further down the ballot, Montana Free Press reported that Blackfeet Democrats Tyson Running Wolf and Thedis Crowe won their legislative races17.

These are net moves. The count cannot separate Republicans who switched sides from Democrats who turned out in larger numbers. With so much of the vote coming from Missoula and Gallatin, turnout there matters as much as any shift. NPR reported that Forstag credited his victory partly to crossover Republican votes6, but the county numbers can neither confirm nor rule that out. The Daily Montanan reported that statewide, this was the second-largest midterm vote on record, behind only 201818.

What it means

Forstag is the first Democrat Montana has sent to the House since Pat Williams in 199419. Zinke had held the district since 2023, after it was drawn following the 2020 census.2 No incumbent lost; the seat changed parties as an open race. Forstag will be the only Democrat in Montana's federal delegation19.

Flint called Forstag to concede around midnight.1 The AP called the race at 10:02 a.m. Mountain time Thursday20 (12:02 p.m. Eastern). AP has counted 97.7 percent of the expected vote. Gallatin (96.5 percent) and Ravalli and Deer Lodge (96.6 each) are the furthest behind. By AP's estimate, about 6,000 ballots are left, fewer than Forstag's lead.

Democrats have 252 House seats called nationally, past the 218 needed for a majority. On Montana Public Radio, Rob Saldin noted that even in a strong Democratic year, Forstag's margin was narrow, and in the half of the state that leans more toward Democrats21.
