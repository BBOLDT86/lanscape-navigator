The House · Ohio 9

Marcy Kaptur holds Toledo seat as northwest Ohio swings toward Democrats

The longest-serving woman in congressional history beat Republican Derek Merrin by 5.5 points in their rematch. The map had been redrawn to favor Republicans, and every county in the district moved toward Democrats from 2024.

Two years ago, the race between Marcy Kaptur and Derek Merrin was so close that the AP did not project a winner until Nov. 20, 2024, three weeks after Election Day1. This year's rematch was decided before dawn. The Associated Press called it for Kaptur, the Democratic incumbent, at 2:53 a.m. Wednesday2. With 98 percent of the expected vote counted, she had 159,306 votes to Merrin's 142,830. That is a margin of 5.5 points, about 16,500 votes out of 302,136.

She won on district lines that had been redrawn to make winning harder. On Oct. 31, 2025, the Ohio Redistricting Commission voted unanimously to approve a new congressional map3, and Toledo's WTVG reported that the 9th District was drawn to give Republicans a stronger chance at winning the seat4. Inside Elections found that only one Democrat would have carried the new district in a statewide race over the past decade: Brown in 20185.

The rematch

Merrin, a real estate investor and former four-term state representative6, ran with Donald Trump's endorsement. The Hill reported that he was part of the Republican House campaign arm's "MAGA Majority" program7 and was helped by Ohio visits from Trump and Vice President JD Vance. CBS News reported that House Speaker Mike Johnson, campaigning with Merrin in early October, called the rematch a "top-five race"8. According to WTOL, Merrin emphasized deporting illegal immigrants, requiring voter ID and being a new, younger representative2. Kaptur campaigned on being tough on data centers, updating Social Security and her long tenure2.

Kaptur had more money. Through June 30, her campaign reported raising approximately $4.3 million9, while Merrin's committees reported raising approximately $1.51 million10, WTOL reported.

Local reporters kept coming back to data centers. The Ohio Capital Journal reported that Merrin voted for Ohio's data center tax breaks as a state representative in 201711, while Kaptur argued the companies, not residents, should pay for the power and water they use11. Merrin told WTOL that Kaptur voted in 2021 for a federal bill that created a tax break for data centers12. He also pushed back on her ads blaming his vote on a scandal-linked energy law for high electric bills. He said the law had since been repealed and that he had co-sponsored the repeal12.

The issue came up all the way down the ballot. PBS NewsHour reported that Brown attacked Husted throughout the race over his support for AI data centers and his ties to a statehouse bribery scandal13. In Oregon, the Lucas County city east of Toledo, voters approved a ban on building data centers14, according to WTOL's results.

Toledo, the lakeshore and the farm counties

The district covers Toledo, surrounding northwestern communities and the Lake Erie shore east to Sandusky15, along with parts of Wood County2. Inside Elections described the new map as adding a few conservative inland counties5.

The Watch's wave map measures how far each county moved from the 2024 Harris–Trump vote to this year's Senate and governor's races. All ten counties in the district moved toward Democrats, by about 14 points when weighted by votes. Ohio as a whole moved further: 16 points in the Senate race and 19 in the governor's. Two counties flipped. Wood, home to Bowling Green, went from R+10.2 to D+4.6. Erie, around Sandusky, went from R+14.2 to D+1.0. Lucas, the district's anchor with 152,674 votes, moved 13 points, from D+12.6 to D+25.7. The biggest move, 18 points, came in Paulding County on the Indiana line, which still voted R+38.3. The smallest, 10 points, was in Putnam, still R+59.3.

Before the election, reporting from rural Ohio pointed to costs. PBS NewsHour wrote that the president's tariffs, trade war with Canada and the war with Iran that sent fuel prices soaring gave Brown a fresh opening with rural and working-class voters13. WOSU reported that farmers' better-than-expected profits would be offset by rising expenses, with many farm costs pushed up by Trump's tariffs16.

Still, a county moving toward one party does not mean voters changed their minds. The county count cannot tell a 2024 Trump voter who switched sides from one who stayed home. This year's totals, from 152,674 votes in Lucas to 8,931 in Paulding, come from a midterm electorate, not a presidential one. Lucas County election officials did report that early voter turnout for the 2026 midterm election has more than doubled17 compared with 2022.

What the result means

Kaptur, the longest-serving woman in Congress, has held the seat since 19838, and she begins her 23rd term in January6. WTVG reported that Republicans had seen the seat as one of their strongest pickup opportunities of 20261. Going into Election Day, the seat was rated as leaning Democratic by the nonpartisan Cook Political Report15.

Kaptur said voters gave her a clear win despite "a map statehouse Republicans redrew three times to make this one of the toughest districts in our country."1 In his statement, Merrin said his team "left it all on the field" and congratulated Kaptur on her victory1.

About 2 percent of the expected vote was still uncounted Saturday morning, but the AP has called the race. The seat is one of 252 the AP has called for Democrats nationally, against 161 for Republicans, with 22 races not yet called.
