San Bernardino County swings 21 points to Becerra after backing Trump

The Inland Empire county that narrowly favored Donald Trump in 2024 gave Xavier Becerra an 18.7-point margin for governor, the third-largest move in California, on a count about a fifth smaller than two years ago.

Two years ago, San Bernardino County, which runs from the suburbs east of Los Angeles deep into the Mojave Desert, gave Donald Trump a 2.1-point edge over Kamala Harris. On Nov. 3 it gave the Democrat Xavier Becerra an 18.7-point win over the Republican Steve Hilton for governor, 362,375 votes to 248,239, with about 97 percent of the expected vote counted.

What the county did

The 21-point move ranks third among California's 58 counties, and 54 of those counties moved left. Statewide, Becerra led by 31.5 points with 83.3 percent of the expected vote counted. That is 11.4 points better than Harris's 2024 margin, and the typical California county moved about 6 points. Riverside County, San Bernardino's partner in the Inland Empire, moved 17 points, from R+1.3 to D+15.7. Los Angeles moved 14, San Diego 8 and Orange 6. Outside California, Maricopa County, Arizona, which Trump carried by 3.5 points, moved 20. Duval and Hillsborough in Florida moved about 12 each, and Nassau, New York, moved 4. The typical county nationwide moved 14.

The county has about 2.2 million residents, roughly 56 percent of them Hispanic, and a median household income of $85,478, against $99,122 statewide1, according to Census Bureau figures.

The county counted 610,614 votes for governor, 80 percent of its 2024 total. A county's move mixes who turned out with who changed sides. With a fifth fewer ballots than in 2024, the county count cannot show how much of the 21 points came from voters changing their minds and how much from who stayed home.

Why it moved

Hilton, a former Fox News host, ran on the cost of living. The Associated Press reported that he pledged to abolish the DMV and cut gas prices to $3 a gallon2. He brought that pitch to a "Califordable" town hall in Colton in October. Inland Empire Community News reported that roughly 50 people attended3. Hilton had received an endorsement from President Trump4, and KPBS reported that he struggled to rid himself of the baggage5 of that endorsement. Becerra won to become the first Latino elected governor of the Golden State6, KTLA reported.

Immigration enforcement was a local story before the vote. KVCR, the Inland Empire's public station, reported in October on federal agents arresting three men outside the Rancho Cucamonga courthouse in April 20267. It also reported that the Department of Homeland Security launched enforcement campaigns in several parts of California, including San Diego, the Inland Empire and the Central Valley8. Secretary of State Shirley Weber warned that fear of being targeted could keep people away from the polls7. The county figures alone cannot show whether that fear kept voters home or brought them out.

The Watch found no exit poll for the county itself. A national UnidosUS poll of more than 3,000 Latino voters, conducted by BSP Research, found that seventeen percent of Latino Trump voters from the previous presidential election crossed over to Democratic candidates9. It also found that four out of 10 Latinos who voted in the 2026 mid-term cast their ballots for the very first time9. In that poll, cost of living and inflation9 topped voters' concerns. These are national figures, not San Bernardino County's, but they suggest the move here likely included both new voters and voters who switched sides.

What it means

At the top of the ticket, the county is back on the Democratic side after narrowly backing Trump. The San Bernardino Sun has long noted the limits of that label. It wrote in 2021 that Republicans can still win in the region, where Democrats tend to be more blue collar and socially conservative than Democrats in coastal counties10.

The clearest test is the 23rd Congressional District, which covers most of the county's high desert and mountains. Its Republican congressman is Jay Obernolte, who served on the city council and was the mayor of Big Bear Lake11. KQED reported Thursday that it was the only Democrat-versus-Republican contest that had yet to be called in California12, with Obernolte narrowly ahead of the Democrat Tessa Lynn Hodge. The Hill noted that DDHQ forecasted the seat was safely Republican13.

The count is not finished. The registrar's office says it will continue to process and count additional ballots in the weeks following the election14, and that results will be certified by the Registrar of Voters no later than December 3, 202614. Mailed ballots count if they were postmarked on or before Tuesday, November 3 and received by the Registrar of Voters within seven days following the election15. About 3 percent of the expected vote is still out, so the county's numbers may still shift slightly.
