San Bernardino County swings 19 points, from Trump to Becerra

The huge inland county voted narrowly for Donald Trump in 2024. With about 70 percent of the expected vote counted, Xavier Becerra leads Steve Hilton there by 17 points, one of the largest swings in California.

What the county did

San Bernardino County runs from the warehouse suburbs of Ontario and Fontana across the Mojave Desert to the Nevada and Arizona state lines. With an area of 20,105 square miles, it is the largest county in the contiguous United States by area.1 The Census Bureau estimates its population at 2,224,091 as of July 1, 20252. In that estimate, 56.9 percent of residents are Hispanic or Latino and 22.6 percent are non-Hispanic white.

In 2024, Donald Trump carried the county by 2.1 points out of 761,714 votes cast. In this year's race for governor, Democrat Xavier Becerra leads Republican Steve Hilton 245,935 to 175,286, a margin of 16.8 points, with 69.72 percent of the expected vote counted. That is a 19-point move toward the Democrats. Only three of California's 58 counties moved further. Statewide, the move was 10.3 points: Becerra leads by 30.4 with about 71 percent counted, up from Kamala Harris's 20.1-point margin. Fifty-four counties moved left, and the typical county moved about 6 points.

Neighboring Riverside County followed almost the same path, from a 1.3-point Trump margin to a 15.5-point Becerra lead, a move of 17 points. The coastal giants moved far less: Orange and San Diego about 5 points each, Los Angeles 12. Outside California, the closest match is Maricopa County, Arizona, which went from R+3.5 to D+16.2, a 20-point move. The typical county nationally moved 14 points.

The count is not finished. The registrar will count mailed ballots postmarked on or before Nov. 3 and received within seven days following the election3, and the results will be certified by the Registrar of Voters no later than December 3, 2026.4

Why it moved

Start with who voted. The 421,221 governor's votes counted so far equal 55 percent of the 2024 total. Even the full expected count would leave this electorate well short of the presidential year's. That means the 19-point move mixes two things the county numbers cannot separate: which voters showed up, and which ones changed sides.

Statewide polling points to national politics. The final UC Berkeley Institute of Governmental Studies poll, co-sponsored by the Los Angeles Times, found voters increasingly identified opposition to President Trump's administration as a top priority, ahead of concerns about the cost of living5, CBS Sacramento reported. The poll's director, Mark DiCamillo, attributed the surge in anti-Trump sentiment to the federal government's quest to find illegal voting in California, continuing immigration raids and the president's downplaying of inflation and gas prices.6

Hilton, whom President Trump had endorsed7, ran on prices. At a town hall in Colton, Inland Empire Community News reported, roughly 50 people attended as Hilton discussed administrative bloat and the rising cost of living8. He told the paper that voters were less interested in political ideology than practical solutions to their financial difficulties.8 His closing pitch, NBC Los Angeles reported, promised $3 per gallon gas and lower vehicle registration fees9.

The county's Latino majority is likely part of the story, but the evidence is national, not local. Politico reported that heavily Latino counties swung more toward Democrats than any other type of place, according to preliminary returns.10 A post-election UnidosUS survey found that 17 percent of Latino voters who supported Trump in 2024 backed Democratic House candidates11, citing living costs and immigration enforcement. Becerra will be the first Latino elected governor of the Golden State12. Still, the Berkeley poll found that 52% of Latino voters believed electing the state's first Latino governor was "not too important" or "not at all important."13 None of these surveys reports results for San Bernardino County alone, so how much each factor counted here is not yet known.

What it means

The swing reached the High Desert, where Republicans have long been strongest. CalMatters reported that Mojave Desert Republican Rep. Jay Obernolte was hanging on for his political life against Democrat Tessa Lynn Hodge, in a race not considered competitive before Election Day.14 KQED reported that by Thursday it was the only Democrat-versus-Republican contest that had yet to be called in California15.

The county's House map had already changed. Under Proposition 50, KQED noted, the 41st District was transformed from a competitive Inland Empire seat held by Republican Rep. Ken Calvert into a safe Democratic district covering parts of Los Angeles and Orange counties.15

What the vote shows is narrow. A smaller midterm electorate, voting in an open governor's race against a Republican tied to an unpopular president, turned a county Trump won into a solid Democratic one. The 2024 result came from a much larger electorate. The two counts describe different groups of voters, and only later elections will show which one the county's politics resemble.
