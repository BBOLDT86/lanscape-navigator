The wave · the biggest counties · Hennepin County, Minnesota

Hennepin gave Flanagan her Senate cushion but moved less than neighbors

The Minneapolis county gave Peggy Flanagan a 46-point margin, and her lead there in raw votes was larger than her lead statewide. Its shift since 2024 was smaller than Ramsey's, Anoka's and Minnesota's as a whole.

What Hennepin did

Hennepin County, which takes in Minneapolis and suburbs from Bloomington to Maple Grove, cast 664,237 votes in the Nov. 3 U.S. Senate race. That is the most of any Minnesota county and the 13th-most of any county in a state with a race this year. Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan, the Democrat, won 477,416 votes to 172,527 for Republican Michele Tafoya, a margin of 45.9 points with 98.8 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris carried the county by 42.6 points over Donald Trump in 2024, so Hennepin moved 3 points toward the Democrats.

That is a big margin but a modest move. Statewide, Flanagan won by 10.1 points in a state Harris carried by 4.3, a shift of 5.9 points. In the governor's race, Amy Klobuchar's margin moved 8.6. Eighty-four of Minnesota's 87 counties moved left, and Hennepin ranked 51st. Neighboring Ramsey County (St. Paul) moved 8 points. Anoka County moved 6 and flipped from Republican to a narrow Democratic edge. Dakota moved 5, and Washington and Scott each moved 3. Big counties elsewhere that started from similar 2024 margins moved much further: Cook County, Illinois, 18 points; Kings County, New York, 15; Fulton County, Georgia, 14. Hennepin looks more like Middlesex County, Massachusetts (5) and Santa Clara County, California (1). Across the country, the typical county moved 14 points.

Hennepin still carried the state for Flanagan. Her lead in the county, about 305,000 votes, was larger than her statewide lead of about 287,000. MinnPost reported that after trailing Michele Tafoya in early returns, Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan pulled ahead after Hennepin County votes were finally tabulated.1

Why it moved

Hennepin is the state's most highly educated county. About 1.28 million people live there, and 54.1%2 of adults have a bachelor's degree, 1st of 872 counties in the state. Foreign-born residents make up 13.6%2 of the population, compared with 8.7 percent statewide.

Local reporting points first to Operation Metro Surge, the federal immigration crackdown of last winter. Alex Pretti was shot and killed by U.S. Customs and Border Protection officers nine months ago amid the operation; he was an ICU nurse and the second person fatally shot by federal agents in a matter of weeks.3 The Minnesota Star Tribune reported that in Hennepin and Ramsey counties — where the federal agents were most active during Operation Metro Surge — voters turned out in force4 compared with the last midterm. WCCO found heavy turnout at a polling place in Cedar-Riverside, home to thousands with Somali heritage5. Statewide exit polling published by NBC News found that 59 percent of voters said the administration's immigration enforcement had gone too far6.

Tafoya's campaign centered on fraud in state programs. Before the vote, the Star Tribune reported that former DFL Party chair Mike Erlandson thought fraud could be particularly consequential among moderate voters, especially suburban women7. Afterward, FOX 9 analyst David Schultz said that while voters cared about fraud, the immigration crackdown and the rising cost of living carried more weight.8

The county numbers cannot sort out how much of the move came from people who changed sides and how much from who voted. Hennepin cast 93 percent as many votes as it did in 2024, about 52,000 fewer ballots, and Flanagan's lead in raw votes was almost exactly Harris's. The county's 3-point move mixes the people who stayed home with the people who switched parties, and the totals cannot separate the two.

What it means locally

The DFL gains reached the county's outer suburbs. MinnPost reported that DFLers Kristy Janigo wins Maple Grove's open Senate District 37 over Republican Brady Hoag.1 In the open race for county attorney, Cedrick Frazier has declared victory in the race for Hennepin County Attorney over Anders Folk1, both DFLers.

Republicans had been trying to build support in diverse urban neighborhoods, and they say the crackdown set that back. Republican analyst Preya Samsundar told MPR News that the party's gains among Black, Hispanic, Asian and young voters since 2016 were "all wiped away during last night's election,"9 and she linked part of that to immigration enforcement. She was talking about the whole state, not just Hennepin. For now, Hennepin remains what it was in 2024: the county where Democrats build their statewide lead. This year it moved left by less than most of the state did.
