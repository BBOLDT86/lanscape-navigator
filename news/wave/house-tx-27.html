Cloud holds redrawn Texas 27 by 2.3 points as Caldwell, Bastrop flip

Republican Michael Cloud beat teacher Tanya Lloyd by 6,543 votes in a Gulf Coast-to-Austin district drawn in 2025. In the Senate and governor races, the district's counties moved about 21 points toward Democrats.

The race

Michael Cloud, the Victoria Republican who has represented Texas' 27th Congressional District since 2018, kept his seat on Nov. 3. He beat Democrat Tanya Lloyd 144,471 votes to 137,928, a margin of 6,543 votes, or 2.3 percentage points, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race for Cloud on Wednesday afternoon1, at 5:15 p.m. Eastern time.

The race was a rematch. It was Cloud's second general election in a row against Lloyd, a teacher from Lockhart2, and the lead changed hands on election night. The Victoria Advocate reported that Lloyd led in early returns before Cloud took the lead back about an hour later3. At his watch party in downtown Victoria, Cloud told supporters, "It's going to be long."2

Lloyd ran as a former Republican. She has said the overturning of Roe v. Wade led her to the Democratic Party after a lifetime of voting Republican4. Her platform centered on lowering costs for working families, fully funding public education, rural health care and infrastructure5. The Austin American-Statesman's editorial board backed her in the March primary for her stands on reproductive rights and affordability6. Over the summer, she joined Victoria residents in raising concerns about what large data centers could do to housing, infrastructure and the environment7.

Cloud is a former business owner who chaired the Victoria County Republican Party for seven years8 and a member of the House Freedom Caucus9. He told Washington County's KWHI that he would keep working on defense, energy, water and manufacturing while watching the country's fiscal health10. He also had more money. Federal reports through Oct. 14 showed Cloud had raised $1.2 million to Lloyd's $271,00011, according to a tally by the site VoteSee, which listed no public polls of the race11.

Both campaigns ran beneath a Senate race that AdImpact called the most expensive non-presidential race on record12. James Talarico beat Ken Paxton statewide by 12.1 points, and Gina Hinojosa unseated Gov. Greg Abbott by 7.1. Fox 7 Austin reported that voters repeatedly cited the cost of living, gas, groceries and health care13.

The district

This was the first congressional election under the district lines redrawn in 20253. KRIS-TV in Corpus Christi reported that the 27th shifted north and many Corpus Christi residents moved into Rep. Vicente Gonzalez's district14. KWHI reported that the plan moved Washington, Austin, Colorado and Fayette counties out of Rep. Michael McCaul's 10th District and into the 27th15. KXAN describes a district that includes parts of Bastrop, Hays and Travis counties and all of Caldwell and Fayette, then runs southeast to the Gulf coast16.

A warning about the map: the county figures cover whole counties and come from the Senate and governor races, not the House race. The 17 counties cast about 964,000 votes, more than three times the 282,399 cast for Cloud and Lloyd, because most of Travis, Hays and Nueces lies in other districts. Travis alone cast 548,521. That is why Travis outweighs everything else in the counties' combined 20.8-point move toward Democrats.

Even so, the pattern is clear. The largest moves came at the Austin end. Caldwell, Lloyd's home county, went from R+14.4 in 2024 to D+15.7, a 30-point move. Bastrop went from R+18.4 to D+10.9, a 29-point move, and Hays moved 26 points. Nueces also flipped, from R+11.5 to D+11.2. Along the coast, San Patricio moved 24 points, Matagorda 21 and Cloud's Victoria County nearly 20, from R+42.6 to R+23.1. Aransas, Washington and Colorado moved least, 13 to 14 points, and the farm and ranch counties stayed solidly Republican: Jackson at R+54.3, Austin at R+46.1 and Fayette at R+45.1.

A county's move does not show that voters changed their minds. The totals cannot separate people who switched parties from new or returning voters; Hays County alone cast 119,693 votes in the top races. The Texas Tribune reported that early voting set a modern record for a Texas midterm17. Fox 7, citing the League of United Latin American Citizens, reported that Latino turnout came close to doubling from the last midterm13.

What the result means

The incumbent held on. Cloud won the seat in a June 2018 special election after Republican Blake Farenthold resigned18. The Advocate reported that Cloud won his home county easily but the districtwide margin was much closer3. In a statement after the race, he said "I don't take it lightly."19

A 2.3-point margin is close for a congressman who beat Lloyd by a wide margin in 20242 under the old lines. About 1 percent of the expected vote is still uncounted, but the margin was enough for AP to call the race. Elsewhere in Texas, ABC News reported that Democrats flipped two largely Hispanic South Texas districts that Trump had previously won by double digits20. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled, so Cloud will be part of the minority when the new Congress meets.
