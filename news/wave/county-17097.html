North of Chicago, Lake County swings 14 points toward Stratton

Juliana Stratton carried Lake County, Illinois, by 34 points, 14 better than Kamala Harris did in 2024. Democrats also took every Republican-held county board seat on the ballot, and voters still rejected a school sales tax.

Lake County fills the northeast corner of Illinois, between Chicago's North Shore and the Wisconsin line. It runs from lakefront suburbs like Highland Park to the county seat, Waukegan, and the Round Lake villages to the west. The Census Bureau counts 719,3391 residents. Households there have a median income of $110,416, against $80,734 nationally2, and 26.0%2 of residents are Hispanic.

What Lake County did

Lt. Gov. Juliana Stratton, the Democrat, beat Don Tracy, a former chairman of the Illinois Republican Party, 148,530 to 71,649 in the county's U.S. Senate vote. That is a margin of 34.4 points, with 97.68 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris won the county by 20.8 points over Donald Trump in 2024, so the county moved 14 points toward the Democrats.

That is almost exactly the statewide move. Stratton won Illinois by 24.3 points, 13.3 better than Harris's 11.0, and Gov. JB Pritzker won a third term over Darren Bailey by 23.5. Lake ranked 13th of the state's 102 counties by move, and 95 of the 102 moved left.

In the Chicago area, though, Lake moved the least. Cook and Will moved about 18 points, Kane 17.5, and DuPage and McHenry 16. That was enough to turn McHenry, which Trump won by 5.3 points, into an 11-point Democratic county. Lake started further left than any of them except Cook. Nationally, its move matched the typical county's (+14.1). It trailed big counties that started near the same margin, such as Allegheny and Montgomery in Pennsylvania and Dallas, each about +23, but beat Sacramento's +10.

Turnout fell sharply, as it usually does from a presidential year to a midterm. Lake cast 223,587 votes, 72 percent of its 309,323 in 2024, about 86,000 fewer. Even so, Stratton's raw lead of about 76,900 votes was bigger than Harris's of roughly 64,000. County totals cannot tell Republicans staying home from Democrats turning out more reliably or voters switching parties. A county's move mixes all three. The count is not final, either: the clerk's office says results are unofficial until Provisional and late-arriving Vote By Mail ballots are counted 14 days after the election3.

Why, according to the reporting

Both Senate candidates ran on cost of living. NBC Chicago reported that the two agree affordability is the top issue in the Senate race4. NPR Illinois reported that Stratton aimed to tie her opponent to President Donald Trump, who had flailing approval ratings5, while Tracy made affordability his top priority on the campaign trail5, blaming Democratic taxes and spending. Tracy was also notably absent from Trump's list of endorsements for Illinois Republicans5.

Local party leaders said much the same. Before the election, the Lake County News-Sun reported that leaders of the Democratic and Republican parties in Lake County agree on the most important issue — affordability6. Afterward, the Daily Herald found that the tide against President Donald Trump's policies ousted myriad moderate and MAGA Republican state lawmakers from Cook and the collar counties7, and that experts in both parties agreed. Across the Chicago area, the Sun-Times reported, voters say they were motivated by inflation, the cost of living and Trump's deportation campaign8. That campaign, Operation Midway Blitz, began in September 2025. Suburban turnout ran ahead of the last midterm: the Sun-Times counted more than 242,000 suburbanites had cast early ballots as of Monday morning, up 27% from the same point four years ago9.

Voters' worry about money also showed up in a separate vote. The same electorate decisively rejected a proposal to allow the county to impose a 1% sales tax with proceeds targeted for specific school-related uses10, the Daily Herald reported. The county Republican committee had led an active volunteer campaign with yard signs and flyers and door knocking across all 18 Lake County townships10 against it. Of six local questions asking voters for money, only one11 passed. Regional Superintendent Michael Karner said pocketbook issues and a lack of information were factors in the defeat11.

What it means for county politics

Down the ballot, the Daily Herald reported that every Republican Lake County Board seat up for election this year was flipped to Democrat12. If the results hold, there will be just two Republicans on the Lake County Board come December12, along with 17 Democrats. Daniel Didech, the county Democratic chairman, called the night a "historic win for Democrats in Lake County," and a "complete wipeout" for Republicans12. He credited years of organizing that helped turn a once solid Republican county into what is now almost entirely Democrat held12. The paper said the local Republican Party struggled to organize12 this cycle, and it could not reach the Republican chair, Dawn Abernathy. Democrats also held countywide offices: The Associated Press declared Sandy Hart the winner of the Lake County Treasurer race.13

The result leaves Democrats with near-total control of county government. It also leaves a split: voters backed the party's candidates by wide margins, then turned down the new school tax.
