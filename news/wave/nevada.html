The wave · state by state · Nevada

Clark County's 13-point swing puts Aaron Ford in Nevada's governor's office

Democrat Aaron Ford unseated Gov. Joe Lombardo in a state Donald Trump carried two years ago. Fifteen of Nevada's 17 counties moved left, but most of the swing came from Las Vegas, and no county changed sides.

Polls had Nevada's race for governor as a tie. The Las Vegas Review-Journal noted that Emerson College Polling surveyed likely Nevada voters twice: in November 2025 and early September. Both showed Ford and Lombardo in a statistical dead heat.1 The result was not close. With 94.6 percent of the expected vote counted, Attorney General Aaron Ford, a Democrat, has 561,367 votes to 477,556 for the Republican governor, Joe Lombardo. That is a lead of 7.7 points out of 1,086,311 ballots. Donald Trump carried Nevada by 3.1 points in 2024, so the state has moved 10.8 points toward the Democrats. About 47,000 votes, 4.4 percent, went to the independent Danielle Ford (no relation) or to "None of These Candidates."

The Associated Press called the race, and Lombardo conceded to Ford shortly before 9:30 a.m.2 the next day. Ford will be Nevada's first Black governor, as well as the first Black governor of any state west of the Mississippi River.3

A campaign about the Las Vegas economy

Ford ran on the state's economy. He framed the race as a direct referendum on the "Lombardo-Trump economy."4 The AP reported that Democrats blamed Trump's tariffs for hurting Las Vegas tourism, which saw a 17% drop in Canadian visitors in 2025.5 Lombardo, a former Clark County sheriff, came out against some of Trump's tariffs last year and highlighted other areas where he pushed back on Trump, including solar power. All the while, Lombardo used his career in law enforcement to portray Democrats as weak on crime.6 He also sought to tie him to Democrat-controlled California, claiming Ford would bring the "Newsom playbook" of high taxes and more regulations.5

Lombardo had far more money. Post-election reporting put his campaign and allied committees at $42.9 million to Ford's $9.1 million.3 Ford had the Culinary Union, which hospitality workers in Las Vegas and Reno belong to. NPR described it as a voter turnout machine.7

Who moved: Las Vegas first, the rest of the state much less

Clark County, which includes Las Vegas, decided the race. Kamala Harris won it by 2.6 points in 2024. Ford leads there by 15.5, a move of 13 points, on 741,044 votes, which is 68 percent of the state's ballots. Washoe County (Reno) moved 8 points, from Harris by 1.0 to Ford by 8.6. Together the two counties cast 87 percent of the vote and moved 11.7 points, weighted by votes. The 14 counties Trump won by 30 points or more cast 11 percent of the vote and moved only 4.2. The 10 counties with fewer than 10,000 votes moved 4.8. In Nevada, the bigger the county, the bigger the swing. No county changed sides. The closest was Carson City, the state capital, which is governed as a combined city and county. It went from Trump by 11.3 to Lombardo by 6.5.

Counties are not voters, and these numbers cannot tell people who changed their minds apart from people who stayed home. Across the state, 73 percent as many ballots have been counted as in 2024: 72 percent in Clark and 78 percent in Washoe. Counties where turnout fell more tended to move further left, but the link is weak (a correlation of -0.33). The three counties where turnout held up best, at 80 to 95 percent of 2024, moved less than 1 point. That pattern fits Trump's 2024 voters skipping a midterm. Fewer people voting does not fully explain Clark, though. The Review-Journal reported that more than 209,000 Nevadans showed up to vote Tuesday, a figure comparable to the previous midterm election1, and that more than 60,000 more mail ballots had come back than in 2022. Fox News Voter Analysis, a survey and not a count, found Latino voters were 15 percent of the electorate and backed Ford 61%8 to 34%. Earlier, the Nevada Independent's early-voting model had put the Latino share in a normal midterm electorate9 at about 15.5 percent. 8 News Now reported that Ford also won enough support from nonpartisan voters2 to win.

The exceptions, and what's still being counted

Douglas County, which covers the Carson Valley and Lake Tahoe's Nevada shore, moved the other way. Trump won it by 32.9 points; Lombardo leads there by 35.5, a 3-point move toward the Republicans on 28,945 votes. Turnout there was 81 percent of 2024's, among the highest in the state. The local Record-Courier reported that the majority of Douglas County voters used the ballots they received in the mail10, and that the county's mail return rate was the highest in the state. None of the reporting reviewed for this piece explains why Douglas moved right. Two tiny counties hardly moved: Eureka shifted 1 point toward Lombardo and Lincoln less than 1 point toward Ford.

Elko County shows the reverse. Lombardo leads there by 46.1 points, down from Trump's 56.6, a 10.6-point move. Elko has the lowest turnout in the state so far, 65 percent of its 2024 vote, with 91 percent counted.

Counting continues. Clark is at 93.5 percent of its expected vote, so most of the uncounted ballots are there. Margins may still change a little. The winner will not.

Nevada moved less than the rest of the country. The median county nationwide moved 14.1 points left. Nevada's median county moved 5.8.
