Sarasota County barely budged as Florida swung toward Democrats

Ashley Moody carried Sarasota County by 16.5 points, almost as much as Donald Trump did in 2024. The state moved 10 points toward Democrats. Turnout was a record for a midterm, and Amendment 3 was on the ballot.

Sarasota County, the Gulf Coast county between Tampa Bay and Fort Myers that includes the city of Sarasota, Venice and North Port, did something few Florida counties did on Nov. 3. It voted almost exactly as it had in 2024. Republican Sen. Ashley Moody beat Democrat Angie Nixon there by 16.5 points, 128,786 votes to 91,990, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Two years ago Donald Trump carried the county over Kamala Harris by 18.2 points. That is a move of about 2 points toward Democrats.

A small move in a state that moved a lot

Statewide, Moody won by 2.9 points, 10.2 points closer than Trump's 13.1-point Florida win. Sixty-six of Florida's 67 counties moved left, and the typical county moved 7 points. Sarasota ranked 64th. Across the bay, Pinellas moved 6 points and Hillsborough 12. Miami-Dade moved 19. Big counties elsewhere that Trump won by similar margins moved much further: Macomb County outside Detroit 12 points, Suffolk County on Long Island 11, and Collin County north of Dallas 23, enough to flip it. The typical county nationwide moved 14.

The Sarasota Herald-Tribune noted that Moody and Byron Donalds, the Republican elected governor, both won by much wider margins in Sarasota County1 than statewide. The county's age profile is one reason it stands apart. Residents age 65 and older made up 38.8 percent of Sarasota County's population in 20252, Sarasota Magazine reported from Census Bureau estimates, and its median age reached 57.8.

Who turned out, and why

The 223,509 votes cast in the Senate race were 80 percent of the 2024 total, a high figure for a midterm. Suncoast Searchlight, a local nonprofit newsroom, reported that county turnout was the highest for a midterm election in the county since at least 19983. It found that nearly 76% of registered Democrats voted, compared to about 68% of registered Republicans.3 So the small move was not simply Democrats staying home. Still, a county's move mixes changes in who turned out with voters who switched sides, and the county count cannot separate the two. Florida's Republican registration edge, independents and voters who changed sides could all play a part. The numbers cannot say how much each mattered.

Reporters pointed to three forces. The first was Amendment 3, a homestead property-tax cut that Sarasota voters approved, the Observer reported4. Gov. Ron DeSantis credited it with bringing out voters, saying many who came to vote yes ended up voting Republican later on down the ballot5. A political scientist told the USA Today Network-Florida that it drove Republican affordability voters and just Republican fiscal conservatives who want to see low taxes6.

The second was the Democratic nominee. The Associated Press described Nixon as a democratic socialist who tested the strength of Democrats' left flank7 and noted she had upset establishment Democrats' more moderate favorite in the primary.7 At a campaign stop in Sarasota the day before the election, Moody used the opportunity to paint her opponent as a radical8, WSLR News reported.

The third was a split among local Republicans that did not change the result. Suncoast Searchlight described an unusual election cycle where several prominent local Republicans campaigned for Democrats9 over development and home rule. Former U.S. Rep. Dan Miller and Manatee County Commissioner George Kruse backed the campaign10 of Democrat Kelly Kirschner, a former Sarasota mayor, in the redrawn 16th Congressional District. Kirschner still lost to Republican Sydney Gruters. In a Sarasota County Commission race, the head of the Sarasota MAGA Patriots said it was the first time in her adult life that she has pushed for a Democrat11. The Republican, Jim DeNiro, won the District 4 seat12.

What it means for the county

Locally, the party lines held. On the Charter Review Board, WSLR reported, none of the three Democratic candidates got elected13. The Herald-Tribune's summary was that Sarasota County voters largely stuck with Republicans on Election Day even as Democrats captured both the U.S. House and Senate nationally.14

The next fight is over the budget. An independent research institute projected that Amendment 3 would leave Sarasota County shortfalls of $241 million over the first two years alone15, Suncoast Searchlight reported. County Commissioner Tom Knight said he's going to start acting like the government is in a recession during the next budget cycle.16

The result does not settle the argument among Republicans. Kruse said his party should not get comfortable, and he said of the close statewide races: "These should not have been competitive."9 Days before the vote, Democrat David Jolly had told a Sarasota rally that the county started to show us how to tear down that red wall!17 In the Senate race, the wall moved by about 2 points.
