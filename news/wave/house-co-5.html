Colorado Springs sends its first Democrat to Congress as Killin ousts Crank

Army veteran Jessica Killin beat first-term Republican Jeff Crank by 5.1 points in Colorado's 5th District, a seat around Colorado Springs that had never elected a Democrat. Democrats also ran far ahead statewide.

For more than half a century, Colorado's 5th Congressional District elected only Republicans. On Nov. 3 it chose Jessica Killin, a Democrat running for office for the first time, over first-term Republican Rep. Jeff Crank. With 99% of the expected vote counted, Killin has 157,799 votes and Crank has 141,984. That is a lead of 15,815 votes, or 5.1 points, out of 311,268 cast. Two independents, Mark Elworth and Christopher Mitchell, split the remaining 11,485 votes.

The race

Killin is an Army veteran who served from 1995 to 2003 and attained the rank of captain. She later worked as a congressional staffer and served as chief of staff to Doug Emhoff, then the second gentleman.1 Colorado Public Radio reported that Killin and her family moved to Colorado Springs to run for the district seat.2 Crank, a first-term lawmaker and former conservative radio host3, won his seat in 2024 after former Republican Rep. Doug Lamborn retired.4

The candidates argued over costs. At a debate hosted by Denver7 and KOAA 5 in August, Killin and Crank agreed housing and healthcare cost too much.5 They split on Medicaid: Crank defended work requirements, while Killin warned the changes would leave people without coverage.5 Axios Colorado Springs found the two divided on tariffs, the war in Iran and data centers. Killin supports a moratorium on data centers.6 Space Command was a local issue: during Crank's first term the U.S. Space Command was permanently moved to the Redstone Arsenal in Huntsville, Alabama.7 Late in the campaign, Crank told CPR that the move was "not something that I'm going to necessarily fight."8

The race's most-discussed ad featured relatives of a woman sexually abused by the late financier Jeffrey Epstein criticizing Crank9. Crank's side answered that he "voted to release the Epstein files in the only up or down vote on the floor"10, and Roll Call reported that he called the spot "intentionally vague"11. Crank's campaign tried to paint Killin as an outsider. The National Republican Congressional Committee called her a "D.C.-insider and carpetbagger"12.

Killin had more money of her own to spend. She had raised about $5.6 million through mid-October, including more than $1 million in personal funds13; Crank reported raising about $3 million13. Outside groups mostly helped Crank. Colorado Politics counted just over $5.8 million from Republican-aligned groups and $2.1 million from Democratic groups12. Rocky Mountain PBS later put outside money for Killin at $2.7 million14. After winning, Killin said the result underscored voters' frustrations with the Iran war, tariffs and the Jeffrey Epstein scandal.15

The district

The 5th covers most of El Paso County16, a county that's home to five major military installations.3 Reuters described Colorado Springs as a military town and global center for Christian evangelism15. KKTV reported that redistricting and growth in the suburbs have shifted it leftward in recent years.17 The Gazette noted that unaffiliated voters make up the largest cohort of voters18. President Donald Trump won the district in both 2020 and 2024.2

The Watch's results file for this seat has no county breakdown, so the county-by-county move cannot be mapped here. The statewide comparison shows the size of the wave around it. Sen. John Hickenlooper won by 25.5 points and Phil Weiser won the governor's race by 27.9. Kamala Harris carried Colorado by 11.0 points in 2024, so those results are 14.5 and 16.9 points better for Democrats. These totals cannot show how much of the change came from voters switching parties and how much came from who turned out. The 311,268 votes in this race cannot answer that either.

What it means

This was an upset by the forecasters' measure. Killin defied election forecasters who favored Crank3. The Cook Political Report moved the race from "Likely Republican" to "Lean Republican" in September6, and Sabato's Crystal Ball recently moved the race to a toss-up19 in late October. The Gazette reported that Killin would also become the first female representative for the 5th District20.

The Associated Press called the race at 2:38 p.m. Eastern. Colorado outlets reported that the call came on Thursday, two days after polls closed21. In his own Facebook post on Thursday, Crank said that he had called Killin to congratulate her and would help with her transition into Congress.22 Colorado Newsline reported that two other Colorado seats held by Republicans also flipped, and that the state's congressional delegation will favor Democrats 7-1.23 Nationally, Democrats had been called the winners of 256 House seats and Republicans 169, with 10 seats not yet called.

Some ballots are still being counted. The El Paso County clerk's office said officials would be curing ballots and processing those received under the Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voter Act for multiple days after the election, delaying any certified results.24
