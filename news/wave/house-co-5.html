Colorado Springs elects its first Democrat to Congress as Killin ousts Crank

Jessica Killin, an Army veteran, beat first-term Republican Jeff Crank by 4.7 points in a district that had never sent a Democrat to Washington. El Paso County moved 14 points toward Democrats from its 2024 presidential vote.

For more than half a century, the House district built around Colorado Springs sent only Republicans to Washington. The Colorado Sun noted that voters in the 5th District have not sent a Democrat to Washington, D.C., in the 54 years since it was created1. On Nov. 3 they did. With about 95 percent of the expected vote counted, Democrat Jessica Killin had 155,598 votes and Republican Rep. Jeff Crank had 141,074. That is a lead of 14,524 votes, or 4.7 points. Two independents, Mark Elworth and Christopher Mitchell, drew 11,323 votes between them.

The Associated Press called the race at 2:38 p.m. Eastern on Thursday, two days after polls closed1. Crank said on Facebook that he had called Killin to congratulate her and would help with her transition. He made no public appearance that day.2

The race: veterans, costs and a fight over residency

Killin, 53, grew up in Colorado Springs. She served in the Army from 1995 to 2003 and rose to captain3, and she was most recently chief of staff to Doug Emhoff, Kamala Harris' husband, before returning to Colorado Springs in 20254. Crank is a former conservative radio host and Americans for Prosperity executive who won the seat in 2024 after Rep. Doug Lamborn retired5.

Axios reported that Killin made President Trump her campaign's foil while pressing Republicans on rising costs6. She also faulted Crank for failing to stop the Trump administration from moving U.S. Space Command from Colorado Springs to Huntsville, Ala.7 Reuters reported from the district after the vote. It found that her win relied on veterans, service members and their families, about a third of the population, for whom the war in Iran is personal8. Canvassers met conservatives willing to back Killin after Crank supported a war that drove up energy prices8.

The Colorado Sun reported that Crank's campaign focused on the tax cuts he voted for in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and spent most of its time attacking Killin's background1. Republicans pointed out that she and her husband kept their house in suburban Washington after she moved back9. State records show she registered to vote in Colorado in July 2025.10

The ads were harsh on both sides. Killin's first attack ads claimed Crank sought an ethics waiver to take gifts from lobbyists11, and the Sun found they featured a misleading claim12. In another Killin ad, Virginia Giuffre's brother and sister-in-law criticized Crank for not meeting with them. Crank called that spot "intentionally vague."7 Killin told KRDO that ads from Crank's campaign and the No Going Back PAC showed a picture of her family's home13.

The Gazette reported that Killin raised nearly $5.6 million through Oct. 16, against just under $2.9 million for Crank's campaign committee14. The Sun counted more than $7 million in super PAC spending, most of it to benefit Crank15. Reuters concluded that outside money gave the two similar war chests16.

The district: one county, a 14-point move

The 5th encompasses Colorado Springs and nearly all of El Paso County4. The area is home to the Focus on the Family evangelical organization8 and to five major military installations6. KKTV noted that redistricting and suburban growth had already shifted it leftward3.

On The Watch's map, El Paso County moved 14 points toward Democrats. Donald Trump carried it by 9.8 points in 2024. This year the Democrats at the top of the ticket carried it by 3.8. The state as a whole moved a bit more. Sen. John Hickenlooper beat Mark Baisley by 25.3 points, and Phil Weiser beat Victor Marx for governor by 27.7. Measured against Harris' 11.0-point statewide win, those are moves of 14.3 and 16.7 points. Killin's 4.7-point margin ran about a point ahead of the top of the ticket, though the district does not cover the whole county.

That move does not prove that voters were persuaded. The county's 313,166 votes in the statewide races, with 98.5 percent counted, come from a midterm electorate. County totals cannot tell voters who switched sides apart from changes in who voted. County officials told the Gazette that turnout was trending above the state's estimate17.

What the result means

Crank, a first-term incumbent, is the first Republican nominee to lose the seat. He had waited a long time for it: he lost primaries to Lamborn in 2006 and 200814. Axios noted that Killin defied election forecasters who favored Crank6. Lamborn told the Gazette he was surprised to see how far the blue wave swept into Colorado2.

The count is not final. The district stood at about 95 percent of the expected vote Saturday morning. El Paso County officials will be curing ballots and processing military and overseas ballots for days, delaying certified results17. Nationally, 252 House seats had been called for Democrats, past the 218 needed for a majority, and 22 were still uncalled.

Killin would also become the district's first female representative18. She promised to hold quarterly in-person town halls and said, "This is not a safe district any longer; it's a tough district."1
