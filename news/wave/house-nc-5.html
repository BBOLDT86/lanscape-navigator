Virginia Foxx trails by 99 votes in North Carolina's 5th District

Democrat Chuck Hubbard leads Republican Virginia Foxx 139,034 to 138,935 with 99% of the expected vote counted and no AP call, as every county in the district moved 18 to 27 points toward Democrats in the Senate race.

Virginia Foxx has represented North Carolina's 5th Congressional District since 2005. Four days after the Nov. 3 election, she trails by 99 votes. As of Saturday morning, AP's count gives Democrat Chuck Hubbard 139,034 votes and Foxx 138,935. Robert Luffman has 7,529; AP lists him as an independent, but NC Newsline1 identifies him as the Libertarian candidate. With 99% of the expected vote counted, the margin rounds to 0.0 points. AP has not called the race.

A rematch few were watching

Hubbard is a retired local newspaper journalist and publisher2 and a Wilkes County native3. He lost to Foxx in 2024 and ran again. Carolina Public Press reported that he campaigned on Helene recovery funding, Medicare and Medicaid, government transparency and LGBTQ+ rights, and did not take money from political action committees4. He also held town halls across the district and said Foxx had not held one in more than a decade.5 Foxx, 831, is chair of the powerful House Rules Committee2, and Donald Trump endorsed her6.

The money favored Foxx. FEC filings cited by Carolina Public Press showed she had raised about $2.2 million to Hubbard's roughly $863,000 as of Oct. 14.4 Axios found that the only national party spending was a little under $350,000 from the Democratic group American Bridge2. WUNC reported that in late October, after national Republican groups stopped running ads for Senate candidate Michael Whatley, TV ads started for Foxx and several other GOP incumbents7.

Axios put the result down to a Republican "dummy-mander" and strong coattails from Democratic Senate candidate Roy Cooper2, who won statewide by 19.6 points. Carolina Public Press reported that Helene funding and affordability were key issues in the 5th4. Hubbard told WFMY that the gerrymander "actually helped us," because the district lost several heavily Republican areas and gained much of Greensboro8. He said his campaign focused on turnout in Greensboro, including among students at N.C. A&T and UNC Greensboro8. For much of election night Foxx led by several hundred votes. Her lead disappeared when the last two precincts reported, both in Guilford County.9

Mountains to Greensboro

The district includes all of Alexander, Alleghany, Ashe, Caldwell, Rockingham, Stokes, Surry, Watauga and Wilkes counties.10 Its towns include Boone, Sparta, Wilkesboro, Reidsville and Mount Airy6. The 2023 redistricting also put most of Greensboro in the 5th, including UNC Greensboro and N.C. A&T, along with parts of northwestern Guilford.11

The Watch's wave map measures each county's move in the Senate race. Every county in the district moved toward Democrats, by between 18 and 27 points. Weighted by votes, the district's counties moved 21.2 points, a little less than the state's 22.8. Surry moved the most, from R+53.3 to R+26.7. Alexander moved the least, 18 points, and is still the most Republican at R+42.2. Of the counties Donald Trump carried in 2024, Rockingham came closest to flipping, at R+14.4. Watauga, home to Appalachian State, went from D+6.0 to D+27.7, and Guilford from D+21.8 to D+42.1. No county changed sides.

Guilford's numbers cover the whole county, and only part of it is in the district. Even so, its 217,239 Senate votes outnumber the other nine counties combined (180,306). The House race drew 285,498 votes. County totals cannot show whether these moves came from Trump voters switching sides or from Trump voters staying home. They show the result, not the reasons for it.

Still counting

Foxx has won 11 consecutive general elections in the district.1 NC Newsline said it has long been considered one of the state's safest GOP seats1, and she is the longest-serving member of the state's delegation4. If Hubbard's lead holds, a sitting incumbent loses. Nationally, this is one of 22 seats AP has not called; Democrats have 252 called seats and Republicans 161.

Foxx said the count "has not ended under state law."12 She pointed to overseas and provisional ballots still to be counted, and said provisional ballots in 2024 gave Republicans enough votes to pass the current margin.1 Local outlets have reported different counts of pending provisional ballots (10, 11), and the number in the district's part of Guilford isn't known11. WFMY noted that even the low estimate is enough to change the outcome, but how those voters voted, and how many ballots will be accepted, is unknown13. Guilford's elections director said that historically, fewer than half of the county's provisional ballots have been counted.14

Guilford officials review provisional ballots on Nov. 1015, and the county canvass is Nov. 1316. After the canvass, state law lets a candidate trailing by no more than 1% of the votes cast ask for a recount.9 Reports differ on whether Foxx has asked for one. Carolina Public Press reported she called for a recount in a Facebook post4, while the Wilkes Journal-Patriot said her Wednesday statement did not mention a recount11. Hubbard has claimed victory1, but said "we will have to fight for this seat."1
