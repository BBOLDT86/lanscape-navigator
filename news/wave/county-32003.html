The wave · the biggest counties · Clark County, Nevada

Clark County, home of Las Vegas, delivers Nevada's governorship to Aaron Ford

Las Vegas and its suburbs went from a 2.6-point Harris edge to a 15.5-point win for Ford over Gov. Joe Lombardo, the county's former sheriff. It was the largest move of any Nevada county, and it came from a smaller midterm electorate.

Nevada's governor's race was decided in Clark County. Attorney General Aaron Ford, a Democrat, beat Republican Gov. Joe Lombardo there 412,700 to 297,864, a margin of 15.5 points and about 114,800 votes. That is more than Ford's statewide lead of about 83,800, which means Lombardo carried the other 16 counties combined and still lost. Ford will become the first Black governor of a state west of the Mississippi River.1

The result is sharper because Clark is Lombardo's home ground. The first-term governor previously served as Clark County sheriff from 2014 to 2022.2

What Clark County did

In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county by 2.6 points while Donald Trump won Nevada by 3.1. This year's move of 13 points toward Democrats was the largest of Nevada's 17 counties. Statewide the move was 11 points; Washoe County (Reno) moved 8 and Elko 11. Clark cast about 68 percent of the state's counted governor votes.

Compared with other big counties that were close in 2024, Clark falls in the middle. Orange County, Calif., started from the same D+2.6 and moved only 5 points. Palm Beach moved 10 and Kent County, Mich., 8. Fort Bend, Tex., moved 27 and Bergen, N.J., 21. Nationally the typical county moved 14 points, slightly more than Clark.

The count is not finished: 93.5 percent of the expected vote is in. Ballots postmarked by Election Day count if received by Nov. 7 at 5 p.m., and voters can cure ballots until 5 p.m. on Nov. 9.3 The registrar said about 4,800 mail ballots need to be cured.4

It is a big, diverse county. The Census Bureau estimates 2,407,2265 residents, of whom 33.4 percent are Hispanic, 14.4 percent Black and 12.5 percent Asian. Non-Hispanic white residents make up 35.6 percent.

Why: turnout, prices and a former sheriff

The 741,044 votes counted so far equal 72 percent of the county's 2024 total. So the shift mixes two things the county count cannot separate: who stayed home and who changed sides.

Turnout was clearly part of it. The Nevada Independent reported that the GOP had a 21,000 person advantage in Election Day voting in Clark County four years ago.6 This year, it found, Democrats even built on their lead in Clark County, besting Republicans by more than 3,000 votes on Election Day.7 The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported that mail and early voting participation increased from the previous midterm, while in-person voting on Election Day remained almost flat compared with 2022.8

Not all of it was turnout. KLAS-TV reported that Ford, dominating the vote in Southern Nevada, won enough support from nonpartisan voters to defeat Lombardo.9 In NBC News' statewide exit poll, Latino10 voters backed Ford 61%10 to 33%10. The network's 2024 exit poll had them split almost evenly between Harris and Trump. Those polls cover all of Nevada, not just Clark.

Reporters mostly named the cost of living as the reason. NBC News wrote that the rising costs of goods, gas, housing and healthcare weighed heavily on voters' minds.11 Tourism mattered too in a county whose economy depends on it. The Associated Press reported that Democrats worked to connect Lombardo with President Donald Trump's tariffs, saying they harmed Las Vegas tourism, which saw a 17% drop in Canadian visitors in 2025.12 The Culinary Union, which represents most of the casino resorts on the Las Vegas Strip and in Downtown Las Vegas13, said it ran the largest political program in Southern Nevada to elect Aaron Ford.14

Lombardo had real local support. He campaigned heavily on his law-and-order record while attempting to keep distance from national politics.15 Clark County's Democratic district attorney, Steve Wolfson, endorsed him, and so did the Clark County Education Association.16 Before the election, Nevada Independent founder Jon Ralston expected Ford to not run as strongly in Clark County17 as other Democrats because of Lombardo's past as sheriff. Ford won the county anyway.

What it means for Clark County's politics

Democrats also did well further down the ballot. The Nevada Independent reported that several Democrats won against Republican or former Republican candidates, even in nonpartisan races.18 Minja Yan, a Democrat, won County Commission District F over Assemblywoman Heidi Kasama. Assemblywoman Shea Backus defeated Jill Douglass, the Clark County Republican Party chair.19

Ford has pledged to bring back bills outgoing Lombardo killed in his record-breaking vetoes, including limiting the number of homes large corporations can buy1.

The county also has to answer for its own election. Some voters waited nearly five hours after polls closed20. Registrar Lorena Portillo blamed the two-card ballot this year — with 142 contests and 254 candidates8 and said the county would review its process. The results are unofficial until ballot curing ends and the canvass is complete.
