The wave · the biggest counties · Palm Beach County, Florida

Trump's home county gives Nixon a 10-point win as Moody holds Florida

Palm Beach County moved nearly 10 points toward Democrats from 2024, a little less than Florida as a whole. Local reporting points mainly to turnout: registered Democrats voted at far higher rates than Republicans.

What the county did

Palm Beach County gave Democrat Angie Nixon 311,531 votes to 252,999 for Republican Sen. Ashley Moody in Florida's special U.S. Senate election. That is a margin of 10.3 points, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Two years ago Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 0.8 points, so the county moved nearly 10 points toward Democrats. It is the county where President Donald Trump is registered to vote and spends much of his time1. It stretches from Boca Raton in the south to farm towns like Belle Glade, Wellington, Westlake and rural areas that stretch to the Glades2.

The move was large, but about average for Florida this year. Moody won statewide by 2.9 points in a state Trump carried by 13.1, a 10.2-point move. Sixty-six of Florida's 67 counties moved left, and Palm Beach ranked 20th. Among the state's biggest counties it moved less than Miami-Dade (19 points), Broward (15), Orange (15), Duval (12) and Hillsborough (12). Only Pinellas moved less, at 6 points. Elsewhere, big counties that were about as close in 2024 split widely: Fort Bend County, Texas, moved 27 points and Riverside County, California, 17, but Orange County, California, moved only 5.

The Boca Post reported that Palm Beach County voted Democratic in all five statewide contests3, including the governor's race that Republican Byron Donalds won statewide. That reverses the last midterm. In 2022, WLRN noted, a Republican governor won the county — an unheard-of feat in the once-Democratic stronghold.4

Who voted, and who moved

The county cast 570,658 votes, 77 percent of its 2024 total. With roughly 175,000 fewer voters than in the presidential year, a county's move mixes two things: who turned out and who changed sides. The county-level count cannot separate them.

Local reporting points mainly to turnout. Florida Politics, using figures from Supervisor of Elections Wendy Sartory Link's office as of mid-afternoon on Election Day, reported that registered Democrats made up 41.3% (206,618)1 of ballots cast and Republicans 33.5 percent. On the voter rolls, Democrats outnumber Republicans on Palm Beach County's voter rolls 317,496 to 307,690, or about 35.4% to 34.3%.1 WLRN found that roughly 73% of Democratic voters in the county cast a ballot, compared to only 55% in 20224. Republican turnout declined only slightly between both midterms: 66% in 2022, to 64% in 20264. Turnout among voters with no party affiliation also rose.

The split showed up in how people voted. The Palm Beach Post reported that early in-person voting ran 60%, more than in 20225. Its count of mail ballots showed county Democrats have cast nearly twice as many ballots as Republicans5. The Boca Post found that Republican candidates won the Election Day vote in 18 of the county's 25 partisan contests3, but Democrats won the mail vote in all 25.

There is less direct evidence that voters changed sides, and most of it comes from statewide polls, not the county. A University of North Florida poll taken before the election found that among independent voters, Nixon led Moody by 15 percentage points6. The same poll found 30 percent said Democratic messaging about the Hope Florida grand jury report made them less likely to back Moody. In a September Stetson University poll, cost of living/inflation ranks as the top concern7. Nixon ran on affordability, pointing to health care costs, home insurance and child care6, while Moody has built her affordability message around the federal tax cuts she voted for.8

What it means locally

Further down the ballot, the Boca Post reported that Democrats flipped state House Districts 93 and 94, and Republican Jon Maples retook District 873, the seat that includes Mar-a-Lago. Democrat Katherine Waldron unseated county Mayor Sara Baxter, who was endorsed by President Donald Trump9. The Palm Beach Post blamed Baxter's loss partly on data-center missteps and a feud with the county's homeless coalition. It quoted the Republican Elizabeth Accomando, whom Baxter beat in the primary, as saying it is clear that Republicans voted for Waldron2. Democrat Yvette Drucker won the clerk's office over Mike Caruso, who had been arrested on child sex abuse charges and was suspended from his post in August4.

None of this settles the longer trend. In July, WLRN reported an analysis by a Latino voter-mobilization group projecting that Republicans could overtake Democrats in Palm Beach County — a longtime Democratic stronghold — as early as this year10. Jodi Schwartz, the county's Republican state committeewoman, told WLRN she was disappointed by GOP turnout. She argued that Republicans' statewide wins tell a different story than the one Palm Beach County Democrats are telling themselves.4 The results are still unofficial until they are certified.
