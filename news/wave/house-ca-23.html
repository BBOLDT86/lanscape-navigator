The House · California 23

Obernolte holds narrow, uncalled lead in California's High Desert House seat

Republican Rep. Jay Obernolte, who chairs the House GOP's policy committee, leads first-time Democratic candidate Tessa Lynn Hodge by 3,384 votes. Forecasters had rated the district safely Republican. The AP has not called the race.

A week after Election Day, California's 23rd Congressional District is still undecided. In the AP count as of early Tuesday, Eastern time, Republican Rep. Jay Obernolte had 104,956 votes and Democrat Tessa Lynn Hodge had 101,572. That is a lead of 3,384 votes, or 1.6 points, out of 206,528 counted. About 93 percent of the expected vote is in, and the AP has not called the race.

Before the election, almost nobody expected a close race here. The Bay Area Reporter noted that all four of the most watched outfits that rate House races1 had called the seat solidly Republican. The Hill reported that the district voted for Trump by over 19 points in 2024, and DDHQ forecasted the seat was safely Republican.2 KQED reported that by Thursday it was the only Democrat-versus-Republican contest that had yet to be called in California3.

The race

Obernolte lives in Big Bear. He used to be mayor of Big Bear Lake and founded a video game company, FarSight Studios4. He has been in Congress since 2021. In April, Politico reported, House Republicans elected him GOP conference policy chair by voice vote5. He is also one of the party's main voices on artificial intelligence and once co-chaired the House's bipartisan AI task force.

This is Hodge's first campaign. She is a licensed clinical social worker who grew up in the High Desert. Her grandfather served 22 years in the Air Force and later ran a print shop in Victorville6. In KVCR's voter guide, she said she would fight for lower costs, accessible care, good jobs, and stronger services close to home7. Obernolte did not respond7 to the station's questionnaire. A local paper, Tri-Community NewsPlus, reported that healthcare access was a central issue in her campaign8.

According to the Bay Area Reporter, Obernolte's campaign sent out mailers attacking Hodge as being too progressive for the district1 before the June primary. Hodge said she read those mailers as a sign that he saw her as the easiest opponent to beat. She also told the paper that, because of redistricting, "we were a sacrificial lamb."1 The money was lopsided. Federal Election Commission figures compiled by LocalCandidates.org show Obernolte raised about $1.9M9 and Hodge about $127,000. No reporting found for this piece described significant outside spending.

KQED's account of the statewide result said Democrats were propelled by redistricting and voter disapproval of President Donald Trump3. In California's inland battleground seats, the station also pointed to voter frustration over high prices.

The district

This is the High Desert east of Los Angeles. It takes in cities such as Victorville and Barstow10. Proposition 50, the Democratic-drawn map voters approved in 2025, redrew the lines. The district now reaches into Kern, Los Angeles, Riverside, San Bernardino11 counties, and the Morongo Basin newsletter Desert Trumpet reported that the majority of the Morongo Basin is in District 2312. As KQED noted, the new map actually shifted to the right3 this seat, because it moved Democratic voters into neighboring districts.

The Watch's data for this seat does not split the vote by county, so this piece cannot say which parts of the district moved most. The statewide picture is clear, though. Xavier Becerra won the governor's race over Steve Hilton by 31.5 points with 83.3 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris won the state by 20.1 points in 2024, so that is an 11.4-point move toward Democrats. Neither that move nor the closeness here shows that voters changed their minds. County totals cannot separate persuasion from changes in who turned out, and this data block has no turnout comparison for the district.

What the result means

Obernolte has never had a close general election in this district. The Hill reported that he won his reelection in 2024 by double digits2. If Hodge overtook him, the seat would flip to Democrats.

That would take a lot. Going by the AP's estimate of the expected vote, about 16,000 ballots remain. Hodge would need roughly 61 percent of them to pass Obernolte. Counting continues. The California Voter Foundation noted that ballots postmarked by Election Day can still arrive within the state's 7-day acceptance window, and that voters with signature problems have until November 25 to fix them.11

This seat will not decide who controls the House. Democrats have already been called the winners in 252 seats, more than the 218 needed for a majority. Republicans have 165, and 18 races, including this one, are still uncalled.
