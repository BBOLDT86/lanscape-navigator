The House · California 23

Obernolte's High Desert seat, drawn to be safe, remains uncalled

Republican Jay Obernolte leads Democrat Tessa Lynn Hodge by 3,768 votes with 68 percent of the expected vote counted, in a High Desert district where all four counties swung toward Democrats for governor.

Four days after the polls closed, Rep. Jay Obernolte leads Democrat Tessa Lynn Hodge 78,437 to 74,669 in California's 23rd Congressional District. That is a gap of 3,768 votes, or 2.5 percentage points. The count covers 68.4 percent of the expected vote, and AP has not called the race. CalMatters described the Republican as "hanging on for his political life"1 in a contest that nobody saw as competitive before Election Day.

The race

Hodge is a first-time candidate. She is a licensed clinical social worker and lifelong High Desert resident2. Bakersfield station KBAK reported that she decided to run after hearing her clients' worries about losing their health care in the aftermath of the "Big Beautiful Bill."3 Health care stayed at the center of her campaign. She pointed to overwhelmed hospitals and long emergency room waits at Desert Valley Hospital and St. Mary's Hospital2, and she backed Medicare for All along with more health services for the rural district.4 She also made an issue of Obernolte's personal wealth, which she put at almost $100 million.3

Obernolte, a former state Assembly member and founder of the video game developer FarSight Studios5, was the clear favorite. The Bay Area Reporter noted in September that all four of the most-watched House race raters called the seat solidly Republican.6 The same outlet reported that before the primary, Obernolte's campaign sent mailers attacking Hodge as too progressive for the district, which raised her profile6. Hodge took the mailers as a sign that he wanted her as his November opponent because he thought she would be the easiest to beat.6 The reporting reviewed for this piece showed no major outside spending or television advertising in the race.

The district

The district's lines changed under Proposition 50, which voters approved in November 2025.7 CalMatters reported that the new map packed conservative voters into a few reliably Republican districts8. The Bay Area Reporter said the redistricting left Hodge facing even longer odds.6 The seat covers the High Desert, including Phelan and Piñon Hills2 and cities such as Victorville and Barstow9. It also reaches south toward Yucaipa and Calimesa10. Parts of four counties fall inside it.

All four counties moved toward Democrats in the governor's race, by a weighted 13.8 points. That is more than California's 10.3-point move, as Xavier Becerra beat Steve Hilton by 30.4 points with 71 percent of the expected statewide vote counted. San Bernardino County went from R+2.1 for Donald Trump in 2024 to D+16.8, a move of 19 points. Riverside County went from R+1.3 to D+15.5. Both counties flipped. Kern County moved the most, 20 points, from R+21.1 to R+0.7, and still stayed narrowly Republican. Los Angeles County was already strongly Democratic and moved 12 points, to D+45.1.

These are countywide numbers from the governor's race, and the 23rd District holds only part of each county. That is how Obernolte can lead the House count even though San Bernardino County as a whole went for Becerra by nearly 17 points. The county results also cannot show whether voters changed their minds or a different set of voters turned out. Seventy percent of San Bernardino County's expected vote is counted, but only 53 percent of Riverside's and 54 percent of Kern's. Even the makeup of this year's electorate is not yet known.

What it means

Obernolte was sworn in in January 2021 to represent what was then the 8th District, which covered the northern half of San Bernardino County11. He was expected to win a fourth term.6 If Hodge overtakes him, a Republican incumbent will have lost a seat the new map built for his party.

If AP's estimate of the expected vote holds, about 70,000 votes are still to be counted, and Hodge would need roughly 53 percent of them to pull ahead. The California Voter Foundation's Close Count project is tracking the 23rd District among California's close contests12. San Bernardino County planned its next results update for Nov. 7 by 6 p.m.13

The seat will not decide control of the House. AP has called 252 seats for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority, and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. According to CalMatters, California's Republican delegation is expected to drop from nine members to four or even three.8 Whether Obernolte is one of the members left depends on the ballots still being counted.
