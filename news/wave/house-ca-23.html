The House · California 23

Obernolte leads by 3,687 votes in California's redrawn High Desert seat

Jay Obernolte, a Republican, holds a 1.8-point lead over Tessa Hodge, a first-time Democratic candidate, in a district forecasters rated safely Republican. About 5 percent of the expected vote remains, and AP has not called the race.

More than a week after Election Day, Rep. Jay Obernolte, a Republican, leads Tessa Hodge, a Democrat, 106,453 votes to 102,766 in California's 23rd Congressional District. That is a margin of 3,687 votes, or 1.8 points, with 95.18 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press has not called the race. It was not expected to be close. Before the election, the Bay Area Reporter noted that all four of the most watched outfits that rate House races1 had rated the seat solidly Republican.

The race

Obernolte was seeking a fourth term. Fox News describes him as a computer scientist and video game developer2 who holds a graduate degree in artificial intelligence. Hodge is 38 and running for office for the first time. She told KVCR, the public station in San Bernardino, that she is a licensed clinical social worker and business owner from Victorville3. Her stated priorities were to lower costs, expand healthcare, and create good-paying jobs3. Obernolte did not answer KVCR's candidate questionnaire.

The Bay Area Reporter reported that Hodge, who grew up in the Victorville area, quit her full-time job in order to have more time to campaign1 but kept seeing patients. Before the June primary, Obernolte's campaign sent out mailers attacking Hodge as being too progressive for the district1, which raised her profile. Hodge told the paper she took the mailers as a sign that he saw her as the easiest opponent to beat in November. In the general election, she hit Obernolte for holding virtual town halls1, saying the format let him screen questions. She also focused on the cost of health care and the region's shortage of medical facilities.

The money was lopsided. According to FEC reports through Oct. 14, as compiled by VoteSee, Obernolte raised $1.9M and Hodge raised $127K4. Local reporting has not yet explained why the race tightened so much. The national result is part of the picture: by NBC News projections, Democrats seized control of both the U.S. House and Senate5. In California, the Democrat Xavier Becerra won the governor's race over Steve Hilton by 31.3 points, about 11 points better for Democrats than Kamala Harris's 2024 margin in the state.

The district

These were new lines. In 2025, California voters approved Proposition 50, voting to amend their state's constitution so that the new map could be used from 2026 to 20306. The measure was California's response to the Republican redraw in Texas. Ballotpedia's analysis of the plan showed the 23rd becoming slightly more Republican than the commission-drawn version. Hodge told the Bay Area Reporter the change made it even harder for a Democrat to win.

The Antelope Valley Press reports that the district is shared between LA, Kern, Riverside and San Bernardino counties7. Its Los Angeles and Kern County pieces include Llano and Valyermo to the south, Lake Los Angeles and California City to the North8. Most of the district is the High Desert of San Bernardino County, the area around Victorville and Big Bear. At its southern end it stretches south toward Yucaipa and Calimesa and into the desert communities9.

The Watch's data for this seat does not separate the district's share of each of those four counties, so this report cannot say which part of the district moved most. Even a full county breakdown would have a limit. Vote counts cannot show whether a shift came from people changing their minds or from a change in who turned out, and comparable turnout numbers for this district are not available here.

What the result means

Obernolte has held this seat since 2021, when it was numbered the 8th District. Fox News called the district a High Desert seat in San Bernardino County that was rated Solid R10 before the midterms. If his lead holds, he will be one of the few California Republicans left in the House. RealClearPolitics counted him among four Republicans on track to make up a state delegation that stood at nine after the 2024 election11.

The count is not finished. If AP's estimate of the expected vote is right, about 10,600 ballots remain. Hodge would need roughly two-thirds of them to pull ahead. The California Voter Foundation notes that voters with signature problems have until November 25 to fix them12, and the Antelope Valley Press reports that results of Nov. 3 election will be final on Dec. 118. Nationally, AP has called 254 House seats for Democrats and 167 for Republicans. This race is one of 14 still open.
