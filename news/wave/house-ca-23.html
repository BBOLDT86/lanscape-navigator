The House · California 23

Obernolte holds a narrow, uncalled lead in California's redrawn 23rd District

Republican Jay Obernolte leads Democrat Tessa Hodge by 3.7 points with about three-quarters of the expected vote counted. All four counties that share the Mojave Desert seat moved 13 to 20 points toward Democrats in the governor's race.

Four days after the polls closed, Rep. Jay Obernolte, the Republican who has represented the High Desert in Congress since 2021, led Democrat Tessa Hodge 89,837 votes to 83,401 in California's 23rd District. That is a gap of 6,436 votes, or 3.7 points, with 74 percent of the expected vote counted. As of Saturday evening, the Associated Press had not called the race. CalMatters described Obernolte as hanging on for his political life against Tessa Lynn Hodge, a Democrat, in a race that wasn't even considered competitive before Election Day.1

The race

Hodge is 38, a licensed clinical social worker and business owner from Victorville2, and this is her first run for office. She told Bakersfield's KBAK that she was motivated to enter after hearing concerns from her clients about losing their healthcare in the aftermath of the "Big Beautiful Bill."3 In interviews with local papers she pointed to overwhelmed hospitals and long emergency room wait times at Desert Valley Hospital and St. Mary's Hospital4. She also came out against the proposed opening of the state's largest immigration detention prison in California City3, which KBAK in 2025 described as part of the district.

Obernolte served on the city council and was the mayor of Big Bear Lake5 before going to Sacramento, and he founded the video game developer FarSight Studios, according to the Victorville Daily Press6. In April, House Republicans elected him Chairman of the House Republican Policy Committee for the remainder of the 119th Congress.7 The Bay Area Reporter wrote that ahead of the primary, Obernolte's campaign sent out mailers attacking Hodge as being too progressive for the district8.

He had far more money. Obernolte raised $1.9M and Hodge raised $127K, according to FEC reports through Oct 14, 2026.9 The Watch found no reporting of major outside spending here. Hodge herself had low expectations for the new lines: "With Prop 50, we were a sacrificial lamb,"8 she told the Bay Area Reporter.

The district

Proposition 50, the redistricting measure voters approved in 2025, allowed the state to use a new, legislature-drawn congressional district map for 2026 through 2030.10 ABC10 described the redrawn 23rd as lying east of Los Angeles11, taking in part of Riverside County and cities such as Victorville and Barstow. According to CalMatters reporting, the new 23rd also would expand south of Redlands, absorbing parts of the Republican-controlled District 41.12 The district touches four counties: Kern, Los Angeles, Riverside, San Bernardino13.

On The Watch's wave map, all four moved sharply toward Democrats compared with 2024. In Kern County, Donald Trump won by 21.1 points in 2024; in this year's governor's race, Republican Steve Hilton led by just 0.7, a 20-point move. San Bernardino County went from Trump by 2.1 to Democrat Xavier Becerra by 16.1, a move of 18 points. Riverside County went from Trump by 1.3 to Becerra by 15.5, a 17-point move. Los Angeles County, already deep blue, moved 13 points, from D+32.9 to D+46.1. Weighted together, the district's counties moved 14.4 points, more than the state as a whole. Statewide, Becerra leads by 31.0 points, against Kamala Harris's 20.1-point margin in 2024.

Two cautions apply. First, these are countywide figures, and most voters in each of these counties live outside the 23rd. Second, a move is not the same as persuasion. A county count cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from differences in who turned out, and the count is far from finished. Kern had 54 percent of its expected vote in, with 142,475 ballots counted, and Riverside had 57 percent, with 472,891.

What the result means

On the old lines the seat was safe. When Obernolte first won the 23rd in 2022, the Daily Press reported he was easily defeating Democratic challenger Derek Marshall14. A 3.7-point lead is much narrower than that, but no incumbent has lost here yet.

By The Watch's arithmetic, about 60,000 votes remain if the expected-vote estimate holds. To overtake Obernolte, Hodge would need roughly 55 percent of them. In San Bernardino County, which holds most of the district, mail ballots count if they were postmarked on or before Tuesday, November 3 and received by the Registrar of Voters within seven days following the election.15 State law also gives voters another chance to correct their mail ballot or provisional ballot up until November 25, 2026.15 The county's next results update is scheduled for Monday, November 9, 2026, by 6 p.m.16 The AP calls a race when it determines the leading candidate's margin is too large for the remaining ballots to change the outcome.13

Control of the House does not depend on this seat. Democrats already have 252 seats called, well past the 218 needed for a majority, with 22 races still uncalled nationally.
