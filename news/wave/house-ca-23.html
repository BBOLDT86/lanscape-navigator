The House · California 23

Obernolte holds California's High Desert seat by 3,552 votes

In a district drawn to stay Republican under Proposition 50, Rep. Jay Obernolte beat first-time Democratic candidate Tessa Hodge, a social worker, by 1.6 points. Few people had treated the race as competitive before Election Day.

Jay Obernolte, a Republican who has represented California's High Desert in Congress since 2021, kept his seat in the 23rd District by 3,552 votes. He had 109,335 votes to 105,783 for Democrat Tessa Hodge, a margin of 1.6 points out of 215,118 votes cast. As of late Nov. 16, about 98 percent of the expected vote had been counted, and the Associated Press has called the race for Obernolte.

The margin was much closer than most people expected. The day after the election, CalMatters described Obernolte as hanging on for his political life against Tessa Lynn Hodge, a Democrat, in a race that wasn't even considered competitive before Election Day.1

The race

Obernolte is a computer scientist and video game developer2. He was the mayor of Big Bear Lake3 and later served in the state Assembly, and in Congress he chairs the House Republican Policy Committee. Hodge, 38, is a licensed clinical social worker, former Serrano High School student, and a lifelong High Desert resident4, and this was her first run for office. She told KBAK in Bakersfield that she decided to run after hearing concerns from her clients about losing their healthcare in the aftermath of the "Big Beautiful Bill."5 The Antelope Valley Daily News reported that her campaign rested on affordable and accessible healthcare, creating good local jobs, tackling housing affordability, and advancing humane immigration reform.6

Health care has extra weight here. In 2025, CalMatters reported that 48 percent of residents in Obernolte's High Desert district were on Medicaid, among the state's most conservative areas that benefit disproportionately from Medicaid7. In her ballot statement, Hodge described herself as the granddaughter of immigrants from Mexico8. Obernolte's statement made the case for him as an independent incumbent: he backed term limits and a ban on stock trading by members of Congress, and he called himself one of the most bipartisan members of the House.

Obernolte had far more money. FEC reports compiled by VoteSee show he raised $1.9M and Tessa Lynn Hodge has raised $127K, according to FEC reports through Oct 14, 2026.9 The Bay Area Reporter wrote that before the primary, Obernolte's campaign sent out mailers attacking Hodge as being too progressive for the district10. Hodge read the mailers as a sign that Obernolte saw her as the easiest to beat in the general election.10 We found no reporting since the election that explains which issue moved voters here.

The district

The district is centered on the Mojave Desert communities of San Bernardino County. Under the new map it also stretches south toward Yucaipa and Calimesa and into the desert communities.11 Proposition 50 also added parts of Riverside County to the 23rd District12. Ballotpedia's comparison of the old and new lines found the redrawn seat slightly more Republican than the one it replaced. Hodge argued during the campaign that health care has not kept up with growth. She noted that the High Desert still has the same three hospitals it had when she was born, even though the local population has grown from 200,000 to 700,000.10

Our data for this seat has no county-level counts, so the county view on the wave map isn't available for the 23rd District in this release. The statewide numbers show the broader pattern. Democrat Xavier Becerra won the governor's race over Republican Steve Hilton by 31.2 points, 8,636,035 votes to 4,531,091. Kamala Harris carried California by 20.1 points in 2024, so the state moved 11 points toward Democrats, though that compares a governor's race with a presidential one. These are totals, and they can't tell voters who changed their minds apart from shifts in who voted. That matters here because the data block has no 2024 turnout figure to set beside this year's 215,118 district votes or 13.2 million statewide.

What the result means

Obernolte first won the seat in 2020, when it was numbered as the 8th district.3 His survival stands out in a bad year for California Republicans. Proposition 50 complicated reelection bids for some Republican House incumbents in California13, and CalMatters reported that the state's GOP delegation is expected to drop from nine to just four or even three members.1 The paper added that the Republicans who survived were in solidly red districts that shielded them from voters' fierce pushback elsewhere.1 The 23rd was drawn as one of those districts and still came within 1.6 points.

Nationally, AP has called 256 House seats for Democrats and 171 for Republicans, with 8 still uncalled. Obernolte's seat doesn't change control of the chamber. No incumbent lost here, and the roughly 2 percent of the expected vote still out is small next to his lead. The results become final later: county elections officials must complete final official results by December 3, 202614, and the Secretary of State will certify results on December 11, 202614.
