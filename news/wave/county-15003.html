Honolulu's Democratic margin doubles for Josh Green as turnout falls

Gov. Josh Green carried the City and County of Honolulu by 44 points, double Kamala Harris's 2024 margin, on 27 percent fewer ballots. Local analysts said many of Trump's 2024 voters stayed home, and Republicans lost Oahu House seats.

What Honolulu did

The City and County of Honolulu is one government for all of Oahu. It runs from the downtown towers to the old plantation towns on the Leeward coast. On Nov. 3 it cast 248,995 votes for governor, about two of every three in Hawaii. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Democratic Gov. Josh Green had 179,144 votes and the Republican, Gary Cordery, had 69,851. That is a margin of 43.9 points. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 21.6 points, so Honolulu moved 22 points toward the Democrats. That is exactly the state's move, because Oahu makes up most of the state's vote. Statewide, Green won by 45.3 points, compared with Harris's 23.1.

All four of Hawaii's counties moved left. Honolulu's move was the second-largest, behind Kauai's 29 points and ahead of Hawaii County and Maui at 21 each. Even so, Honolulu ended as the least Democratic of the four: Hawaii County finished at D+48.9, Kauai at D+48.3 and Maui at D+46.4. Big mainland counties that started from similar 2024 margins moved about as much. Dallas and Allegheny counties each moved 23 points, and Montgomery County, Pa., also 23. Queens moved 20 and Sacramento 10. The typical county nationwide moved 14.

Honolulu Civil Beat reported that Green won the largest margin of victory in a general election gubernatorial race since statehood1, even though he essentially ignored Cordery this year.1

Who voted, and why

County totals cannot tell voters who switched sides from voters who stayed home, and a move this size mixes both. Honolulu's 248,995 votes for governor were 73 percent of its 2024 presidential total, roughly 92,000 fewer ballots. Civil Beat noted before the election that there is no publicly available independent poll data2 on the race, so no Hawaii-specific survey is available to divide the two.

Local analysts mostly pointed to turnout. University of Hawaii political scientist Colin Moore told Hawaii News Now, "I think a lot of Republicans stayed home. They weren't excited. President Trump wasn't on the ballot."3 He also told the Star-Advertiser that local Republicans suffered when voters across the country Tuesday night overwhelmingly rebuked Trump and his policies as costs continue to rise.4 House Minority Leader Lauren Matsumoto blamed "a combination of the national scene and our extremely low, abysmal voter turnout."5 Before Election Day, experts told KHON2 that slow ballot returns reflected a lack of high-profile competitive races, voter apathy and a lengthy ballot6.

The campaign itself was lopsided. Hawaii Public Radio reported that both Green and Cordery listed the cost of living as their top issue. Green touted tax cuts he worked on with the Legislature and their doubling of the earned income tax credit and food tax credit. Cordery said he would eliminate the general excise tax on food and medicine.7 Cordery, a general contractor making his second run, raised only $430,800 for the campaign, which was not nearly enough to counter a flood of advertising on behalf of the Democrats.1

What it means on Oahu

The 2024 results had made Republicans hopeful about Oahu. Civil Beat found that in 2024 Trump made the greatest gains in the last eight years in neighborhoods like Waipahu and Kalihi8. The Star-Advertiser reported that most of the House and Senate Republicans represent districts in West Oahu9. Republican legislators there said their support comes from voters' frustration at Democrats for not doing more to address the cost of living10.

This year those gains shrank. According to Civil Beat, the House Republican caucus will be down to just six, thanks to the loss of incumbents Joe Gedeon (District 18 in the Hawaiʻi Kai area) and Garner Shimizu (District 32 in Moanalua and Āliamanu)11. Rep. Elijah Pierick gave up his House seat only to lose to incumbent Sen. Rachele Lamosao (D, Pearl City-Waipahu-West Loch).4 Democrat Corey Rosenlee won Pierick's open seat. The Republican core in Mililani and Ewa Beach held: Matsumoto and Rep. David Alcos both ran unopposed11. One Oahu House race, a court-ordered rerun of a tied Republican primary, is still being counted5.

Local outlets differ on what caused the Republican losses. The Star-Advertiser tied them to the "blue tsunami" that swept the rest of the country12. Civil Beat reported that they were seen less as part of the "blue tsunami" that swept mainland races Tuesday and more as a matter of unique circumstances and familiar personalities.11 Moore said the party now has to decide whether to move toward the middle or double down on more conservative positions.3

The county's figures are close to final but not certified. Hawaii Public Radio reported that a last report would come after a 5-business day voter signature curing period13, so the totals here could still change slightly.
