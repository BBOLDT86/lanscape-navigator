Kingston keeps Georgia's coastal House seat by 2.8 points against the wave

Republican Jim Kingston, son of the man who held the seat for 22 years, beat Democrat Amanda Hollowell by 7,724 votes. All 15 counties from Savannah to the Florida line moved toward Democrats in the statewide races.

Georgia Democrats won the Senate race by 18 points and the governor's race by nearly 9, but the state's coastal congressional seat stayed Republican by a small margin. James "Jim" Kingston, an insurance professional and the son of former U.S. Rep. Jack Kingston1, had 140,222 votes. Democrat Amanda Hollowell had 132,498. That is a lead of 7,724 votes, or 2.8 points, with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press called the race at 12:15 a.m. Wednesday.

Kingston, a first-time candidate, will succeed Rep. Buddy Carter1, who stepped down for an unsuccessful bid for U.S. Senate2. The Current GA reported that the unofficial result is the closest a Republican has come to losing Coastal Georgia's congressional seat3 since Jack Kingston first won it in 1992.

The race

Kingston started as the favorite. President Donald Trump endorsed Kingston ahead of the May primary2, and he defeated his closest rival in a six-candidate field by 35 points, avoiding a runoff3. The Cook Political Report had rated the district as solid Republican.4 Hollowell, a political strategist and racial justice organizer2, defeated Joyce Griggs in the Democratic primary runoff5 in June.

One side had far more money. Kingston had a cash advantage of nearly 10-to-1: more than $2.3 million raised to Hollowell's just over $227,0006, The Current GA reported. With little backing from the state Democratic Party, Hollowell relied on social media and some 200 volunteers, especially in Glynn, Liberty and Chatham counties.6 A former Democratic congressional candidate, Randy Zurcher, organized a billboard campaign in the Chatham area titled "Nepo Baby Kingston."6

Both candidates campaigned on prices. WTOC reported that Kingston focused on affordability, support for military families, and alignment with Trump7, along with tax cuts, port workers and the agriculture industry8. Hollowell's 10-point affordability plan included protecting labor unions, raising the federal minimum wage, expanding affordable housing, and addressing health care costs.9 She also cited historically high diesel prices in a region that relies heavily on trucking10. Many Republicans in close races were distancing themselves from Trump, but not Jim Kingston10, The Current reported. Its post-election analysis described a carefully curated campaign that avoided large, diverse and possibly critical audiences6.

The district

The district stretches from Chatham to Charlton counties.8 It takes in Savannah and its suburbs, three military installations and the ports of Savannah and Brunswick11, and inland counties that grow onions, tobacco, blueberries, cattle11. Chatham alone cast 111,830 votes in the statewide races, about two in five of the district's total.

The Watch's wave map uses the Senate and governor vote, and on it all 15 counties moved toward Democrats. Weighted by votes, the district moved 16.5 points. That is less than the state's 20.2-point move in the Senate race and more than its 10.9-point move for governor. The biggest swing was in Long County, which went from R+29.5 in 2024 to R+1.2, a 28-point move. Bryan and Liberty counties each moved 22 points; Liberty, home to Hinesville and Fort Stewart, went from D+17.4 to D+39.4. Chatham's Democratic margin nearly doubled, from 18 points to 34.5. Effingham moved nearly 20 points, to R+29.6. There, The Current reported, anger about higher prices and a local Republican decision to approve a $20 billion data center12 helped flip a state House seat. Pierce County moved least, 8 points, and stayed at R+69.4. No county changed sides.

Added together, the 2026 county figures put the Democratic side about a point ahead across the district. In the House race, Hollowell finished 2.8 points behind. County totals cannot show whether voters changed their minds or a different set of voters turned out. The data here covers only 2026 turnout, and The Current described the race as marked by significant shifts in voter turnout3.

What it means

The seat has stayed Republican for the 18th straight election dating to Jack Kingston's first win in 19921. No incumbent was on the ballot. Georgia's 14-member U.S. House delegation kept its partisan split of nine Republicans and five Democrats.13 Nationally, Democrats have 252 seats called to Republicans' 161, with 22 still uncalled; 218 is a majority.

The count is nearly complete. Ninety-nine percent of the expected vote is in, and Bacon and Charlton counties are at about 97 percent. Hollowell has yet to concede.14 Campaign officials and local Democratic Party leaders said they would ask election officials to cure ballots.15 But under Georgia law, a candidate can request a recount only if the margin is 0.5% or less14. She can still contest the race once the counties and the state certify the results.14 Aaron "A-Dot" Whitely, chairman of the Chatham County Democratic Party14, told WTOC that Hollowell had managed to "move the needle"14 further than any Democrat in the district in a long time. Kingston expressed confidence that his win would prevail, once ballot counts became official15.
