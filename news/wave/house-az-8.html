Hamadeh holds Arizona's 8th by 2.5 points as Maricopa swings left

Republican Abe Hamadeh beat Democrat Bernadette Greene-Placentia, a first-time candidate and long-haul trucker, by 7,057 votes in the Phoenix suburbs. Maricopa County moved 20 points toward Democrats in the governor's race.

Republican Abe Hamadeh will return to Congress from Arizona's 8th District. KTAR noted that in a year of Democratic gains, his bid for reelection in the West Valley district was much tighter1 than his first race. With 98.59 percent of the expected vote counted, Hamadeh had 146,140 votes to 139,083 for Democrat Bernadette Greene-Placentia. That is a lead of 7,057 votes, or 2.5 points. The Associated Press called the race at 1:25 p.m. Eastern on November 62, three days after the polls closed. KJZZ, Phoenix's public radio station, said it took longer than expected3.

The lead changed hands during the count. Greene-Placentia held an early lead over Hamadeh on election night, but Hamadeh pulled ahead on Wednesday and has since maintained a growing lead.4 The Surprise Independent reported that the first updates focused on early voting tallies5.

The race

Hamadeh is finishing his first term. He served as a Maricopa County prosecutor after getting his law degree6 and was an Army Reserve intelligence officer. He narrowly lost the 2022 attorney general race to Democrat Kris Mayes, and President Trump endorsed him for reelection4. He told The Center Square that he had supported the 2025 tax law known as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act7 and that he wanted to write Trump's border executive orders into law7. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce endorsed him in September.8

Greene-Placentia was a small-business owner and long-haul trucker making her first run for office1. AZFamily reported that she built her campaign around reproductive rights and strengthening Social Security and Medicare4. Her platform also put affordability first6. She raised far less money. Federal Election Commission records cited by The Center Square showed Hamadeh raising $1.2 million to her $209,358, and she took no money from political action committees9. She told CBS News, "we are not a well-funded campaign."10 Cook Political Report rated the district solidly Republican.10 The Watch found no reporting of major outside spending in the race.

The most widely covered event of the campaign was not about policy. On Aug. 31, Greene-Placentia told the Maricopa County Sheriff's Office that two men had knocked her unconscious and sexually assaulted her while she was repairing campaign signs in Sun City early on Aug. 14. The assault became public in an Oct. 7 Phoenix New Times story.1 Hamadeh called on authorities to investigate thoroughly11. On Oct. 20 he offered a $10,000 reward for information leading to an arrest and conviction. The sheriff's office says its investigation is still active.1

The district

The 8th covers the northern and western suburbs of Phoenix, including Glendale, Peoria, Sun City, Sun City West, Anthem and New River6. KTAR adds Surprise and part of Yavapai County12. The Watch's county table puts the district only in Maricopa County, so the Yavapai part is not in the comparison below.

Maricopa voted for Donald Trump by 3.5 points in 2024. This year it backed Democratic Gov. Katie Hobbs over Republican Andy Biggs by 16.2 points. That is a 20-point move, about a point more than the 18.7-point shift across Arizona, where Hobbs won by 13.1. Fox 10 reported that Hobbs spent more than $17 million, outspending Biggs by $12 million13. KJZZ reported that she spent much of the campaign arguing that Biggs was too extreme to be governor14.

The county figure includes Phoenix, Tempe and the East Valley as well as the West Valley. It cannot show how far the 8th District itself moved. The House result is the only district-level number here, and it comes from a different race. The count also cannot separate voters who changed their minds from changes in who voted. About 1.53 million votes were counted in Maricopa's governor race, and the totals alone do not show how much of the 20-point move came from voters switching sides and how much from who turned out or stayed home. Fox 10 reported that Biggs acknowledged lagging Republican turnout13.

What the result means

The 8th has stayed Republican, and its incumbent did not lose. Hamadeh was elected in 2024 after Debbie Lesko retired15. KJZZ said he was swept into office3 two years ago and this year narrowly held off3 Greene-Placentia.

Not every Arizona Republican held on. Fox 10 reported the projected defeats of Jay Feely (1st District) and Juan Ciscomani (6th District)16. Mark Lamb narrowly won Biggs' old seat in the 5th District.17 KJZZ reported that Democrats won the Governor's Office and both legislative chambers for the first time since 196618.

About 1.4 percent of the expected vote in the 8th is still uncounted. By the expected-vote estimate, that is roughly 4,000 ballots, fewer than Hamadeh's lead. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled. That gives Democrats a majority, so Hamadeh will return as a member of the minority.
