LaLota keeps Long Island's 1st District as Suffolk swings toward Democrats

Republican Nick LaLota won a third term by about 2 points over Chris Gallant, a first-time Democratic candidate. Suffolk County moved 11 points toward Democrats in the governor's race, and Kathy Hochul narrowly carried it.

Nick LaLota, the Republican who represents eastern and northern Suffolk County, won a third term on Tuesday. The county around him moved sharply toward Democrats. With 97 percent of the expected vote counted, LaLota had 152,546 votes to 145,869 for Democrat Christopher Gallant. That is a margin of 2.2 points out of 298,585 votes. The Associated Press called the race for LaLota on election night. RiverheadLOCAL reported that the margin was substantially smaller than in either of his previous elections.1 LaLota goes back to a House where Democrats have been called the winners in 252 seats. That is well past the 218 needed for a majority, and 22 races are still uncalled.

The race

Gallant, 372, had never run for office. He is an Army National Guard Black Hawk pilot and a former FAA air traffic controller.2 He has also served as a union leader and volunteered with the Copiague Fire Department.3 In June he won the Democratic primary against Lukas Ventouras.4 He told The Suffolk Times that affordability was the "No. 1 key priority" of his campaign2. He called for a permanent rise in the SALT deduction cap, an end to price gouging on groceries and gasoline, and bigger child tax credits.5 He also argued that last year's Republican tax-and-spending law put Social Security, SNAP, Medicaid, veterans' care and other benefits at risk.6

LaLota built his campaign around that same law. He presented it as his signature achievement because it raised the cap on the State and Local Tax deduction.5 The cap went up fourfold, to $40,000, and will drop back to $10,000 after 2029.5 He also pointed to federal money he secured for police vehicles and radios in Southold, an environmental cleanup in Riverhead and public-water hookups for homes in Calverton.3 He said he would not back future federal project funding for towns whose policies, in his view, deliberately obstruct cooperation with federal authorities.2

The two men never debated. Gallant turned down every debate invitation, including ones from Newsday, News 12 and The Express News Group.5 LaLota told Patch that Gallant initially accepted, then declined4. Gallant said he would rather meet voters at their doors and at town halls. LaLota, for his part, was invited to civic-group candidate forums that Gallant attended but did not go.5 In September, explicit photos of Gallant were published. He called them a "coordinated political hit" designed to drive him out of the race.5 CBS New York later reported a surge in his fundraising after he acknowledged the photos were of him.7

On election night, Gallant led early, but LaLota pulled ahead as more votes came in.8 Gallant conceded on Wednesday afternoon9. He said voters' concerns about rent, property taxes, your parents' healthcare, your kids leaving Long Island9 had not gone away.

The district

Since 2022 the district has covered the East End towns, central and northern Brookhaven, and the towns of Huntington and Smithtown.10 It leaves out Suffolk's South Shore, where both candidates live.10

Donald Trump carried Suffolk as a whole by 10.0 points in 2024. On Tuesday, Democrat Kathy Hochul carried it by 0.8 points in the governor's race, an 11-point move toward Democrats on 538,227 votes counted. That swing was bigger than the state's. Statewide, with about 93 percent counted, Hochul beat Bruce Blakeman, the Nassau County executive11, by 19.6 points, 7.0 points better than Kamala Harris did in New York. CBS New York reported that analysts thought Blakeman's close ties to Trump likely hurt him and pulled down Republicans across the island.12 The South Shore Press pointed to voter worry over fuel prices and affordability.13

Two cautions apply. First, the county figure covers all of Suffolk, including South Shore towns outside the 1st District. The House race drew a little more than half as many votes as the governor's race did countywide. Because these counts do not break the governor's vote down by district, they cannot show how far LaLota ran ahead of Blakeman inside NY-1. Second, a county's move is not proof that voters were persuaded. The count cannot tell voters who changed their minds apart from changes in who turned out.

Further down the ballot, Democrats did better still. Chris Murray unseated state Sen. Anthony Palumbo in a 1st Senate District seat held by Republicans since 1914.14

What it means

No Democrat has held the district in more than a decade.15 LaLota, who entered Congress in 202316, keeps the seat for Republicans, and no incumbent lost here. Still, the Shelter Island Reporter noted that the race was far closer than his 2024 win over Democrat John Avlon.17 LaLota said he had "mixed feelings"16. He was disappointed by Republican losses around the country and worried about the coming Democratic majorities, but said he would work with anyone who delivers for Long Island.14

About 3 percent of the expected vote has not been counted, and absentee ballots postmarked by Election Day are still arriving.9 To close a 6,677-vote gap, Gallant would need nearly all of those ballots, and he has already conceded.
