The wave · state by state · Minnesota

In Minnesota, the regional cities moved most as Flanagan won by 10

Peggy Flanagan beat Michele Tafoya by 10.1 points, about six points better than Kamala Harris did. The biggest swings came in counties around Duluth, Rochester, Moorhead, Austin and Worthington. The deep-red countryside moved far less, and one suburb moved the other way.

Peggy Flanagan, Minnesota's lieutenant governor, won the state's open U.S. Senate seat on Nov. 3. She beat Michele Tafoya 1,520,871 votes to 1,233,467, a margin of 10.1 points, with 98.85 percent of the expected vote counted. Kamala Harris carried Minnesota by 4.3 points in 2024, so the margin moved 5.9 points toward the Democrats. Flanagan will become the first Native American woman to be elected to the Senate.1 In the governor's race on the same ballot, Amy Klobuchar beat Lisa Demuth, the state House speaker, by 12.8 points.

The shift reached almost every county, but it was modest by this year's standards. Eighty-four of Minnesota's 87 counties moved toward the Democrats. The typical county moved 3.7 points; across the 2,775 counties counted nationally, the typical move was 14.1. Six counties that Donald Trump carried went for Flanagan: Anoka, Rice, Blue Earth, Winona, Carlton and Nicollet. None went the other way. The results are still unofficial; the State Canvassing Board meets to officially certify the election results on November 19.2

A race about the surge, fraud and Trump

Axios reported that the race unfolded against the backdrop of President Trump's immigration crackdown and scrutiny of fraud scandals in Minnesota.3 The Minnesota Star Tribune described two different campaigns. Tafoya sought to localize the race in Minnesota with an emphasis on fraud and public safety.4 Flanagan, by contrast, nationalized it, relentlessly connecting Tafoya to Trump, the Iran war, tariffs and gas prices.4

The immigration operation known as Operation Metro Surge came up again and again. It brought a historic surge of federal agents to Minnesota, weeks of clashes with protesters and the fatal shootings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti.5 After the election, state Republican chair Alex Plechash conceded that the surge and the war in Iran had mattered more than he anticipated.5 The Star Tribune also pointed to Tafoya's own campaign: she campaigned sporadically in Minnesota during the general election6. The paper also reported that national Republicans would not commit to spending big money on her campaign6.

The fraud attacks, the Star Tribune concluded, neither blocked her victory nor created an obvious break between her performance and the rest of the DFL ticket.7 Klobuchar still ran a little ahead. She received only about 50,000 more votes statewide8. Flanagan lost some urban fringe precincts in places such as Lakeville, western Hennepin County and the St. Croix River Valley that Klobuchar won in 2024.8

Where the map moved

The biggest swings came in counties built around regional cities, especially in southern Minnesota. Clay County (Moorhead) went from Harris by 0.5 points to Flanagan by 11.2, a move of 11. Mower County (Austin) moved 10 points, from Trump by 10.5 to Tafoya by just 0.5. Winona moved 9 points and flipped. St. Louis County (Duluth) moved 9, Olmsted (Rochester) 8, Blue Earth (Mankato) 8 and Freeborn (Albert Lea) 8.

The largest move of all was in Nobles County, home to Worthington. Trump won it by 35.6 points; Tafoya won it by 21.0, a move of 15. Much of this ground lies in the 1st Congressional District, where the House race is not settled. Democrat Jake Johnson [has] 323 votes more than Finstad9, and Rep. Brad Finstad is seeking a recount. WCCO radio reported that Somos the Future, a Latino voter group, worked in a lot of targeted areas with Latino populations, especially in CD110. The group's political director said cost of living mattered more to those voters than the surge did. County totals cannot confirm that.

In the Twin Cities, Ramsey County (St. Paul) moved 8 points, to Flanagan by 51.5. Hennepin County (Minneapolis) cast 664,237 votes, the most of any county, but moved only 3. Anoka, with 181,952 votes, went from Trump by 4.4 to Flanagan by 1.9. Carver County, in the west metro, moved the other way: from Trump by 5.4 to Tafoya by 7.0. Two small western counties, Traverse and Grant, also moved slightly right. Ticket-splitting was rare. In just two counties, Mower and Beltrami, Klobuchar and Republican Senate candidate Michele Tafoya won a majority of votes.8

Who moved: counties, not voters

The charts beside this section group counties. Counties are not voters, so a county's swing does not tell us how any one person voted.

Sorted by size, the mid-sized counties moved most. The ten counties casting 50,000 to 250,000 votes held 45 percent of the vote and moved 5.6 points, weighted by votes cast. The 40 counties under 10,000 votes moved 3.6. Hennepin, which cast 24 percent of the vote by itself, moved 3.3.

Sorted by the 2024 result, the 52 counties Trump won by 30 points or more cast a fifth of the vote and moved only 3.2. The three Harris counties in the 10-to-30-point range moved most, 6.3 weighted. Those are Dakota, St. Louis and Olmsted.

Turnout raises the question of persuasion. These counties cast 2,831,786 votes, 87 percent of their 2024 total. That is still a midterm record: the previous record was set in 2018, with 2.6 million votes cast.11 Turnout fell by a similar share almost everywhere. Seventy-eight counties, with 98 percent of the vote, cast between 80 and 95 percent of their 2024 totals. The link between a county's turnout drop and its swing was weak (a correlation of −0.17). It points the way a turnout story would predict: counties that kept more of their 2024 voters moved slightly less. Clay and Nobles each cast only about 80 to 82 percent of their 2024 vote and swung hardest; Hennepin cast 93 percent and moved 3. Democrats, Fox 9 reported, said ICE actions in Minnesota at the direction of President Donald Trump pushed many voters to the polls12. But the county totals cannot tell changed minds apart from changes in who voted, and the pattern here is too weak to settle it.
