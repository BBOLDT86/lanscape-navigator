The wave · state by state · Pennsylvania

All 67 Pennsylvania counties moved toward Shapiro, and 11 Trump counties flipped

Gov. Josh Shapiro won a second term by 22 points, a 24-point move from Donald Trump's 2024 margin. The biggest moves came in the southwestern gas counties and the northeastern coal valleys. The smallest came in the most Republican rural counties.

Josh Shapiro, the Democratic governor, won a second term on Nov. 3. He beat Stacy Garrity, the Republican state treasurer, by 22.1 points, 3,012,429 votes to 1,908,797, a margin of about 1.1 million votes. As of Saturday morning, 98.8 percent of the expected vote had been counted. Two years earlier, Donald Trump carried Pennsylvania by 1.7 points. That is a move of 23.8 points toward the Democrats, well above the national move of about 14 points across the counties The Watch has counted.

The race was never close. The Associated Press called it minutes after polls closed at 8 p.m.1 Spotlight PA reported that despite holding statewide office and receiving President Donald Trump's endorsement, Garrity was hampered by lackluster fundraising, limited support from the GOP2 and Shapiro's incumbency. WVIA reported that the Democrat out-fundraised Garrity by a 10-to-1 margin.3

Every county moved the same way

All 67 counties moved toward the Democrats. The typical county moved 23 points. Eleven counties that Trump carried went to Shapiro: Bucks, Lancaster, Berks, Northampton, Cumberland, Luzerne, Erie, Washington, Beaver, Monroe and Montour. The Philadelphia Inquirer's analysis counted 10, so its tally and the AP count used for this map differ by one. The smallest flip in our count is Montour, which Shapiro carried by 3.5 points on fewer than 7,000 votes.

Lancaster County went from Trump by 15.9 points to Shapiro by 4.4. LNP | LancasterOnline reported that Shapiro became the first Democratic gubernatorial candidate to win Lancaster County since Robert P. Casey won here in 1990.4 Bucks County, which Trump won by a tenth of a point, went to Shapiro by 25.7. In the same county, Democrat Bob Harvie ousted Brian Fitzpatrick5 in the U.S. House race.

Greene County, in the state's southwest corner, made the biggest move: 30 points, from Trump by 44.8 to Garrity by 14.6. Lackawanna County, home to Scranton, was close behind. It went from Harris by 2.8 to Shapiro by 32.8. Next came Schuylkill, Luzerne, Carbon and Washington. Washington went from Trump by 25.5 to Shapiro by 2.4, and Beaver from Trump by 20.7 to Shapiro by 6.1. Westmoreland ended up within 2.6 points.

That southwestern swing came in natural-gas country, where Garrity made drilling the center of her campaign. At the candidates' only debate, Garrity said she would support policies that promote natural gas and energy development in Pennsylvania.6 The county figures cannot say why those counties moved as they did.

The smallest moves came in the most Republican places. Fulton County moved 14 points and still went to Garrity by 58.6. Potter, Tioga and Bradford followed. Bradford, where Garrity grew up, close to the New York border, and still lives there today7, moved 16.5 points.

Counting is not finished. Lawrence County had 89 percent of its expected vote counted, Wyoming 90 percent and Lebanon 93 percent.

Who moved: turnout or persuasion

Counties are not voters, and these figures describe places, not people. The biggest moves came in mid-sized counties, those that cast 50,000 to 250,000 votes. Together they cast 48 percent of the vote and moved 24.1 points. The four largest, Allegheny, Philadelphia, Montgomery and Bucks, moved 22.8. The seven smallest moved 19.9.

The middle of the 2024 map moved most. The five counties Trump won by under 10 points moved 25.3 points, and the four Kamala Harris won by under 10 moved 25.0. The 43 counties Trump won by 30 or more moved 22.8. Philadelphia, already Democratic by 58.8 points, moved 20.1, the least of any large county.

Turnout fell everywhere. About 5 million votes were cast, 71 percent of the 2024 total. A drop like that can change a margin without anyone changing their mind, so a move is not the same as persuasion. But turnout does little to explain which counties moved most. The seven counties where the vote fell below 65 percent of 2024 moved 24.1 points, against 23.4 for the rest. The link between a county's turnout and its move was weak (a correlation of +0.14). Fayette and Fulton both cast about 61 percent of their 2024 vote. Fayette moved 26.5 points; Fulton moved 14.

Some of the reporting points to crossover voting. LancasterOnline reported that about 10,000 more voters chose Shapiro over Smucker's Democratic opponent4 in the U.S. House race there. In the 14th District, which covers Greene, Washington and Fayette counties, Guy Reschenthaler, the Republican incumbent, defeated Democrat Alan Bradstock8, even as Shapiro carried Washington. There was also a gap in who voted early. Of more than 1.1 million mail ballots returned before Election Day, 718,641 ballots — were from registered Democrats5, though that shows voters' party registration, not how they voted. The likeliest reading is that both turnout and crossover voting played a part. The county numbers cannot split the two.

What the campaign turned on

Garrity argued, the Inquirer reported, that under Shapiro's leadership, Pennsylvania is lagging other states in national rankings in its economic growth, education, and more.9 She also spent much of her campaign casting doubt on Shapiro's political ambitions as he sizes up an anticipated presidential run in 2028.1 The Pennsylvania Capital-Star reported that Shapiro tried to tie Garrity to her support for President Donald Trump, whose approval rating has cratered throughout his second term.10 In September, an Inquirer/New York Times/Siena poll put Trump's approval among the state's likely voters at 34%, with 63% disapproval11.

Data centers were the issue that drew the most argument. NBC News reported that Garrity came out in favor of a moratorium, followed by Shapiro announcing new, tougher mandates for developers12. Polls before the election had found Shapiro with even a notable sliver of support from voters who backed Trump two years ago.9

The result reached down the ballot. Democrats flipped four congressional seats and won complete control of the Pennsylvania legislature13, Spotlight PA reported. By the Inquirer's analysis, Shapiro outperformed his 2022 gubernatorial win in 65 of Pennsylvania's 67 counties14.
