Essex County swings nearly nine points to Markey as turnout falls

Massachusetts' third-biggest county, home to Lawrence, Lynn and Salem, gave Sen. Edward Markey a 29-point win over John Deaton. Kamala Harris won it by 20 points in 2024. This year's count was a quarter smaller.

What the county did

Essex County runs from the old mill cities of the Merrimack Valley, including Lawrence, Haverhill and Methuen, down through Lynn and Salem to the harbor towns of Cape Ann. It has 809,829 people, according to the United States Census Bureau1, and does not have a county seat1. Its votes are counted city by city and town by town.

On Nov. 3 the county gave Sen. Edward Markey, the Democrat, 186,678 votes to 99,877 for John Deaton, the Republican. That is a margin of 29.1 points with all of the expected vote counted. In 2024 Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 20.3 points, so the move was about 9 points toward the Democrats. Statewide, Markey won by 31.2 points with about 99 percent of the expected vote in. That was about 6 points better than Harris's 25.3.

All 14 Massachusetts counties moved left. Essex's move ranked third, behind Bristol (11 points) and Suffolk (7). It was ahead of Worcester (6), Middlesex (5), Plymouth (4) and Norfolk (3). In 2024 Essex was 5 points less Democratic than the state as a whole. This year the gap was 2 points.

Nationally, though, the shift was modest. The typical county counted so far moved 14 points left, and counties that started about where Essex did moved much further. Allegheny County, Pa., which Harris also won by 20.3, moved 23 points, as did Montgomery County, Pa., and Dallas County, Texas. Broward County, Fla., moved 15. Of the comparison counties, only Sacramento County, Calif., at 10 points, moved by about as little as Essex.

Turnout fell sharply. The county cast 298,772 votes, 75 percent of its 2024 total of 399,719, or about 101,000 fewer ballots. Even so, Markey's margin of roughly 86,800 votes was bigger than Harris's of roughly 81,000. Because the electorate shrank by a quarter, the 9-point move mixes two things the county count cannot pull apart: voters who changed sides and voters who stayed home. Turnout has always varied widely across the county. In 2024, according to the Impact Essex County data project, Lawrence had the lowest participation rate at 32% while West Newbury had the highest, at 90%2. CommonWealth Beacon reported that Lawrence's turnout this year landed at 25.6 percent, according to unofficial results3. That was a little higher than in 2022, when the city had the state's lowest midterm turnout. The Beacon also reported that more than 50,000 people were registered to vote in Lawrence this election, the highest that number has been in any election in well over a decade3.

Why it moved

There is no county-level exit poll, so the best evidence is statewide. In NBC News' Massachusetts exit poll, 74%4 of voters disapproved of Trump's handling of the presidency, and 72%5 said higher-than-usual prices were more the result of his policies. A Suffolk University/Boston Globe poll two weeks before the election found that Massachusetts voters' enduring distaste for President Trump supersedes more parochial concerns6.

The Senate race had its own North Shore story. Markey's primary challenger was Rep. Seth Moulton, who has represented Massachusetts's 6th congressional district, which encompasses the entire North Shore, since 20157. The Marblehead Independent reported that Moulton carried only a handful of communities, mostly North Shore towns such as Boxford and Topsfield8, and lost his home city of Salem. When he conceded, Moulton said Markey has my full support as our Democratic nominee9. In the race for Moulton's open House seat, WBUR reported that Democrat Dan Koh defeated Republican Micah Jones10.

Immigration enforcement was also an issue close to home. GBH News found that nearly a third of the total immigration arrests at Massachusetts courthouses in 2025 were in the region that includes Lynn, Lawrence and Lowell11. Nationally, the Associated Press reported that Latino voters moved sharply to the left in the midterm elections12. The county count cannot show whether that happened in Lawrence and Lynn, two heavily Latino cities. Answering that will take precinct results and the voter files.

What it means

Essex has long been one of the less reliably Democratic big counties in Massachusetts, a mix of working-class cities and well-off suburbs. This year it voted more like the state as a whole. The Republican campaign had pitched itself to those suburbs: in the governor's race, Minogue has tried to distance himself from the president by avoiding using his name and appealing to voters in the center13. That race moved only about 2 points statewide. The Watch's numbers don't show how Essex voted in it.

Some local questions remain open. In Lawrence, the Beacon reported that voters grappled Tuesday with the city's recent political scandals, historically low turnout, and uncontested races3. Results reported by the cities and towns are considered "Unofficial" until the official certified results are released14.
