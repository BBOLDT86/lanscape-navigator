The House · Florida 9

Darren Soto holds Florida's redrawn 9th District by 1,506 votes

Florida Republicans made Darren Soto's Central Florida district three times as big and pushed it south to Lake Okeechobee. The Kissimmee Democrat still beat Navy veteran Dan Green, finishing just above the state's automatic recount line.

Darren Soto, a Democrat from Kissimmee, won a sixth term in the House by 1,506 votes. In the Associated Press count, Soto had 143,314 votes and Republican Dan Green had 141,808, out of 285,122 cast. That is a margin of D+0.5 with 99 percent of the expected vote counted. Local stations reported that the Associated Press declared Soto the winner at 2:09 p.m. EST1 on Nov. 10, one week after Election Day2. His lead works out to a little more than half a point. Florida orders an automatic recount when the margin is half a point or less, and state election officials said no recount is required.3

The race

The map shaped the race before either nominee was chosen. Central Florida Public Media reported that Florida Republicans redrew the state's congressional map, making what was a majority Hispanic and Democratic-leaning area more white and Republican.4 Florida Politics called Soto the most endangered Democrat in Congress5.

Green came out of a seven-way Republican primary in August. He is a Vero Beach High School graduate and an active drilling reserve officer with 23 years of Navy service6 who served five tours in Iraq and Afghanistan. Trump appointed him deputy assistant secretary of defense for strategy and force development in 20197. CBS12 reported that Green focused on affordability too, arguing that reducing regulation and federal bureaucracy8 and encouraging competition could bring prices down. He also highlighted Florida's property insurance costs and concerns about overdevelopment.7

Soto went after the Republican voters the new map brought him. One ad campaign, which cost in the high six figures, featured a conservative voter from Vero Beach. The campaign has stressed that Soto belongs to the congressional "Problem Solvers Caucus."9 Another ad promotes his record of capping prescription drug costs while asserting that Republican Dan Green would cut Social Security.10 He also made Spanish-language outreach a central part of the campaign. Afterward he summed up his pitch as "lowering costs, stopping corruption, protecting our health care."11

Soto had more money. Florida Politics reported that he raised more than $3.65 million, outpacing Green's $2.69 million. Green's total included $2.24 million in candidate loans5. Central Florida Public Media found that outside groups accounted for nearly a quarter of the money spent on the race. Soto has benefited from spending by the Progressive Turnout Project and a fund promoting Hispanic candidates.12 On the Republican side, the House GOP campaign arm put Green in its MAGA Majority program13.

The district

The new 9th District stretch[es] from Lake Nona south to Lake Okeechobee, sprawling across an area the size of Connecticut covering seven counties.11 The old seat was Osceola County plus parts of Orange. The new one adds Polk, Indian River, Highlands, Okeechobee and Glades counties.14 The redraw cut the Hispanic share of the district from 55% to 40%.15 WPBF reported that Green held a sizable vote advantage in Glades, Highlands, Indian River, Okeechobee and Polk counties16, while Soto won big in the more populous Osceola and Orange counties.

The Watch's data for this seat has only the districtwide total. There is no county-by-county count and no 2024 baseline, so the wave map cannot show how each part of the district moved. Even with those numbers, a county's shift would not tell us whether voters changed their minds or whether different people turned out. Turnout clearly rose in Osceola: a county election official called it "the highest turnout in a midterm general [for Osceola] since 1994."15

The statewide results give some context. Ashley Moody won the Senate race by 2.9 points and Byron Donalds won for governor by 1.3. In 2024, Donald Trump carried Florida by 13.1, so the Senate race moved about 10 points toward Democrats and the governor's race about 12. Republicans still won both.

What it means

Before the redraw, Central Florida Public Media noted, the seat had been held by a Democrat since 201317. Soto has held it since 2017. The station said the result reflects the seismic shift toward Democrats this cycle15. Soto credited his heavy campaigning across the new district and voters' frustration with the redistricting. The station also reported that voters were also galvanized by a divisive property tax ballot measure, Amendment 3, and by President Trump's unpopular governance15.

The map may still change in court. When the Florida Supreme Court declined to block it in June, the lawsuit will continue in lower courts.18

The county totals are unofficial until they are certified. WPBF said it contacted the Dan Green campaign for comment and is awaiting a response.16 Nationally, AP has called 254 House seats for Democrats and 167 for Republicans, with 14 still uncalled.
