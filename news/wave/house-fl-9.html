Soto leads by 1,507 votes in Florida's redrawn, Republican-leaning 9th

Rep. Darren Soto leads Republican Dan Green by half a point in a district redrawn this spring to run from Kissimmee to Vero Beach and Lake Okeechobee. AP has not called it, and the margin sits near Florida's recount line.

Four days after the polls closed, Rep. Darren Soto, the Kissimmee Democrat, leads Republican Dan Green 143,311 to 141,804 in Florida's 9th Congressional District. That is a gap of 1,507 votes out of 285,115, with 99% of the expected vote counted. The Associated Press has not called the race. News 6 in Orlando reported that Soto has declared victory in the newly redrawn District 9, but the results are still pending.1 Green's campaign manager told Spectrum News 13 only that he would be in touch when more was known.2

The race

Green, 50, grew up in Vero Beach.3 He graduated from Vero Beach High School, where his mother taught for 25 years, and has served 23 years in the Navy, including five tours in Iraq and Afghanistan.4 He was working at the Pentagon on Sept. 11, 2001, when the building was attacked.3 In 2019, President Donald Trump named him a deputy assistant secretary of defense.5 In August he won a seven-candidate Republican primary, beating the party's 2024 nominee among others.6 Trump endorsed him.7

Green campaigned on property insurance costs and overdevelopment8 and made national security the center of his campaign.8 Soto spent the fall in cattle-ranch and citrus-grove country that was new to him.9 He said his message was the same across the whole district: lower costs, protect health care and stop corruption, along with restoring the Indian River Lagoon and helping growers and cattle ranchers.10 A memo from Soto's pollster said about two-thirds of voters were worried about beef imports, which matters in a district full of cattle farms.11 The issue followed the White House's decision last year to quadruple the low-tariff quota for beef from Argentina.12

Florida Politics reported that Soto raised more than $3.65 million and Green $2.69 million. Of Green's total, $2.24 million was loans from the candidate himself.13 House Majority PAC, the super PAC tied to House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, spent about $2.86 million in the district, including $1.1 million in the West Palm Beach TV market,14 which reaches the areas added under the new map.14 Central Florida Public Media reported that Trump's super PAC sent about a third of a million dollars to help Green.15 Soto got help from the Progressive Turnout Project and a fund that backs Hispanic candidates.15 The Cook Political Report rated the seat as leaning Republican.16

The district

In May, Gov. Ron DeSantis signed a new congressional map that changed 21 of Florida's 28 House districts.17 Soto's old district was Osceola County, a small part of Orange County and a sliver of Polk County.18 The new district is three times as big, about the size of Connecticut.18 It runs about 130 miles from near the Orlando airport south to Glades County and takes in all of Indian River, Highlands and Okeechobee counties.18 Rollins College political scientist Patrick Rickert said the old district was about 55% Hispanic and the new one is about 40%.18 Soto says the district's races are decided in three places: Central Florida, the heartland and the coast.19

The Watch's wave compares each county's 2024 Harris–Trump margin with this year's statewide Senate and governor vote. All seven of the district's counties moved toward Democrats, by a combined +14.1 when weighted by votes. Statewide, Florida moved 10.2 points in the Senate race and 11.8 in the governor's race. Osceola moved the most, 20 points, flipping from R+1.4 for Trump to D+18.4. Orange went from D+13.6 to D+28.5 and Polk from R+20.7 to R+6.7, but only part of each county is in the district. Okeechobee moved 13 points and Glades 11, both still deeply Republican. Highlands moved 5 points and Indian River 4. Indian River, Green's home county, remains at R+22.9.

These are whole-county numbers from the statewide races, not from the House race. They also can't show whether voters changed their minds or whether different voters turned out. The counties are very different in size: Indian River, Highlands, Okeechobee and Glades together cast 132,185 votes, about the same as Osceola's 131,845. The Orlando Sentinel credited strong Democratic turnout and a favorable national environment for Democrats.6

What the result means

Every election rater had listed Soto as Florida's most endangered Democrat.20 The new map turned his Democratic-leaning seat into one that Trump won in 2024.21 The Lake Okeechobee News noted that if his lead holds, he would be the first Democrat to represent Okeechobee's district since Tim Mahoney in 2009.22

By the count above, the gap is just over half a percent of the votes cast. Under Florida law, a recount starts automatically if the margin falls below 0.5%. Officials then have five days to finish it, unless one candidate concedes first.19 Osceola County Supervisor of Elections Mary Jane Arrington said her office had more than 100 provisional ballots to review Friday. She said it might be late Friday or Saturday before anyone knows whether a recount is required.2 Kari Ewalt of the same office said she did not believe a recount at that margin would change the outcome.23

Some outlets have reported a winner before the official count is done, and they disagree. The Highlands News-Sun reported that Soto will keep his seat.24 A story on CBS12's website said Green had won.25 AP has not called the race for either candidate.

Statewide, Republicans won both top races. Ashley Moody beat Angie Nixon for the Senate by 2.9 points, and Byron Donalds beat David Jolly for governor by 1.3. Nationally, AP has called 252 House seats for Democrats, more than the 218 needed for a majority, so the 9th will not decide control of the House. It is one of 22 seats still uncalled.
