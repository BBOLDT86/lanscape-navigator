Orange County moves eight points left, less than California as a whole

Xavier Becerra carried Orange County by 10.5 points, up from Kamala Harris's 2.6 in 2024. The county moved left by eight points. That beat most California counties but trailed the state and the nation.

Two years ago, Orange County split almost evenly. Kamala Harris won it by 2.6 points. On Nov. 3, Democratic former secretary of health and human services Xavier Becerra1 beat Republican political commentator Steve Hilton1 in the county by 10.5 points, 630,711 votes to 510,460, with about 95 percent of the expected vote counted. That is a move of about eight points toward the Democrats. Ballots are still being counted, so the final figures may change.

What the county did

Statewide, Mr. Becerra led by 31.4 points with about 91 percent of the expected vote counted. That is 11.2 points better than Ms. Harris's 20.1-point margin in California. Orange County ranks 25th of the state's 58 counties by the size of its move. Fifty-four of the 58 moved left, and the typical county moved about six points. Its southern California neighbors moved further. Los Angeles County moved 14 points. Riverside County went from R+1.3 to D+16.2, and San Bernardino County moved 21 points. San Diego County moved eight, the same as Orange. Outside California, Clark County, Nevada, also started at D+2.6 in 2024 and moved 13 points. Palm Beach County, Florida, moved about 10, and Kent County, Michigan, about eight. Nationally, the typical county moved 14 points. The biggest counties generally moved less, measured in points.

Orange County has a 2025 population estimate of 3,149,5072. According to the Census Bureau, 34.5%3 of residents are Hispanic and 25.0%3 are Asian. The median household income is $116,2894, compared with $99,1224 statewide. Patch reported that in September, 36.46 percent of voters registered as Democrats, 33.59 percent as Republicans.5

About 1.14 million votes have been counted for governor, roughly 82 percent of the 1.39 million cast for president in 2024. A county-level count cannot separate voters who changed sides from voters who stayed home or newly turned out. Orange County's eight-point move mixes both. LAist reported that county turnout officially surpassed the 2022 midterm election — although still short of recent presidential elections6.

Who moved, and why

Over the summer, the race looked close. A UC Irvine poll of Orange County found Republican Steve Hilton leads Democrat Xavier Becerra 47% to 44% — a margin well within the poll's ±4.2-point error range7. The poll found Mr. Becerra leads among Latino voters, Asian-American voters, women and Independents, yet these groups trail in reported certainty of voting7. Only about half of Latino residents and 59% of Asian-Americans say they are absolutely certain to vote in November, compared with 82% of White residents.7 The final count ran well ahead of that poll for Mr. Becerra. The county numbers cannot show whether more of his groups voted or whether some voters changed their minds.

An earlier UC Irvine poll found that the way the administration has carried out immigration enforcement and deportations is deeply unpopular among Orange County voters, many of whom are immigrants themselves8. That reporting noted that about 30% of county residents were born outside the United States9. Patch reported that voters also faced high gas and grocery prices, the effects of climate change on wildfires and rising seas amid a shortage in insurance coverage5 and high housing costs.

National figures point the same way, but they do not measure Orange County. In a voter survey reported by NPR, Latinos swung 21 points toward Democratic House candidates, compared to two years ago.10 Asian Americans also sharply diverged from 2024, also going 21 points more in Democrats' favor10.

What it means for the county's politics

The biggest local effects were further down the ballot. Voice of OC reported that several of Orange County's incumbent Republican state legislators are on track to lose their seats to challengers this year11. In the 36th Senate District, Republican Sen. Tony Strickland wrote on Facebook that "it looks like the numbers aren't going to turn in our direction"11 against Democrat Chris Duncan. Republican Assemblywoman Laurie Davies was also trailing. Voice of OC reported that Democrat San Juan Capistrano Councilman Sergio Farias11 held a lead of more than 3,700 votes. KCRA reported that Tri Ta conceded in his race, according to the Orange County Register.12

Republicans held on in some places, at least for now. Republican Assembly members Phillip Chen and Kate Sanchez11 were leading their challengers. In the coastal 72nd Assembly District, Republican Huntington Beach Councilwoman Gracey Van Der Mark expanded her lead over Democrat Chris Kluwe13. That race had not been decided when Voice of OC last reported on it.

In Congress, the new Proposition 50 map put two Republican incumbents in the same Orange County seat. Incumbent Ken Calvert (R) defeated incumbent Young Kim (R) in the general election to represent California's 40th Congressional District14. Voice of OC described it as a district that has a stronger Republican voter registration15.
