Orange County moves left, but half as far as California does

Xavier Becerra carried Orange County by about 8 points, 5 points better than Kamala Harris did there. But the state's second-biggest county by votes moved about half as much as California did, and much less than its inland neighbors.

Democrat Xavier Becerra carried Orange County over Republican Steve Hilton by 7.9 points, 507,683 votes to 433,007, with 79 percent of the expected vote counted. That is 5 points more Democratic than the county's 2024 vote, when Kamala Harris beat Donald Trump there by 2.6 points. Statewide, Becerra won by 30.4 points, 10.3 points better than Harris did in California. The second-biggest county in the state moved about half as far.

What the county did

Orange County ranks 32nd of California's 58 counties by how far it moved. Its 5-point move matches the typical county in the state, where 54 of 58 moved left. It falls short of the 9-point average when counties are weighted by votes, because the big counties moved more. San Diego County moved exactly as much. Los Angeles County moved 12 points. The inland neighbors moved much further: Riverside and San Bernardino counties both voted for Trump in 2024, then moved 17 and 19 points and ended up about twice as Democratic as Orange County. Outside California, Clark County, Nevada, started from the same D+2.6 and moved 13 points. Palm Beach County, Florida, moved 10. Nationally, the typical county moved 14 points. Among the eight biggest counties listed, Orange and San Diego moved the least.

Orange County is a large, suburban county with a mixed electorate. LAist puts it at 34 cities and over 3.1 million residents1. In September, Patch reported, 36.46 percent of voters were registered as Democrats, 33.59 percent as Republicans.2

So far, 940,690 votes have been counted, 68 percent of the 2024 total, with about a fifth of the expected vote still out. Before Election Day, Registrar of Voters Bob Page said turnout was running "significant below" the presidential election in 2024, but much higher than 2022.3 On election night, Voice of OC found that 40 had wait times over 41 minutes4 among in-person voting sites at 8:30 p.m. Because the 2026 electorate is smaller and different from 2024's, the county's 5-point move mixes two things: who turned out and who changed sides. The county totals cannot separate them.

Why it moved

Statewide, CalMatters reported, Becerra reminded voters of Hilton's link to Trump at every turn5, while Hilton portrayed Becerra as the embodiment of the California political status quo5. In the last poll before the election, from UC Berkeley's Institute of Governmental Studies, voters increasingly identified opposition to President Trump's administration6 as a top priority, ahead of the cost of living, CBS Sacramento reported. Hilton conceded in Garden Grove. The Sacramento Bee reported that he joked "maybe this isn't the best year to be running for office as a Republican"7.

Local Republicans saw it coming. Will O'Neill, chairman of the county GOP, told City News Service before Election Day that "Democratic turnout in (the district) is stronger than I'd like to see"3, meaning Supervisor Katrina Foley's district. Mike Moodian, a political scientist at Chapman University, told Voice of OC that state legislative races along the coast, usually reliably red8, had become unusually close. None of the reporting reviewed for this piece explains why the county as a whole moved less than the state.

What it means locally

The biggest local question is control of the Board of Supervisors. Democrats hold a 3-2 majority on the board. Republicans have been hoping to win back control since 20221, LAist reported, and need to win one of these two races1 to get it. Foley is running in a district where Republicans hold a voter registration advantage of around 20,000 voters9. In the other race, LAist reported that Democrat Connor Traut and Republican Tim Shaw are so close they have taken turns in the lead.1 The two outlets read the early count differently. As of Wednesday evening, Voice of OC said Democrats were on track to keep their majority9, while LAist said the final outcome remains uncertain with many votes left to count.1 Neither race has been called.

Republican voters are still a large force in parts of the county. After the Proposition 50 redistricting, Voice of OC reported, Republican Reps. Ken Calvert and Young Kim faced each other in a new 40th District that reaches into Rancho Santa Margarita, Anaheim Hills and Mission Viejo10. O'Neill said the new map made it a lot harder to flip seats3.

The margin may still change. In June, UC Irvine political scientist Louis DeSipio said that over the past decade, vote-by-mail ballots that roll in after election day favor Democrats11, Patch reported. The county is scheduled to certify its results on or before Dec.1
