The wave · the biggest counties · Orange County, California

Orange County moves toward Becerra, but only about half as far as California

Xavier Becerra carried Orange County by about 9 points, a 6-point move left from Kamala Harris's narrow 2024 win there. That was a little over half of California's shift, and far smaller than the swings next door in Riverside and San Bernardino.

Steve Hilton ended his campaign for governor in Orange County. On election night he conceded at the Hyatt Regency Orange County in Garden Grove1, LAist reported. The county where he spoke went to his opponent. With about 90 percent of the expected vote counted, Democrat Xavier Becerra led Hilton in Orange County 571,278 to 476,031, a margin of 9.1 points.

What Orange County did

In 2024, Kamala Harris carried the county over Donald Trump by 2.6 points out of 1,390,965 votes. Becerra's 9.1-point lead is a move of 6 points toward the Democrats. Statewide, Becerra leads by 31.5 points with 83.3 percent of the expected vote counted, up from Harris's 20.1. That is a statewide move of 11.4 points, nearly twice Orange County's.

Orange County ranks 29th of California's 58 counties by size of move, close to the typical county's shift of 6 points. Fifty-four of the 58 moved left. The big counties around it moved more. Los Angeles moved 14 points and San Diego 8. Riverside went from R+1.3 to D+15.7, a 17-point move, and San Bernardino went from R+2.1 to D+18.7, a 21-point move. Of the big California counties, only Santa Clara moved less, by 3 points. Elsewhere, Clark County, Nevada, started from the same D+2.6 and moved 13 points. Palm Beach moved 10 and Kent County, Michigan, 8. Harris and Fort Bend counties in Texas each moved 27. Nationally the typical county moved 14 points, and among the eight biggest counties only Orange moved less than San Diego.

Orange County is home to 3,149,5072 people, according to the Census Bureau's 2025 estimate. Of them, 34.5%2 are Hispanic, 25 percent are Asian and 35.5 percent are non-Hispanic white, and the median household income is $116,289. Patch reported that its voters are split almost evenly by party, with 36.46 percent of voters registered as Democrats, 33.59 percent as Republicans.3

Why it moved, and who moved

Turnout is the first thing to account for. So far the count equals 75 percent of the 2024 vote. On election day, Registrar of Voters Bob Page said the pace of voting was significantly below the presidential election in 2024, but much higher than 2022.4 Because so many fewer people voted than in 2024, the county-level count cannot show how much of the 6-point move came from voters who switched sides and how much from who voted and who stayed home.

The campaign was fought over national issues. The Associated Press reported that Becerra repeatedly tied his opponent, Republican Steve Hilton, to the president5. Hilton, for his part, argued that one-party rule was to blame for the state's high cost of living and homelessness crisis.5 In AP's voter poll, most voters who said the economy was the top issue facing the country supported Becerra5. In October, the Public Policy Institute of California found that cost of living is most likely to be chosen as the state's top issue across parties, regions, and demographic groups.6 LAist reported LAist reported that Hilton had done his best to separate himself from President Donald Trump, who backed his campaign.1

LAist cited national exit polls showing that Latinos swung 21 points toward Democratic House candidates7 and that Asian Americans moved by a similar amount. Both groups are large in Orange County. Those figures are national, though, and no poll of Orange County alone was available. None of the reporting found for this piece explains why the county moved so much less than its Inland Empire neighbors. In his concession speech, Hilton said, "Maybe this wasn't the best year to be running for office as a Republican"8.

What it means for the county's politics

The county's contested House seats stayed with Democrats. NBC Los Angeles reported that Rep. Derek Tran, a son of Vietnamese refugees, defeated Vo, a retired police officer and another son of Vietnamese refugees9, in the 45th District. The Irvine Community News & Views reported that Rep. Dave Min won re-election in the 47th. The Proposition 50 redistricting changed the map too. Voice of OC reported that it led to Republican Reps. Ken Calvert and Young Kim running in the new 40th district that has a stronger Republican voter registration.10 Further down the ballot, Voice of OC found the coastal state Senate race between Republican Sen. Tony Strickland and Democrat Chris Duncan very close in early returns.

Several of these races are still being counted. The registrar's office updates results each weekday until the county certifies on or before Dec. 3.11 For now, Orange County voted Democratic again, but Becerra's lead there is less than a third of his lead statewide. That leaves the county one of California's most closely divided big counties.
