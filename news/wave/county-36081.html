Queens swings 20 points toward Democrats as Hochul wins back the borough

Two years after Kamala Harris carried Queens by less than 25 points, Gov. Kathy Hochul won it by 44. The move was the second-largest of any county in New York. Flushing and the Rockaways show where Republicans held on.

In 2024, Queens gave Kamala Harris a 24.6-point margin over Donald Trump. On Nov. 3, Gov. Kathy Hochul carried the borough over Republican Bruce Blakeman, the Nassau County executive, by 44.2 points, 355,969 votes to 137,468, with 92 percent of the expected vote counted. That move of 20 points toward the Democrats was the second-largest of New York's 62 counties.

What Queens did

Statewide, Hochul won by 19.6 points with 93 percent of the vote counted. That was 7.0 points better for Democrats than Harris's 12.7-point margin in 2024. Queens moved almost three times as far. Only 31 of New York's 62 counties moved left at all, and the typical county did not move. Among the state's biggest counties, only Brooklyn (Kings County) came close, moving 15 points to a 59.0-point Democratic margin. Manhattan barely changed. Westchester started almost exactly where Queens did in 2024 but moved only 5 points. Nassau and Suffolk, the Long Island counties next door, both tipped narrowly to Hochul after voting for Trump.

Nationally, the typical county moved 14 points. Queens's move puts it in the same range as other big Democratic counties: Dallas (23 points), Montgomery County outside Philadelphia (23) and Wake County in North Carolina (22). It also topped Franklin County in Ohio (14). Even so, Hochul won the borough with 72 percent of the vote over Republican Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman – although it was her lowest margin of victory among the boroughs she won1, the Queens Daily Eagle noted.

Queens is one of the country's main immigrant gateways. The Census Bureau estimates its population at 2,358,1822, with 47.6%2 born abroad. Asian and Hispanic residents are each 29.5%2 of the population, Black residents 21.4 percent and non-Hispanic white residents 22.1 percent.

Why it moved

Both campaigns worked the borough. According to the Eagle, Blakeman is running on a conservative platform that he claims would lower taxes and utility bills.3 Hochul ran on her resistance to the Trump administration and policies like universal childcare, which she has championed alongside Mayor Zohran Mamdani.3 Her running mate was Adrienne Adams, the former City Council speaker and a lifelong Queens resident. On the final weekend, Hochul and Mamdani campaigned together in Queens. Blakeman held a rally at Juniper Valley Park and went to a Queens Chinese restaurant, where he decried Local Law 97, which is meant to slash emissions.4 In August, City & State reported that Blakeman was copying the approach Lee Zeldin used in 2022, when the Republican defeated the governor by more than 500 votes in Assembly District 40, which covers most of the neighborhood5 of Flushing.

Living costs were the main issue. In a Quinnipiac poll, New York City voters were slightly more6 likely than voters elsewhere to name immigration and affordable housing. In NBC News' New York exit poll, nine in 10 voters called the cost of living crucial or a major factor in their vote.

On who moved, Democratic consultant Alex Elmasri told New York Focus that Hochul's gains downstate came mainly from traditional blue voters who returned to the party this year after defecting to support Trump two years ago7. New York Focus also found that Black, Latino and Asian voters swung heavily toward Democrats this year after drifting toward the GOP in recent years.7 The Eagle's precinct results show the same pattern, with limits. In Downtown Flushing, Hochul raised her share in key election districts, but Blakeman still won most of the districts1 there. Hochul narrowly carried the northern Queens council district of Vickie Paladino, who had endorsed Blakeman. The only Queens Council district Hochul lost was the one belonging to Republican Joann Ariola1. In Far Rockaway, four heavily Orthodox Jewish districts1 voted for Blakeman for governor and for the Democrat for the Assembly.

Turnout is the other half of the story. Queens cast 494,464 ballots for governor in the count so far, 70 percent of its 2024 total. With nearly a third fewer voters than in 2024, the county count cannot tell how much of the move came from people changing sides and how much from who turned out. Turnout also varied by neighborhood. Gothamist reported during early voting that Southeast Queens, home to many Black Democratic voters, was turning out at higher rates than in 2025, while some of the drops in the city's early voting turnout were in parts of the city where Mamdani overperformed last year.8

What it means

Down the ballot, Queens incumbent Democrats breezed to victory Tuesday, defeating Republican challengers by significant margins across all corners of the borough.9 Democrat Pesach Osina held the open Rockaway Assembly seat. Adams will become the second-ever lieutenant governor from Queens.9

The results also show where Republicans still win in Queens: the western Rockaway peninsula, Ariola's district and parts of Downtown Flushing. Flushing had drifted toward Republicans since 2022, and the Eagle wrote that this year's results show Democrats holding the line, and reversing trends from two years ago.1 About 8 percent of the expected Queens vote is still uncounted, so the final margin may change.
