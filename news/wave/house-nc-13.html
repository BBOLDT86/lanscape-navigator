The House · North Carolina 13

A Sanford Democrat unseats Brad Knott in the ring around Raleigh

Paul Barringer, a first-time candidate from Lee County, won North Carolina's 13th District by 2.4 points as every county in the Republican-drawn seat moved 19 to 28 points toward Democrats in the Senate race.

Paul Barringer, a Democrat and political newcomer1 from Sanford2, has unseated first-term Republican Rep. Brad Knott in North Carolina's 13th Congressional District. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, Barringer had 159,981 votes and Knott had 152,329. That is a lead of 7,652 votes, or 2.4 points, out of 316,789 cast. Steven Swinton is listed as an independent in the AP results file, but the Johnston County Report identified him as the Libertarian candidate3. He drew 4,479 votes. The Associated Press has called the race for Barringer.

A newcomer against a quiet incumbent

The Sanford Herald described Barringer as a lawyer, health care consultant and tree farmer whose family has owned property in Lee County since the 1800s.4 According to NC Newsline, he spent about 20 years living in Maryland and working in Washington before returning to Lee County to care for his ailing mother.5 Last fall he rode his bike from Lee County to Yanceyville, 183 miles by the route he took through the district6. At an early-voting site in Clayton, he told NC Newsline his bet was that a moderate running in a gerrymandered district "might have a chance."6 His campaign emphasized lowering living costs, improving access to affordable health care, combating corruption in Washington and representing rural communities.2

Knott, a former prosecutor endorsed by Trump, won the district in 2024 after a primary runoff.6 The district wraps around the Triangle but does not include his home in Raleigh7, and WUNC reported that he had kept a relatively low profile since his election two years ago.7 Barringer began airing TV ads shortly before early voting started. Knott, who had not run ads this cycle either, quickly followed.5 Knott's ad accused Barringer of being from Maryland; Barringer is a Sanford native who had recently moved back from Washington.7 WUNC reported that Barringer's campaign had raised $1.2 million and Knott $1.6 million.7

National Democrats added the seat to their "Districts in Play" program at the start of October5. They focused on Knott's vote for President Donald Trump's "One Big Beautiful Bill" Act, the tax-cut and spending-cut package8. Even so, Cook Political Report moved the race only from "solid Republican" to "likely Republican"5 in late October. Republican money was moving elsewhere. On Oct. 2, NBC News reported that a top Republican super PAC was canceling its ad spending in North Carolina's Senate race9. WUNC noted that Knott's ads started not long after national Republican groups stopped their ads in support of U.S. Senate candidate Michael Whatley7. NC Newsline reported that AP's voter survey found the economy and "the state of democracy" tied as North Carolina voters' top issues. Immigration and crime, the issues Republican candidates campaigned on most, ranked far lower.1

A fish hook around Raleigh

State lawmakers redrew the 13th in 2023 to heavily favor a Republican.6 WRAL describes it as starting in Caswell County and stretching east along the state's northern border before dropping into Franklin, Johnston and Harnett counties. It then turns west to take in the southern edge of Wake County and ends in Lee County.10 It includes all of six counties along with parts of Wake and Granville.2

On The Watch's wave map, every one of those counties moved toward Democrats in the Senate race, which Roy Cooper won statewide by 19.6 points. Weighted by votes, the district's counties moved 22.6 points from the 2024 presidential vote, nearly the state's 22.8. Johnston and Lee moved the most, 28 points each. Johnston went from R+21.5 for Trump to D+6.0 for Cooper. Lee, Barringer's home county, went from R+17.5 to D+10.1. Harnett moved 26 points, to a bare D+0.6. Franklin (21 points) and Granville (19) also flipped. Person and Caswell, at the rural northern end, moved 19 points each but stayed Republican, at R+4.6 and R+5.7. Wake went from D+25.6 to D+47.3, but those figures cover the whole county. The district holds only its southern edge and a thin piece east of Raleigh5.

The House race was closer than those Senate numbers. Cooper carried four of the six whole counties, three of them by more than Barringer's districtwide 2.4 points. The county count also cannot say why the lines moved. Johnston cast 88,773 votes in the Senate race and Harnett 44,986, but those totals cannot tell Trump voters who switched apart from Trump voters who stayed home while Democrats turned out. NC Newsline reported that Democrats built a strong lead in early voting1.

A seat drawn red, now blue

Knott becomes a one-term congressman. Democrat Wiley Nickel, the previous incumbent, chose not to run again after Republicans redrew the district to heavily favor the GOP.8 Knott then won the seat easily in 2024.8 The Rant, a Sanford news site, noted that Barringer would likely be the first member of Congress from Lee County11. CBS 17 reported Barringer's statement that he and Knott "had a good conversation and I thanked him for his service."12

The seat is part of a larger shift. As of Thursday, NPR reported, the AP had called nine of the state's 14 House seats for Democrats and none for Republicans, for a delegation that entered Election Day with 10 Republicans and four Democrats.13 Nationally, 252 seats have been called for Democrats and 161 for Republicans, with 22 still uncalled.

The results are still unofficial. With 99 percent of the expected vote counted, county boards still have to certify. Harnett County's board is scheduled to meet on November 13, 2026, to certify its official results.14
