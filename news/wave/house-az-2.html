Eli Crane narrowly leads Jonathan Nez as northern Arizona shifts left

Rep. Eli Crane, a Freedom Caucus Republican, leads former Navajo Nation President Jonathan Nez by 3,885 votes with nearly all ballots counted. Every county the district touches moved toward Democrats in the governor's race.

With 98.93 percent of the expected vote counted, Republican Rep. Eli Crane leads Democrat Jonathan Nez 162,741 to 158,856 in Arizona's 2nd Congressional District. That is a 1.2-point margin out of 327,575 votes. The Associated Press had not called the race as of Saturday morning. Curtis Goodwin, a third candidate, has 5,978 votes, which is more than the gap between the top two. AP lists him as an independent, but the Arizona Daily Sun and the Navajo Times identify him as the Libertarian candidate1, 2.

Crane beat Nez easily in 2024. This time, KNAU reported, the lead flip-flopped multiple times between the two as counties tallied additional ballots3 on election night and early Wednesday. The Daily Courier in Prescott reported that Crane moved ahead early Thursday4.

A rematch in a different year

Crane is a former Navy SEAL and member of the House Freedom Caucus5, and he was seeking a third term. Axios noted that he helped oust former House Speaker Kevin McCarthy in 20236. Nez, a former Navajo Nation president, sought to become the first Native American to represent Arizona in Congress5.

Nez was betting on what the Arizona Mirror called voter "buyer's remorse." The Mirror reported that his campaign focused on Crane's votes to continue the Iran War, give tax breaks to companies that build data centers and cut food assistance and healthcare programs7, including through the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act"7. KNAU reported that Nez pointed to the war with Iran pushing up the costs of gas and groceries8. He told the Mirror he was going after independents who had backed Trump and Crane last time.

According to KNAU, Crane pivoted to a softer message9, declined to debate Nez9 and focused on economic and immigration issues3. A Crane spokesman told Axios that rural voters were tired of career politicians like Nez6.

Outside money favored Crane. A KTAR News analysis of FEC filings found that outside groups spent $7.6 million after the July primary, and groups backing Crane outspent those backing Nez by more than 2-to-110. The largest spender, No Going Back PAC, put $3.4 million into supporting Crane and opposing Nez10. The Arizona Daily Star reported that one of that group's TV ads showed photos of Myron Lizer, who served as Nez's vice president11, instead of Nez, and has since been taken down11. On the Democratic side, Axios reported that the district was among the districts where House Majority PAC has scaled back its spending recently6. KNAU noted that as recently as September, the Cook Political Report considered the race a likely Republican win.3

Flagstaff, Prescott and tribal land

CD2 is the largest district in Arizona geographically12. It includes Flagstaff, Prescott and a huge swath of northern and central Arizona, and stretches down to the outskirts of Tucson3. It takes in Apache, Coconino, Gila, Navajo, and Yavapai counties in their entirety and portions of Graham, Maricopa, Mohave, and Pinal counties13. Fourteen tribes call the district home and nearly 20% of its residents are Native American.14

The Watch's county figures measure each county's full vote for governor, not the House vote inside the district lines. The district's weighted move of 18.7 points matches the state's because Maricopa County dominates it: Maricopa cast 1,526,333 votes for governor, but only part of the county is in the district.

The counties that sit entirely inside the district moved by different amounts. Apache County moved 20 points, from D+19.0 to D+38.5. Coconino, home to Flagstaff, moved 18. Navajo County moved 16, from R+17.2 to R+1.6, almost flipping. Yavapai is the largest county entirely in the district, with 120,215 votes for governor. It moved 12 points, from R+33.9 to R+21.8, and Gila moved 13. Mohave, which is only partly in the district, moved 12. The Navajo Times reported that during one Wednesday update, most of Crane's gains came from Yavapai County2.

These moves compare a presidential-year electorate with a midterm one. Take Apache County, where 26,730 people voted for governor: its 20-point move could come from who turned out as much as from voters changing their minds. The county counts can't tell the two apart.

What's still out

Crane was first elected to Congress in 2022, when he beat Democrat Tom O'Halleran15. Based on AP's estimate, about 3,500 ballots remain to be counted in the district. That is fewer than Crane's lead. Under state law, an automatic recount is required when the margin between the top two candidates is one-half of 1% or less of the votes cast for both2. On the current count, that line is about 1,600 votes, and Crane's lead is more than twice that. On Wednesday, Nez's campaign said "there are many ballots that remain to be counted."12 Counties must finish their canvass by Nov. 19, and the official statewide canvass is Nov. 2316.

The race is close against a strong Democratic year statewide. Gov. Katie Hobbs beat Andy Biggs by 13.1 points, an 18.7-point move from Donald Trump's 5.5-point win in Arizona in 2024. Democrats have already won 252 House seats, past the 218 needed for a majority, so this seat will not decide control of the House. KTAR reported that if Crane's lead holds, Democrats will have a 5-4 majority in the state's U.S. House caucus after Republicans entered the election with a 6-3 advantage.5
