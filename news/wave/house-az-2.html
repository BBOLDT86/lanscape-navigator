Eli Crane holds Arizona's sprawling 2nd District by 3,834 votes

Eli Crane, a Republican in the House Freedom Caucus, beat former Navajo Nation President Jonathan Nez by 1.2 points in their rematch. Gov. Katie Hobbs carried Arizona by 13, and the rural counties around the district moved sharply toward Democrats.

Rep. Eli Crane is going back to Washington, but by a much narrower margin than last time. With 99% of the expected vote counted, the Republican had 162,753 votes and Democrat Jonathan Nez had 158,919. That is a lead of 3,834 votes, or 1.2 points, out of 327,651 cast. Curtis Goodwin, the Libertarian candidate1 according to the Arizona Daily Sun, won 5,979 votes. The Associated Press called the race for Crane on Monday, almost a week after Election Day2, which gives him his third term in the U.S. House2.

A rematch that tightened

Nez led the Navajo Nation from 2019-2023, but lost his reelection bid to President Buu Nygren in 20223. Crane is a former Navy SEAL, an ally of President Donald Trump and a member of the hard-right House Freedom Caucus2. He beat Nez easily in 2024. This time, KJZZ reported, the race looked closer after both Republicans and Democrats began directing more resources toward the race in the weeks leading up to the election4.

The Arizona Mirror reported that the Nez campaign went after Crane's votes to continue the Iran War, give tax breaks to companies that build data centers and cut food assistance and healthcare programs5. Nez also pointed out that Crane lives in Oro Valley, which is in Congressional District 65, and said he was counting on buyer's remorse from those who supported Crane and Trump in 20246. Crane's campaign called Nez a career politician and cited Nez's defeat in his 2022 reelection7. The Navajo-Hopi Observer reported that Nez attended the state Clean Elections debate in September, while Crane did not respond to the request to participate8.

Crane had more money. He raised more than $11.9 million to fund his reelection bid to Nez's $5.8 million6, and outside groups added about $5.3 million from groups backing Crane and $2.4 million to boost Nez6. The biggest outside spender, the Trump-linked No Going Back PAC, put $3.4 million into supporting Crane and opposing Nez9. One of its TV ads went wrong: according to the Arizona Republic, it showed photos of Myron Lizer, who served as Nez's vice president from 2019-2310. On the Democratic side, House Majority PAC, House Democrats' main super PAC7, spent for Nez. During the count, the lead in the race switched hands multiple times as counties tallied ballots.4

The district on the wave

The 2nd District covers about 60% of Arizona, and tribal land makes up a large portion of it11. It includes Flagstaff, Prescott and a huge swath of northern and central Arizona, and stretches down to the outskirts of Tucson12, and it takes in portions of nine of Arizona's 15 counties13.

The county numbers on the wave map come from the governor's race and cover each whole county, so they include voters outside the district. That matters most for Maricopa County. Its move of 20 points, from R+3.5 to D+16.2 on more than 1.5 million votes, was driven by the Phoenix area, and the district holds only a small part of the county. Maricopa also dominates the district's weighted move of +18.7.

In the rural counties the pattern is clearer. The two counties Democrats already won moved the most. Apache County, home to Window Rock, went from D+19.0 to D+38.5, and Coconino County, around Flagstaff, went from D+19.9 to D+37.9. Navajo County nearly flipped, falling from R+17.2 to R+1.6. Graham County moved 18 points but stayed at R+30.3. The deepest-red counties moved the least: Yavapai, around Prescott, went from R+33.9 to R+21.8; Mohave moved 12 points and Gila 13. Those counties still gave Republicans large margins, and that was enough for Crane.

These moves do not show voters changing their minds. The data block has the 2026 vote totals but no 2024 county turnout to compare them with. So the county figures cannot tell people who switched parties apart from changes in who turned out.

What the result means

Crane first won the seat in 2022, defeating the Democratic congressman at the time, Tom O'Halleran14. No incumbent lost here. KJZZ said the 2nd was one of three districts in Arizona — along with CD5 and CD8 — once considered safe Republican strongholds that the GOP narrowly managed to hold4. KNAU described Nez's defeat as a rare example of Democrats' blue wave failing to materialize in Arizona2. Statewide, Katie Hobbs beat Andy Biggs by 13.1 points.

Crane will return to a House run by Democrats, who have 252 seats called against 162 for Republicans, with 21 races still uncalled. About 1% of the district's expected vote is still out. KTAR reported that the margin sits outside the threshold to trigger an automatic recount9. Nez did not immediately comment on the AP's projection on Monday.2 Goodwin's 5,979 votes are more than Crane's margin, but the count cannot show how his voters would have split without him on the ballot.
